description: >-
  The Caucasian Rock Lizard is a small, fast lizard of rocky places in the Greater Caucasus. It is nonvenomous, but colour alone makes it hard to tell from similar lizards.
overview: >-
  The Caucasian Rock Lizard (Darevskia caucasica) is a small, fast rock lizard. In Georgia it lives in rocky areas of the Greater Caucasus, and both its finds here and DNA samples from here have been confirmed. The species contains groups that differ in their DNA, though the boundaries between them are still being clarified. It has no venom and is not dangerous to people. You cannot, however, tell it apart from [similar rock lizards](/lizards/darevskia) by color alone.
habitat: >-
  The Caucasian Rock Lizard lives on rocky slopes, among piles of stones and along the edges of valleys. In Georgia, it has been found in rocky parts of the Greater Caucasus, including the Truso Valley, Tbatana and Lebarde.

  traits:

      It has a slender body and a long tail. After an injury, its tail may look short or appear to have grown back.
    - >-
      Its color may be greenish, yellowish-brown, gray or brown. It may have light stripes and dark spots on its sides. Other rock lizards have similar colors too, so you cannot identify the Caucasian Rock Lizard by color alone.
    - >-
      Scale details on the head and the rear part of the body may help a specialist tell the species apart. There is no need to catch the lizard, though.
    - >-
      In the upper reaches of the Terek, the ranges of the Caucasian Rock Lizard and the [Daghestanian Rock Lizard](darevskia-daghestanica) border each other. The two species can even interbreed. There, you will find it especially hard to tell them apart by color alone.
    - >-
      In the Greater Caucasus, the ranges of the Caucasian Rock Lizard and the [Mixed Rock Lizard](darevskia-mixta) meet along only a short stretch. The two lizards look alike, so do not rely on colour alone.
    - >-
      Two lizards were studied together in a valley in northern Georgia, and a 2023 paper called one of them D. rudis. According to a 2026 list, the lizards known by that name in Georgia tentatively belong to the [Obscure Rock Lizard](darevskia-obscura). Seen from above, when their size is not apparent, the two lizards from the valley may look alike: both have flat heads and long legs. The D. rudis studied had, on average, more scales around the middle of the body than the other lizard. In a poor photo, though, you cannot count these scales.
    - >-
      [Brauner's Rock Lizard](darevskia-brauneri) also occurs in the valleys of western Georgia. From a distance, you may mistake it for another rock lizard: both may look small and greenish. Brauner's Rock Lizard has a larger body and head than the other lizard, but a single photo does not always show the difference clearly.
    - >-
      In a valley in northern Georgia, researchers studied the Caucasian rock lizard alongside [Derjugin’s lizard](darevskia-derjugini). At a glance, these small lizards can be confused when their body shape is hard to see. Derjugin’s lizard mostly moves on the ground. The difference shows from the side: the Caucasian rock lizard has a flatter head and longer legs. So if you can, take a photo of the lizard from the side as well.
