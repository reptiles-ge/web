commonName: Western barn owl
location: Year-round resident in Georgia
description: >-
  A pale barn owl with a heart-shaped face and no ear tufts. Confirmed in Georgia
  as a year-round resident and breeder on the 2024 national bird checklist.
overview: >-
  The western barn owl (Tyto alba) is a pale, medium-sized owl with a heart-shaped
  face and dark eyes. It lives and breeds in Georgia year-round, mainly in lowland
  farmland. It normally poses no threat to people, but may defend itself near a
  nest.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, western barn owls occur mainly in lowland farmland. A 2025 study
  confirmed breeding in Abkhazia, Adjara, Imereti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli and
  Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. That breeding list covers those study sites, and it
  leaves the rest of each region open. On this page, region shading comes from
  the records-by-region table: a region counts as distribution only when its
  status is confirmed. The study list stays a separate literature note. They
  often nest in cavities in old houses and churches and hunt in nearby open
  areas.
diet: >-
  Small mammals made up 98.6% of prey found in pellets collected in Georgia. By
  number, roughly half were rodents and nearly as many were shrews; rodents
  accounted for more prey by weight. Shrews were more common at some sites. Birds
  and other animals were rare. The owl regurgitates indigestible remains as
  pellets, which reveal what it has eaten.
behavior: >-
  It hunts mainly at night and rests in a cavity or quiet building by day. Rather
  than the deep hoot of other owls, it gives a long, harsh screech; it may hiss
  near the nest. Its heart-shaped face directs sound towards its ears, helping it
  find prey in the dark. It does not build a stick nest: it lays eggs in a building
  cavity, tree hollow, cliff crevice or nest box. The number of eggs varies with
  food supply. The female does most of the incubation while the male brings food.
conservation: >-
  Georgia's 2024 scientific bird checklist gives the western barn owl a national
  category of Least Concern. Georgia's official 2014 Red List still lists it as
  Endangered, based on a then very small population. Its range in Georgia has
  expanded since then; that does not itself
  change the official listing. Road deaths, electrocution and shooting have been
  documented in Georgia. Do not catch wild birds.
interaction: >-
  The western barn owl normally poses no threat to people, but it may strike in
  defence near a nest. Do not approach the nest or catch the bird.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A pale, medium-sized owl with a heart-shaped face, dark eyes and no ear tufts.
    Its harsh screech is another useful identification clue.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length at species level is about 33–39 cm, wingspan about 80–95 cm, mass
      often about 250–350 g (heavier in some subspecies). Females are usually
      slightly heavier than males. The head is smoothly rounded with no ear
      tufts. The facial disc is heart-shaped and usually pale; the eyes are
      dark. The bill is short and hooked. The legs are long; the tail is short
      and rather square. In flight the bird looks pale, with dangling legs and
      an undulating path.
    - >-
      Upperparts are grey-brown to buff with fine mottling; underparts are
      white to buff, sometimes with dark speckles. Plumage varies, so colour
      alone is unreliable for identifying a subspecies.
    - >-
      In car headlights at night, the underside of a [tawny owl](strix-aluco)
      (Strix aluco)
      can look very pale and be mistaken for a barn owl. The barn owl has a
      white, heart-shaped face; the tawny owl's face is rounded and brown or
      grey. In flight, the barn owl moves slowly back and forth over open fields,
      while the tawny owl often flies directly between trees.
    - >-
      If you hear a harsh screech from a pale owl over lowland farmland at night,
      check for the heart-shaped face and dark eyes too. Do not catch the bird.

facts:
  - >-
    Tyto alba is called Western Barn Owl in English. In older sources, Common
    Barn Owl sometimes referred to a broader group. The American and eastern
    barn owls in that group (Tyto furcata and Tyto javanica) are now treated as
    separate species.
faq:
  - question: Is western barn owl found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. It lives and breeds in Georgia year-round. It has spread through
      lowland farmland in recent decades.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Mainly in lowland farmland, especially near old buildings and open areas.
      A 2025 study reported breeding in Abkhazia, Adjara, Imereti, Kakheti,
      Kvemo Kartli and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. On the map, a region counts as
      distribution only when the records table marks its status as confirmed.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      Heart-shaped pale face, dark eyes, no ear tufts, pale underparts, long
      legs. At night listen for a harsh screech, not a deep woodland hoot.
      Tawny owl is darker and round-faced.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 33–39 cm long, with a wingspan of about 80–95 cm.
  - question: What does it eat in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Mainly small mammals. In a 2023–2025 pellet study, mammals were 98.6% of
      prey items; rodents dominated biomass, with shrews also common. Birds and
      other prey were rare.
  - question: When is it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      All year, according to the 2024 checklist. It is easiest to detect at
      night by voice or at dusk near nest and roost buildings.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. It often nests in cavities in old houses and churches.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It normally poses no threat to people, but may strike in defence near a
      nest; do not approach or catch it. Georgia's official 2014 Red List still
      lists it as Endangered.
