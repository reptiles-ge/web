commonName: Common nightingale
location: Breeding migrant in Georgia
description: >-
  A small, plain brown songbird with a rusty tail and a powerful song. Confirmed
  in Georgia as a breeding migrant on the 2024 national bird checklist; it does
  not stay the winter.
overview: >-
  The common nightingale (Luscinia megarhynchos) is a small songbird in the
  family Muscicapidae. In Georgia it is confirmed as a breeding migrant and a
  bird of passage. In the non-breeding season, including winter, it usually does
  not remain in the country.

  Adults are about 15–16.5 cm long. The plumage is mostly plain brown, with a
  rustier tail. It is harmless to people.

  IUCN lists the species as Least Concern. The 2024 Georgian bird checklist also
  assigns it national LC status.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia the common nightingale is found in the breeding season and on
  passage, while it usually does not remain in winter. It favours dense scrub,
  woodland edges, river valleys, gardens, and other places with thick low
  vegetation. It is often found near water.

  It nests on or very near the ground in dense cover. This page does not have a
  full list of Georgian regions or municipalities, so the range map is empty.
  The species is confirmed at country level, but that does not mean it occurs
  evenly in every region or every habitat type.
diet: >-
  The common nightingale feeds mainly on insects and other small invertebrates,
  often searched for on the ground and in fallen leaves. It also eats berries
  seasonally.

  This is general species biology.
behavior: >-
  The common nightingale is active by day, but its famous song is often heard at
  night too. The male sings. The song is loud and varied, with whistles, trills,
  and gurgling sounds. One characteristic element is a strong, gradually rising
  whistle.

  Unpaired males often sing actively at night. After pairing, song is heard more
  around dawn and evening. The bird often hides in dense scrub, so it is easier
  to hear than to see.

  The common nightingale is a long-distance migrant and winters in sub-Saharan
  Africa. In Georgia it is a breeding migrant and a bird of passage, not a
  winter visitor.

  It nests on or near the ground. At species level a clutch is often about 4–5
  eggs, and incubation lasts about two weeks.
conservation: >-
  IUCN lists the common nightingale as Least Concern. The 2024 Georgian bird
  checklist also gives it national LC status. It is not included in Georgia’s
  2014 Red List.

  In some countries, for example the United Kingdom, the species has a more
  serious local status and is red-listed because its range has contracted. That
  does not describe its Georgian status.

  Do not catch wild common nightingales.
interaction: >-
  The common nightingale is not a threat to people. Do not catch wild birds or
  take them home. The nest is often on or near the ground, so avoid damaging
  nesting places when moving through scrub or other dense cover.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The common nightingale is a mostly plain brown bird, roughly the size of a
    European robin. One of the best visual marks is the rusty-brown tail. A pale
    eye-ring is often visible.

    More than its appearance, its strong and varied song draws attention. In
    Georgia the common nightingale is a breeding migrant and is not a typical
    winter bird. It is most often confused with thrush nightingale or European
    robin.
  traits:
    - >-
      The common nightingale is about 15–16.5 cm long. In adult birds studied
      through ringing in the United Kingdom, average mass is about 21 g and
      often falls around 18–24 g. Those data come from British samples, not from
      Georgia.

      The body is compact, the tail relatively broad, and the bill straight.
      Male and female look very similar.
    - >-
      Adult common nightingales are plain warm brown above, with a paler,
      whitish or buff underside. One of the most useful visual marks is the
      rusty-brown tail. A pale eye-ring is often visible.

      Some field guides describe the Caucasian form as paler above, with a more
      distinct supercilium, but in the field in Georgia identification to species
      is more reliable. Young birds are more mottled than adults.
    - >-
      The thrush nightingale (Luscinia luscinia) is also a confirmed breeding
      migrant in Georgia. It is usually darker, more mottled on the breast, and
      less rusty on the tail. Its song is different too: it lacks the loud,
      rising whistle typical of the common nightingale.

      The European robin (Erithacus rubecula) is easy to separate by its orange
      face and breast, and is found in Georgia year-round. Male bluethroats
      (Luscinia svecica / Cyanecula svecica) have a bright blue throat.
    - >-
      The common nightingale is harmless to people. In Georgia during the warm
      months, if you hear strong song from scrub and the bird is plain brown
      with a rusty tail, it may be this species.

      An orange breast points to European robin, while a bright blue throat
      points to bluethroat. Do not catch wild birds.

facts:
  - >-
    Described by C. L. Brehm in 1831. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows IOC
    World Bird List and OSME Region List names: order Passeriformes, family
    Muscicapidae, genus Luscinia. Chats were formerly placed in Turdidae; NPLG
    Wiki Dictionaries still lists thrushes. Modern classification is in the Old
    World flycatchers.
  - >-
    Widely recognised subspecies are L. m. megarhynchos (western Europe, North
    Africa, Asia Minor), L. m. africana (the Caucasus and adjacent areas, from
    eastern Turkey to southwestern Iran), and L. m. golzii (Central Asia). The
    expected Caucasian form is africana. Field identification in Georgia is to
    species. No Georgian series assigned to subspecies is cited on this page.
  - >-
    The usual Georgian name is ბულბული. The more precise term is სამხრეთული
    ბულბული (Ilia State University dictionary; NPLG uses სამხრული ბულბული).
    იადონი is also used for the genus Luscinia. English names include common
    nightingale, rufous nightingale, and nightingale. The 2024 checklist uses
    Common Nightingale. The thrush nightingale (Luscinia luscinia) is a
    separate species.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes are BB; M, seasonality quality A, global and national
    Red List LC, occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph), RD (ringing
    data), and SR (sound recording). That is country-level confirmation, not a
    regional map. The same list also codes Luscinia luscinia as BB; M. Some
    popular Georgian sources treat the thrush nightingale as passage only; the
    2024 checklist accepts it as a breeder too.
