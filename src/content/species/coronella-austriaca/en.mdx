description: >-
  A harmless, smooth-scaled snake of Georgia — secretive and often
  mistaken for a viper.
overview: >-
  The smooth snake (Coronella austriaca) is a nonvenomous snake found in Georgia. Because of its coloring, it is often [mistaken for a viper](/snakes/shxamiani-gvelis-amocnoba). Do not rely on color alone to identify it. Look at its smooth scales, round pupils and the pattern on its head. It is usually about 50–70 cm long and rarely grows to 90 cm. Worldwide, it faces little threat. The smooth snake is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
habitat: >-
  On this page’s map, a region is marked as having a confirmed range if there are at least five records from it—photos from the editors or public observations. There are at least five such records from Adjara, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. There are fewer than five records from Guria, Shida Kartli, Imereti and Abkhazia; this does not mean the species is absent there. It is found at forest edges, in open woodland, among bushes, on rocky slopes and in foothills. It often hides under rocks, tree stumps and other cover. It is found more often in damp and mountainous areas than in the very dry semi-desert of the southeast.
diet: >-
  The smooth snake mainly hunts lizards. It wraps its body around its prey to hold it. Adults may also eat small mammals and, rarely, other small snakes.
behavior: >-
  The smooth snake is secretive and often hides under a stone or other cover. It may come out briefly during the day to warm up. When threatened, it flattens its head and sometimes looks more like a viper. If caught, it may [bite](/snakes/gvelis-nakbeni), but it has no venom. It usually gives birth to 2–15 live young.
conservation: >-
  Worldwide, the smooth snake faces a low risk of extinction. It is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List. In some places, changes to where it lives and people killing it may pose a threat, especially when people mistake it for a viper.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The smooth snake belongs to the same family as grass snakes and is harmless to people, though it sometimes looks like a viper. To tell them apart, look at the scales, the shape of the pupil and the markings on the head. You do not need to pick up the snake to count its scales.
  traits:
    - >-
      The copper snake has smooth scales on its back, with no raised line down the middle. Vipers have a small raised line on each scale along the back. The copper snake’s head is narrower than a viper’s and less clearly set off from its neck, though it may flatten its head when threatened. Head shape alone is therefore not a reliable way to tell them apart.
    - >-
      The smooth snake has round pupils, while vipers in Georgia have narrow, vertical pupils. A dark stripe often runs from its eye toward the back of its head. It may also have a dark crown- or heart-shaped marking on top of its head.
    - >-
      Its color may be coppery, brownish or gray. It often has two rows of small dark spots along its back rather than one continuous zigzag. Its belly is often dark gray or reddish. The markings on young copper snakes may be more distinct. It is usually about 50–70 cm long, though it rarely approaches 90 cm.
    - >-
      The [nose-horned viper](vipera-transcaucasiana) may also have separate dark spots on its gray back. It has a clearly visible horn on its nose, a raised ridge down the middle of its scales, and vertical pupils. The [Transcaucasian racer](zamenis-hohenackeri) also often has two rows of dark spots on its back and a dark stripe behind its eye. Do not identify a snake by its back pattern alone, and do not approach a snake you do not recognize.
    - >-
      The smooth snake is not venomous. If you encounter one, keep your distance and let it pass. You might mistake it for a viper, so do not pick up a snake you cannot identify.

faq:
  - question: Is the smooth snake found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. According to 2026 records, the smooth snake has been confirmed in Georgia.
  - question: Where might I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      On this page’s map, its presence is marked as confirmed in Adjara, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. In Guria, Shida Kartli, Imereti and Abkhazia, there are too few records to mark its presence as confirmed on the map. You may encounter it at forest edges, in open woodland and scrub, on rocky slopes, and in foothills.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to humans?
    answer: >-
      No. The smooth snake has no venom and is not dangerous to people. It may bite if you catch it, so don’t pick it up.
  - question: How do I tell it from a viper?
    answer: >-
      Look for smooth scales, round pupils and small dark spots arranged in two rows along the back. Vipers have a raised ridge down the middle of their scales and vertical pupils. Colour or head shape alone is not a reliable way to tell them apart.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      The smooth snake is usually about 50–70 cm long, though it rarely approaches 90 cm.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      It mainly eats lizards. It may also eat small mammals and, rarely, other small snakes.
  - question: Does it lay eggs or give live birth?
    answer: >-
      The smooth snake’s young hatch from eggs inside the mother’s body and are born alive. Usually, 2–15 young are born at a time.
  - question: What should I do if I meet one?
    answer: >-
      If you come across a smooth snake, keep your distance and let it pass. Don’t kill it or pick it up. Don’t touch a snake you don’t recognize to find out what it is — it could be a viper.
