overview: |-
  The European glass lizard (Pseudopus apodus) is a large legless lizard in the Anguidae family. In Georgia, it is often mistaken for a snake. It can be [told apart from a snake](/lizards/xvlikis-da-gvelxokeras-gansxvaveba) by its movable eyelids, ear openings and a noticeable fold along the sides of its body.
  A 2026 scientific list confirms that it occurs in Georgia, but lists it only in eastern Georgia. The map on this page also shows publicly reported observations in Abkhazia and Adjara. The map’s “confirmed” label is based on the number of records. This does not mean the 2026 list also confirms its presence in western Georgia.
  The European glass lizard is not venomous and poses no danger to people. It reaches about one meter in length. Some large ones grow to 1.35 meters. Its risk of extinction has been assessed as low worldwide. A separate assessment in 2021 also found the risk low in Georgia. It is not on Georgia’s Red List.
  If you encounter one, leave it where it is and do not pick it up. To identify it, take a photo from a safe distance.
habitat: |-
  The glass lizard is found in warm, open places: fields, sparse woodlands, among bushes, on rocky slopes, in river valleys, in gardens and along the edges of village yards.
  In eastern Georgia, it also lives in dry and semi-dry parts of the Mtkvari, Iori and Alazani river valleys.
diet: >-
  The glass lizard often eats snails and slugs. It also catches insects and sometimes small lizards and other small animals. Its strong jaws make it easier to eat animals with shells. Because it eats snails and slugs, it may be useful in a garden.
behavior: |-
  The glass lizard lives on the ground and is mostly active during the day. In eastern Georgia, it is most active in spring and early summer. The female lays eggs and sometimes stays near the nest to protect them.
  When frightened, it may hiss or bite. This is how it defends itself and has nothing to do with venom. It can also shed its tail. Don’t touch a wild animal with your hands—take a photo to identify it.
conservation: |-
  A global assessment found that Pseudopus apodus faces little threat. It is not on Georgia’s Red List. A 2021 national assessment also found that it faces little threat.
interaction: >-
  It is harmless to people and has no venom. If you see it in your yard or garden, leave it where it is. Do not kill it just because you think it is a snake. If you are unsure what animal it is, keep a safe distance and take a photo without touching it. Call 112 only if there is a real danger.
