commonName: "Pond Slider"
location: "Vicinity of Tbilisi and Batumi — introduced"
description: >-
  An introduced freshwater turtle — a pet species, not native to Georgia.
overview: >-
  The pond slider (Trachemys scripta) is not native to Georgia. Tarkhnishvili
  et al. 2026: it was unintentionally introduced during the last decade as a
  common pet species; reproducing populations exist in the vicinities of
  Tbilisi and Batumi. The subspecies is T. s. elegans. The checklist record
  cites Reshetnikov et al. 2023.
habitat: >-
  Ponds, lakes, and other fresh waters. Reproducing populations are reported
  from the vicinities of Tbilisi and Batumi — not the whole country.
diet: >-
  An omnivorous freshwater turtle — invertebrates, plant material, and small
  vertebrates.
behavior: >-
  Swims and often basks on the bank. Not a land tortoise.
conservation: >-
  Introduced, not native. A pet species of the last decade; releasing animals
  into the wild founds new populations.

faq:
  - question: Is it native to Georgia?
    answer: >-
      No. Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026 list it as introduced: a pet species of the
      last decade. Native water turtles are the pond turtle and the Caspian
      turtle.
  - question: Where is it confirmed?
    answer: >-
      Reproducing populations in the vicinities of Tbilisi and Batumi
      (Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026). Those are two foci, not all of Georgia.
  - question: How do I tell it from native water turtles?
    answer: >-
      T. s. elegans has a broad red stripe behind the eye. Pond and Caspian
      turtles lack that stripe. The land tortoise (Testudo graeca) has a
      high-domed shell.
  - question: Can I release a pet turtle into a lake?
    answer: >-
      No. The checklist treats this species as a pet and an unintentional
      introduction. Do not release pet turtles into ponds or lakes.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      Site classification: Harmless. It has no venom.
