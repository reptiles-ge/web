description: >-
  A large burrowing wolf spider confirmed in Georgia. The name “tarantula” here
  means a large Lycosa wolf spider, not a tropical pet tarantula.
overview: >-
  The South Russian tarantula or Songarian wolf spider (Lycosa singoriensis) is
  a wolf spider in the family Lycosidae. In Georgian, “tarantula” is often used
  more broadly, but on this page it means Lycosa singoriensis specifically, not
  tropical Theraphosidae spiders. The species is confirmed in Georgia. Sourced
  records are known from [Kvemo Kartli](/regions/kvemo-kartli) and
  [Kakheti](/regions/kakheti), although this does not mean it is evenly
  distributed across every region of the country. At species level, female body
  length is about 18–31 mm and male body length is 14–27 mm. Legs are not
  included in that size.
habitat: >-
  The tarantula is associated with dry, open habitats: steppe and semi-steppe
  grassland, dry plains, and soil where it can dig a burrow. In Georgia, the
  sourced confirmed regions are Kvemo Kartli and Kakheti. These records do not
  mean the species is distributed throughout the whole country.
diet: >-
  The tarantula is an active predator and does not use an orb web to catch prey.
  It mainly hunts insects and other small invertebrates on the ground or near
  the burrow entrance. The diet of this species in Georgia has not been
  separately fully studied.
behavior: >-
  Large Lycosa species often live in silk-lined soil burrows and hunt from the
  entrance. The female carries the egg sac on the rear of the body, and newly
  hatched spiderlings stay on the mother’s back for a while, a behavior typical
  of wolf spiders. A full seasonal activity calendar for the species in Georgia
  is not described separately.
conservation: >-
  IUCN has not assessed Lycosa singoriensis separately. The species is not on
  Georgia’s 2014 Red List. This page does not have enough data on the size or
  trend of the Georgian population.
interaction: >-
  This spider uses venom to subdue prey. For people, a
  [bite](/spiders/obobis-nakbeni) is usually not life-threatening, but it can
  cause local pain, redness, and swelling. The spider does not try to attack
  people and usually bites only in defense. Do not touch it or pick it up. If
  bitten, wash the area with soap and water and watch the symptoms. If pain
  becomes strong or keeps increasing, swelling is significant, breathing becomes
  difficult, or other serious symptoms appear, seek medical help and call 112.
  Do not cut, suck, or burn the bite.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A large, darkly patterned wolf spider with strong legs. It does not sit in a
    capture orb web and is often found on the ground or near a burrow entrance.
  traits:
    - >-
      At species level, female body length is about 18–31 mm and male body
      length is 14–27 mm. Legs are not included in that size.
    - >-
      The body is hairy, the legs often have a ringed pattern, and the abdomen
      is dark and spotted. Wolf spiders also have a characteristic eye
      arrangement: the middle pair is relatively large and visible from the
      front.
    - >-
      The tarantula does not spin a capture orb web. If a large spider with bold
      yellow-and-black colouring is sitting at the center of an orb web, you are
      more likely seeing [Argiope](argiope-bruennichi), not Lycosa singoriensis.
