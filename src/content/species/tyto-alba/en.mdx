commonName: Western barn owl
location: Year-round resident in Georgia
description: >-
  A pale barn owl with a heart-shaped face and no ear tufts. Confirmed in Georgia
  as a year-round resident and breeder on the 2024 national bird checklist.
overview: >-
  Tyto alba is a light-colored, medium-sized owl with a heart-shaped face and dark eyes. It is found in Georgia year-round and nests mainly in rural lowland areas. It is generally not dangerous to people, but it may defend itself if someone approaches its nest.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the western barn owl is found mainly in lowland farming areas. Its nesting was confirmed in 2025 in Abkhazia, Adjara, Imereti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. This does not mean it nests throughout these regions. It often nests in cavities in old houses and churches and hunts in nearby open areas.
diet: >-
  The western barn owl regurgitates undigested parts of its food in clumps. Studying these clumps reveals what it ate. Of the prey found in clumps collected in Georgia, 98.6% were small mammals. By number, about half were rodents and almost as many were shrews. Rodents made up the larger share by weight. In some places, shrews were more common. Birds and other animals were rarely found.
behavior: >-
  It hunts mainly at night and rests during the day in a cavity or a quiet building. Instead of the deep “hoo-hoo” of other owls, it makes a long, sharp cry; hissing can also be heard near the nest. Its heart-shaped face directs sound toward its ears, helping it find prey in the dark. It does not build a nest from twigs: it lays its eggs in a cavity in a building, a hollow tree, a rock crevice or a nest box. The number of eggs varies with how much food is available. The female usually keeps the eggs warm, while the male brings food.
conservation: >-
  A 2024 scientific checklist of Georgia’s birds lists the western barn owl as facing relatively little threat in Georgia. Georgia’s official 2014 Red List, however, lists it as threatened with extinction because there were very few of these birds in the country at the time. Since then, it has spread across a wider area of Georgia, though this does not automatically change its status on the official list. There have been confirmed cases in Georgia of these birds dying on roads, being electrocuted and being shot at. Do not catch wild birds.
interaction: >-
  The western barn owl is usually not dangerous to people, but it may attack to defend itself if you approach its nest. Do not approach the nest or catch the bird.

identification:
  summary: >-
    It is a medium-sized, light-colored owl. It has a heart-shaped face and dark eyes, but no tufts of feathers near its ears. Its sharp call can also help you recognize it.
  traits:
    - >-
      This bird is about 33–39 cm long, with a wingspan of about 80–95 cm. It often weighs about 250–350 g, though birds in some groups within the species are heavier. Females are usually slightly heavier than males. Its head is round and smooth, with no ear-like tufts of feathers. The feathers around its face form a heart shape and are usually pale. Its eyes are dark, and its beak is short and hooked. It has long legs and a short, almost square tail. In flight, it looks pale, holds its legs down and follows a gently undulating path.
    - >-
      The upper side ranges from gray-brown to brownish beige, with small spots. The underside ranges from white to beige, sometimes with dark dots. Color varies, so color alone is not a reliable way to identify the subspecies.
    - >-
      At night, [Strix aluco](strix-aluco) can look very pale from below in car headlights, making it easy to mistake for the western barn owl. The western barn owl has a white, heart-shaped face; Strix aluco has a round, brown or gray face. The western barn owl flies slowly back and forth over an open field, while Strix aluco often flies straight between trees.
    - >-
      If you hear the sharp cry of a light-colored owl at night in a rural lowland area, look for a heart-shaped face and dark eyes. Do not catch the bird.

faq:
  - question: Is western barn owl found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The western barn owl is found in Georgia year-round and nests there. In recent decades, it has spread into rural lowland areas.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      It is found mostly in rural lowland areas, especially near old buildings and open spaces. Its nesting was described in 2025 in Abkhazia, Adjara, Imereti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      This owl has a pale, heart-shaped face and dark eyes. Its belly is pale, and its legs are long. It has no ear-like feather tufts on its head. At night, its sharp cry can be heard rather than the deep “hoo-hoo” heard in the forest. The common owl is darker and has a rounder face.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      Its length is about 33–39 cm, and its wingspan is about 80–95 cm.
  - question: What does it eat in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      It mainly eats small mammals. Of the prey counted in regurgitated remains studied in 2023–2025, 98.6% were mammals. Rodents made up the largest share by total weight, and shrews were also common. Birds and other prey were rare.
  - question: When is it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      According to the 2024 list, it can be found year-round. It is easiest to hear at night or see at dusk near the building where it nests and rests.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. It nests in Georgia, often in cavities in old houses and churches.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It is not usually dangerous to people, but it may attack in self-defense near its nest. Do not approach or catch the bird. It is listed as threatened with extinction in Georgia’s official 2014 Red List.
