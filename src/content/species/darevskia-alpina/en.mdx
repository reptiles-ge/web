overview: >-
  The Abkhazian rock lizard (Darevskia alpina) is a non-venomous rock lizard of the high mountains of the western Caucasus. It is listed as a candidate in Georgia’s 2026 checklist. Darevsky first described it in 1967 as a subspecies of the Caucasian lizard. The place named in the original description is Terskol, in Kabardino-Balkaria, Russia, at about 2,200 metres above sea level — not in Georgia. Its Georgian name refers to Abkhazia, while the English name alpine rock lizard and the Russian name “alpine lizard” refer to altitude. The species remains on the list in the 2026 report, but the report gives only brief information about it. It may have arisen from interbreeding between the “caucasica” and “saxicola” groups. The report provides no list of records from Georgia, no DNA data for identifying the species, and no specifically named location. For that reason, this atlas does not shade its map. Other international publications mention the species in the western Caucasus, including Abkhazia and western Georgia, but those reports of its presence do not come from the 2026 report. It has no venom and is not dangerous to people.
habitat: >-
  This species is a high-mountain rock lizard. It occurs in the western part of the Greater Caucasus, west of the Baksan Valley, at roughly 1,650 m and above. Its range is limited to the Western Caucasus: the mountainous parts of Russia, Abkhazia and western Georgia. This describes the species’ overall range, not the regional map in this atlas. The 2026 list gives no specific Georgian municipality, elevation range or genetic barcode. In a 2013 paper, Doronin linked specimens from 1912 to Upper Bachiani / Upper Ksani, at about 2,296 m. This site is separate from the species’ known western range, so it is not shown on the map here. Suitable habitat alone cannot tell us which administrative region of Georgia the species occurs in.

It is likely active for only a short part of the year at high elevations, but this has not been measured in Georgia. Do not take a lizard from the wild.
conservation: >-
  In Georgia’s 2026 checklist, it is listed as a candidate for recognition as a separate species. It may have arisen through interbreeding between other species, so more evidence is needed to recognize it as a separate species. In 2009, it was assessed as Vulnerable (VU) on the IUCN Red List. A 2024 assessment gives it the same category. Of the Darevskia species, Georgia’s 2014 Red List includes D. clarkorum, D. dahli and D. mixta, but not D. alpina. This page does not show what status the species has under CITES. A 2013 assessment says that its habitats in the Caucasus are deteriorating because of human activity and climate change. That assessment covers the Caucasus as a whole; it does not establish a separate trend for Georgia. Its numbers in Georgia have not been measured for this atlas.

  traits:

      Its body and head have the typical shape of a rock lizard: a flattened body, a long tail and clearly visible legs. This page does not give measurements of lizards from Georgia. Do not identify the species by its colour in a photo alone.
    - >-
      In appearance, it falls between Brauner’s rock lizard (D. brauneri, part of the saxicola group) and the Caucasian lizard (D. caucasica). The other species in the saxicola group also have almost no obvious physical features that make them easy to tell apart.
    - >-
      The Caucasian rock lizard (D. caucasica) is confirmed on the list. The places named for it in Georgia are Truso (Zakagori), Tbatana and Lebarde. Brauner’s rock lizard is listed only as a possibility; the places named for it are Mestia (Manshura), Martvili and Ertso Lake. The two rock lizards may look similar in color.
    - >-
      The Dagestani rock lizard (D. daghestanica) belongs to the caucasica group. Their ranges are continuous along the upper Terek River. The Adjarian rock lizard (D. adjarica) is listed in southwestern Georgia, a different geographic area. Color cannot distinguish these pairs either.
    - >-
      It is not venomous. It poses almost no risk to people if they do not catch it. Watch it in the wild, but do not pick it up.
