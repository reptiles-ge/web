commonName: Mediterranean black widow
location: Eastern steppe and Kartli
description: >-
  The Mediterranean black widow. It is recorded in Georgia; a bite is medically
  significant.
overview: >-
  The Mediterranean black widow (Latrodectus tredecimguttatus) is a cobweb spider
  in the family Theridiidae — irregular tangle webs, not orb webs. The accepted
  name is Latrodectus tredecimguttatus (Rossi, 1790); it is the type species of
  the genus. In Georgian it is ყარაყურთი (from Turkish karakurt) and შავი ქვრივი;
  English names are Mediterranean black widow and European black widow. The
  southern American black widow Latrodectus mactans is a different species; the
  World Spider Catalog treats it as probably native only to North America. Older
  literature often listed Caucasian records as a subspecies of L. mactans; cited
  records in Otto 2022 still show the formula Latrodectus mactans
  (+tredecimguttatus) for Georgia. If you are searching for a “black widow” in
  Georgia, this page is the species that is actually recorded here. Otto 2022
  lists it for Georgia and calls it abundant in the Caucasus ecoregion. That is
  an ecoregion-level assessment, not even density in all twelve regions. At
  species level the female body is about 7–15 mm, the male about 4–7 mm. The
  venom is neurotoxic; a bite can cause latrodectism. IUCN has not assessed the
  species. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List; the arachnid on that list is the
  fen raft spider (Dolomedes plantarius).
habitat: >-
  Look for it in dry, open ground: steppe, semi-desert shrub, under stones and
  debris, and in low vegetation. Nentwig et al. give species-level habitat as dry
  places — dunes, sandy and shingle beaches, and low vegetation. That is not a
  measured Georgian elevation belt. This atlas colours regions only from named
  records. Otto 2022 names, for Georgia, Tbilisi (Mcheidze 1964 and 1997;
  Pkhakadze 2006), Gori (Mcheidze 1964 and 1997; Shida Kartli), Aspindza
  (Mcheidze 1997; Samtskhe–Javakheti), and the Shiraki and Eldari steppes
  (Mcheidze 1964 and 1997; Kakheti). There is no named record on this page for
  western Georgia, Adjara, Racha, Mtskheta–Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, or the other
  regions — those are not coloured. “Abundant in the Caucasus” does not mean it
  occurs in every high-mountain belt. Photos on this page were not taken in
  Georgia.
diet: >-
  There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page. At species level it
  takes insects and other arthropods in an irregular web. Prey is wrapped, then
  bitten. That is the general trophic niche, not a measured Georgian menu. Do
  not treat it as biological control in a garden.
behavior: >-
  The female usually sits in a web retreat, not in the hub of an orb. The web is
  irregular, often near the ground, among stones and debris or in low plants.
  The species does not hunt people; bites typically happen when clothing, a shoe,
  or a hand presses the spider. Nentwig et al. write that bites have become very
  rare in Europe in recent decades. A full Georgian seasonal calendar is not
  measured on this page. Eggs are in silk sacs; one photo here shows two sacs —
  that specimen is from Turkey, not a Georgian sample. Do not pick it up.
conservation: >-
  IUCN has not assessed the species. Government Resolution No. 190 of 2014 lists
  the fen raft spider (Dolomedes plantarius, VU) among arachnids, not this
  species. A Georgian population trend is not measured on this page. Do not catch
  wild spiders.
interaction: >-
  The spider has venom for prey; it contains latrotoxins. A bite can cause
  latrodectism — severe, lasting pain and systemic symptoms. At species level
  Nentwig et al. write: pain is severe and long-lasting in about two-thirds of
  cases; in more than half it increases in the first hour and often radiates into
  the limbs or abdomen; sweating is common; nausea, raised temperature,
  neuromuscular signs, and hypertension occur in a smaller share. Pain usually
  lasts 1–2 days, other symptoms 1–4 days. This is not a viper bite and not an
  Argiope bite. Death is rare in modern European material; that does not make a
  bite “mild”. If bitten, call 112. Do not handle it, cut, suck, or burn the
  bite. This is educational text, not a medical protocol. An allergic reaction to
  any bite is possible.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A small glossy black spider with a globose abdomen. The female often has red
    dorsal spots, sometimes none. The web is an irregular cobweb, not an orb.
  traits:
    - >-
      At species level female body length is about 7–15 mm (Nentwig et al.); male
      about 4–7 mm. Legs are not included. There is no separate Georgian
      measurement series on this page.
    - >-
      The female cephalothorax (prosoma) is black and glossy; the legs are black.
      The abdomen (opisthosoma) is globose. The back often has three longitudinal
      rows of red blotches — tredecimguttatus means “thirteen-spotted”. Spots may
      be yellow or orange, or absent so the abdomen is entirely black. Ventrally
      there is a red blotch behind the epigastric furrow; this is not always the
      American L. mactans hourglass. The male is smaller; Nentwig et al. describe
      its blotches as whitish.
    - >-
      Seropian et al. 2024 write that in local populations the thirteen red spots
      of adult females are often reduced or lost, leaving thin white margins. A
      fully black female in Georgia does not rule this species out.
    - >-
      The web is irregular (Theridiidae), usually near the ground. It is not an
      Argiope orb with a white zigzag. An adult female often sits in a retreat,
      not in the web centre.
    - >-
      The same genus was confirmed in Georgia in 2024 as Latrodectus pallidus
      (Seropian et al.; Chachuna, Dedoplistskaro, about 648 m) — the abdomen is
      creamy-white, not black. That is the first Caucasus record of that species.
      Latrodectus dahli is in Otto 2022 for Azerbaijan, not from a named Georgian
      record. Steatoda (“false widows”) also occur in Georgia (e.g. Steatoda
      paykulliana, Otto 2022 / Nentwig et al.); they are usually brown, not a
      glossy black globose abdomen. This atlas has no separate profile yet for
      L. pallidus or Steatoda.
    - >-
      This atlas has separate profiles for the wasp spider (Argiope bruennichi)
      and the lobed argiope (Argiope lobata). Those are larger orb-weavers with
      banded or lobed abdomens — not this widow.
facts:
  - >-
    The accepted name is Latrodectus tredecimguttatus (Rossi, 1790). The World
    Spider Catalog treats the species as accepted and as the type species of the
    genus. The original combination was Aranea 13-guttata. Range: Mediterranean,
    Ukraine, the Caucasus, Russia (Europe to southern Siberia), Kazakhstan, the
    Middle East, Iran, Central Asia, China.
  - >-
    Latrodectus mactans (Fabricius, 1775) is a separate species. WSC: probably
    native to North America only; introduced elsewhere. The sources on this page
    do not record L. mactans in Georgia’s fauna.
  - >-
    Georgian names: ყარაყურთი and შავი ქვრივი. English: Mediterranean black
    widow / European black widow. Russian: каракурт. Turkish: karakurt.
  - >-
    Otto 2022 lists 30 records in the Caucasus ecoregion. The Georgian map on
    this page is coloured only from the named points above. Seropian et al. 2024
    do not list L. tredecimguttatus among that paper’s specimens; the new record
    there is L. pallidus.
faq:
  - question: Is the Mediterranean black widow found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Otto 2022 and Nentwig et al. list the species for Georgia. That is
      country-level confirmation, not even occurrence in every region.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Named records are Tbilisi, Gori, Aspindza, and the Shiraki and Eldari
      steppes. Look on dry steppe, under stones, and in low plants. The west and
      the high mountains are not coloured on this page.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      A small black spider with a globose abdomen; the back often has red spots,
      sometimes none. The web is irregular. A creamy-white abdomen points to
      L. pallidus. A yellow-and-black orb-weaver is an Argiope, not this widow.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      At species level the female is about 7–15 mm, the male about 4–7 mm. Legs
      are not included. There is no Georgian series on this page.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      Yes: the venom is medically significant and a bite can cause severe pain
      and systemic symptoms. Call 112 if bitten. Do not handle it. This is not a
      viper bite.
  - question: Is this Latrodectus mactans?
    answer: >-
      No. L. mactans is a North American species. The species recorded in Georgia
      is L. tredecimguttatus. Older texts often mixed the two names.
  - question: Is it protected?
    answer: >-
      IUCN has not assessed it. It is not on the 2014 Red List.
