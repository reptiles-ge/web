# Reptiles.ge writing style

This is the owner-approved house style for every text a reader sees on a species page. It is binding, not advisory. Follow it even where your usual writing habits differ. Do not fall back to a generic "encyclopedia", "AI assistant" or "science blog" voice.

It applies to: `description`, `overview`, `interaction`, `identification.summary`, `identification.traits.*`, `identification.coloration`, `habitat`, `diet`, `behavior`, `conservation`, `faq.*.question`, `faq.*.answer` and `stats.*.value`. Georgian (KA) is written first and is the standard. EN, RU and TR are rewritten in the same style from the final KA.

The content-integrity rules in AGENTS.md and in the stage instructions always win over style: no new facts, no source names in prose, no stronger or weaker claims, no lost numbers, scientific names, links, warnings or 112. Style changes the order, emphasis, sentence shape and voice of facts already present. It never changes what is true. Integrity never requires flat writing: a correct text can and must also be engaging.

## 1. The target in one paragraph

Write like a knowledgeable Georgian field naturalist talking to an intelligent person who has never studied biology. Three registers work together in the same text, often in the same paragraph:

- **A. Field-guide facts.** Short, confident statements. The strongest facts come first and are stated plainly, without a pile of qualifiers.
- **B. A picture.** The reader can see the animal, the place and the moment: dry stony slopes, a summer night, a stone it hides under. Every image comes from facts already in the supplied content.
- **C. Talking to the reader.** At the point where it matters, address the reader directly (polite თქვენ) with one practical consequence or one memorable line.

The result must be simple, human and attractive to read, and still dense with real information. Quality over quantity: remove repetition, filler and hedging, never information. A page that is shorter because it lost facts is a failure. A page that is longer because it repeats itself is also a failure.

## 2. The reference example

This rewrite was approved by the owner as the standard for the whole site. Match its voice, structure and density. Its facts belong to the Levantine viper (გიურზა) only; never copy them to another species.

Before (correct but flat):

> გიურზა (Macrovipera lebetinus) მსხვილი შხამიანი გველგესლაა, რომელიც საქართველოში ძირითადად აღმოსავლეთის მშრალ და ნახევრად მშრალ ადგილებში გვხვდება. მხოლოდ ზურგის ლაქებით ან ზომით მისი ამოცნობა სანდო არ არის; გველს ნუ მიუახლოვდებით. ამ ატლასში გამოიყენება სახელი…

After (the standard):

> გიურზა საქართველოს ყველაზე დიდი გველგესლაა და ერთადერთი, რომელიც კვერცხს დებს. აღმოსავლეთის მშრალ, ქვიან ფერდობებზე ცხოვრობს, შირაქიდან და გარეჯიდან ქვემო ქართლის მთისწინეთამდე. ბოლო ათწლეულებში ალაზნის ხეობამდე და თბილისამდეც მიაღწია. მისი ნაკბენი ჩვენს გველებს შორის ერთ-ერთი ყველაზე საშიშია. ლაქებიანი ზურგით კი უწყინარ მცურავებს ჰგავს. ამიტომ ლაქებს ნუ ენდობით და ახლოს ნუ მიხვალთ.

Why it works:

1. The first sentence carries the two most striking facts (largest viper, the only egg-laying one). Both already existed in the old page, buried in an FAQ answer and in the middle of the behavior field.
2. The name appears once. The following sentences drop the subject, as natural Georgian allows.
3. No hedge stands on a solid fact. "ერთ-ერთი ყველაზე საშიში" keeps the original qualification exactly.
4. Places are concrete (შირაქი, გარეჯი, ქვემო ქართლი) and there is movement (ბოლო ათწლეულებში… მიაღწია).
5. Risk and the lookalike problem take one sentence each, then one direct instruction closes the paragraph.
6. Nothing talks about the website. No fact was added.

## 3. Hard rules

Every rule is checkable. Apply all of them to every field you write or rewrite.

### R1. Lead with the strongest fact

The first sentence of `description` and `overview` states the most striking and most useful supported fact about the animal: a superlative, something unique in Georgia, the main risk, or the single trait that defines it. Do not open with taxonomy, alternative names, a definition of the group, or a sentence about the site.

Inside every other field, the first sentence answers that field's question directly: what it looks like, where it lives, what it eats, when it is active, what its status is.

### R2. Rank facts inside a field

Order: the most interesting or useful fact → supporting detail → limits and caution → one takeaway. A fact that the reader will remember goes before a fact the reader needs only for completeness. Name notes, synonyms and nomenclature go last.

### R3. Sentence shape

- One main idea per sentence.
- Most sentences are 6–18 words. A sentence over 25 words must be split unless splitting would break a qualification.
- Vary length. Do not write three sentences in a row with the same structure ("X არის…", "X გვხვდება…", "X ჭამს…").
- Do not chop connected ideas into fragments of three or four words. Short is a tool, not a goal.

### R4. Do not repeat the species name as the subject

Georgian lets the subject drop. Use that. Within one field:

- Never start two consecutive sentences with the species name.
- At most one sentence in three may start with the species name.
- Use the name when the subject would otherwise be ambiguous (for example, after a sentence about a lookalike species).

### R5. Hedge only where the facts are uncertain

Hedging words: ჩვეულებრივ, შეიძლება, დაახლოებით, ზოგჯერ, უმეტესად, ძირითადად, როგორც წესი, სავარაუდოდ, ხშირად (and usually, may, about, sometimes, mostly, generally, often in EN).

- Keep a hedge only when the supplied content contains that uncertainty (a range, a "may", a "some individuals", a disputed claim).
- One hedge per claim. Never stack them ("ჩვეულებრივ, შეიძლება, დაახლოებით…").
- Never hedge a solid fact. "საქართველოს ყველაზე დიდი გველგესლაა" needs no qualifier.
- Never remove a hedge that carries real meaning. "may occur" stays "may occur".
- Budget: no more than about one hedge word per 30 words in a field. If you are over budget, check each hedge: is it real uncertainty, or habit?

### R6. Write about the animal, not about the website

Do not write sentences that describe the atlas, this page, its map, its table, its records or its editorial choices. Banned framings include: „ამ ატლასში“, „ამ გვერდზე“, „ამ ატლასის რუკაზე“, „რუკაზე აღნიშნულია“, „ჩანაწერებში მითითებულია“, „ასეთი ჩანაწერი მხოლოდ იმას ადასტურებს“, "this atlas", "on this page", "the map shows".

State the fact behind it instead: „ამ ატლასის რუკაზე აღნიშნულია კახეთში“ → „კახეთში გვხვდება“ (only if that is the supplied fact and its scope). A record caveat becomes a sentence about the animal: „ეს ნიშნავს, რომ იქ ერთხელ მაინც ნახეს, და არა იმას, რომ ქალაქში ყველგან ცხოვრობს.“

One exception: a note explaining the scientific name or an alternative name may stay, phrased about the name rather than the site („ზოგ წყაროში Macrovipera lebetina წერია“). It goes at the end of its field.

### R7. Every fact has one home

Say each fact fully once, in its home field. Elsewhere, refer to it in at most one short clause, and only where the other field must stand on its own (description, an FAQ answer, a collapsed section).

| Fact                                              | Home field                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Size, shape, colour, pattern, how to tell apart   | identification                             |
| Where it lives, elevation, shelters, range change | habitat                                    |
| Named localities and city records                 | habitat (short) or the matching FAQ (full) |
| Food and hunting                                  | diet                                       |
| Season, daily rhythm, reproduction, wintering     | behavior                                   |
| Danger, venom effect, what to do                  | interaction and the danger/bite FAQ        |
| Red List status, threats, role in nature          | conservation                               |

If the same list of places, the same size range or the same warning appears in full in two fields, keep the full version in the home field and reduce the other to a clause or remove it.

### R8. Substance: no one-liners, no padding

- A body paragraph has at least three sentences of real information. If a paragraph would have one or two sentences, merge it with the neighbouring paragraph on the same topic.
- An FAQ answer starts with the direct answer (დიახ / არა / the place / the number) and continues with two to five sentences that explain, qualify or give the practical consequence. A bare "Yes. Call 112." is not an answer.
- If the supplied facts truly support only a short answer, keep it short and accurate. Never fill the space with generic statements ("ბუნებაში მნიშვნელოვან როლს ასრულებს"), repetition from another field, or guesses. A short text because the facts end is correct; a short text because of laziness is not.

### R9. Concrete and visual, from supplied facts only

Prefer what the reader can see, the place they might be, and the time it happens: "ზაფხულის პაპანაქებაში დღეს ქვების ქვეშ ატარებს" rather than "მაღალ ტემპერატურაზე აქტივობა მცირდება". Every concrete detail must already be in the supplied content.

A plain comparison from everyday life is allowed when it adds no biological claim and the trait is already stated: "ვერტიკალური გუგა, კატასავით". Use at most one or two such comparisons per page.

### R10. End with a takeaway

The last sentence of `overview`, `interaction`, each identification trait that involves a risk or a lookalike, and each FAQ answer gives the practical consequence or the one line worth remembering: "თუ გუგის სანახავად მიდიხართ, უკვე ზედმეტად ახლოს ხართ." One takeaway per field, never a slogan, never a moral lecture. Biology fields such as diet or conservation may end on their most meaningful fact instead.

### R11. Address the reader where it helps

Use the polite თქვენ form when the reader can act: identification, encounters, safety, interaction and FAQ. Do not address the reader in descriptive biology just for effect. Never mix თქვენ and შენ.

### R12. Tone limits

- Calm, confident and warm. Not sensational, not cute, not promotional, not a textbook.
- No exclamation marks. No rhetorical questions in prose (FAQ questions are questions, of course).
- No praise words unless a supplied fact supports them: საოცარი, განსაცვიფრებელი, შესანიშნავი, უნიკალური, unique, amazing, fascinating, remarkable.
- Danger is stated as strongly as the facts say: never dramatised, never softened.

### R13. Banned phrases (AI and bureaucratic tells)

Never use these or close variants. Rewrite the sentence so it does not need them.

Georgian empty openers and pointers: მნიშვნელოვანია აღინიშნოს, უნდა ითქვას, უნდა აღინიშნოს, აღსანიშნავია, საინტერესოა, რომ, თავის მხრივ, როგორც უკვე აღვნიშნეთ, როგორც ზემოთ ითქვა, ზემოხსენებული, აღნიშნული (as a pointer), მოცემული (as a pointer).

Georgian bureaucratic verbs: გააჩნია → აქვს; წარმოადგენს → არის; ხასიათდება / ახასიათებს → აქვს, არის; ფიქსირდება → გვხვდება, ჩანს (დაფიქსირდა is acceptable only for a specific record); შეინიშნება → აქვს, ჩანს; ვრცელდება (meaning occurs) → გვხვდება; ნადირობა ხდება → ნადირობს.

Georgian filler: მნიშვნელოვან როლს ასრულებს ეკოსისტემაში (unless a supplied fact names the role), ბუნების ნამდვილი…, ერთ-ერთი ყველაზე საინტერესო…, არა მხოლოდ…, არამედ… (as decoration).

English: it is worth noting, it's important to note, interestingly, notably, plays a crucial/vital role, fascinating, remarkable, delve, not only… but also (as decoration), in conclusion, overall.

Russian: стоит отметить, важно отметить, интересно, что, играет важную роль, удивительный, не только…, но и (as decoration).

Turkish: belirtmek gerekir ki, ilginç bir şekilde, önemli bir rol oynar, büyüleyici.

Punctuation tells: do not open the description with a label and a colon ("გიურზა: …"). Use at most one dash for an aside per paragraph. Do not build sentences from three parallel adjectives just for rhythm.

### R14. Georgian typography

- Thousands: no separator below 10 000: „1500 მ“, never „1,500 მ“ (the comma is the Georgian decimal mark, so „1,500“ reads as one and a half). From 10 000 use a space: „12 000“.
- Decimals with a comma: „1,5 მ“, „2,5 სმ“.
- Ranges with an en dash and no spaces: „70–90 სმ“, „აპრილ–მაისში“.
- Georgian quotes „ “. Official hyphenated region names. Case endings after Latin, numbers and abbreviations with a hyphen: „1500 მ-მდე“, „D. rudis-ისგან“.
- Elevation that starts in the lowlands is written as an upper limit („1500 მ-მდე“), not as „0–1500 მ“, unless the supplied fact really says sea level.

### R15. Translations follow the same style

Write EN, RU and TR from the final KA. Keep the same order of facts, the same lead, the same takeaways and the same hedges. Do not translate Georgian syntax word for word; each language must read as if a good editor wrote it in that language, under the same rules R1–R13. Use each language's own number and quote conventions. A translation may not be flatter, longer, more hedged or more dramatic than the KA.

## 4. Field playbook

**description** (meta description and hero line). One or two sentences, about 120–155 characters in KA. Strongest fact + where in Georgia + the main risk or the main identification point. No colon label, no site talk.

**overview** (3–6 sentences, one paragraph). The reference example in section 2 is the model: striking fact → where → one line on risk or lookalike → one direct takeaway. Name notes go last, if they must stay.

**identification.summary** (2–4 sentences). What you notice first, from a safe distance: build, head, the most reliable visible feature. End with the main lookalike risk if there is one.

**identification.traits.*** (2–4 sentences each, one cluster of features per trait). Visible feature first, then how it differs from the lookalike, then the limit or caution. Never send the reader closer to check a feature on a dangerous animal.

**identification.coloration**. Base colours, markings and variation as supplied. Picture the animal; do not list colours like a table.

**habitat**. Type of place first (dry, stony, rocky) → where in Georgia → shelters, seasonal shifts, elevation → what is not its habitat, if supplied. Range changes are a story: say what moved where and when, in one or two sentences.

**diet**. What it mainly eats, then what else, then how it hunts. Keep it vivid but exact.

**behavior**. When in the year → when in the day and why (heat, temperature) → reproduction (for Giurza, the second paragraph must stay about reproduction) → how it reacts to people, ending with the practical line.

**conservation**. Status with year and scope (global vs Georgian) → threats → why the animal matters, using supplied facts only.

**faq.\*.question**. Phrase it the way people actually ask or search, in plain words. Do not change its meaning.

**faq.\*.answer**. Direct answer first, then two to five sentences of explanation, conditions and the practical consequence. It must stand alone, but it must not copy a whole paragraph from the body: rephrase the essentials briefly.

**stats.\*.value**. Register A only: short, exact, no sentences. Typography rules apply.

## 5. What style may never do

"Engaging" never justifies any of the following. Each is a failure even if the text reads well.

- Inventing a scene, behaviour, place, number, comparison of size or danger, or superlative that is not in the supplied content ("ქვაზე მზეს ეფიცხება" when basking is not supplied).
- Making a record sound like a population ("ქალაქში ხშირად ჩანს") or a "may" sound certain.
- Dramatising venom or danger beyond the supplied wording, or softening it.
- Dropping a number, scientific name, link destination, 112, a warning or a scope qualifier (global vs Georgian, year, observation vs estimate).
- Naming an author, organisation or survey as a source in prose.
- Moving a fact into a field where it changes meaning.

## 6. How to write a field

1. **Inventory.** List every fact, number, name, link, warning and qualifier in the current field.
2. **Rank.** Mark the one or two facts a reader would remember and repeat to a friend. They go first.
3. **De-duplicate.** Check the page map (R7). Remove what is fully said elsewhere; keep a clause if the field must stand alone.
4. **Write KA.** Apply R1–R14. Read it aloud in your head. If any sentence sounds like a form, a textbook or a translation, rewrite it.
5. **Check** with section 7.
6. **Translate** under R15.

## 7. Self-check before returning

Go through every field you changed and fix any failure before you output anything:

1. Does the first sentence state the strongest or most useful fact (R1)?
2. Count sentences starting with the species name: none consecutive, at most one in three (R4)?
3. Count hedge words: each one is real uncertainty, none stacked, roughly one per 30 words at most (R5)?
4. Any sentence about the atlas, page, map or records (R6)?
5. Any fact said in full in two fields (R7)?
6. Any body paragraph with fewer than three sentences, or any FAQ answer that is only one sentence (R8)?
7. Any sentence over 25 words, or three same-shaped sentences in a row (R3)?
8. Any banned phrase from R13, in any locale?
9. Typography: „1500“ not „1,500“; decimals with a comma; en-dash ranges; „ “ quotes (R14)?
10. Does the field end with a takeaway where R10 requires one?
11. Compared with the original: every number, scientific name, link, warning, 112, hedge with real meaning and scope qualifier is still there, and no new fact appeared (section 5)?
12. Do EN, RU and TR say the same thing in the same order, in the same voice, without being flatter or more hedged (R15)?

> ურარტუს მცურავს მსხვილი სხეული და ზურგზე დიდი, მომრგვალებული მუქი ლაქები აქვს. ნაირფერი მცურავის მუქი ლაქებიანი შეფერილობაც შეიძლება გიურზასას ჰგავდეს, განსაკუთრებით მაშინ, როცა გველის თავი არ ჩანს. ორივე მცურავს მრგვალი გუგა აქვს, გიურზას კი — ვერტიკალური. გუგის დასათვალიერებლად გველს არ მიუახლოვდეთ.

After:

> ურარტუს და ნაირფერ მცურავებსაც ლაქებიანი ზურგი აქვთ, ამიტომ შორიდან, განსაკუთრებით თუ თავი არ ჩანს, ადვილად ერევა. განსხვავება თვალშია: მცურავს მრგვალი გუგა აქვს, გიურზას კი ვერტიკალური, კატასავით. მაგრამ თუ გუგის სანახავად მიდიხართ, უკვე ზედმეტად ახლოს ხართ.
