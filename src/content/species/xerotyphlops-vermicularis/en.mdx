location: "Georgia"
description: >-
  A worm-like harmless blind snake confirmed in Georgia from Chachuna and Tbilisi records.
overview: >-
  The European blind snake (Xerotyphlops vermicularis) is a harmless blind snake
  that lives mostly underground and is rarely seen on the surface. In Georgia it
  is treated as biologically confirmed by Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026: a barcoded
  specimen comes from Chachuna Managed Reserve, and the paper's distribution
  figure also shows Dalis Mta reservoir. The site range map also marks Kakheti
  and Tbilisi, including a photographed record from Dighomi. It has a worm-like
  body, eyes reduced to dark spots under the scales, and published size data
  place it at about 22-35 cm total length. It is not dangerous to people.
habitat: >-
  This species lives mainly in loose, dry or moderately dry soil, moving under
  stones, roots and soil cracks. Confirmed Georgian information points to dry
  eastern habitats: Chachuna Managed Reserve, the Dalis Mta reservoir area and
  Tbilisi. The exact elevation range in Georgia still needs verification.
diet: >-
  It feeds on small underground invertebrates, especially ants, termites and
  their eggs or larvae. That diet explains why the species is tied so closely to
  soil, stones and underground microhabitats.
behavior: >-
  It spends most of its time underground. It may appear after rain, when soil is
  turned over, or when a stone is lifted, but even active search rarely finds it.
  If you find one, do not handle it; photograph it and leave the animal and its
  shelter in place.
conservation: >-
  The global IUCN assessment lists the species as Least Concern. A Georgian
  national risk assessment is not verified on this page, so local conservation
  status still needs confirmation. Practical threats are likely linked to loss or
  disturbance of dry habitats, soil turnover and accidental killing of small,
  easily overlooked animals.

  summary: >-
    A small, worm-like blind snake with glossy scales, a rounded head and eyes
    hidden under the scales.
  traits:
    - >-
      The body is very slender and almost the same width from head to tail. The
      upper side is usually brownish, yellowish or flesh-coloured, with a paler
      underside.
    - >-
      The eyes are strongly reduced and show only as small dark spots beneath
      the scales. The head is not sharply set off from the neck.
    - >-
      The tail is very short and the whole body is covered in smooth, shiny
      scales. These marks separate it from ordinary small snakes.
    - >-
      The [javelin sand boa](eryx-jaculus) is much stouter, with small but
      visible eyes and a blunt, boa-like tail. The blind snake is thinner and
      more evenly cylindrical.
    - >-
      [Collared dwarf snake](eirenis-collaris) and [ring-headed dwarf snake](eirenis-modestus)
      look like true small snakes: they have visible eyes, a clearer head, and
      often a neck collar or dorsal markings.
    - >-
      It is non-venomous and not dangerous to people. If found in a yard, garden
      or under a stone, take a photo, replace the shelter and let it move away.
