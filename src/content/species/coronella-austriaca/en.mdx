commonName: Smooth snake
location: 6 regions
description: >-
  The smooth snake (Coronella austriaca) is a harmless snake found in Georgia that is often mistaken for a viper. Its smooth scales and round pupils set it apart from vipers.
overview: >-
  The smooth snake (Coronella austriaca) is nonvenomous, but because of its coloring it is often [mistaken for a viper](/snakes/shxamiani-gvelis-amocnoba). It is found in Georgia and is usually about 50–70 cm long, rarely growing to 90 cm. Worldwide, it faces little threat of extinction, and it is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Do not rely on color alone to identify it: look at its smooth scales, round pupils and the pattern on its head.
habitat: >-
  The smooth snake lives at forest edges, in open woodland, among bushes, on rocky slopes and in foothills. It is found more often in damp and mountainous areas than in the very dry semi-desert of the southeast. It often hides under rocks, tree stumps and other cover. It has been seen at least five times in each of these regions: Adjara, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. There are fewer than five observations from Guria, Shida Kartli, Imereti and Abkhazia, but this does not mean the smooth snake is absent there.
diet: >-
  The smooth snake mainly hunts lizards. Adults may also eat small mammals and, rarely, other small snakes. It wraps its body around its prey to hold it.
behavior: >-
  The smooth snake is secretive and likes to hide under a stone or other cover. It can come out briefly during the day to warm up. It usually gives birth to 2–15 live young. When threatened, it flattens its head and sometimes looks more like a viper. If you catch it, it can [bite](/snakes/gvelis-nakbeni), but it has no venom.
conservation: >-
  Worldwide, the smooth snake faces a low risk of extinction. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. In some places, changes to the places where it lives may threaten it. People may also kill it, especially when they mistake it for a viper.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The smooth snake belongs to the same family as grass snakes and is harmless to people. Still, it sometimes looks like a viper. To tell them apart, check three things: the scales, the shape of the pupil and the markings on the head. You do not need to pick up the snake to count its scales.
  traits:
    - >-
      The smooth snake has smooth scales on its back, with no raised line down the middle. Vipers, by contrast, have a small raised line on each scale along the back. Compared with vipers, the smooth snake has a narrower head that is less clearly set off from its neck. But when threatened, it may flatten its head, so do not rely on head shape alone to tell them apart.
    - >-
      The smooth snake has round pupils, while vipers in Georgia have narrow, vertical ones. A dark stripe often runs from its eye toward the back of its head. It may also have a dark crown- or heart-shaped marking on top of its head. Don't get close to an unknown snake just to see its pupils.
    - >-
      It often has two rows of small dark spots along its back rather than one continuous zigzag, and a dark gray or reddish belly. The snake itself is coppery, brownish or gray. Young smooth snakes may have more distinct markings. It is about 50–70 cm long and only rarely approaches 90 cm.
    - >-
      The [nose-horned viper](vipera-transcaucasiana) may also have separate dark spots on its gray back. But it has a clearly visible horn on its nose, a raised ridge down the middle of its scales and vertical pupils. The [Transcaucasian ratsnake](zamenis-hohenackeri) also often has two rows of dark spots on its back, with a dark stripe behind the eye. So do not identify a snake by its back pattern alone, and do not go near a snake you do not know.
    - >-
      The smooth snake is not venomous, but you might mistake it for a viper. If you come across one, keep your distance and let it go on its way. Do not pick up a snake you cannot identify.

  - question: Is the smooth snake found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes, the smooth snake occurs in Georgia. Data from 2026 confirm this.
  - question: Where might I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      The smooth snake has been seen in Adjara, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. From Guria, Shida Kartli, Imereti and Abkhazia, there are only a few observations of it. You may come across it at forest edges, in open woodland and scrub, on rocky slopes and in foothills.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to humans?
    answer: >-
      No. The smooth snake has no venom and is not dangerous to people. It may bite if you catch it, so don’t pick it up.
  - question: How do I tell it from a viper?
    answer: >-
      The Smooth snake has smooth scales, round pupils and small dark spots arranged in two rows along its back. Vipers have a raised ridge down the middle of each scale and vertical pupils. You cannot reliably tell them apart by colour or head shape alone, so look at several features together.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      The smooth snake is usually about 50–70 cm long. Only rarely does it get close to 90 cm.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      The smooth snake mainly eats lizards. It may also eat small mammals and, rarely, other small snakes.
  - question: Does it lay eggs or give live birth?
    answer: >-
      The smooth snake does not lay eggs; it gives birth to live young. The young hatch from their eggs while still inside the mother's body. Usually 2–15 young are born at a time.
  - question: What should I do if I meet one?
    answer: >-
      If you come across a smooth snake, keep your distance and let it pass. Don’t kill it or pick it up. If you don’t recognize a snake, don’t touch it to find out what it is: it could be a viper.
