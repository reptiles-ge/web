overview: >-
  The European yellow sac spider (Cheiracanthium punctorium) is a spider in the
  family Cheiracanthiidae. English names vary, so the scientific name is the most
  reliable label for precise identification. There is no well-established
  Georgian common name used on this page.

  The species is confirmed in Georgia. The Caucasian Spiders database lists
  Georgian records from Tbilisi, Betania, Chokhatauri, Sighnaghi, Baghdati,
  Bakuriani, Rokiti near Baghdati, south of Kutaisi, Poti, and east of
  Atsriskhevi. These records confirm occurrence in Georgia, but they do not show
  that the species is evenly distributed across the whole country.

  Female body length is about 10-15 mm; male body length is about 7.5-12 mm,
  excluding the legs. The spider is usually pale yellowish green or yellowish
  brown, with a greenish brown to orange-red prosoma and relatively strong,
  dark-tipped chelicerae.

  Bites are uncommon, but they can be painful and may cause local redness or
  swelling. Modern verified European bite records do not support necrosis as a
  typical outcome. If pain is strong or increasing, swelling spreads, or general
  symptoms appear, call 112 in Georgia.
habitat: >-
  The species is associated with warm, open habitats. European sources describe
  it from tall grass, shrub layer, dry or semi-dry meadows, and vegetation above
  the ground. It does not build a large round orb web for catching prey.

  Georgian records include both western lowland localities and localities in
  eastern and southern Georgia. Poti, Chokhatauri, Baghdati, south of Kutaisi,
  Bakuriani, Sighnaghi, Tbilisi, and Betania show evidence from several regions.

  The available records do not define an exact Georgian elevation range or a
  complete national distribution map. The atlas map therefore marks only regions
  with a named Georgian locality.

behavior: >-
  The species is more active at night. During the day it often stays in a silk
  retreat built in vegetation. Females may be defensive when guarding eggs or
  young, especially if the silk retreat is opened or damaged.

  Cheiracanthium punctorium does not seek out people. Accidental contact may
  happen when someone handles vegetation, disturbs tall grass or shrubs, or traps
  the spider in clothing or an object.

  European material makes adults more visible in the warm season, but the sources
  used here do not establish a precise seasonal calendar for Georgia.
conservation: >-
  World Spider Catalog treats Cheiracanthium punctorium (Villers, 1789) as the
  accepted name. Its range includes Europe, Turkey, the Caucasus, parts of
  Russia, Iran, and Central Asia.

  Cheiracanthium punctorium is not included in Georgia's 2014 Red List. The
  sources used here do not define a quantified population trend, special threat,
  or national protection status for Georgia.

  There is no need to kill or handle wild individuals. If one is found indoors,
  avoid direct contact and move it outside only with a no-touch method if removal
  is necessary.
interaction: >-
  Cheiracanthium punctorium is venomous in the normal spider sense: it uses venom
  to subdue prey. An accidental human bite may be painful. Reported symptoms
  include burning pain, local redness, and swelling; nausea, headache, mild fever,
  or other general symptoms are also possible.

  Modern reviews of verified cases do not show necrosis as a typical or expected
  result of European Cheiracanthium bites. Even so, any bite can vary between
  people, and allergic or unusual reactions are possible.

  Do not pick up the spider by hand and do not open its silk retreat. If a bite
  happens, wash the area with soap and water and monitor your condition. If pain
  is strong or increasing, swelling spreads quickly, breathing becomes difficult,
  the face or throat swells, nausea or fever appears, or your general condition
  worsens, call 112. This is educational information, not a medical protocol.

  traits:
    - >-
      Female body length is about 10-15 mm; male body length is about 7.5-12 mm.
      Legs are not included, so a live spider can look larger.
    - >-
      The abdomen is usually pale yellowish green. The prosoma may be greenish
      brown, orange, or reddish. The chelicerae are relatively large and dark at
      the tips.
    - >-
      The front legs are long and conspicuous. The species lacks the bold
      yellow-and-black bands of [the wasp spider](argiope-bruennichi) and the
      white abdominal cross of [the European garden spider](araneus-diadematus).
    - >-
      [Wasp spider](argiope-bruennichi) and [lobed argiope](argiope-lobata) sit
      in large orb webs and have strong abdominal patterns. Cheiracanthium
      punctorium is not a spider sitting in the centre of that kind of web.
    - >-
      [False black widow](steatoda-paykulliana) and [Mediterranean black widow](latrodectus-tredecimguttatus)
      are dark, rounded Theridiidae spiders and look very different from this
      pale, long-legged Cheiracanthium.
