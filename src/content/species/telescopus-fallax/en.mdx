overview: >-
  The European cat snake (Telescopus fallax) is a slender, mostly nocturnal
  snake that in Georgia is mainly associated with dry, rocky habitats in the
  east. It belongs to the family Colubridae. The 2026 Georgian checklist lists
  the species as biologically confirmed, with medium distribution confidence.

  The 2021 Georgian study lists it as nocturnal and places it among dry-climate
  species of rocky or grassy habitats. This page presents no specific record
  from western Georgia or Colchis.

  Caucasian populations are often assigned to the subspecies T. f. iberus; its
  type locality is Tbilisi (“Tiflis”). The cat snake has rear fangs at the back
  of the mouth and uses venom to subdue prey. It is not considered a medically
  significant venomous snake for people, though it should not be handled.

  Species length is usually about 60–100 cm. IUCN lists it as Least Concern.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the cat snake is mainly associated with dry, rocky, or grassy
  places. It may occur on rocky slopes, around crevices, in scrub, along open
  woodland edges, in ruins, and near dry-stone walls. It climbs rocks and walls
  well.

  By day it often hides under stones, in crevices, or under bark. The atlas map
  marks Shida Kartli, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti, and Tbilisi.
  Specific photographs are from Mtatsminda and Iagluji Mountain near Rustavi.

  This page presents no specific record from Samtskhe-Javakheti. Across the
  wider species range it has been described up to about 2,000 m elevation, but a
  separate elevation range for Georgia has not been established here.
diet: >-
  The cat snake’s diet has not been studied separately in Georgia. In other
  parts of the species range it mainly hunts lizards. It also takes small
  snakes, nestling birds, and small mammals.

  Geckos are often included in its diet around the Mediterranean, but that
  should not be treated as a directly confirmed diet for the Georgian
  population.

  It restrains prey with the body and uses rear fangs at the back of the mouth
  to subdue it.
behavior: >-
  The cat snake is nocturnal. It hides by day and hunts in the evening and at
  night. Its large eyes and vertical pupil are linked to activity in low light.

  When threatened, it mostly tries to flee or hide. It may sometimes coil its
  body, hiss loudly, or bite defensively, so do not handle it.

  It is egg-laying. Species-level literature often cites 5–9 eggs, but mating
  season and egg number for the Georgian population have not been published
  separately. Overwintering details in Georgia are also not defined by the
  sources used on this page.
conservation: >-
  IUCN lists the cat snake as Least Concern. It is not included in Georgia’s
  2014 Red List.

  In Europe, the species is included in Bern Convention Annex II and EU Habitats
  Directive Annex IV, but the EU legal regime does not automatically apply in
  Georgia.

  In the 2026 Georgian checklist, the cat snake is listed as a biologically
  confirmed species.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A slender, mostly nocturnal snake with large eyes and a vertical pupil. The
    vertical pupil can make it look like a viper, so do not rely on the pupil
    alone when identifying it — consider body build, smooth scales, and head
    shields.
  traits:
    - >-
      The body is slender and almost cylindrical. The head is flattened, oval,
      and moderately distinct from the neck. The eyes are large, and the pupil
      is vertical, narrowing to a slit in strong light.

      The dorsal scales are smooth; there are often about 19 rows at mid-body.
      The top of the head has large, symmetrical shields. The head surface of
      vipers is often covered with smaller scales.

      Do not pick up a snake to count scales.
    - >-
      The main body colour may be grey, sandy, brownish, or olive. The back has
      dark cross-blotches or bands that often become less distinct toward the
      rear of the body.

      The head often has a dark pattern, and a dark oblique stripe runs from
      the eye toward the corner of the mouth. The belly is pale and may have
      fine dark dots. Juveniles are usually more strongly marked.

      Species length is usually about 60–100 cm. A separately measured maximum
      length for Georgia is not presented on this page.
    - >-
      The Transcaucasian nose-horned viper (Vipera transcaucasiana) and other
      vipers are more heavily built than the cat snake, with keeled dorsal
      scales and a broader, more triangular head. The blunt-nosed viper
      (Macrovipera lebetina) is larger still and is a medically significant
      venomous snake.

      The cat snake has a slim body, large eyes, and smooth dorsal scales. The
      smooth snake (Coronella austriaca), steppe rat snake (Elaphe dione), and
      Dahl’s whip snake (Platyceps najadum) have round pupils.

      Do not rely on a single character when identifying an unknown snake.
    - >-
      The cat snake has rear fangs at the back of the mouth. It uses venom
      mainly to subdue prey and is not considered a medically significant danger
      to people. A bite may still hurt.

      Do not handle it. If you are not sure about a vertical-pupilled snake,
      keep a safe distance and let it move away.
    - >-
      Yes. The cat snake is a confirmed species in Georgia. In the 2026
      Georgian checklist it is listed as biologically confirmed.

      The 2021 Georgian study links it mainly with dry, rocky, or grassy
      habitats in eastern Georgia and describes it as nocturnal. This page
      presents no specific record from western Georgia or Colchis.

      Caucasian populations are often assigned to the subspecies T. f. iberus;
      its type locality is listed as Tbilisi.

facts:
  - >-
    Described in 1831 (Fleischmann), originally as Tarbophis fallax, from the
    eastern Adriatic. The Caucasian form iberus (Eichwald, 1831) has its type
    locality at Tbilisi (“Tiflis”) — iberus refers to ancient Caucasian Iberia,
    not the Iberian Peninsula. The 2026 checklist uses Telescopus fallax at
    species rank.
  - >-
    A vertical pupil is unusual among colubrids. Georgian vipers also have
    vertical pupils — the eye alone is not enough for identification.
  - >-
    A 2019 phylogenetic study notes that the T. fallax group needs revision;
    in practice Georgia is still treated as Telescopus fallax.
  - >-
    This atlas map shows five regions; named photographs are from Mtatsminda
    and Iagluji Mountain near Rustavi. The national picture is still uneven.

  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      Look for a slim body, large eyes, vertical pupil, smooth dorsal scales,
      large shields on the top of the head, and dark cross-blotches on the
      back. It is mainly active in the evening and at night.

      Vipers are usually more heavily built and have keeled scales. Smooth
      snakes and many day-active racers have round pupils.

  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      It is mainly active at dusk and at night. The 2021 Georgian study lists
      it as nocturnal. By day it often hides under stones, in crevices, or in
      other shelters, so it is sometimes found by chance when lifting stones or
      on walls.

      If you find a snake under a stone, do not touch it by hand.
  - question: What does it eat, and does it lay eggs?
    answer: >-

      number have not been fully studied separately.
  - question: What should I do if I encounter one?
    answer: >-
      Do not approach the cat snake or pick it up. It does not seek people and
      usually avoids conflict.

      If you are not sure about the species of a vertical-pupilled snake, keep
      your distance for safety and let the snake move away.
