commonName: "Obscure Rock Lizard"
location: "Samtskhe — Javakheti, Adjara"
description: >-
  The Obscure Rock Lizard is harmless and occurs in Samtskhe-Javakheti and Adjara. The 2026 checklist lists it as a candidate species, and you cannot identify it by colour alone.
overview: >-
  The Obscure Rock Lizard (Darevskia obscura) is harmless to people, and it definitely occurs in Georgia. The open question is a different one: whether it is a separate species. A 2026 Georgian list put local lizards that were once grouped within a broader D. rudis under this name. It is counted as a separate species mainly because of data from one type of DNA. Whether genes can pass between the two groups has not yet been studied. That is why the list includes the Obscure Rock Lizard as a possible separate species. It has been seen and recorded in Samtskhe-Javakheti and Adjara, while Shida Kartli has only one record. In older sources and some observations, you may find it under the name D. rudis or Darevskia rudis obscura.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the Obscure Rock Lizard is known from a few precisely named places. These are the Meskheti Range in Akhaltsikhe Municipality, Rveli in the Baniskhevi Valley, Mount Saaghdgomo near Akhalkalaki, and the Borjomi Valley. Lizards were also collected near the Kirkhbulakhi River to study their reproduction. The 2026 list treats this species in a broad sense and includes parts of Trialeti, Meskheti, Javakheti and the Enguri basin in its range. This is a general outline, and it does not mean lizards were collected at every one of these places.
behavior: >-
  In Georgia, 13 females caught near the Kirkhbulakhi River laid 2–5 eggs at a time. They laid them while temporarily kept in a laboratory. This small group came from just one location. So it cannot tell us how many eggs the species generally lays at a time, or what time of year it breeds elsewhere in Georgia.
conservation: >-
  In the 2026 list, the Obscure Rock Lizard is treated as a “candidate” species. The word refers to whether it is a separate species, not to whether it is under threat.

faq:
  - question: Does it occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2026 Georgian list includes records and specimens of it. “Candidate” means further checking is needed to determine whether it is a separate species.
  - question: Where has it been recorded?
    answer: >-
      In Samtskhe-Javakheti and Adjara. It has been seen and recorded in both regions. The specific places named are the Meskheti Range in Akhaltsikhe Municipality, Rveli in the Baniskhevi Valley, Saagdgomo Mountain near Akhalkalaki, and the Borjomi Valley. There is a single record from Shida Kartli, and the 2026 list mentions the Enguri basin only in general terms. So neither counts as part of its confirmed range.
  - question: Why is it a candidate species?
    answer: >-
      The Obscure Rock Lizard is told apart from D. rudis mainly by differences in one type of DNA. Where the two forms meet, it has not yet been studied whether they can exchange genes. So it has not yet been conclusively confirmed that they are separate forms.
  - question: How do I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Do not rely on color or a single partial photo. Note exactly where you saw the lizard, and photograph its head and scales from several angles. If you are unsure of the species, record it as "Darevskia sp.", meaning the exact species is unclear, or as "probable D. obscura".
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. The Obscure Rock Lizard is not venomous and is harmless to people.
