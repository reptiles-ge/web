overview: |-
  Gvelkhokera (Pseudopus apodus) is a large legless lizard in the Anguidae family. In Georgia, people often mistake it for a snake. You can tell it apart by its movable eyelids, ear openings and a distinct fold along the sides of its body.
  A 2026 Georgian list confirms its presence in Georgia but lists it only in the eastern part of the country. The map marks Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli, Shida Kartli, Mtskheta-Mtianeti and Tbilisi.
  Gvelkhokera is not venomous and poses no danger to people. It reaches about one metre in length, though large ones sometimes grow to 1.35 metres. Its worldwide status is Least Concern (LC), meaning it faces a low risk of extinction. It is not on Georgia’s Red List.
  If you see one, leave it where it is and do not pick it up. To help identify it, take a photo from a safe distance.

behavior: >-
  The European glass lizard is a ground-dwelling, mostly day-active legless
  lizard. Moving eyelids, ear openings, and the deep fold along the sides of the
  body help tell it apart from snakes. A small rudimentary limb may sometimes be
  visible near the base of the tail.

  When frightened, it may hiss or bite, but this is a defensive reaction and is
  not connected with venom. It can shed its tail, though this is rare in this
  species. Do not touch wild animals by hand — use a photo for identification.
