description: >-
  Rock Dove (Columba livia) breeds and remains in Georgia year round. Learn how to identify it, what the Georgian evidence shows, and how it differs from the woodpigeon.
overview: >-
  The Rock Dove (Columba livia) is a member of the pigeon family, Columbidae. Georgia's 2024 scientific bird checklist records it as a year-round resident breeder. A typical bird is 30–36 cm long, blue-grey, with two black wingbars and a dark tail tip. Domestic and free-living city pigeons belong to the same species, but their plumage is often different. Colour alone cannot tell whether a city bird belongs to a genetically wild population or descends from domestic pigeons. On this page's map, the records-by-region table confirms distribution in Tbilisi, Adjara, Mtskheta — Mtianeti, Abkhazia, Kakheti, Samtskhe — Javakheti, Kvemo Kartli, Imereti, and Samegrelo — Zemo Svaneti. Guria and Shida Kartli have records, but the status does not confirm distribution, so those regions are not treated as part of the range.
habitat: >-
  The national checklist confirms presence and breeding in Georgia. Ilia State University's Alazani Special Protection Area species list also includes it; that list does not identify a particular nest or observation date. The typical wild form uses rocky cliffs and crevices. Feral birds use ledges on buildings and bridges and forage in open areas. Regional distribution on this page comes from the map table and counts only confirmed status: Tbilisi, Adjara, Mtskheta — Mtianeti, Abkhazia, Kakheti, Samtskhe — Javakheti, Kvemo Kartli, Imereti, and Samegrelo — Zemo Svaneti. Guria and Shida Kartli have only scattered records, so those regions are not treated as part of the range. An elevation band is not established on this page. The map does not separate wild birds from city pigeons.
diet: >-
  It mainly eats seeds and grain found on the ground, and sometimes takes fruit and invertebrates. Urban birds can also use food left by people. This describes the species generally, not a diet study in Georgia.
behavior: >-
  Active by day, it often walks and feeds in groups on the ground. Its call is a low coo. It nests on a cliff or building ledge or in a crevice. It usually lays two white eggs, and both parents feed the young with food produced in the crop. These breeding details come from general sources; precise breeding months in Georgia are not established here.
conservation: >-
  The 2024 scientific checklist gives the species global Least Concern (LC) status and a draft national LC assessment. The draft national assessment is not a legal protection status. Wild and domestic-derived pigeons can be hard to separate, so the number of city pigeons cannot be used to assess a genetically wild population.
interaction: >-
  It is not venomous or directly dangerous to people. Leave nests and young undisturbed and do not catch wild birds. Colour or nesting on a building does not by itself establish wild ancestry.

  summary: >-
    In typical plumage, blue-grey with two black wingbars, a dark tail tip and often a green-purple sheen on the neck. Feral birds can be white, reddish, black or mottled; a bird without two wingbars can still be this species.
  traits:
    - >-
      About 30–36 cm long, with a stocky body, relatively small head and pointed wings. The two dark bars show even on a folded wing in typically coloured birds. Males and females look much alike.
    - >-
      The [common woodpigeon](columba-palumbus) is larger. Adults show white patches on the neck and broad white wing bands in flight. Juveniles may lack the neck patch, so check the wing too. The Stock Dove (Columba oenas) is closer in size but lacks the typical Rock Dove's two bold black wingbars. A Stock Dove profile is not yet available here.
    - >-
      The [European Turtle Dove](streptopelia-turtur) is slimmer, with a chestnut-and-black scaly pattern on the back. The Eurasian Collared Dove is paler and has a black half-collar on the nape; it does not yet have a profile here.

    answer: >-

  - question: Are city pigeons the same species?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Free-living city pigeons descend from domestic lines of Columba livia. Plumage alone does not prove wild ancestry.
