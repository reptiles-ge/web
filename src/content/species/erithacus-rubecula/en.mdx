description: >-
  A small woodland bird with an orange face and breast. It breeds in Georgia and
  can be found there throughout the year.
overview: >-
  Erithacus rubecula is a small songbird. Adults have orange faces and breasts; young birds do not yet. It breeds in Georgia and is found there year-round, mainly in forests and areas with dense vegetation. It is not dangerous to people. In winter, it can also be seen in gardens and parks.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, look for გულწითელა in dense forests, among shrubs between the trees, and at forest edges. It often looks for food on the ground near bushes, in places that also have open areas. You may also see it in gardens and parks in winter. It was photographed at the Batumi Botanical Garden on June 24, 2016.
diet: >-
  It mainly eats animals without backbones that live on the ground, such as insects, spiders and earthworms. It finds food by hopping on the ground or coming down from a low branch. In autumn and winter, it also eats berries and seeds.
behavior: >-
  It sings in high, clear notes separated by short pauses. Both males and females defend their territories, and its song can be heard outside the nesting season. It usually builds its nest close to the ground, among roots, on a slope or in a hollow. It usually lays 4–6 eggs in a nest and sits on them for about two weeks. Nesting has been confirmed in Georgia.
conservation: >-
  Globally, the species called “გულწითელა” is considered to face a low level of threat. It is not included in Georgia’s official 2014 Red List. The category assigned to it for Georgia in a 2024 scientific list is not an official Red List status. A 2021 European assessment estimated that 48,900–490,000 pairs nested in Georgia during 2013–2017. This wide range is an estimate, not an exact count.
interaction: >-
  It is not dangerous to people. Do not catch a wild bird or approach its nest.

  summary: >-
    It is a sparrow-sized songbird with an orange face and breast, an olive-brown back, and a pale belly. Males and females look alike. Young birds are brown with spots and have no orange breast.
  traits:
    - >-
      Its body is about 14 cm long, with a wingspan of 20–22 cm. It often weighs 14–21 g. It has a rounded body, a thin, dark beak and brownish legs. It has no large white patch on its wing or long orange tail.
    - >-
      Adults have uniformly orange-rufous foreheads, cheeks, throats and chests. Their backs are olive-brown. A bluish-grey stripe is often visible beside the chest. The belly is pale and almost white in the middle. Males and females cannot be distinguished by appearance in the wild. Young birds are sandy-brown with dense pale spots. Their faces and chests are not orange.
    - >-
      [ბულბული](luscinia-megarhynchos) is larger and does not have an orange face. Phoenicurus phoenicurus and Phoenicurus ochruros have reddish tails. A young გულწითელა does not yet have the orange breast of an adult, so do not rely on that feature alone to identify it.

facts:
  - >-
    The European robin was formerly placed with thrushes. It is now classified
    with the Old World flycatchers (Muscicapidae).
  - >-
    The Caucasian subspecies E. r. caucasicus breeds in Georgia; the European
    E. r. rubecula also occurs during migration and in winter. Differences in
    appearance between subspecies are slight, so field identification is more
    reliable at the species level.
  - >-
    The Georgian name გულწითელა has the variant ბულწითელა. რუხი გულწითელა refers
    to another bird, the [yellowhammer](emberiza-citrinella), not this species.
    Its Russian name is зарянка and its Turkish name is kızılgerdan.
