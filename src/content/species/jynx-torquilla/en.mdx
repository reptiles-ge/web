commonName: Eurasian wryneck
location: Kakheti, Adjara, Mtskheta — Mtianeti
description: >-
  A sparrow-sized, bark-coloured woodpecker. It breeds in Georgia and also
  occurs on passage; it usually searches for ants on the ground.
overview: >-
  Jynx torquilla is a small bird in the woodpecker family. Unlike woodpeckers, it often looks for food on the ground. As of 2024, it nests in Georgia and is also seen there during spring and autumn migration. It is not dangerous to people. Its mottled, bark-colored feathers and the dark stripe running from the back of its head down its back can help you identify it.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, მაქცია is found during the breeding season and during spring and autumn migration. The 2024 list does not identify it as a species that winters in Georgia. It is found in partly open places such as forest edges, gardens and parks. These places have cavities suitable for nesting, short grass and plenty of ants on the ground.
diet: >-
  It feeds mainly on ants, their larvae and pupae, picking them up on the ground with its long, sticky tongue. It also eats other small animals without backbones. It does not usually peck at tree trunks to find food.
behavior: >-
  It is active during the day. It repeatedly makes loud, nasal sounds. Knocking on trees is not typical of it. When threatened, it moves its neck like a snake and may chirp. It usually does not dig out its own nest hole: it uses a hollow in a tree or a man-made nesting place and lays white eggs.
conservation: >-
  Globally, მაქცია has been assessed as having a low risk of extinction. The species is not included in Georgia’s official 2014 Red List. The 2024 bird list also gives it a low risk of extinction in Georgia. That updated assessment had not yet been approved at the time and is not its status on the official 2014 list. Do not catch or keep a wild bird.
interaction: >-
  It poses no direct threat to people; do not take in wild birds.
danger: Harmless

identification:
  summary: >-
    It is a sparrow-sized, bark-colored bird in the woodpecker group. A dark stripe runs from the back of its head down its back. It has a short beak and a rounded tail. It does not look like a green or spotted woodpecker.
  traits:
    - >-
      This species is about 16–18 cm long. The wing measures 80–95 mm, the tail 60–75 mm and the beak 15–16 mm. It weighs 30–45 g. Its wingspan is often about 25–30 cm. The beak is short and pointed; it does not resemble a woodpecker’s chisel-shaped beak. The tail is rounded and relatively soft and does not serve as a stiff support. The back is grayish brown, mottled and striped like tree bark. A darker stripe runs from the back of the head along the back. The throat is rust-colored with fine stripes; the underside is pale with small arrow-shaped marks. The legs and beak are brown. Males and females are hard to tell apart by appearance in the wild. Males are only slightly larger.
    - >-
      The young bird resembles an adult, but its markings are slightly less distinct. In flight, it rises and dips. It often feeds on the ground. It may cling to a tree trunk, but it does not climb it like a spotted woodpecker. Its call is a series of nasal sounds that carry far.
    - >-
      [Picus viridis](picus-viridis) is much larger, with a green back and a red crown. [Dendrocopos major](dendrocopos-major) is black and white with red markings. By contrast, the bird called მაქცია is brown like tree bark and often looks for food on the ground.
    - >-
      From a distance, you might mistake Maqtsia for a female [Lanius collurio](lanius-collurio) at a glance. The female Lanius collurio has a plainer upper side and perches in a more open spot. Maqtsia has fine, bark-like markings and a dark stripe running from the nape onto the back.
facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows the OSME
    Region List for names: order Piciformes, family Picidae, genus Jynx. IOC
    World Bird List treats two living Jynx species: this one and red-throated
    wryneck (Jynx ruficollis) of sub-Saharan Africa. The African species is
    not on the Georgian list.
  - >-
    The Georgian name is მაქცია; dictionaries also record ჩვეულებრივი მაქცია.
faq:
  - question: Is the Eurasian wryneck found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Photos and records from rings attached to birds confirm that this bird breeds in Georgia and is also seen there during migration.
  - question: Where is it confirmed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Kakheti, Adjara and Mtskheta-Mtianeti. There are also isolated records from Tbilisi, Samtskhe-Javakheti and Samegrelo-Upper Svaneti, but these do not mean the species occurs everywhere in those places.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      It is about the size of a sparrow, brown like tree bark and striped. A dark stripe runs from the back of its head down its back, and it has a short beak. It often feeds on the ground. In Georgia, green and spotted woodpeckers look quite different. Its song sounds like a nasal “qui-qui-qui.”
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      It is about 16–18 cm long and weighs 30–45 g.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      It mainly eats ants and their larvae, which it looks for on the ground.
  - question: When is it present in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      It is seen during the nesting season and during spring and autumn migration. The 2024 list does not identify it as wintering in Georgia.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. According to the 2024 list, it nests. It usually uses an existing hollow in a tree.
  - question: Is it dangerous or on Georgia’s Red List?
    answer: >-
      It is not dangerous to people. It is not on Georgia’s official 2014 Red List. Globally, it has LC status. Do not catch wild birds.
