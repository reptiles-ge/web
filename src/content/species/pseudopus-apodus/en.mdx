overview: |-
  Gvelkhokera (Pseudopus apodus) is a large legless lizard in the Anguidae family. In Georgia, people often mistake it for a snake. You can tell it apart by its movable eyelids, ear openings and a distinct fold along the sides of its body.
  A 2026 scientific checklist confirms its presence in Georgia but describes its range only in the east. This page's map also shows public observations from Abkhazia and Adjara. Its “confirmed” status is based on the number of records and does not mean the scientific checklist confirms a western Georgian range.
  Gvelkhokera is not venomous and poses no danger to people. It reaches about one metre in length, though large ones sometimes grow to 1.35 metres. Its worldwide status is Least Concern (LC), meaning it faces a low risk of extinction. It is not on Georgia’s Red List; a separate 2021 national assessment also classifies it as LC.
  If you see one, leave it where it is and do not pick it up. To help identify it, take a photo from a safe distance.
habitat: |-
  The glass lizard is found in open, warm places — in fields, sparse woodlands, areas with bushes, rocky slopes, river valleys, gardens and along the edges of village yards.
  In eastern Georgia, it also lives in dry and semi-arid areas of the Mtkvari, Iori and Alazani valleys.
diet: >-
  The glass lizard often eats snails and slugs. It also catches insects and sometimes small lizards or other small animals. Its strong jaws are especially helpful when eating animals with shells. Eating snails and slugs may also make it helpful in a garden.
behavior: |-
  The gvelkhokera lives on the ground and is mostly active during the day. In eastern Georgia, it is most active in spring and early summer.
  When frightened, it may hiss or bite. These are ways of defending itself and have nothing to do with venom. It can also shed its tail, though this rarely happens in this species. Do not touch a wild animal to identify it — take a photo instead.
conservation: |-
  According to the IUCN, Pseudopus apodus is classified as Least Concern (LC) worldwide. It is not on Georgia’s Red List, but a 2021 national assessment by Ilia State University also classifies it as LC. These are separate assessments.
interaction: >-
  It is harmless to people and has no venom. If you find it in your yard or garden, leave it where it is. Do not kill it just because you think it is a snake. If you cannot identify it for certain, keep a safe distance and take a photo without touching it. Call 112 only if there is a real danger.
