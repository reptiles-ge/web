commonName: Transcaucasian Ratsnake
location: Georgia — dry rocky and grassy habitats
description: >-
  A harmless, blotched ratsnake of dry rocky and grassland habitats in Georgia.
overview: >-
  The Transcaucasian snake (Zamenis hohenackeri) is spotted and nonvenomous. Its presence in Georgia has been confirmed. Its subspecies Z. h. hohenackeri is found here. It lives mainly in dry, rocky and grassy places. Adults are often less than one metre long. Its spots may cause people to mistake it for another snake, including a viper.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, this species is found mainly in dry, rocky or grassy places in the east. In 2021, it was confirmed in suitable places in the west as well. There are specific records from the villages of Gomaro and Boshuri in Adigeni Municipality. Tbilisi is mentioned in the material for the species’ first description. The atlas map marks Imereti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Shida Kartli, Kvemo Kartli and Tbilisi. The map is still incomplete. These records do not mean the species is evenly distributed across the country.
diet: >-
  It mainly hunts rodents and birds. It also eats lizards. It catches prey with its body. These sources do not include a separate study of what it eats in Georgia.
behavior: >-
  It tends to stay hidden and is often active at dusk or at night, though it may also appear during the day. Descriptions of the species say it lays 3–7 eggs at a time. These sources do not make clear when it breeds in Georgia.
interaction: >-
  It has no venom. Do not pick up a wild snake: its spots may make you mistake it for a viper. Do not get close to look at the shape of its pupil or head. If a snake bites you and you are not sure what kind of snake it was, call 112.
conservation: >-
  A 2009 assessment found that the species was at low risk of extinction worldwide. It was not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Neither fact shows how the species’ numbers were changing in Georgia.

  summary: >-
    It is a relatively slender racer. Its back usually has two rows of dark spots with a light line between them. The spots remain into adulthood.
  traits:
    - >-
      It has round pupils. It has no horn on its nose, and the scales on its back are mostly smooth. Pupil shape alone is not enough to identify a snake.
    - >-
      The subspecies found in Georgia usually has two rows of brownish spots with a pale line between them. The spots sometimes join together. A dark stripe is visible behind the eye. Color and body markings may vary from one animal to another.
    - >-
      An adult Aesculapian snake (Zamenis longissimus) is often more evenly colored, while this species retains its spots. The horn-nosed viper has a horn on its nose and scales that are raised in the middle. It is venomous. You can compare these features in a photo, but do not approach a snake to examine them.
facts:
  - >-
    Strauch described the species in 1873 as Coluber hohenackeri. Older Georgian
    and Soviet books also use Elaphe hohenackeri.
  - >-
    The 2026 checklist suggests an old, unconfirmed Tbilisi report of Elaphe
    situla may instead refer to this snake or Elaphe dione.
faq:
  - question: Does the Transcaucasian ratsnake occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. According to the 2026 list, biological data confirm its existence. The same list names the subspecies Z. h. hohenackeri for Georgia.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      It is found mainly in dry, rocky and grassy areas. Its presence has been confirmed in the east and in suitable areas in the west. It has been recorded in Gomaro (Adigeni Municipality) and Boshuri. The atlas map is incomplete.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. This snake belongs to the grass snake family and is not venomous. Do not pick it up — you could mistake it for another snake.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      Its pupil is round. Two rows of dark spots run along its back, with a pale line between them. A dark stripe runs from its eye to the corner of its mouth. These spots remain into adulthood.
  - question: How does it differ from the Aesculapian snake?
    answer: >-
      Adult Aesculapian snakes often have a more uniform color and are found in wetter forests. Transcaucasian snakes keep their spots into adulthood and prefer dry, rocky places. Neither is venomous.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      An adult is often less than one metre long. These sources do not provide a separate set of measurements for Georgia.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      It feeds mainly on rodents and birds, and sometimes on lizards. These sources do not mention a separate study of what it eats in Georgia.
  - question: Is it on Georgia's Red List?
    answer: >-
      It is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List. In a worldwide assessment in 2009, it was placed in the LC category. This was not a separate assessment of its status in Georgia.
