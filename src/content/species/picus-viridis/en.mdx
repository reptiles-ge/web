commonName: European green woodpecker
location: Year-round resident in Georgia
description: >-
  A medium-sized green woodpecker with a red crown, black mask, and yellow rump.
  A year-round resident and breeder in Georgia.
overview: >-
  The European green woodpecker (Picus viridis) is a medium-sized woodpecker that occurs
  in Georgia year-round and breeds here. Its presence in the country is confirmed by
  photographs and sound recordings.

  An adult bird is about 30–36 cm long. It is easy to recognize by its olive-green back,
  bright yellowish rump, and red crown.

  The European green woodpecker does not pose a threat to people. According to the IUCN,
  the species is globally Least Concern (LC). The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of
  Georgia gives it the same category.
habitat: >-
  The European green woodpecker is a year-round resident in Georgia, but its distribution
  by municipality is not documented equally well. The 2024 Georgian bird checklist
  confirms the species at country level, but does not publish a complete list of records
  by region. Therefore this page does not colour a municipality map, and it should not be
  assumed that the species occurs evenly across the whole country, including treeless high
  plateaus.

  In one Georgian study, the European green woodpecker was recorded at 15% of 80 forest
  survey points in Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park, and at 3.85% of 26 points in
  Pshav-Khevsureti Protected Areas. In the same study, the species was found more often in
  medium-density forest with openings and meadows.

  In general, semi-open habitat is typical for the European green woodpecker: deciduous
  and mixed woodland, forest edge, floodplain woodland, gardens, and parks. Nearby open,
  short-grass areas are often important too, because it regularly comes down to the ground
  to look for food, especially ants. Dense, fully closed forest is less typical for it
  than for the black woodpecker.

  Exact elevational distribution data for Georgia are not published separately on this
  page.
diet: >-
  Most of the European green woodpecker's diet consists of ants. Species-level data show
  especially frequent hunting of ants in the genera Lasius and Formica. It usually looks
  for food on the ground — finding ant nests and eating both adult insects and their
  larvae.

  Besides ants, it also eats other insects. Species-level literature rarely describes
  small vertebrates too, although this is not a separately studied diet of the Georgian
  population.

  A large green woodpecker feeding on a lawn or meadow is behaving completely typically.
  Unlike spotted woodpeckers, searching for larvae living in tree trunks is not its main
  daily feeding method.

  A separate detailed diet study for Georgia is not presented on this page.
behavior: >-
  The European green woodpecker is active by day and is often heard before the bird itself
  is seen. Its territorial call is a loud, rapid series of laughing notes. Compared with
  other woodpeckers, it rarely “drums” on wood.

  Flight is undulating, and the bright yellow rump is especially visible in flight. The
  species is shy and avoids close contact with people. Handling a found bird by hand is
  not recommended.

  It nests in a cavity excavated in a tree trunk. Species-level data indicate that the
  female usually lays about 4–6 eggs, and incubation lasts about 19–20 days. These are
  data from the wider range, not an average from nests specifically studied in Georgia.

  The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia treats the European green
  woodpecker as a breeding species in the country. In 2022 surveys in Borjomi-Kharagauli
  and Pshav-Khevsureti, calls typical of the breeding season were recorded from late April
  to July.

  The European green woodpecker is a year-round resident in Georgia and does not migrate
  long distances.
conservation: >-
  The European green woodpecker (Picus viridis) is assessed as Least Concern (LC) on the
  IUCN Red List. The same category is also given at European and EU level. The 2024
  Georgian bird checklist assigns the species LC both globally and nationally.

  The data presented on this page do not assess whether Georgia's population is increasing
  or decreasing. The 2022 surveys in Borjomi-Kharagauli and Pshav-Khevsureti show only a
  local picture for those areas and cannot be treated as countrywide monitoring.

  European sources connect the European green woodpecker with several international
  conservation instruments, but this does not mean that the species is included in
  Georgia's Red List.

  Old trees with suitable nesting cavities are important for the species, as are ant-rich
  meadows and other open feeding areas. Loss of such habitats can be considered an
  important species-level pressure, although a separate ranking of local threat importance
  in Georgia is not presented on this page.

  Do not catch wild European green woodpeckers or take them from nature.

  summary: >-
    The European green woodpecker is a large, conspicuous woodpecker with a red crown, black
    “mask”, and bright yellow rump. In Georgia, according to the 2024 bird checklist, it
    occurs throughout the year.

    It is most often confused with the grey-headed woodpecker. The main difference is on the
    head: the European green woodpecker has more extensive red on the top of the head, while
    the grey-headed woodpecker does not have a full red crown.
  traits:
    - >-
      The European green woodpecker is about 30–36 cm long, with a wingspan of 45–51 cm. NPLG
      gives wing length as 150–170 mm, tail length as 105–116 mm, and weight as 185–250 g.

      The back is olive-green, the rump is bright yellow, and the underparts are paler
      yellowish-green. Both sexes have a red crown and nape. A clear black patch around the
      pale eye forms a visible “mask”.

      The bill is straight, strong, and chisel-like, as is typical of woodpeckers.
    - >-
      Adult European green woodpeckers can be sexed by the cheek stripe, the so-called
      “moustache”. The male has red in the middle of the black stripe, while the female's
      stripe is entirely black.

      A juvenile looks more patterned: it has many bars and spots on the body, making the
      plumage look scaly. Even so, the green colour and red crown are still visible in young
      birds.

      A similar species is the Iberian green woodpecker (Picus sharpei), which has little or
      none of the characteristic black “mask” around the eye. It occurs in western Europe and
      is not included in the Georgian bird checklist.
    - >-
      The European green woodpecker is most often confused with the grey-headed woodpecker
      (Picus canus). The grey-headed woodpecker is also a year-round resident in Georgia, but
      its head is greyer and it lacks a full red crown. The male has only a small red patch on
      the forehead, and the female has none.

      The black woodpecker (Dryocopus martius) is much larger and almost entirely black. The
      male has a broad red area on the head, while the female shows red mainly on the nape.

      [Great spotted woodpecker](dendrocopos-major), as well as Syrian, middle spotted,
      white-backed, and lesser spotted woodpeckers, are mainly black-and-white birds with red
      markings and do not have the olive-green colour of the European green woodpecker.

      [Eurasian wryneck](jynx-torquilla) is much smaller and cryptically brown. In Georgia it
      is a breeding migrant and, unlike the European green woodpecker, does not remain
      throughout the year.
    - >-
      The European green woodpecker does not pose a direct threat to people. If you find a
      weakened or injured bird, handling it by hand is not recommended.

      In Georgia, especially at forest edge or in meadow habitat, a large green woodpecker on
      the ground is often this species. Unlike spotted woodpeckers, the European green
      woodpecker often comes down to the ground while feeding.
facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows the
    OSME Region List for names: order Piciformes, family Picidae, genus Picus.
    IOC World Bird List treats Picus viridis as a species separate from Iberian
    green woodpecker (Picus sharpei) and Levaillant’s woodpecker (Picus
    vaillantii).
  - >-
    Widely recognised subspecies include P. v. viridis, P. v. karelini
    (Italy and the Balkans east through Asia Minor and the Caucasus to
    northern Iran and south-west Turkmenistan), and P. v. innominatus in the
    Zagros. Georgian dictionaries name the form in the country as southern
    green woodpecker, P. v. karelini (Brandt, 1841). Field identification is to
    species. Some genetic work has questioned whether karelini is distinct from
    nominate viridis; handbooks still list it.
  - >-
    Georgian is მწვანე კოდალა. English names in use are European green
    woodpecker (2024 checklist), Eurasian green woodpecker (BirdLife), and
    green woodpecker. Older English includes yaffle, from the call. An older
    Georgian dictionary statement that only one Picus species occurs in Georgia
    is out of date: the 2024 checklist also confirms grey-headed woodpecker
    (Picus canus).
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: YR-R, data quality A, global and national Red List
    LC, occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph) and SR (sound
    recording). That is country-level confirmation plus the named protected-area
    surveys cited above, not a complete regional map.
