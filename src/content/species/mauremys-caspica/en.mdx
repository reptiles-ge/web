location: "Southeastern Georgia"
description: >-
  A native freshwater turtle of southeastern Georgia — not a land tortoise and
  not the slider.
overview: >-
  The Caspian turtle (Mauremys caspica) is a native freshwater turtle.
  Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026 list it as biologically confirmed and found in
  southeastern Georgia. The checklist form is M. c. caspica; the western form
  M. c. rivulata is treated as a separate species. The figure specimen is from
  Tbilisi — Lisi Lake; that is a locality, not a city-wide map.
habitat: >-
  Ponds, lakes, and slow waters in southeastern Georgia. Lisi Lake (Tbilisi) is
  a figure-specimen locality, not the full range.
diet: >-
  An omnivorous freshwater turtle — invertebrates, plant material, and small
  vertebrates.
behavior: >-
  Swims and often basks on the bank. Not a land tortoise.
conservation: >-
  A native, biologically confirmed species. This profile does not cite a
  national Red List entry.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A native water turtle of the southeast. A domed shell is the land tortoise;
    a red ear stripe is the introduced slider.
  traits:
    - >-
      A native water turtle with a flatter shell than a land tortoise’s dome.
      Not a land tortoise — it swims.
    - >-
      Pale lengthwise stripes often on the neck. A high-domed shell is the land
      tortoise (Testudo graeca).
    - >-
      Webbed feet; often basks on the bank of ponds, lakes, and slow waters.
    - >-
      No red stripe behind the eye — that mark belongs to the introduced slider
      (Trachemys scripta, T. s. elegans).
    - >-
      Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026 name southeastern Georgia. The pond turtle (Emys
      orbicularis) occurs in western and eastern Georgia; its shell is often
      darker with yellow speckling.
faq:
  - question: Is this a land tortoise?
    answer: >-
      No. Mauremys caspica is a freshwater turtle. Georgia’s only land tortoise
      is Testudo graeca.
  - question: Where does it occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026: southeastern Georgia. The figure specimen is
      from Tbilisi — Lisi Lake; that is a locality, not a complete map.
  - question: How do I tell it from a red-eared slider?
    answer: >-
      The slider (Trachemys scripta) is introduced; T. s. elegans has a broad
      red stripe behind the eye. The Caspian turtle is native and lacks that
      stripe.
  - question: How do I tell it from a European pond turtle?
    answer: >-
      The pond turtle (Emys orbicularis) occurs in western and eastern Georgia;
      its shell is often darker with yellow speckling. The Caspian turtle is
      recorded from the southeast and often has lengthwise neck stripes.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      Site classification: Harmless. It has no venom.
