description: >-
  European blind snake (Xerotyphlops vermicularis), a small, non-venomous snake
  of eastern Georgia. Learn how to recognise it and where it occurs.
overview: >-
  Xerotyphlops vermicularis is a small, nonvenomous blind snake that spends most of its life underground. It has been recorded in eastern Georgia. It has a slender, wormlike body, and its eyes appear as dark dots beneath its scales. It is not dangerous to people.
habitat: >-
  The European blind snake lives underground, often beneath stones. Even in dry areas, it seems to prefer relatively damp spots. As of 2026, a specimen from Chachuna Reserve had been studied, and there were photographic records from near Dali Mountain Reservoir and from Tbilisi.
diet: >-
  It feeds mainly on ants, including their eggs, larvae and pupae. It also eats other small animals without backbones.
behavior: >-
  It spends most of its time underground. It is rarely seen on the surface, mostly at dusk or at night; you may also find it under a stone. If you find it, take a photo and leave it where it is; do not pick it up. If you have already lifted a stone, carefully put it back.
conservation: >-
  A 2017 global assessment rated the extinction risk for the European blind snake as low. It was made before another species was separated in 2020 and does not assess the species now called the European blind snake separately.

identification:
  summary: >-
    It is a small, very slender blind snake. Its body looks like a worm, its scales are smooth and shiny, and its head is barely distinct from its neck. Its eyes are hidden beneath the scales and appear only as small dark dots.
  traits:
    - >-
      The body is very thin and tube-shaped, with almost the same thickness from head to tail. It is usually brownish, yellowish or flesh-colored, with a lighter underside.
    - >-
      Its eyes are very small and appear as dark spots beneath the scales. Its head is not clearly distinct from its neck, making it look even more like a worm.
    - >-
      Its entire body is covered in smooth, shiny scales. Its slender body, which is almost the same width along its length, head that is barely distinct from the body, and very short tail distinguish it from other small snakes.
    - >-
      [Eryx jaculus](eryx-jaculus) is much thicker than the European blind snake. It has small but clearly visible eyes and a short, thick, blunt tail. The European blind snake is much thinner, almost the same thickness along its length, and looks like a worm.
faq:
  - question: Does the European blind snake occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The European blind snake is among the confirmed species in the [2026 Georgian checklist](/news/georgia-herpetofauna-checklist-2026). The specimen examined came from Chachuna Reserve.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      As of 2026, a specimen is known from Chachuna Reserve in eastern Georgia, along with photos from near Dali Mountain Reservoir and from Tbilisi.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. The European blind snake is nonvenomous and is not dangerous to people.
  - question: Is this the javelin sand boa?
    answer: >-
      No. The European blind snake and the [javelin sand boa](eryx-jaculus) are different species and look very different. The European blind snake has a very thin, worm-like body and eyes that are barely visible. The javelin sand boa is much thicker, with clearly visible eyes and a short, thick, blunt tail.
