description: >-
  A small gecko with vertical pupils and expanded toes. In Georgia, when
  separating it from the Caucasian agama, look beyond color: check the eyes,
  toes, body shape, and the setting where you saw the lizard.
overview: >-
  The Caspian gecko (Tenuidactylus caspius) is the only gecko species confirmed
  in Georgia. When you see a small lizard on a wall it can be confused with the
  Caucasian agama, but vertical pupils and expanded toes are especially useful
  for telling it apart.

  In Georgia, its natural range covers the country’s extreme southeast. One
  important locality is Samukhi Valley in Dedoplistskaro Municipality, where the
  species is confirmed. Specimens from Tbilisi have also been studied.

  Apart from the natural range, separate urban populations of the Caspian gecko
  are also known. Records exist from towns in eastern Georgia, as well as
  Kutaisi and Sukhumi. Its presence in cities does not mean that every small
  lizard seen on a wall in Georgia is a gecko; often it may be the Caucasian
  agama or another lizard species.

  The Caspian gecko is harmless to people and does not pose a danger.

faq:
  - question: Does the Caspian gecko occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. As of the 2026 checklist, the Caspian gecko is a biologically
      confirmed species in Georgia. Its natural range is in the country’s extreme
      southeast, and separate urban populations are known from several large
      settlements.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. The Caspian gecko is harmless to people and has no venom. If you find
      one, there is no need to catch it; it is better to leave it where it is.
  - question: Where is it confirmed?
    answer: >-
      Confirmed specimens are known from Tbilisi and Samukhi Valley; one record
      is tied to Samukhi Valley in Dedoplistskaro Municipality. The sources also
      mention some large settlements in eastern Georgia, Kutaisi, and Sukhumi,
      but these data do not mean the species occurs across all of Georgia.
  - question: How does it differ from the Caucasian agama?
    answer: >-
      The Caucasian agama belongs to the agama family, while the Caspian gecko
      belongs to the gecko family. Vertical pupils and expanded toes are
      especially useful for identifying the Caspian gecko; those traits are not
      characteristic of the Caucasian agama.
  - question: How do I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Look for vertical pupils, expanded toes, and a relatively flattened,
      gecko-like body. Color alone is not enough; many small lizards can look
      similar against walls and stone.
