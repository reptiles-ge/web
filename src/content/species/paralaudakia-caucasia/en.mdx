commonName: "Caucasian Agama"
location: "eastern and southern Georgia"
description: >-
  The Caucasian agama (Paralaudakia caucasia) is the only lizard of the agama family in Georgia. It lives in rocky places, is non-venomous and can be recognized by the spiny rings on its tail.
overview: >-
  The Caucasian Agama (Paralaudakia caucasia) is the only lizard of the agama family in Georgia. It is active by day and is not venomous to humans. It has been recorded in Kakheti, Shida Kartli, Kvemo Kartli, Tbilisi, Mtskheta-Mtianeti and Samtskhe-Javakheti. In Imereti and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti it has been seen only in isolated cases, and these regions are not considered part of its range. Its body is flattened, but to identify it, look at the tail: the spines on it are arranged in rings.
habitat: >-
  The Caucasian Agama lives on dry, rocky slopes and steep cliffs. In Georgia it has been found in Fia and Tmogvi in Aspindza Municipality, and around Datviskhevi and Pantishara in Dedoplistskaro Municipality. It also occurs on the Iori Plateau, in Kartli, Borjomi Municipality and Meskheti. Its flattened body lets it slip easily into narrow crevices. It also shelters under rocks and in burrows.
diet: >-
  The Caucasian agama eats insects, spiders and other small animals like them. It also feeds on plants.
behavior: >-
  The Caucasian Agama is active during the day and often warms itself on rocks and cliff ledges. It lays eggs. When threatened, it quickly hides in a crevice, and to defend itself it may also puff up its body. Do not touch a wild animal with your hands.
conservation: >-
  According to a 2017 assessment, the Caucasian Agama faces a low risk of extinction worldwide. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The Caucasian Agama is a sturdy lizard with a flattened body and a triangular head. Look at the tail: it is spiny, and its scales form distinct rings. In a photo taken from a distance, a small Caucasian Agama and a [Caspian Thick-toed Gecko](tenuidactylus-caspius) may be confused if the eye and the tail scales are not clearly visible.
  traits:
    - >-
      The Caucasian Agama has a flattened, triangular head, and its ear opening is clearly visible.
    - >-
      The scales on the back are not all alike: they are relatively large along the spine and more spiny on the sides of the body.
    - >-
      The tail is one of the most noticeable features for recognising the Caucasian Agama. It is covered in rings of spiny scales and is about twice as long as the body.
    - >-
      Its usual back colour is olive or grey. Some individuals have yellowish spots on the body, others a dark, net-like pattern. During the breeding season, the male's chest and the sides of its belly may darken.
    - >-
      A young Caucasian Agama may have dark spots or crosswise stripes on its back. The Caspian Thick-toed Gecko has a similar pattern. Up close, check two features: the gecko has a vertical pupil, while the agama has rings of spines on its tail. The gecko is mostly active at night, but it has also been seen by day. So do not rely only on whether you saw the animal by day or at night.

      Yes. The Caucasian Agama (Paralaudakia caucasia) is the only member of the agama family in Georgia. No other lizard from this family is found here.

  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous?
    answer: >-
      No, the Caucasian Agama is not venomous. It may bite you if you pick it up, though. So do not touch it, and leave it in the wild.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      The Caucasian Agama's body measures up to 15 cm from the tip of the snout to the base of the tail. The tail is about twice as long as the body, so with its tail the lizard looks much longer.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      To identify the Caucasian Agama, look at its tail: the spines on it are arranged in rings. Its body is firm and flattened, its head triangular, and its scales uneven and spiny. To [tell it apart from a gecko](/lizards/identifikacia), check the pupil and the tail scales. Don't rely on colour alone, or on when you saw it.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      The Caucasian Agama eats insects, spiders and other small animals like them. It also feeds on plants.
  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      During the day, especially in warm weather. That is when the Caucasian Agama is often seen on rocks and stones. If you want to spot one, look carefully at rocks and stones on a warm day.
