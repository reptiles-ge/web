overview: >-
  Brauner’s rock lizard (Darevskia brauneri) is associated with the western and central Greater Caucasus. The species has been recorded in Georgia and is not venomous. However, a 2026 paper by Tarkhnishvili and coauthors leaves open the question of whether it is a separate species. The paper mentions Mestia, the Manshura River, Martvili, Ertso Lake, the Enguri and Tekhuri river basins, and Lebarde. It also mentions northwestern Georgia in connection with D. b. myusserica. This list shows which places in Georgia appear in records of the lizard. But color or a single photo is not enough to identify every similar lizard as Darevskia brauneri. It belongs to the D. saxicola group. It is widely accepted as a separate species, but the paper remains cautious: the boundaries between it and D. saxicola and D. szczerbaki, where they meet, and how their features change gradually from place to place have not yet been fully checked. The D. arribasi described near Ertso is not treated as a separate species in this paper and remains D. b. arribasi.
habitat: >-
  Sources on this species link Brauner’s rock lizard in Georgia to the western and central Greater Caucasus. They specifically name Mestia and the Manshura River; the paper includes an illustration and barcodes associated with them. Martvili, Ertso Lake, the Enguri and Tekhuri river basins, and Lebarde are mentioned in the discussion of the closest genetic sequences. D. b. myusserica is mentioned from northwestern Georgia. We should not assume it occurs beyond these places based only on a general idea of where a Caucasian rock lizard might live. For a field record, the most useful details are the exact location, the elevation and a description of the surroundings if available, and a full set of photos, especially of the head and the area in front of the anal opening.
