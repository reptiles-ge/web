commonName: Common blackbird
location: Year-round resident in Georgia
description: >-
  A medium thrush: males are black with a yellow-orange bill; females are dark
  brown. Confirmed in Georgia as a year-round resident on the 2024 national bird
  checklist.
overview: >-
  The common blackbird (Turdus merula) is a true thrush (family Turdidae)
  confirmed in Georgia as a year-round resident. The 2024 annotated checklist of
  the birds of Georgia codes it YR-R: a breeder present throughout the year.
  Seasonality is classed as high-quality (code A). Occurrence quality is high
  (H), with photographs, ringing data, and sound recordings. Adults are about
  23.5–29 cm long at species level. It is not a threat to people. IUCN lists the
  species as Least Concern. The 2024 checklist assigns LC globally and
  nationally. The usual Georgian name is შავი შაშვი.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia look for it in woodland with undergrowth, parks, gardens, orchards,
  and scrub — including towns. The 2024 checklist confirms country-level
  year-round resident status but does not publish a municipality gazetteer. Do
  not assume it occurs evenly across Georgia, including treeless high plateaus
  and arid open steppe without cover. Species-level habitat is deciduous, mixed,
  or partly coniferous woodland, forest edge, and well-vegetated gardens. A
  separate national elevation band for Georgia is not published here; in
  mountain regions of the wider range it can breed into montane woodland, but
  this page does not invent a Georgian upper limit. The atlas map stays empty
  until named Georgian localities are compiled from sources that list them.
diet: >-
  There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page. Across the range it is
  omnivorous: earthworms, insects, snails and other invertebrates on the ground,
  plus berries and fruit in season. It often forages by hopping on short grass
  or leaf litter and pausing to listen or probe. That is species-level feeding,
  not a measured Georgian menu.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. The male’s song is a rich, fluty phrase from a high perch
  — roof ridge, tree top, or antenna — especially in the breeding season.
  Contact and alarm calls include sharp “chink” and rattling notes. In much of
  the milder west Palearctic the species is resident; northern birds may move
  south in winter. The 2024 Georgian checklist treats it as YR-R only (not coded
  as a separate migrant or winter visitor), so do not read Georgia as a pure
  passage stop on that list. Nesting is a mud-lined cup in a bush, hedge, or
  low tree; species-level clutches are often about three to five eggs, pale
  blue-green with freckles. Those figures are from the wider range, not a
  Georgian nest sample on this page. The checklist treats the species as a
  breeder in Georgia.
conservation: >-
  BirdLife International / IUCN assess Turdus merula as Least Concern. The range
  and population are very large. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List
  (Government Resolution No. 190). The 2024 Georgian bird checklist assigns
  global and national Red List category LC. A Georgia-specific population trend
  is not quantified on this page. Local pressures elsewhere include cats,
  window collisions, and habitat change in farmland; those are not measured here
  for Georgia. Do not catch or keep wild birds.
interaction: >-
  It is not venomous, poisonous, or a threat to people. Wild birds must not be
  captured. Keep a distance from nests in hedges and garden shrubs.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A thrush-sized bird. Adult males are glossy black with a yellow-orange bill
    and a yellow eye-ring. Females are dark brown with a duller bill. In Georgia
    it is a year-round resident on the 2024 checklist. The usual confusion is
    other thrushes, especially ring ouzel in mountain habitat.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length about 23.5–29 cm at species level; wingspan about 34–38.5 cm; mass
      commonly about 80–125 g. The bill is fairly long and straight. The tail is
      medium-long. Flight is often low and undulating with closed-wing dips.
    - >-
      Adult males are black, with an orange-yellow bill and a yellow orbital
      ring. First-year males can look duller, with a darker bill and browner
      wings. Adult females are sooty- or chocolate-brown above, paler and
      mottled below, with a brownish bill. Juveniles are brown with pale spotting
      on the upperparts and a speckled breast.
    - >-
      Ring ouzel (Turdus torquatus) is also a year-round resident in Georgia on
      the 2024 checklist; adult males show a clear white breast crescent and
      usually prefer higher, rockier or mountain habitats than garden blackbirds.
      Song thrush (Turdus philomelos) is smaller, brown above, with a boldly
      spotted pale breast — not solid black. Mistle thrush (Turdus viscivorus)
      is larger and greyer, with bold underpart spotting. Fieldfare (Turdus
      pilaris) is a migrant and winter visitor with a grey head and rump and
      chestnut back. This atlas does not yet have separate pages for those
      thrushes.
    - >-
      It is not a threat to people. Leave it alone. A solid black garden thrush
      with a yellow-orange bill in Georgian towns and woodland edges is this
      species; a black mountain thrush with a white crescent is more likely ring
      ouzel.

facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows IOC /
    OSME Region List names: order Passeriformes, family Turdidae, genus Turdus.
    English names in use include common blackbird, Eurasian blackbird, and
    simply blackbird.
  - >-
    Handbook treatments place the Caucasus, Transcaucasia, and northern Iran in
    subspecies T. m. aterrimus (type locality in the northern Caucasus). Birds
    of the World maps aterrimus through south-east Europe, Turkey, the Caucasus,
    and northern Iran. NPLG Wiki Dictionaries instead assign nominate T. m.
    merula to Georgia. Subspecies are hard to name in the field; treat birds in
    Georgia to species unless a specialist source assigns a form.
  - >-
    The usual Georgian name is შავი შაშვი. English is common blackbird or
    Eurasian blackbird. Older or regional names include European blackbird.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: YR-R, data quality A, global and national Red List LC,
    occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph), RD (ringing data), and SR
    (sound recording). That is country-level confirmation, not a regional map.

  - question: When is it present in Georgia?

      Year-round, according to the 2024 checklist (YR-R). Song is strongest in
      the breeding season, but birds may be seen in gardens and woodland in any
      month.

      breeder present throughout the year.
