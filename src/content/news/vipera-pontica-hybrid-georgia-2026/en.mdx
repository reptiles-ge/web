## What happened?

The observation was at 10:45 on 7 May, in the Borjomi valley in <Region id="samtskhe-javakheti">Samtskhe — Javakheti</Region>. The authors place the site in the upper Kura valley, above 800 m. That morning they had already found five adult <Species id="vipera-transcaucasiana">nose-horned vipers</Species> at the same place. The presumed hybrid was first basking at a forest edge, then again close to a nose-horned viper.

<Figure src="/images/news/vipera-pontica-hybrid-georgia-2026/figure-2.jpg" />

They describe the animal as a possible adult male. Snout–vent length was 447 mm, tail 61 mm, total 508 mm. No specimen was collected; identification rests on field notes and photographs. The paper has no DNA from this individual.

## What is Vipera pontica?

The name <Sci>Vipera pontica</Sci> was given in 1990 to two juveniles from the Çoruh valley in Artvin, northeastern Türkiye. The taxon has been disputed ever since. Genomic studies cited in this paper treat those snakes as rare hybrids between <Species id="vipera-kaznakovi">Caucasus viper</Species> (<Sci>Vipera kaznakovi</Sci>) and nose-horned viper (<Sci>Vipera ammodytes transcaucasiana</Sci>) — not as a separate lineage.

<Figure src="/images/news/vipera-pontica-hybrid-georgia-2026/figure-1.jpg" />

The Borjomi record extends this putative hybrid form about 150 km east of the type locality. An unusual specimen from Gori was described in 1983; the authors leave its status uncertain and do not rest this observation on it.

## What characters did it show?

The snake had an upturned but hornless snout, fragmented head scalation, intermediate ventral and subcaudal counts, a mixed dorsal zigzag, and a bicoloured tail tip. The authors call this a combination of diagnostic traits from both parental species.

<Figure src="/images/news/vipera-pontica-hybrid-georgia-2026/figure-4.jpg" />

They argue that such a coordinated mix is not explained by a single anomaly or by local variation, and that it fits an F1 hybrid. A later backcross is not excluded without genetic data. They consider involvement of <Sci>Vipera berus barani</Sci> unlikely: the species is not known from Georgia.

## What does this mean for the atlas?

This is not a third viper species. The paper leaves <Sci>V. pontica</Sci> in the synonymy of both parents. On this atlas Georgian nose-horned vipers are <Sci>Vipera ammodytes</Sci>, subspecies transcaucasiana; the type locality is Borjomi. The 2026 [checklist](https://doi.org/10.3897/caucasiana.5.e189214) takes the same line.

The authors describe a narrow contact zone in the Borjomi valley: <Species id="vipera-kaznakovi">Caucasus viper</Species> in moister meadow and forest-edge habitat, nose-horned viper on rocky slopes. Where those microhabitats meet, rare hybridisation can occur. Both parents are venomous. This page does not assign a separate risk to the hybrid. If you meet a viper, keep your distance and do not pick it up. For a bite, call 112.
