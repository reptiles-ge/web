commonName: Rock Dove
location: Year-round resident and breeder in Georgia
description: >-
  Rock Dove (Columba livia) breeds and remains in Georgia year round. Learn how to identify it, what the Georgian evidence shows, and how it differs from the woodpigeon.
overview: >-
  Columba livia lives in Georgia year-round and nests there. It is usually bluish-gray, with two black bars on its wings and a dark tip to its tail. Pigeons in cities belong to the same species; some are descendants of domesticated pigeons. Color alone cannot tell us whether a pigeon seen in a city is of wild origin. It is not venomous.
habitat: >-
  The wild pigeon is also on the list of birds of the Alazani Valley. In the wild, it usually lives on cliffs. Pigeons living freely in cities also use ledges on buildings and bridges, and look for food in open areas.
diet: >-
  It mainly eats seeds and grains it finds on the ground. It sometimes eats fruit and, rarely, animals without backbones. In cities, it also eats food left by people.
behavior: >-
  It is active during the day; it often walks on the ground and feeds in groups. It has a low coo. It nests in a crevice or on a ledge of a cliff or building. It usually lays two white eggs; both parents feed the chicks a milk-like food brought up from their crops.
conservation: >-
  The 2024 assessment does not consider this species threatened globally or in Georgia. The assessment for Georgia is a draft and does not establish a legal status. Pigeons of wild origin are hard to distinguish from domesticated pigeons living in cities. So the number of city pigeons cannot tell us how the wild population is doing.
interaction: >-
  It is not poisonous, but breathing in dust from accumulated, dried droppings can cause an infection. Do not disturb the nest or chick, and do not catch the wild bird.

identification:
  summary: >-
    It is usually bluish gray, with two black bars on the wing and a dark band at the end of the tail. Its neck often has a green and purple sheen. Birds living in cities may be white, reddish, black or mottled. A bird without two bars on its wing may still belong to this species.
  traits:
    - >-
      It is about 30–36 cm long. It has a stocky body, a relatively small head and pointed wings. Two distinctive black bars on each wing are clearly visible even when the wing is folded. Males and females look very similar.
    - >-
      [Columba palumbus](columba-palumbus) is larger. Adults have a white patch on the side of the neck and a broad white band on the wing that shows in flight. Young birds may not yet have the white neck patch, so check the wing too. Columba oenas grows to a similar size but lacks the two distinct black wing bars typical of a wild pigeon.
    - >-
      The Eurasian collared dove is lighter in color and has a black half-ring on the back of its head.
facts:
  - >-
    The accepted scientific name is Columba livia, in order Columbiformes and family Columbidae. Rock Pigeon is another English name. Feral Pigeon describes free-living descendants of domestic pigeons, not a separate species.
faq:
  - question: Does the Rock Dove occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. According to 2024 records, this species lives year-round and nests.
  - question: Where is it listed within Georgia?
    answer: >-
      It is on the list of birds of the Alazani Valley. City pigeons are also found in Georgia, but where they are found alone cannot tell us whether they are of wild origin.
  - question: How can I tell it from a woodpigeon?
    answer: >-
      The common woodpigeon is larger. Adults have a white patch on the neck and a broad white band on the wing that is visible in flight. A typical wild pigeon has two black bands on its wing.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      An adult bird is about 30–36 cm long.
  - question: Are city pigeons the same species?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Pigeons living freely in cities also belong to Columba livia; some are descendants of domesticated pigeons. Their coloring does not prove wild ancestry.
  - question: What does it eat, and is it dangerous?
    answer: >-
      It mainly eats seeds and grains. It is not venomous, but avoid breathing in dust from accumulated dried droppings; do not disturb its nest or young.
