overview: >-
  The European blind snake (Xerotyphlops vermicularis) is a small, harmless blind
  snake that spends most of its life underground and is rarely seen on the
  surface. In Georgia, the species is confirmed in the 2026 checklist: a barcoded
  specimen is known from Chachuna Managed Reserve, and the distribution material
  also marks the Dalis Mta reservoir area. This atlas map additionally shows
  Kakheti and Tbilisi, including a photo-confirmed record from Dighomi.

  The body is slender and worm-like, while the eyes are strongly reduced and show
  as small dark spots under the scales. Published data place total length at
  about 22-35 cm. It is not dangerous to people.
habitat: >-
  The European blind snake lives mainly in loose, dry or moderately dry soil. It
  moves underground around stones, roots and soil cracks.

  Confirmed Georgian information mostly points to dry eastern zones: Chachuna
  Managed Reserve, the Dalis Mta reservoir area and Tbilisi. With the data used
  on this page, an exact elevation range for Georgia is not separately defined.
diet: >-
  It feeds on small underground invertebrates, especially ants, termites and
  their eggs or larvae. This diet explains its strong link with soil, stones and
  other underground microhabitats.
behavior: >-
  It spends most of its life underground. It may appear after rain, when soil is
  turned over, or when a stone is lifted, but in general it is rarely seen.

  If you find one, it is best to take a photo and leave the animal in place.
  Small underground snakes are sensitive to physical handling, so picking them up
  is not necessary.
conservation: >-
  The global IUCN assessment lists the European blind snake as Least Concern. A
  separate national risk assessment for Georgia is not confirmed by the sources
  used on this page.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A small, very slender blind snake. The body looks worm-like, the scales are
    smooth and glossy, and the head is barely set off from the neck. The eyes are
    hidden under the scales and show only as small dark spots.
  traits:
    - >-
      The body is very slender, cylindrical and almost the same width from head
      to tail. Colour is usually brownish, yellowish or flesh-coloured, with a
      paler underside.
    - >-
      The eyes are strongly reduced and show only as small dark spots beneath
      the scales. The head is not sharply set off from the neck, which
      strengthens its worm-like appearance.
    - >-
      The tail is very short. The whole body is covered in smooth, glossy scales.
      The slender cylindrical body, almost indistinct head and short tail
      distinguish it from other small snakes.
    - >-
      The [javelin sand boa](eryx-jaculus) is much stouter than the blind snake.
      It has small but clearly visible eyes and a short, thick, blunt tail. The
      blind snake is much thinner, almost even in width and worm-like.
    - >-
      [Collared dwarf snake](eirenis-collaris) and [ring-headed dwarf snake](eirenis-modestus)
      look like ordinary small snakes: their eyes are easy to see, the head is
      more clearly set off from the body, and they may have a neck collar or
      dorsal markings. The blind snake has almost unnoticeable eyes and a much
      more worm-like body.
    - >-
      The European blind snake is non-venomous and not dangerous to people. If
      found in a yard, garden or under a stone, it is best to take a photo, put
      the shelter back in place and let the animal move away.

  - question: Does the European blind snake occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The European blind snake is a confirmed species in Georgia. In the

      area.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Confirmed information mostly points to dry zones of eastern Georgia,
      including Kakheti, Chachuna Managed Reserve, the Dalis Mta reservoir area
      and Tbilisi. The species' exact full distribution in Georgia still needs
      additional data.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. It is a non-venomous blind snake and is not dangerous to people.
  - question: Is this the javelin sand boa?
    answer: >-
      No. The European blind snake and the javelin sand boa are different
      species and also differ strongly in appearance. The blind snake has a very
      slender, worm-like body and almost unnoticeable eyes. The javelin sand boa
      is much stouter, with clearly visible eyes and a short, thick, blunt tail.
  - question: How large does it get?
    answer: >-
      According to morphological data summarized in Reptile Database, blind
      snakes in this group reach about 22-35 cm in total length.
  - question: What does it eat?

      It feeds mainly on ants, termites and their eggs or larvae.
  - question: How do I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Look for a very slender, worm-like body, glossy scales, a head barely
      distinct from the neck and eyes visible as small dark spots under the
      scales.
  - question: When is it seen above ground?
    answer: >-
      It spends most of its time underground. It may appear on the surface after
