overview: >-
  The Mingrelian scorpion (Euscorpius mingrelicus) is a true scorpion and belongs
  to the arachnid class (Arachnida). It is neither an insect nor a spider.

  This page uses Euscorpius mingrelicus as the main scientific name. According
  to the 2015 revision, Euscorpius mingrelicus sensu stricto is linked to
  Georgia and Turkey. Later, a 2019 paper treated Alpiscorpius as a separate
  genus, so the same taxon also appears in some modern databases as
  Alpiscorpius mingrelicus.

  For Georgia, the reliable conclusion is that the species occurs in western
  Georgia. The map marks Adjara, Samegrelo — Zemo Svaneti, Guria, and Imereti.

  The Mingrelian scorpion is small and dark. According to the Scorpion Files,
  adults reach about 38 mm. It has venom, but available data do not treat it as
  a species of high medical risk to people.
habitat: >-
  Existing sources mainly link the Mingrelian scorpion with humid, mountainous
  and forested environments.

  By day, the scorpion may hide under stones, logs, dead bark, or other natural
  cover. This differs from the drier and more open places more typical of the
  mottled scorpion.

  If you see a scorpion in a humid forested valley of western Georgia,
  Euscorpius mingrelicus is one possible candidate, although habitat alone cannot
  identify it exactly.
diet: >-
  The diet of the Mingrelian scorpion in Georgia has not been fully studied
  separately. Like other scorpions, it is predatory and hunts small arthropods.

  It uses its pincers to catch prey, and its sting and venom to subdue it. This
  is general scorpion biology, not the result of a separate diet study in
  Georgia.
behavior: >-
  The Mingrelian scorpion has a secretive way of life. It is most likely to be
  found when stones, logs, bark, or other natural cover are lifted, especially in
  humid and forested environments.

  Do not pick it up by hand. If moving it from a house or yard is necessary, use
  a container and stiff card instead of direct contact, or ask a specialist.

  For exact identification, sharp photos from above and the side, plus the exact
  discovery location, are especially useful.
conservation: >-

interaction: >-
  The Mingrelian scorpion is venomous in the biological sense that it uses venom
  to subdue prey. Available data indicate that the sting effects of
  Euscorpius/Alpiscorpius group scorpions are mostly local, and the Mingrelian
  scorpion is not considered a species of high medical risk to people.

  A sting can still be painful. Do not pick up the scorpion by hand, and do not
  let children or pets touch it. If pain is strong, a child is affected,
  allergic or other systemic symptoms develop, or the situation is unclear, call
  112 and seek medical care.

      at the end of the tail. This trait makes it easy to separate from a
      pseudoscorpion, which has pincers but no tail or sting.
    - >-

    - >-
