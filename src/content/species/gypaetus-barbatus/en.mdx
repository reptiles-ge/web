overview: >-
  The bearded vulture (Gypaetus barbatus) breeds in Georgia and is present
  year-round. On this page's records-by-region table, distribution is confirmed
  in Mtskheta–Mtianeti, Kakheti, Samtskhe–Javakheti, Samegrelo–Zemo Svaneti,
  and Kvemo Kartli. Imereti and Shida Kartli have only individual records and
  are not counted as distribution. A 2023 survey confirmed breeding at Kazbegi
  in Mtskheta–Mtianeti and recorded the species at Vashlovani without confirming
  breeding there. This large scavenger lives around rocky mountain
  valleys. Its wingspan is 2.3–2.9 m; in flight, look for the long wedge-shaped
  tail and, in adults, the black bristles beneath the bill. It feeds mainly on
  bones. Georgia's legal Red List classifies it as Vulnerable (VU); the global
  category reported in the 2024 Georgian checklist is Near Threatened (NT).
habitat: >-
  Breeding is confirmed in Kazbegi National Park and on nearby cliffs in
  Mtskheta–Mtianeti. A 2023 survey recorded four nests, probably belonging to
  three pairs; one nest held a chick. This is a local survey, not a national
  census. The map on this page counts only the five regions with confirmed
  status in the table: Mtskheta–Mtianeti, Kakheti, Samtskhe–Javakheti,
  Samegrelo–Zemo Svaneti, and Kvemo Kartli. The same report records the species
  at 400 m in Vashlovani Protected
  Areas, Kakheti, without confirming breeding there. Rocky mountain valleys
  and open, stony slopes are characteristic habitat. The report gives 4,500 m
  in the Greater Caucasus as the upper end of Georgian records, not a typical
  nesting altitude.
diet: >-
  Bones make up most of its food. It swallows small bones whole and drops
  larger ones onto rock to break them. It eats and digests the bone fragments
  as well as the marrow. It also eats some soft tissue from carcasses. Despite
  names that suggest lamb hunting, live lambs are not its typical food; by
  using bones it takes a part of the carcass that many other scavengers leave
  behind.
behavior: >-
  The 2024 checklist of Georgia's birds records it as a year-round breeding
  resident. Adults are mostly sedentary, whereas some young birds travel long
  distances. It nests on cliff ledges or in crevices. The clutch contains one
  or two eggs; if both hatch, the older chick attacks the younger, so usually
  only one survives.
conservation: >-
  Georgia's 2014 legal Red List assigns the bearded vulture Vulnerable status
  (VU, D1, small population). The 2024 ornithological checklist gives it NT
  nationally and globally. These two national classifications differ; the
  scientific checklist does not automatically amend the legal listing. The
  Kazbegi survey notes possible disturbance near nests from tourism and
  quarrying.
interaction: >-
  Watch from a distance and keep away from nests. The Kazbegi survey treats
  human disturbance as a possible risk to breeding.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A large bird with a long wedge-shaped tail and relatively narrow wings.
    Adults have black bristles under the bill and often a rusty head and chest;
    juveniles are darker.
  traits:
    - >-
      Adults have dark wings and tail above, and often orange-stained head and
      breast below. The rusty colour comes from bathing in iron-rich water and
      varies between individuals. Black bristles beneath the bill form the
      “beard”; a red ring surrounds the eye.
    - >-
      Juveniles are largely dark and have broader wings than adults. At any
      age, check for the long wedge-shaped tail. The species has a wingspan of
      about 2.3–2.9 m.
    - >-
      The [griffon vulture](gyps-fulvus) has broader wings, a short tail and
      a pale body contrasting with dark flight feathers. The
      [cinereous vulture](aegypius-monachus) is almost uniformly dark but has
      a much shorter tail. For a distant bird, check tail shape as well as
      colour.

facts:
  - >-
    The accepted scientific name is Gypaetus barbatus; its original
    combination was Vultur barbatus. It belongs to Accipitriformes,
    family Accipitridae.
  - >-
    The legal Georgian Red List uses the name ბატკანძერი. The Georgian
    Encyclopedia also records კრავიჭამია; English names include Bearded
    Vulture and Lammergeier. These names should not be read as a description
    of its usual diet.
