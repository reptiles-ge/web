description: >-
  A nonvenomous lizard of rocky places in the Greater Caucasus. Colour alone
  makes it hard to identify.
overview: >-
  The Caucasian rock lizard (Darevskia caucasica) occurs in rocky parts of the Greater Caucasus in Georgia. It is not venomous or dangerous to people. Colour alone will not distinguish it from similar rock lizards.
  Finds and DNA samples from Georgia confirm its presence. However, genetically distinct groups occur within the species, and their boundaries are still being clarified.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the Caucasian rock lizard has been found in rocky parts of the Greater Caucasus, including the Truso Valley, Mount Tbatana and Lebarde. It lives on rocky slopes, among stones and along valleys.
diet: ""
behavior: >-
  It is active by day and warms itself on rocks. When threatened, it hides in a crevice. Females lay eggs.
conservation: >-
  A 2008 global assessment places the Caucasian rock lizard in the Least Concern category. This older assessment does not evaluate the lizards in Georgia separately.
interaction: >-
  It is not venomous or dangerous to people. Do not pick it up: handling stresses this small lizard, and it may bite or drop its tail. Watch from a distance, take a photo and leave it where it is.

  summary: >-
    The Caucasian rock lizard is a small, fast lizard of rocky places. Its colour varies, so colour alone will not distinguish it from similar species. Location and details of its scales may help, but even a photograph may not be enough.
  traits:
    - >-
      It has a slender body and a long tail. After an injury, the tail may look shorter or regrown.
    - >-
      It may be greenish, yellowish-brown, grey or brown, with pale stripes and dark spots along its sides. Other rock lizards can have similar colours.
    - >-
      Details of the scales on its head and near the rear of its body may help a specialist. There is no need to catch the lizard.
    - >-
      Its range meets that of the [Daghestanian rock lizard](darevskia-daghestanica) along the upper Terek, and the two may hybridise. Colour is especially unreliable for telling them apart there.
    - >-
      It has a narrow contact zone with the [Colchian rock lizard](darevskia-mixta) in the Greater Caucasus. These species look alike, so do not rely on colour alone.

      Yes. Finds and DNA samples confirm its presence in Georgia. “Candidate” refers to uncertainty about the species' boundaries, not whether it occurs here.

    answer: >-

  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Colour alone is not enough. Note where you saw it and photograph its back, side and head clearly. Even then, the species may remain uncertain; there is no need to catch the lizard.

    answer: >-
      No. A 2008 global assessment classifies it as Least Concern. That assessment does not cover the lizards in Georgia separately.
