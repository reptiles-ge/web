commonName: Darevsky's viper
location: Samtskhe — Javakheti
description: >-
  A highland viper of southern Georgia, also recorded in the Abul-Samsari and western Trialeti ranges.
overview: >-
  Darevsky’s viper (Vipera darevskii) is a small venomous snake. In Georgia, it is found in the high mountains of the Lesser Caucasus: on the Javakheti Range, in Erusheti and the Akhaltsikhe Highlands, and on the Abul-Samsari Range and in western Trialeti. It lives on stony, grassy slopes above 2,000 metres. The species is still assessed as facing an extremely high risk of extinction, but that assessment is based on data from when it was known only from a much smaller area. It has since been found in new places.
habitat: >-
  In high mountain meadows, it shelters in gaps between rocks. In Georgia, it has been found among piles of small stones and also among piles of large volcanic rocks at Madatapa and Gumbati. It has also been found in a rocky ravine on the Akhaltsikhe upland. At Madatapa and Gumbati, it has been seen at 2,200–2,600 metres above sea level. This does not mean the species occurs only at those elevations.
diet: >-
  Adult vipers eat [rock lizards](/lizards/darevskia) and sometimes small rodents.
behavior: >-
  It is active during the day and shelters among rocks. It gives birth to live young. It spends the winter deep among rocks.
conservation: >-
  The snake has been assessed as facing an extremely high risk of extinction. That assessment is based on old, limited information about where it lives. A study published in 2025 confirmed its presence in other places in Georgia. According to the study, the assessment’s estimate that the snake lives in an area of less than 100 km² is outdated. The assessment has not yet changed. Excessive grazing remains a threat: damage to the grass leaves the snake without shelter and also harms the animals it feeds on.

faq:
  - question: Is Darevsky's viper dangerous to humans?
    answer: >-
      It is venomous. You cannot tell in advance how serious a bite will be. If it bites you, call 112 immediately.
  - question: Where is it found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Samtskhe–Javakheti, it has been found on the Javakheti Range, near Madatapa, in Erusheti and on the Akhaltsikhe Upland. More recent reports also place it on the Abul-Samsari Range and in western Trialeti. In Georgia, it is found above 2,000 metres.
  - question: How does it differ from Dinnik's viper?
    answer: >-
      Darevsky’s viper is found in the high mountains of the Lesser Caucasus, while [Dinnik’s viper](vipera-dinniki) is found in the Greater Caucasus. Small size and body markings alone are not enough to identify the species.
  - question: Why is it critically endangered?
    answer: >-
      The IUCN’s current CR assessment is based on older information, from when only a small area where this species occurs was known. New research shows that its known range is much larger. However, excessive grazing still damages the places where it lives. Its level of threat therefore needs to be reassessed.
  - question: How do I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Its small size, dark zigzag pattern and the rocky meadows high in the Lesser Caucasus can help you identify it. One feature alone is not enough to tell it apart from similar vipers. Do not approach a snake you do not recognize.
  - question: What should I do if bitten?
    answer: >-
      Call 112 immediately and seek medical attention. For more information, see the [snakebite guide](/snakes/gvelis-nakbeni).
