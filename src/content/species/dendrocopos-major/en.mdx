commonName: Great spotted woodpecker
location: Year-round resident in Georgia
description: >-
  A pied black, white and red woodpecker with large white shoulder patches.
  Confirmed in Georgia as a year-round resident and breeder on the 2024 national
  bird checklist.
overview: >-
  The great spotted woodpecker (Dendrocopos major) is a pied black, white and
  red woodpecker (family Picidae) confirmed in Georgia as a year-round resident.
  The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia codes it YR-R: a breeder
  present throughout the year. Seasonality is classed as high-quality (code A).
  Occurrence quality is high (H), with photographs, sound recordings and ringing
  data. Adults are about 22–23 cm long at species level. It is not a threat to
  people. IUCN lists the species as Least Concern. The 2024 checklist assigns
  the same category nationally.
habitat: >-
  This page does not colour a municipality-by-municipality map. The 2024
  checklist confirms country-level resident status but does not publish a
  regional gazetteer. Do not assume it occurs evenly across Georgia, including
  treeless high plateaus. Named survey evidence: Javakhishvili, Melikishvili and
  Metreveli (2022) recorded it at 71.25% of forest sampling points in
  Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park (80 points) and at 34.62% of points in
  Pshav-Khevsureti Protected Areas (26 points). It was the most prevalent
  woodpecker in both areas. In that study it was not tied to any particular
  forest class, but it avoided areas with little vegetation. Forests in
  Pshav-Khevsureti are mostly high-mountain and subalpine and less suitable for
  woodpeckers; even there this species was still the most recorded woodpecker.
  Species-level habitat is deciduous, coniferous or mixed woodland, also parks
  and gardens with trees. A separate elevation belt for Georgia is not published
  here.
diet: >-
  There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page. Across the range it
  takes bark insects and their larvae, conifer-cone seeds, nuts and other seeds
  in winter. RSPB and BTO also note eggs and chicks of other hole-nesting birds;
  that is not a measured Georgian menu. In the field a pied woodpecker hitching
  on a trunk, chiselling bark or smashing cones on an “anvil” is typical.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day and often heard before it is seen. The contact call is a
  short, sharp “kik” or “tchick”. Drumming is a short, rapid burst, often less
  than a second; in spring it advertises territory and pairing. Flight is
  undulating. It is wary; do not handle it. It nests in a hole chiselled in a
  tree, usually a new one. BTO nest records in the United Kingdom give clutches
  of about four to six eggs, incubation about 14–16 days, and young in the hole
  about 20–23 days; those figures are not a Georgian nest sample on this page.
  The 2024 checklist treats the species as a breeder in Georgia. Call counts in
  Borjomi-Kharagauli and Pshav-Khevsureti in 2022 ran from 24 April to 17 May
  and from 25 June to 7 July. It is resident; the checklist does not code it as
  a passage migrant or winter visitor only.
conservation: >-
  BirdLife International assesses Dendrocopos major as Least Concern on the IUCN
  Red List. The 2024 Georgian checklist assigns LC globally and nationally. It
  is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List (Government Decree No. 190). A Georgian
  population trend is not quantified on this page. The 2022 protected-area
  survey is a local snapshot, not a national monitoring series. Do not catch
  wild birds. Loss of old and dead trees is a biologically relevant pressure at
  species level; a local threat ranking for Georgia is not published here.
interaction: >-
  It is not a threat to people; wild birds must not be caught.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A pied woodpecker with large white shoulder patches and a red vent. Adult
    males have a red nape patch; adult females do not. In Georgia it is a
    year-round resident on the 2024 checklist. The usual confusion is Syrian
    woodpecker.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length about 22–23 cm and wingspan 34–39 cm at species level (BirdID). BTO
      live birds in the United Kingdom average 78.7 g for adults. The back is
      black with large white shoulder patches; the wings and tail sides are
      barred black and white. The underparts are white with a red vent. The bill
      is straight and chisel-like. A black cheek stripe links the bill base,
      throat and nape.
    - >-
      Sex is marked on the head: adult males have a red nape patch; adult females
      have an all-black hindcrown. Juveniles have a red crown, more barred
      shoulder patches and a pinker vent. RSPB calls it blackbird-sized but more
      slender. A separate measured Georgian series is not on this page.
    - >-
      Syrian woodpecker (Dendrocopos syriacus) is also a year-round resident in
      Georgia on the 2024 checklist and is the closest field confusion. Its black
      cheek stripe does not join the nape, so the cheek stays white. Middle
      spotted woodpecker (Dendrocoptes medius) has a red crown as an adult and
      lacks the large white shoulder patches. White-backed woodpecker
      (Dendrocopos leucotos) has a white lower back, not large shoulder patches.
      Lesser spotted woodpecker (Dryobates minor) is much smaller and has no red
      vent. This atlas does not yet have separate pages for those species.
    - >-
      [European green woodpecker](picus-viridis) is a year-round resident in
      Georgia on the 2024 checklist: larger, olive-green, with a red crown. This
      atlas has a page for that species. Grey-headed woodpecker (Picus canus) is
      also a resident: grey-headed and green-backed. Black woodpecker (Dryocopus
      martius) is large and black. [Eurasian wryneck](jynx-torquilla) is small,
      cryptically brown, and a breeding migrant, not a pied resident; this atlas
      has a page for that species.
    - >-
      It is not a threat to people. Leave it alone. In Georgian woodland a pied
      woodpecker with large white shoulders is most often this species.
facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758 as Picus major. The 2024 Georgian checklist
    follows the OSME Region List for names: order Piciformes, family Picidae,
    genus Dendrocopos. IOC World Bird List treats Dendrocopos major as a
    species.
  - >-
    NPLG names the form in Georgia as Caucasian D. m. tenuirostris Buturlin,
    1906; Avibase gives the type locality as Kutaisi and Akhaltsikhe. IOC treats
    tenuirostris as the Caucasian subspecies. Birds of the World / Winkler often
    lumps tenuirostris into pinetorum, whose range includes the Caucasus. Field
    identification is to species. A measured Georgian series assigned to
    subspecies is not given on this page.
  - >-
    Georgian is დიდი ჭრელი კოდალა. English names in use are Great Spotted
    Woodpecker (2024 checklist) and Greater Spotted Woodpecker (Ilia State
    University SPA checklist). Russian is большой пёстрый дятел.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: YR-R, data quality A, global and national Red List LC,
    occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph), SR (sound recording) and RD
    (ringing data). That is country-level confirmation plus the named protected-
    area surveys above, not a full regional map.
faq:
  - question: Is the great spotted woodpecker found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia lists it as a
      confirmed year-round resident (YR-R): a breeder present throughout the
      year, with photographs, sound recordings and ringing data.
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In woodland, parks and gardens with trees. Named surveys recorded it in
      Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park (71.25% of 80 forest points) and
      Pshav-Khevsureti Protected Areas (34.62% of 26 points). It was the most
      prevalent woodpecker in both. This page does not map every region.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      A pied woodpecker with large white shoulder patches and a red vent. Males
      have a red nape patch; females do not. Juveniles have a red crown. In
      Syrian woodpecker the black cheek stripe does not join the nape.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 22–23 cm long at species level, wingspan 34–39 cm. BTO gives a mean
      adult mass of 78.7 g in the United Kingdom. A separate measured Georgian
      series is not on this page.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Mainly tree insects and larvae, and seeds and cones in winter. Diet has
      not been studied separately for Georgia on this page.
  - question: When is it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      All year, according to the 2024 checklist. It is not treated there as a
      passage migrant or winter visitor only.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. YR-R on the 2024 checklist means a resident breeder. The 2022
      protected-area survey recorded calling birds from late April to early
      July.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It is harmless. Globally it is Least Concern, and the 2024 checklist codes
      it LC nationally. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Do not catch wild
      birds.
