description: >-
  A silvery orb-weaver with a lobed abdomen. It is confirmed in Georgia and is

overview: >-
  The lobed argiope (Argiope lobata) is an orb-weaving spider in the family
  Araneidae. There is no widely established Georgian name; in this atlas the name
  "lobed argiope" is used descriptively for the lateral lobes of the abdomen.

  At species level, female body length is about 17–26 mm, and male body length is
  about 5–8 mm. Legs are not included in that size. This page does not have a
  separate measured series for Georgia.

  The species is confirmed in Georgia. It uses venom to subdue prey, but it is not
  medically significant for people. IUCN has not assessed its status, and it is
  not included in Georgia's 2014 Red List.
habitat: >-
  The lobed argiope is associated with warm, relatively dry grassy and shrubby
  habitats.

  Confirmed Georgian records are known south of Lake Nadarbazevi, east of Gori,
  and at the entrance to Vashlovani National Park. These records do not mean the
  species is evenly distributed across the country.
diet: >-
  The diet of the lobed argiope in Georgia has not been fully studied separately.
  At species level, the female mainly catches flying and jumping insects in the
  orb web.

  Prey is first wrapped in silk and then bitten.
behavior: >-
  The adult female often sits in the centre of the web by day, head down. A white
  zigzag stabilimentum may sometimes be visible in the middle of the web.

  Adult females are mainly conspicuous in the warm season. The named Georgian
  record on this page is from September, but a complete seasonal calendar for the
  country is not available.
conservation: >-
  IUCN has not assessed the lobed argiope. The species is not included in
  Georgia's 2014 Red List.

  Several records are known across the Caucasus, but that does not separately
  show the size or trend of the Georgian population.
interaction: >-
  The lobed argiope (Argiope lobata) has venom, but uses it to subdue prey. For
  people, its bite usually does not pose a serious medical danger. Bites are rare
  and mainly happen when the spider is touched, pressed, or picked up.

  If bitten, wash the area with soap and water. For local pain or swelling, a cold
  compress may be used. If breathing becomes difficult, the face or throat swells
  strongly, or the general condition worsens, call 112 immediately.

  Do not pick up the spider, and there is no need to destroy the web unless it
  blocks a space people need to use.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A large, conspicuous orb-weaving spider. The female has a silvery abdomen with
    distinct lateral lobes. The male is much smaller and its abdomen is not lobed.
  traits:
    - >-
      At species level, female body length is about 16.8–25.5 mm, and male body
      length is 5.2–7.5 mm. Legs are not included in this size. This page does
      not have a separate measured series for Georgia.
    - >-
      The female's cephalothorax is shiny silver, and the abdomen is silver or
      grey. The abdomen has clearly visible lateral projections — lobes — which
      are one of the main identification marks of the species.

      The legs are yellowish-brown with black rings. The male is smaller and more
      uniform in colour, and it lacks lateral lobes on the abdomen.
    - >-
      The lobed argiope spins a large orb web, often in grass or on low shrubs. A
      white zigzag stripe — the stabilimentum — may sometimes be visible in the
      middle of the web.

      The adult female often sits in the centre of the web, head down.
    - >-
      In Georgia it is most likely to resemble the wasp spider (Argiope
      bruennichi). A. bruennichi has bold yellow-and-black transverse bands on the
      abdomen and no lateral lobes.

      Other garden orb-weavers have different patterns. Widow spiders
      (Latrodectus) are smaller, with a rounded abdomen and no lateral lobes.
facts:
  - >-
    The accepted name is Argiope lobata (Pallas, 1772). The World Spider Catalog
    treats the species as accepted and as the type species of the genus. Range:
    southern Europe to Central Asia and China, northern Africa, Tanzania, South
    Africa, the Middle East, Pakistan, India, Myanmar.
  - >-
    Otto 2022 lists the species for Georgia. Nentwig et al. cite Georgia from
    that source. Otto 2015 counted 28 Caucasus records (Russia / Georgia /
    Azerbaijan; Armenia was not then included). Seropian et al. 2023 publish the
    Nadarbazevi specimen; ZFMK holds Vashlovani specimens.
  - >-
    There is no widely established Georgian vernacular; this page uses ლობებიანი
    არგიოპა. English: lobed argiope. Russian: аргиопа дольчатая. Turkish: loblu
    örümcek.
  - >-
    IUCN has not assessed it. It is not on the 2014 Red List.

      lobes on the abdomen.
