description: >-
  A harmless rock lizard of mountain forest. Confirmed in Georgia; not
  countrywide. Colour will not separate it from other Darevskia.
overview: >-
  Darevskia dahli is a nonvenomous rock lizard found in Georgia, mainly in the wooded and rocky areas of the Trialeti Mountains. It is not dangerous to people. Females reproduce without males and lay eggs. It resembles other rock lizards, so it cannot be identified by color alone.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the lizard called დალის ხვლიკი is found in rocky places, including mountain forests, meadows and areas above the upper edge of the forest. During a survey in 2008–2009, it was found at elevations of 830–1960 m above sea level, mainly in the Trialeti Mountains. In some places, it lives alongside another lizard, მტკვრის ხვლიკი. At one rocky site near Tbilisi, დალის ხვლიკი was found closer to a permanent water source than მტკვრის ხვლიკი.
diet: >-
  Rock lizards usually catch insects, spiders and other small animals without backbones. The species დალის ხვლიკი competes with მტკვრის ხვლიკი for food and shelter.
behavior: >-
  It is active during the day: it basks on rocks and hides in crevices. The lizard called დალის ხვლიკი in Georgian arose from a cross between [D. mixta](darevskia-mixta) and D. portschinskii. Females now reproduce without males and lay eggs. Females collected in Georgia laid 1–4 eggs at a time in captivity.
conservation: >-
  Georgia’s 2014 Red List classifies დალის ხვლიკი as vulnerable because it is found in separate areas within a small range. A global assessment in 2009 found it close to being threatened. That assessment identified the expansion of cities and road construction as threats to it.
interaction: >-

identification:
  summary: >-
    It is a small rock lizard. You cannot tell it apart from [other Darevskia species](/lizards/darevskia) by the color of its back. To identify it, where you saw it and what the scales on its head and in front of its anus look like both matter. Do not pick it up to check its scales.
  traits:
    - >-
      It has a small, slender body, a flattened head and well-developed legs. Adult lizards found in Georgia usually have a body length of about 5.4–5.8 cm. The greatest recorded body length is about 63 mm. The tail is longer than the body and breaks off easily. Do not pick up the lizard to count its scales.
    - >-
      The back is gray, brownish or olive-colored, often with dark spots or a netlike pattern. The belly may be yellowish. Other rock lizards have similar coloring, so color alone is not enough to identify the species. Photos taken in Kojori in 2026 show the scales on the head and the plates in front of the anus.
    - >-
      Dali’s lizard has two plates in front of the anus, while [D. armeniaca](darevskia-armeniaca) has one broad plate. D. armeniaca is larger (its body length reaches about 74 mm) and also occurs in southern Georgia (Ninotsminda / Saghamo, Akhalkalaki). Do not pick up the animal to check this feature.
    - >-
      [D. portschinskii](darevskia-portschinskii) is often found on the same rock as dahli. The two lizards found in Georgia are similar in size. D. portschinskii has both males and females, while almost all dahli lizards are female. To tell them apart reliably, people look at the scales on their heads and features around the rear opening; color alone is not enough.
