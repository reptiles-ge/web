location: Year-round in Georgia
description: >-
  A small, elongated predator with a brown back, white belly, and short tail. The
  tail has no black tip, which is especially useful for separating it from the
  stoat. It lives in Georgia year-round.
overview: >-
  The least weasel (Mustela nivalis) is a small predatory mammal in the weasel family. It occurs naturally in Georgia and lives there year-round. It is considered the smallest of the carnivorous mammals. Its head and body measure about 13–26 cm, and its tail is relatively short. It is not venomous; the main risk to people is a bite when they try to catch it. Worldwide, its risk of extinction is low.
habitat: |-
  In Georgia, weasels may be found in grassy areas, among shrubs, in the countryside, at forest edges, on rocky slopes and in the mountains, especially where there are small rodents. They often avoid completely open places, where birds of prey can easily spot them.
  In Georgia, weasels are found at elevations of up to 3,800 metres above sea level.
  Weasels have been recorded in Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park, Lagodekhi, the Kveseti–Kobi road corridor and Telavi. Publicly available photos also document sightings in Adjara, Kakheti, Mtskheta–Mtianeti and Samtskhe–Javakheti. These records show only that weasels were seen at particular places. They do not tell us whether weasels occur throughout a region or how many live in a given area.
diet: >-
  The weasel mainly hunts small rodents. Its small, narrow body lets it follow prey into burrows. In other places where it lives, it has also been seen eating bird eggs and chicks, small rabbits, lizards, frogs, insects and meat from dead animals.
behavior: |-
  The weasel is active both day and night. It moves quickly and often stands on its hind legs to look around. It rarely digs its own burrow. It often uses rodents’ burrows, piles of stones or other natural shelters.
  A weasel’s pregnancy lasts about 34–37 days. When rodents are plentiful, a female may have more young and give birth again.
  In some northern areas, the weasel’s fur turns white in winter, while in the south it often stays brown. One photo taken in Mtskheta-Mtianeti in March 2023 shows a white weasel. This does not mean that this color is common in Georgia.
  The weasel is rarely heard. The sound recording on this page is from Spain. The person who made the recording is unsure whether the sound is from a weasel.
conservation: |-
  The least weasel is widespread around the world. Both a global assessment and one national assessment in Georgia place it at low risk of extinction. It was not considered a threatened species in Georgia’s 2014 Red List and is not listed in the CITES appendices.
  Locally, loss of habitat and deaths on roads may affect it. It may also be poisoned if it eats an animal poisoned by rodent bait.
interaction: >-
  It is not venomous, but it may bite if you catch it. Do not feed or pick it up. If it bites you, see a doctor to assess the possible risk of rabies.

identification:
  summary: >-
    This small predator has a long body, a brown back, a white belly and a short tail. Its tail tip is not black, which is especially helpful for telling it apart from a stoat. It lives in Georgia year-round.
  traits:
    - >-
      A weasel’s head and body measure about 13–26 cm, and its tail about 1.5–9 cm. Its weight can range from a few dozen grams to about 250 grams. Males described in the Caucasus are about 17.6–26.5 cm long and are usually larger than females. It has a narrow, fairly flat head, a long neck, short legs, and small, rounded ears.
    - >-
      The back, sides, outer sides of the legs and tail are brown, ranging from dark chocolate to pale sandy brown. The belly, throat and inner sides of the legs are white. The boundary between brown and white is almost straight in some animals and uneven in others. The tip of the tail may be slightly darker, but it is not black. This is one of the main ways to tell it apart from an ermine.
    - >-
      The stoat (Mustela erminea) looks most like the weasel. Stoats are usually larger, though the two can sometimes be the same size. In winter, when a stoat’s fur is white, the tip of its tail is still black. The stone marten (Martes foina) and pine marten (Martes martes) are much larger and have bushier tails. The [fox](vulpes-vulpes) is also clearly different in body shape.
    - >-
      It is not venomous. If you see a small, very fast, long-bodied brown animal with a white belly and a short tail in a garden, near a stone wall or in the grass, it may be a weasel. Do not pick it up; watch it from a distance.
facts:
  - >-
    Linnaeus described it in 1766. The accepted name is Mustela nivalis. Order
    Carnivora, suborder Caniformia, family Mustelidae, genus Mustela.
  - >-
    Caucasus literature historically used two names: M. n. caucasica
    (Barrett-Hamilton, 1900) and M. n. dinniki (Satunin, 1907; type from
    Stavropol, held in the Museum of Georgia). Abramov and Baryshnikov 2000 treat
    dinniki as a synonym of caucasica and find little difference between the
    former “large” and “small” Caucasian forms. Georgian encyclopaedias still
    name two subspecies. Field identification is to species.
  - >-
    In Georgian the usual names are დედოფალა and სინდიოფალა. In English: least
    weasel; in Europe often weasel or common weasel. In ethnographic
    dictionaries დედოფალა can also mean a doll; this page means only Mustela
    nivalis.
  - >-
    IUCN lists Georgia as native. It is in Bukhnikashvili and Kandaurov’s 2002
    annotated mammal list. Eliava et al. 2007 list it as LC. That is
    country-level confirmation. The regional map below is based on photo
    observations and does not represent the full range.
