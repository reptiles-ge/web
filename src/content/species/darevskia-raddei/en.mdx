overview: >-
  Radde's Lizard (Darevskia raddei) is a confirmed, harmless rock lizard in
  Georgia, known from the upper Mtkvari in Samtskhe-Javakheti. The 2026 Georgian
  checklist treats it as biologically confirmed, not as a candidate species.
  Confirmed Georgian material is linked to Aspindza Municipality: Pia, Tmogvi,
  and the Mtkvari Gorge near the Turkish border. That does not mean the species
  occurs across Georgia. Taxonomically, the group is difficult: the 2026 checklist
  does not accept a clear species-level split between D. raddei and D. nairensis,
  and places Georgian populations closer to the nairensis lineage while retaining
  nairensis as a subspecies. Globally, the IUCN assessment is Least Concern; a
  separate population trend for Georgia is not documented on this page.
habitat: >-
  The confirmed Georgian range is narrow: the upper Mtkvari in
  Samtskhe-Javakheti, near the Turkish border. Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026 names
  Aspindza (Pia) and Tmogvi, and illustrates an individual from Pia in the
  Mtkvari Gorge. The atlas map shows Samtskhe-Javakheti on the basis of these
  records; it should not be read as proof that every suitable rocky site in the
  region has been surveyed. Outside Georgia, the species is known from Armenia,
  southern Azerbaijan, northwestern Iran, and northeastern Turkey. IUCN gives a
  broad range of 600-2,500 m and lists forest, shrubland, grassland, and rocky
  habitats; that is range-wide information, not a measured Georgian elevation
  limit.

  summary: >-
    This small rock lizard belongs to the genus Darevskia. In Georgia, the main clues to identifying it are where it is found, its body structure and the details of its scales. Colour alone is not enough.
  traits:
    - >-
      It belongs to the true lizard family (Lacertidae). It has a slender body, well-developed legs and a long tail. Unlike a legless lizard, it has legs. You would usually look for it near cracks in rocks or on rocky slopes, not in water.
    - >-
      The back is often brown or olive-brown, with dark and light spots. Color varies, so Georgia’s Darevskia species cannot be told apart by color alone.
    - >-
      In Georgia, range is an important clue: confirmed records are from the upper
      Mtkvari, in the Pia-Tmogvi area. A similar lizard photographed elsewhere in
      Georgia should not automatically be called Radde's Lizard.
    - >-
      A close-up photo or a specimen is needed to examine the scales on the head and the plates in front of the anal opening. The images in the 2026 list show these features on an individual Fia.
    - >-
      The most important similar species is the [Obscure Rock Lizard](darevskia-obscura),
      which is named more widely in the same basin. In this pair, colour is not a
      reliable separator; compare exact locality and scalation.
    - >-
      This page does not present D. nairensis as a separate species in Georgia. The 2026 list also does not consider it clearly distinct from raddei and continues to treat it as a subspecies.
