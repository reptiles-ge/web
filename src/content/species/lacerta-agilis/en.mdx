commonName: "Sand Lizard"

description: >-
  The sand lizard is confirmed in Georgia and harmless to people. It is
  widespread in the west; in the east it occurs above about 600–800 m.
overview: >-
  The sand lizard (Lacerta agilis) is a nonvenomous lizard confirmed in Georgia.
  It is widespread in the west; in the east it occurs above about 600–800 m.
  It has a stocky body and a relatively short head. Males often develop green
  flanks in the breeding season, but colour alone is unreliable for identification.
habitat: >-
  Western populations are also known from the Black Sea coast and the Colchic
  lowland. The species uses varied habitats, including
  meadows, shrubland and woodland edges. Sunny spots often have grass or shrubs
  nearby for cover. It is not confined to sandy places. A 2018 study found the
  Iori-valley subspecies on the northern edge of Tianeti at 1,119–1,185 m;
  researchers did not find it more than 2 km from Tianeti.
diet: >-
  It eats small invertebrates, including insects and spiders.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day, basking and hunting. The time it emerges from winter
  shelter varies by place; March–April has been reported in the south and in
  mountains. A female may lay one or two clutches a year, with 4–14 eggs in
  each. Incubation takes about 50–55 days in general accounts of the species.
conservation: >-
  The 2009 global assessment listed the species as Least Concern, although the
  population trend was decreasing. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. It is
  listed on Appendix II of the Bern Convention. The Iori-valley subspecies has
  a small range: the authors of a 2018 study suggested there might be only one
  population near Tianeti and estimated fewer than 1,000 animals. That is a local
  estimate, not the status of the whole species. Threats described for the
  species include habitat loss, intensive agriculture and road mortality.
interaction: >-
  It is not dangerous to people: it has no venom. If handled, it may bite or
  lose its tail. Watch it and leave it where it is; do not catch wild lizards.

  summary: >-
    A stocky lizard with a relatively short head. Males often develop green
    flanks in the breeding season; females are usually brownish or grey. Colour
    alone cannot distinguish it from the striped or Caucasian green lizard.
  traits:
    - >-
      Females and juveniles often have dark, pale-centred spots on their flanks.
      Male green colour varies with the season and can extend beyond the flanks.
      Consider its build, pattern and habitat together. Do not pick up a lizard
      to identify it.
    - >-
      The [striped lizard](lacerta-strigata) (Lacerta strigata) also inhabits
      semiarid places. In eastern Georgia, the sand lizard is associated with
      higher elevations, but both were found at one site near Tianeti in 2018.
      Location and colour alone are not enough for identification.
    - >-
      The [Caucasian green lizard](lacerta-media) (Lacerta media) is a larger
      green lizard. It occurs mainly in eastern semiarid areas; the 2026
      checklist lists it as a candidate species. Where both occur, compare
      size and build as well.
faq:
  - question: Does the sand lizard occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. It is widespread in the west and has been recorded above about
      600–800 m in the east.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      It has a stocky body and a relatively short head. Males often develop
      green flanks in the breeding season; females are usually brownish.
      Colour alone does not distinguish it from the striped or Caucasian green
      lizard.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      No. It has no venom and is not dangerous to people. If handled, it may
      bite or lose its tail; leave it where it is.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      Head–body length, excluding the tail, is usually about 7–9 cm;
      total length including the tail is about 18–25 cm.
  - question: How does it differ from the striped lizard?
    answer: >-
      In eastern Georgia the sand lizard is associated with higher elevations,
      while the striped lizard also inhabits semiarid areas. Both have been
      found together near Tianeti, so location or colour alone is unreliable.
  - question: Which subspecies occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      The 2026 checklist names three: grusinica in the west, brevicaudata in
      the south and centre, and ioriensis in the east. A single DNA marker
      does not fully distinguish these subspecies.
  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      By day. Emergence from winter shelter varies by place; March–April has
      been reported in the south and in mountains.
