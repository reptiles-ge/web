overview: >-
  The long-bodied cellar spider (Pholcus phalangioides) is a spider in the
  family Pholcidae. In English it is known as the long-bodied cellar spider,
  cosmopolitan cellar spider, and daddy long-legs spider. The last name is also
  used for other arachnids, so the scientific name is more reliable when naming
  the species precisely.

  In Georgia, the species is confirmed. The Caucasian Spiders database includes
  many Georgian records, including Tbilisi, Kutaisi, Batumi, Borjomi-Kharagauli,
  Lagodekhi, Gori, Mtskheta, and Kazbegi.

  It is a small, pale spider with very long and slender legs. It is often found
  in ceiling corners, cellars, garages, caves, and other quiet, relatively dark
  places, where it builds a loose and irregular web. Body length is usually about
  6-10 mm, although the long legs make it look much larger.

  It is not considered dangerous to people. It has venom for subduing prey, but
  its bite is not treated as a medically significant risk. The species has not
  been globally assessed by the IUCN and is not included in Georgia's 2014 Red
  List.
habitat: >-
  The long-bodied cellar spider often lives in environments associated with
  people. In Georgia, it can be found in houses, cellars, stairwells, storage
  rooms, garages, under buildings and bridges, and in natural and artificial
  caves.

  In the Caucasian Spiders data, Georgian records are known from Tbilisi,
  Kutaisi, Batumi, Borjomi-Kharagauli, Lagodekhi, Gori, Mtskheta, Kazbegi,
  Sighnaghi, Zestaponi, Oni, Kobuleti, Akhaltsikhe, Chiatura, and other places.

  The number of records indicates broad occurrence, but it does not confirm the
  species in every municipality of Georgia.
diet: >-
  The long-bodied cellar spider is a predator and catches small arthropods that
  enter or approach its web. Its prey may include small insects, other spiders,
  and other small invertebrates living indoors.

  It often stays in one place for a long time and senses prey movement through
  web vibrations. It also catches other spiders, so its presence in a house
  should not by itself be treated as a sign of a pest problem.
behavior: >-
  The web is loose, irregular, and three-dimensional: not round like orb webs.
  The spider often hangs upside down in it. When disturbed, it rapidly shakes
  its body and web, a defensive behaviour that may confuse a predator or another
  disturbing animal.

  The species is often found in dark places. In buildings it may be seen at
  different times of year, although the sources used here do not establish a
  separate seasonal calendar for Georgia.

  Females carry eggs in a silk sac, often near the mouthparts. Spiderlings
  disperse after hatching. This is general species biology, not the result of a
  separate study of the Georgian population.
conservation: >-
  The current scientific name of the species is Pholcus phalangioides, and it
  belongs to the family Pholcidae. Older sources may list it under other names,
  but modern usage accepts Pholcus phalangioides.

  According to the World Spider Catalog, the species' natural range is associated
  with western Asia, although today it is widespread in many parts of the world,
  including Europe, the Americas, Africa, other regions of Asia, Australia, and
  New Zealand.

  In Georgia, the species is confirmed by the Caucasian Spiders checklist and
  many local records. The IUCN has not globally assessed it, and it is not
  included in Georgia's 2014 Red List. The sources used here do not define a
  special protection status or quantified population trend for Georgia.
interaction: >-
  The long-bodied cellar spider is considered harmless to people. Its presence
  in a house is not a danger by itself, and it does not try to attack people. As
  with any wild spider, there is no need to handle it.

  If an accidental bite happens, wash the area with soap and water and observe
  your condition. If strong pain, increasing swelling, breathing difficulty, or
  worsening general condition occurs, call 112. This is educational information,
  not a medical protocol.

  traits:
    - >-
      Body length is about 6-10 mm, although the very long legs make the spider
      look much larger. The legs are thin and long, and the body is narrow, pale
      brownish or greyish.
    - >-
      A Pholcus web is not round. It is loose and irregular, and is often found
      in ceiling corners, cellars, stairwells, garages, and other quiet places.
    - >-
      When disturbed, Pholcus often rapidly shakes its body and web. This
      characteristic defensive behaviour also helps identify it among many other
      long-legged house spiders.
    - >-
      The long-bodied cellar spider should not be confused with a harvestman, a
      different type of arachnid. A harvestman's body appears as one piece and it
      does not spin webs. Pholcus is a true spider with two main body parts and
      builds silk webs.
    - >-
      The [European garden spider](araneus-diadematus) builds a large round orb
      web and often has a white cross-shaped mark on the upper side of the
      abdomen. The long-bodied cellar spider has a loose, irregular web, and its
      legs are much longer and thinner.
