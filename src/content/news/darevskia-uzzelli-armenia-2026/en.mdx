## What did they find?

Morphological and genetic analyses show that lizards from Shirak Province, near Jradzor in the Akhuryan valley, match Turkish <Sci>D. uzzelli</Sci> and are clearly distinct from the related parthenogen <Sci>D. unisexualis</Sci>. The paper extends the range accepted since 1977 into Armenia. There is no Georgian locality in the paper.

## Why four species together?

The authors call the site the richest known assemblage of parthenogenetic rock lizards: <Sci>D. uzzelli</Sci> lives there with <Species id="darevskia-armeniaca">Armenian rock lizard</Species>, <Species id="darevskia-dahli">Dahl’s rock lizard</Species> and <Sci>D. unisexualis</Sci>. No sexual Darevskia is recorded at this point in the paper. Colour does not separate these species.

## What threatens the site?

The authors ask for continuous study and protection: the scientific value is high. The habitat lies outside protected areas and is under pressure from the Kaps reservoir. This is not a Georgian region.

## What does this mean for the atlas?

<Sci>D. uzzelli</Sci> is not on this atlas and this paper does not add it. <Species id="darevskia-armeniaca">Armenian rock lizard</Species> and <Species id="darevskia-dahli">Dahl’s rock lizard</Species> occur in Georgia in the [2026 checklist](https://doi.org/10.3897/caucasiana.5.e189214); <Sci>D. unisexualis</Sci> is not here. The <Hub id="lizards">lizards hub</Hub> remains the main record; <NewsIndex>news</NewsIndex> only adds context from this Armenian record.
