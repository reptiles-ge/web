commonName: "European Pond Turtle"
location: "Western and eastern Georgia"
description: >-
  The European pond turtle (Emys orbicularis) is Georgia's native water turtle and is harmless to people. It has a low, often dark shell with yellow speckles.
overview: >-
  The European pond turtle (Emys orbicularis) is Georgia’s native freshwater turtle — neither a land tortoise nor the introduced pond slider. Its presence in Georgia is confirmed, and different subspecies are named for the west and the east of the country. In the Caucasus its shell is often under 16 cm long. Globally it is considered near threatened, but it is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. It has no venom and is not dangerous to people.
habitat: >-
  The European pond turtle lives in still or slow-moving fresh water: ponds, lakes, marshes, old river channels and slow rivers. It especially likes banks with plenty of plants. In western Georgia it is confirmed in places including Poti and the Colchic coastal wetlands. It also occurs in eastern Georgia. To lay eggs, the female comes out onto land and often nests in a sunny, sandy spot. Near Churia Fen, the shore and sand dunes are known as nesting sites.
diet: >-
  The European pond turtle eats both animal and plant food, and finds much of it in water. Its food includes invertebrates, small fish, amphibians and plants. What it eats changes with age: young turtles eat more animal food, while adults take relatively more plants.
behavior: >-
  By day the European pond turtle often leaves the water and basks on a bank,
  log, or aquatic plants. When threatened, it quickly enters the water. It is
  mainly aquatic, though females come onto land to lay eggs and turtles may move
  over land between waterbodies. At species level it often spends winter on the
  bottom or in mud, but exact seasonal periods for Georgia are not established on
  this page. Females lay eggs in a terrestrial nest. Broad European data give
  one to three clutches a year, with about six to nine eggs in each; those data
  are not specific to Georgia.
conservation: >-
  Globally, the European Pond Turtle is rated as near threatened, but that assessment is old and needs updating. The 2024 European regional assessment gives it the same status. This native turtle is not included in Georgia's 2014 Red List. Its main threats include the loss of marshes and other bodies of water, changes to shorelines, and deaths on roads, especially when turtles cross land. Different subspecies are named for western and eastern Georgia, but there is no single agreed view on how Caucasian turtles divide into subspecies. So you cannot reliably tell from a photo which subspecies a turtle belongs to.
interaction: |-
  The European pond turtle is not dangerous to people and has no venom. It usually does not attack: when it senses danger, it tries to escape into the water. If you pick it up, disturb it or corner it, it may bite to defend itself. The bite is generally not serious, but it can hurt or damage the skin.

  If a turtle bites you, wash the wound well with soap and clean water. See a doctor if there is heavy bleeding, a deep wound or signs of infection. If you see a turtle basking on a bank, stone or log, leave it where it is. Do not take it home or move it to another body of water.

  summary: >-
    The European pond turtle is a water turtle native to Georgia. Its shell is low and often dark, with yellow spots. A high, domed shell points more to a land tortoise. If you see a broad red stripe behind the ear, you are looking at the introduced pond slider, not a European pond turtle.
  traits:
    - >-
      The European pond turtle’s shell is low and oval, not high and domed. It is usually dark olive, brown or black, often with yellow dots or short streaks on the surface. The lower shell is paler. One feature that helps tell it apart from the Caspian turtle is the movable hinge in the lower shell, which the European pond turtle has.
    - >-
      The head, neck and legs are dark, often with yellowish dots or short streaks. The feet are webbed for swimming. In the Kura basin of the Caucasus, the shell is usually shorter than 16 cm, and the largest described shell is about 18 cm. Males are often smaller than females, with a longer tail and a more concave lower shell. The European Pond Turtle has no broad red stripe behind the ear. If you see such a stripe, you are looking at a different turtle.
    - >-
      The European pond turtle may resemble several other turtles, but each of them has its own giveaway. The Mediterranean tortoise (Testudo graeca) has a high, domed shell and legs built for walking on land. The introduced pond slider (Trachemys scripta) often shows a broad red stripe behind the ear. The Caspian turtle (Mauremys caspica) is found in southeastern Georgia. Pale stripes often run along its neck, and its shell is usually more striped than spotted. So when you try to identify a turtle, look at the shell shape, the head and the neck together.
    - >-
      If you see a turtle in a city pond, including in Tbilisi and Batumi, take special care to check whether it is the introduced pond slider. The European pond turtle is not venomous. If you come across one in nature, watch it and leave it where it is; there is no need to pick it up.
    - >-
      The European pond turtle is native to Georgia and confirmed in both the west and the east of the country. In the west, it is found in Poti and the Colchic coastal wetlands. Turtles in western Georgia are assigned to the subspecies Emys orbicularis orbicularis, and those in the east to E. o. persica.

  - question: How do I tell it from a red-eared slider?
    answer: >-
      The European pond turtle is native to Georgia, while the pond slider is an introduced species. The best-known form of the pond slider has a broad red stripe behind the ear, and the European pond turtle has no such stripe. So if you see a broad red stripe behind the ear, the turtle in front of you is not a European pond turtle.
  - question: How do I tell it from a Caspian turtle?
    answer: >-
      Two features help you tell them apart: the neck and the shell. The Caspian turtle often has pale stripes running along its neck. The European pond turtle’s shell, by contrast, is usually darker and often covered with yellow dots or short streaks. In Georgia, the Caspian turtle is found mainly in the southeast.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous?
    answer: >-
      The European pond turtle is not venomous. But if you pick it up or disturb it, it may bite you in self-defence. So in nature, it is best not to touch it. If you do get bitten, wash the wound well with soap and clean water.
