commonName: "Sand Lizard"
location: "Adjara, Guria, Imereti, Samegrelo–Zemo Svaneti, Samtskhe–Javakheti, Kvemo Kartli"
description: >-
  The sand lizard has no venom and is harmless to people. In Georgia it is widespread in the west, while in the east it occurs above about 600–800 m.
overview: >-
  The Sand Lizard (Lacerta agilis) is not venomous and is not dangerous to people. It is widespread in western Georgia, while in the east it occurs above roughly 600–800 m. It has a stocky body and a relatively short head. During the breeding season, males’ sides often turn green, but [identifying it by colour alone](/lizards/identifikacia) is difficult.
habitat: >-
  The Sand Lizard lives in a variety of places, including meadows, shrubland and forest edges, and is not limited to sandy ground. In the west, it also occurs along the Black Sea coast and in the Colchis Lowland. In sunny spots, it may stay close to grass or a bush where it can hide. During a 2018 study, the Iori Valley subspecies was found on the northern outskirts of Tianeti at 1,119–1,185 m, but it was not found more than 2 km from Tianeti.
diet: >-
  It eats insects, spiders and other small animals without a backbone.
behavior: >-
  When it leaves its winter shelter depends on the place. In the south and in the mountains, it has been seen emerging in March–April. It is active by day, warming itself in the sun and hunting. A female may lay eggs once or twice a year, 4–14 at a time. In general, this species’ eggs hatch in about 50–55 days.
conservation: >-
  A 2009 global assessment found the Sand Lizard to be at low risk, although its numbers were declining. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List, but it is listed in Annex II of the Bern Convention. Reported threats include habitat loss, intensive farming and deaths on roads. The Iori Valley subspecies, meanwhile, lives in a small area. In 2018, it was thought that only one population of this subspecies existed near Tianeti, with fewer than 1,000 lizards. This estimate applied only to the area around Tianeti, not to the species as a whole.
interaction: >-
  It has no venom and is not dangerous to people. If you pick it up, it may bite you or lose its tail. So do not catch a wild lizard: watch it and leave it where it is.

identification:
  summary: >-
    It has a stocky body and a relatively short head. During the breeding season, males often turn green along the sides, while females are usually brownish or gray. Color alone won't let you tell it apart from the Striped Lizard and the Caucasian Green Lizard.
  traits:
    - >-
      Females and young lizards often have dark spots on their sides, each with a pale center. A male's green coloring changes with the seasons, and in some males green covers more of the body. To identify the lizard, look at its body shape, its markings and the place where you saw it. Do not pick it up to do so.
    - >-
      In the east, the Sand Lizard lives at higher elevations, while the [Striped Lizard](lacerta-strigata) (Lacerta strigata) is also found in semi-arid areas. Yet in 2018 both were seen at the same spot near Tianeti. So do not identify a lizard’s species only by where you saw it and what color it is.
    - >-
      The [Caucasian Green Lizard](lacerta-media) (Lacerta media) is a larger green lizard. It is found mainly in semi-arid areas in the east. The 2026 list gives it as a candidate species. Where it lives alongside another green lizard, look at size and body shape as well as colour to tell them apart.
faq:
  - question: Does the sand lizard occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes, it occurs in Georgia. It is widespread in the west, while in the east it has been recorded above roughly 600–800 m.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      It has a stocky body and a relatively short head. During the breeding season, the male's sides often turn greenish, while the female is usually brownish. Color alone won't tell it apart from the Striped Lizard or the Caucasian Green Lizard.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      No. The Sand Lizard has no venom and is not dangerous to people. If you pick it up, it may bite you or lose its tail. So don't pick it up; leave it where it is.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      Including the tail, the Sand Lizard is about 18–25 cm long. The head and body alone, without the tail, are usually about 7–9 cm long.
  - question: How does it differ from the striped lizard?
    answer: >-
      You can't reliably tell a sand lizard from a striped lizard just by where you saw it or by its colour. In the east, the sand lizard is found at higher elevations, while the striped lizard also occurs in semi-arid areas. But both have been seen together near Tianeti, so elevation and surroundings are only a hint.
  - question: Which subspecies occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Three subspecies, according to the 2026 list. In the west it is grusinica, in the south and center brevicaudata, and in the east ioriensis. A single DNA feature is not enough to tell these subspecies fully apart.
  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      During the day. It does not leave its winter shelter at the same time everywhere. In the south and in the mountains, it has been seen coming out in March–April.
