commonName: "European Blind Snake"
location: "Kakheti, Tbilisi, Kvemo Kartli"
description: >-
  European blind snake (Xerotyphlops vermicularis), a small, non-venomous snake
  of eastern Georgia. Learn how to recognise it and where it occurs.
overview: >-
  The European blind snake (Xerotyphlops vermicularis) is a small, non-venomous
  snake that spends most of its life underground. It has been recorded in eastern
  Georgia. Its slender, worm-like body and eyes visible as tiny dark spots beneath
  the scales help identify it; it is not dangerous to people.
habitat: >-
  The European blind snake lives underground, often beneath stones. Even in dry
  landscapes, it apparently favours relatively moist spots. The 2026
  Georgian checklist presents a studied specimen from Chachuna Managed Reserve
  and a photograph from the Dalis Mta reservoir area; a photographic record also
  exists from Tbilisi.
diet: >-
  It feeds mainly on ants, including their eggs, larvae and pupae, as well as
  other small invertebrates.
behavior: >-
  It spends most of its time underground. Above ground it is rarely seen, mainly
  at dusk or at night; it may also be encountered beneath a stone. If you find
  one, take a photo and leave it where it is; do not pick it up. If you have
  lifted a stone, carefully put it back.
conservation: >-
  The 2017 global IUCN assessment listed the European blind snake as Least
  Concern (LC). That assessment predates the 2020 separation of another species
  and does not assess the species in its current scope separately. It is not a
  national assessment for Georgia.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A small, very slender blind snake. The body looks worm-like, the scales are
    smooth and glossy, and the head is barely set off from the neck. The eyes are
    hidden under the scales and show only as small dark spots.
  traits:
    - >-
      The body is very slender, cylindrical and almost the same width from head
      to tail. Colour is usually brownish, yellowish or flesh-coloured, with a
      paler underside.
    - >-
      The eyes are strongly reduced and show only as small dark spots beneath
      the scales. The head is not sharply set off from the neck, which
      strengthens its worm-like appearance.
    - >-
      The tail is very short. The whole body is covered in smooth, glossy scales.
      The slender cylindrical body, almost indistinct head and short tail
      distinguish it from other small snakes.
    - >-
      The [javelin sand boa](eryx-jaculus) is much stouter than the blind snake.
      It has small but clearly visible eyes and a short, thick, blunt tail. The
      blind snake is much thinner, almost even in width and worm-like.
faq:
  - question: Does the European blind snake occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2026 Georgian checklist lists the European blind snake as a
      confirmed species and presents a studied specimen from Chachuna Managed
      Reserve.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In eastern Georgia. The 2026 checklist presents a specimen from Chachuna
      Managed Reserve and a photograph from the Dalis Mta reservoir area. There
      is also a photographic record from Tbilisi.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. It is a non-venomous blind snake and is not dangerous to people.
  - question: Is this the javelin sand boa?
    answer: >-
      No. The European blind snake and the javelin sand boa are different
      species and also differ strongly in appearance. The blind snake has a very
      slender, worm-like body and almost unnoticeable eyes. The javelin sand boa
      is much stouter, with clearly visible eyes and a short, thick, blunt tail.
  - question: How large does it get?
    answer: >-
      Usually 18–30 cm in total length.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Mainly ants, including their eggs, larvae and pupae; it also eats other
      small invertebrates.
  - question: How do I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Look for a very slender, worm-like body, glossy scales, a head barely
      distinct from the neck and eyes visible as small dark spots under the
      scales.
  - question: When is it seen above ground?
    answer: >-
      Rarely, mainly at dusk or at night. It may also be found beneath a stone.
