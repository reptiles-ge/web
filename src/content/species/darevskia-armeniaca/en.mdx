overview: >-
  The Armenian rock lizard (Darevskia armeniaca) is a harmless lizard found in rocky areas of southern Georgia. A 2026 study lists it as a species rather than a candidate species. The main locations it names in Georgia are Lake Saghamo in Ninotsminda Municipality and Akhalkalaki. A lizard with similar coloring in these places is not necessarily D. armeniaca: Darevskia species often look alike. To identify one reliably, you need to consider where it was found and examine a detailed photo and the features of its scales. Females of this species can reproduce without males. Its origins are linked to D. valentini and D. mixta, while its genetic line passed down from the mother also matches those of D. mixta and D. dahli. These genetic links do not provide a simple way to identify it: color, a single photo or one feature is often not enough.
habitat: >-

Similar surroundings or colour alone cannot confirm its presence elsewhere. If you see one in the wild, it helps to provide the exact location and date, along with clear photos showing its back, sides and the pattern of scales on its head. “I saw a green/brown rock lizard in southern Georgia” is not enough to identify the species.
