overview: >-
  The Lebanon lizard (Phoenicolacerta laevis) is confirmed in Georgia from just one place: the area around Anaklia Castle and the meadow next to it. It belongs to the true lizard family and is harmless to people. It was probably brought here by people by accident. The 2026 Georgian species list recognises it as a separate species and lists it as introduced in Georgia. In 2017, the Anaklia lizards were genetically linked to lizards from southern Türkiye. So do not treat a report of it from elsewhere in Georgia as confirmed without evidence.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the Lebanon Lizard is confirmed from one very specific spot: the old stone walls of Anaklia Castle and the grassy patch beside them, where blackberry bushes grow. The site lies near the mouth of the Enguri and the Black Sea coast. The area described in 2017 covers about one hectare, and the lizards moved both on the walls and in the nearby meadow. Finding it at Anaklia does not mean it occurs throughout western Georgia or everywhere along the coast.
diet: >-
  The Lebanon Lizard belongs to the true lizard family. Lizards of this family, including species of the genus Phoenicolacerta, usually feed on small invertebrates. The diet of the Anaklia lizards has not been described separately.
behavior: >-
  A fast lizard that is active by day. At Anaklia, on 6 May 2017 around midday, more than 100 adult and young lizards were seen during one hour of observation. This points to lizards that have settled there but live only in a small area. In the lowlands of Turkey, Lebanon Lizards are active from March to September. A full seasonal calendar for Georgia has not been published.
conservation: >-
  Globally, the Lebanon Lizard is at low risk of extinction. It lives across a wide area of the eastern Mediterranean. In Georgia, however, it is not regarded as a native species and is known from only one small population. These lizards were probably brought here by people, either long ago or more recently. Changes to the Anaklia Castle walls and the plants around them could harm this small, isolated population.

    - >-

To tell them apart, where you saw the lizard, what kind of place it was in and how good your photos are often matter more than back colour alone. So note the location as well as the colour, and take clear photos.
    - >-
      The Lebanon Lizard is non-venomous and poses no health risk to people. Still, don't try to catch wild lizards. It is better to take a photo and note the exact location.
