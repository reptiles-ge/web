  The hedgehog found in Georgia is a small spiny mammal with a short tail and a

overview: >-
  The hedgehog naturally found in Georgia is the southern white-breasted hedgehog
  (Erinaceus concolor). In Georgian it is often also called “European hedgehog”,
  although the western European hedgehog (Erinaceus europaeus) does not occur in
  Georgia.

  Adults are about 20–26 cm in head-body length, with a 2–3 cm tail, and often
  weigh about 550–800 g. It has no venom. The main possible risk to people is
  contact with the spines or a defensive bite if the animal is picked up.

  IUCN lists Erinaceus concolor as Least Concern.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia hedgehogs may be found in gardens, parks, village and farmland edges,
  scrub, and deciduous woodland edges. Ground cover and available invertebrate
  food are important.

  Across the species range it lives in deciduous forest, scrub, meadows, around
  farms, and in parks. It often avoids dense conifer forest, especially where
  ground cover and food are limited.

  The concrete record named on this page is Orkhevi, Tbilisi, photographed on 5
  April 2023. A 2023 review brings together 112 records from Georgia collected
  between 1864 and 2022, but the localities are not listed one by one here. Do
  not assume equal distribution or density across all regions of the country.
diet: >-
  The hedgehog mainly feeds on ground invertebrates: beetles, earthworms, slugs,
  and snails. It may sometimes eat frogs, eggs, carrion, or other small animals.

  This is the species-level diet. The hedgehog’s diet in Georgia has not been
  studied separately in detail on this page. It is not a predator specialized on
  one particular food.
behavior: >-
  The hedgehog is mainly active at night. By day it usually hides in leaves,
  grass, under shrubs, or in another protected place. In colder parts of the range
  it hibernates during the cold season.

  It is mostly solitary. When threatened, it rolls into a ball and covers the soft
  belly and face with spines. It sometimes also covers its body with its own
  saliva; the exact function of this behaviour is not fully settled.

  Breeding takes place in the warm season. Different sources describe litters
  from a few young to about seven.
conservation: >-
  IUCN lists the southern white-breasted hedgehog (Erinaceus concolor) as Least
  Concern. Its natural range extends from Anatolia through the southern Caucasus
  and the Levant.

  In Georgia the species is not treated as a threatened taxon on the 2014 Red
  List. Possible pressures include road mortality, habitat loss, and pesticide
  use. The overall trend of the Georgian population has not been assessed separately
  on this page.

  Do not pick up wild hedgehogs or turn them into pets.
interaction: >-
  The hedgehog is not venomous, and its spines do not contain poison. If you pick
  it up or corner it, it may bite in defence. Any wild mammal can theoretically
  carry rabies, although in Georgia foxes, jackals, wolves, and dogs are more often
  named as the main sources, not hedgehogs.

  In gardens a hedgehog is often useful because it eats different invertebrates.
  Do not pick it up, feed it, or try to make it a pet. Pet “hedgehogs” are often
  African Atelerix species, not the hedgehog naturally found in Georgia.

  traits:
    - >-
      Adult head-body length is about 20–26 cm, the tail is 2–3 cm, and mass is
      often 550–800 g. These are species-level data; this page does not have a
      full separately measured series for the Georgian population.

      The snout is pointed, the ears are short, and the eyes are small. The back
      and sides are covered with spines, while the face, legs, and belly are
      furred. The tail is very short and often almost hidden among the spines.
    - >-
      The southern white-breasted hedgehog has a clear pale or white chest patch
      contrasting with the darker belly. This is one important feature for
      separating it from the western European hedgehog Erinaceus europaeus.

      In Georgia, identifying the species from a single photograph is not always
      simple. If the chest is not visible, range and other features also matter.
      There is no need to pick up the animal to check its chest.
    - >-
      Georgia’s 2023 checklist of insectivorous mammals lists only Erinaceus
      concolor among hedgehogs. IUCN data, however, also include the northern
      white-breasted hedgehog (Erinaceus roumanicus) in Georgia’s native range.

      The two white-breasted hedgehogs are difficult to separate in the wild, so
      this page follows the 2023 national checklist for Georgia and uses Erinaceus
      concolor as the species. The western European Erinaceus europaeus does not
      occur in Georgia.

      The Indian crested porcupine (Hystrix indica) is much larger than a hedgehog,
      has long quills, and does not roll into a tight ball like a hedgehog.
    - >-
      The hedgehog is not venomous. In Georgia, a small spiny mammal with a short
      tail seen at night in a garden, park, or by a road usually belongs to this
      group. You do not need to pick it up or inspect its chest to identify it.
facts:
  - >-
    W. C. Martin described it; the name was published in 1838 (1837 is also
    cited because it appeared in the 1837 volume of Proc. Zool. Soc. Lond.). The
    type locality is near Trabzon, Turkey. The accepted name is Erinaceus
    concolor. Order Eulipotyphla (Insectivora or Erinaceomorpha in older
    systems), family Erinaceidae, genus Erinaceus.
  - >-
    It was formerly included in E. europaeus. White-breasted forms were later
    split into a southern species (concolor) and a northern one (roumanicus).
    The Caucasus is a barrier in that split (Seddon et al. 2002). Historical
    names include E. europaeus transcaucasicus Satunin and E. ponticus Satunin;
    they are now treated as synonyms of E. concolor. Field identification in
    Georgia is to species if you accept a one-species checklist.
  - >-
    In Georgian the usual name is ევროპული ზღარბი; official lists also use
    აღმოსავლეთევროპული ზღარბი; თეთრმკერდა ზღარბი is also used. In English:
    southern white-breasted hedgehog; also eastern hedgehog. Generic ზღარბი
    means hedgehog.
  - >-
    IUCN includes Georgia in the range of E. concolor. It is on Bukhnikashvili
    and Kandaurov’s 2002 annotated list. The 2023 compilation has 112 Georgian
    records. That is country-level confirmation, not a full regional map.
