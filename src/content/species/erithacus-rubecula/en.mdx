location: Breeds and is present year-round in Georgia
description: >-
  A small woodland bird with an orange face and breast. It breeds in Georgia and
  can be found there throughout the year.
overview: >-
  The European robin (Erithacus rubecula) is a small songbird. Adults have an
  orange face and breast; young birds do not yet have these colours. It breeds
  in Georgia and is present year-round, mainly in woodland and places with
  dense cover. It is not dangerous to people. In winter, you may also see it in
  gardens and parks.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, look for European robins in dense woodland, undergrowth and at
  forest edges. They often search for food on the ground near shrubs, where
  there are small open patches. In winter, they may also visit gardens and
  parks. One was photographed at Batumi Botanical Garden on 24 June 2016.
diet: >-
  It mainly eats ground-dwelling invertebrates, including insects, spiders and
  earthworms. It finds food by hopping on the ground or dropping from a low
  perch. In autumn and winter, it also eats berries and seeds.
behavior: >-
  Its song consists of high, clear phrases separated by short pauses. Both
  sexes defend territories, and the bird may sing outside the breeding season.
  It usually nests close to the ground, among roots, in a bank or in a cavity.
  A clutch usually has 4–6 eggs, and incubation lasts about two weeks. Breeding
  in Georgia is confirmed.
conservation: >-
  Globally, the European robin is assessed as Least Concern. It is not listed on
  Georgia’s official 2014 Red List. The national category given in the 2024
  scientific checklist is not an official Red List status. A 2021 European
  assessment estimated 48,900–490,000 breeding pairs in Georgia for 2013–2017.
  This wide range is an estimate, not a precise census.
interaction: >-
  It is not dangerous to people. Do not catch wild birds or approach their
  nests.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A sparrow-sized songbird with an orange face and breast, olive-brown back,
    and a pale belly. The sexes look alike in the field. Juveniles lack the
    orange breast and are mottled brown.
  traits:
    - >-
      Body length is about 14 cm and wingspan 20–22 cm. Mass is often about
      14–21 g. The body is rounded, the bill fine and dark, the legs brownish.
      It has no large white wing patch and no long orange tail.
    - >-
      Adults have a continuous orange-rufous forehead, cheeks, throat, and
      breast. The back is olive-brown. A bluish-grey band often borders the
      breast; the belly is pale, whitest in the centre. The sexes are not
      separable in the field. Juveniles are brown and buff with dense pale
      spotting and no orange face and breast.
    - >-
      The [common nightingale](luscinia-megarhynchos) is larger and lacks an
      orange face. Common redstarts (Phoenicurus phoenicurus) and black redstarts
      (Phoenicurus ochruros) have a rusty tail. Young European robins lack the
      adult’s orange breast, so that mark alone will not identify them.

facts:
  - >-
    The European robin was formerly placed with thrushes. It is now classified
    with the Old World flycatchers (Muscicapidae).
  - >-
    The Caucasian subspecies E. r. caucasicus breeds in Georgia; the European
    E. r. rubecula also occurs during migration and in winter. Differences in
    appearance between subspecies are slight, so field identification is more
    reliable at the species level.
  - >-
    The Georgian name გულწითელა has the variant ბულწითელა. რუხი გულწითელა refers
    to another bird, the [yellowhammer](emberiza-citrinella), not this species.
    Its Russian name is зарянка and its Turkish name is kızılgerdan.
