overview: >-
  Darevskia caucasica is found in rocky areas of the Greater Caucasus in Georgia. It is not venomous and is not dangerous to people. You cannot tell it apart from [similar rock lizards](/lizards/darevskia) by color alone. Its presence in Georgia and DNA samples from there have been confirmed. There are groups within the species that differ in their DNA, though the boundaries between them are still being clarified.

    - >-
      In the Greater Caucasus, the areas where it and [Darevskia mixta](darevskia-mixta) occur meet along only a short stretch. The two species look alike, so do not rely on colour alone.
    - >-
      Of two lizards studied together in a valley in northern Georgia, one was called D. rudis in a 2023 paper. A 2026 list tentatively places the group known by that name in Georgia with [Darevskia obscura](darevskia-obscura). Viewed from above, when their size is not apparent, the two lizards may look alike because of their flat heads and long limbs. The D. rudis studied had, on average, more scales around the middle of its body than the other lizard, but those scales cannot be counted in a poor-quality photo.
    - >-
      [Darevskia brauneri](darevskia-brauneri) also occurs in the valleys of western Georgia. From a distance, it may resemble another rock lizard: both may look small and greenish. Darevskia brauneri has a larger body and head than the other lizard, though the difference is not always clear in a single photo.
