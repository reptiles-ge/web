description: >-
  A breeding and migratory scavenging bird in Georgia. Nesting was confirmed
  by field monitoring in Eagle Canyon, Vashlovani. Adults are white with black
  flight feathers; young birds are dark. Globally Endangered (EN).
overview: >-
  The Egyptian vulture (Neophron percnopterus) is a small scavenging bird in the
  family Accipitridae. It breeds in and migrates through Georgia; nesting is
  confirmed in Eagle Canyon, Vashlovani, Kakheti. Adults have a white body,
  black flight feathers, a yellow face and a wedge-shaped tail; young birds are
  darker. Wingspan is about 155–180 cm. Its global status is EN. Georgia's legal
  Red List says VU, while the 2024 scientific checklist assesses the national
  status as EN.
habitat: >-
  A confirmed Georgian breeding site is Eagle Canyon Natural Monument in the
  Vashlovani Protected Areas, Kakheti. Monitoring in 2023 recorded three pairs;
  two bred successfully and each fledged one chick. This is a count for one
  canyon, not an estimate for all Georgia. The bird forages in open, often dry
  landscapes and nests on cliffs. A map prepared by the Georgian National Museum
  and National Geographic Georgia describes breeding in eastern Georgia and
  passage in the west. It does not identify a western administrative unit where
  nesting is confirmed. Batumi Raptor Count's standardized autumn count recorded
  25 Egyptian vultures in 2025. This is passage evidence, not proof of breeding
  in Adjara.
diet: >-
  It feeds mainly on carrion. The species' diet can also include small
  vertebrates and eggs. As a scavenger, it helps break down dead animals. The
  sources used here do not quantify the proportions of these foods in Georgia;
  general species biology should not be read as a local diet survey.
behavior: >-
  The Egyptian vulture is migratory. According to the Vashlovani monitoring
  report, birds in the northern breeding range are present mainly from March to
  September, and breeding in Georgia begins in April. The report gives a general
  incubation period of about 42 days and fledging at about 70–90 days. In Eagle
  Canyon in 2023, two pairs were incubating in May and each raised a chick by
  July. These observations do not establish an exact schedule for every year.
conservation: >-
  The global IUCN category is Endangered (EN). Georgia's 2014 legal Red List
  lists the species as Vulnerable (VU, criterion D1) because of a small
  population. The 2024 scientific checklist of Georgian birds assigns a national
  EN category. These assessments differ; a scientific reassessment does not by
  itself amend the legal list. Poisoning, electrocution on power lines and
  illegal killing are documented threats internationally. The Vashlovani report
  lacked enough long-term data to conclude how visitor numbers affect breeding
  success at Eagle Canyon.
interaction: >-
  It presents no danger to people during an ordinary encounter. Do not approach
  a nest or try to handle a wild bird. Watch from a distance, especially during
  breeding. Poisoned baits endanger scavenging birds.

identification:
  summary: >-
    An adult has a striking white body and black flight feathers when seen from
    below; its bare yellow face is visible closer up. A young bird is brown and
    much darker. At any age, look for its smaller size compared with the other
    vultures in Georgia and its wedge-shaped tail.
  traits:
    - >-
      Adult: white body, black flight feathers, yellow face and a narrow,
      wedge-shaped tail. Wingspan is about 155–180 cm, a general range for the
      species rather than a series measured in Georgia.
    - >-
      Juvenile: dark brown plumage without the adult's white-and-black contrast.
      Immature plumage gradually becomes paler. Colour alone is not enough for
      a dark bird: check body proportions and tail shape too.
    - >-
      The [griffon vulture](gyps-fulvus) is much larger, with a sandy body and
      broad wings; the adult Egyptian vulture has stronger white-and-black
      contrast. The [cinereous vulture](aegypius-monachus) is uniformly dark and
      also much larger. The [bearded vulture](gypaetus-barbatus) has narrower
      wings and a long, diamond-shaped tail; adults often show a rusty body and
      a black “beard.” Compare size and tail shape as well as colour when
      identifying a dark young Egyptian vulture.

faq:
  - question: Does the Egyptian vulture occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 Georgian bird checklist lists it as a breeder and migrant.
      Nesting was directly confirmed in Eagle Canyon, Vashlovani.
  - question: Where is breeding confirmed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Eagle Canyon, Vashlovani Protected Areas, Kakheti. Three pairs were
      recorded in 2023; two each fledged one chick. This is not a national total.
  - question: How can I identify an adult in flight?
    answer: >-
      Look for a white body contrasting with black flight feathers and a
      wedge-shaped tail. The bare yellow face is visible at closer range.
  - question: Are young Egyptian vultures white too?
    answer: >-
      No. Young birds are mainly dark brown and become paler as they mature.
      Do not identify a dark bird on colour alone.
  - question: What is its wingspan?
    answer: >-
      The general range is about 155–180 cm. The sources here do not provide
      a separate series of measurements from Georgia.
  - question: When might I see one?
    answer: >-
      It breeds in and migrates through Georgia. According to the monitoring
      report, birds are present in the northern breeding range mainly from
      March to September. Autumn passage is also recorded near Batumi.
  - question: What is its status on Georgia's Red List?
    answer: >-
      The 2014 legal list says VU, D1. The 2024 scientific checklist assesses
      the national status as EN; that does not automatically change the legal entry.
