commonName: Collared pratincole
location: Breeding migrant in Georgia (country-level)
description: >-
  A swallow-like wader of open ground near water, with a black throat collar and
  chestnut underwings. Confirmed in Georgia as a breeding migrant on the 2024
  national bird checklist.
overview: >-
  The collared pratincole (Glareola pratincola) is a long-winged wader that
  flies like a swallow and feeds mainly on insects. The 2024 annotated checklist
  of the birds of Georgia codes it BB; M: a breeder absent in the non-breeding
  season, and also a bird of passage. Seasonality is classed as high-quality
  (code A). Occurrence quality is high (H), with photographic evidence. Older
  Georgian encyclopedic sources treated it only as a passage visitor; that
  conflict is stated below. It is not a threat to people. IUCN lists the species
  as Least Concern. The 2024 checklist assigns the same category nationally. The
  usual Georgian species name is მდელოს მერცხალა; მერცხალა is also the genus
  name.
habitat: >-
  This page does not colour a municipality-by-municipality map. The 2024
  checklist confirms country-level breeding-migrant status but does not publish
  a regional gazetteer. Do not assume it occurs evenly across Georgia. Species-
  level habitat is open country near water: short grassland, mudflats, gravel,
  salt flats, and wetland edges where insects are abundant. Birds often hawk
  insects in the air, sometimes with terns and swallows, and also take prey on
  the ground. Nesting is typically a shallow scrape on bare or sparsely
  vegetated open ground, often near water, sometimes in loose colonies. A
  separate national elevation band for Georgia is not published here. Nearby
  breeding colonies are well known at Armash fishponds in Armenia; that is not a
  Georgian locality.
diet: >-
  There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page. Across the range it is
  mainly insectivorous: beetles, grasshoppers, flies and other flying insects,
  caught in flight or on the ground. It is not food for people.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. Flight is fast and agile, with long pointed wings and a
  deeply forked tail; on the ground it runs with short legs. Breeding adults
  show a buff throat framed by a thin black collar. The voice is a tern-like
  chattering or sharp kik calls in flight; no Georgian recording is hosted on
  this page. Species-level clutches are usually two to four eggs in a ground
  scrape; both parents incubate for about 17–19 days, and young fledge at about
  25–30 days — those figures are from the wider range, not a Georgian nest
  sample. The 2024 checklist treats the species as a breeding migrant in
  Georgia, so it is absent in winter. Exact arrival and departure dates for
  Georgia are not tabulated here. Palearctic breeders winter mainly in Africa.
conservation: >-
  BirdLife International assesses Glareola pratincola as Least Concern on the
  IUCN Red List (2025 assessment). The 2024 Georgian bird checklist assigns
  global and national Red List category LC. The species is not listed under its
  Georgian name on the 2014 Georgian Red List (Government Resolution No. 190)
  among the sources used here. It is covered by the Agreement on the
  Conservation of African-Eurasian Migratory Waterbirds (AEWA). A current
  national census of pairs in Georgia is not given on this page. Documented
  pressures at species level include wetland drainage, agricultural
  intensification, disturbance of nesting colonies, and habitat change; a
  measured Georgian threat list is not published here. Do not disturb nesting
  birds.
interaction: >-
  It is not a threat to people. It is not venomous and not edible as wild food
  in any practical sense on this page. Leave wild birds alone; nests on open
  ground are easily destroyed by trampling or vehicles.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A swallow-like wader with long pointed wings, a forked tail, and a black
    throat collar in breeding plumage. Chestnut underwing coverts separate it
    from black-winged pratincole. Confirmed in Georgia on the 2024 checklist as
    a breeding migrant.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length about 24–28 cm at species level; wingspan often given as about
      60–70 cm. Mass is often around 60–100 g. A separate measured series from
      Georgia is not given on this page. The bill is short and curved, with a
      red base in adults; the legs are short and dark. In flight the silhouette
      is tern- or swallow-like: long pointed wings and a deeply forked tail.
    - >-
      Breeding adults are brown above and paler below, with a buff throat
      bordered by a thin black collar (the “collar” of the English name). The
      belly is whitish. The underwing coverts are chestnut; the flight feathers
      are dark. A pale trailing edge to the secondaries is often visible. When
      perched, the outer tail feathers often reach or pass the wing tips —
      longer than in black-winged pratincole. Non-breeding and juvenile birds
      lose a clean collar; the throat looks mottled or plain, so underwing
      colour and tail length matter more.
    - >-
      Black-winged pratincole (Glareola nordmanni) is also confirmed in Georgia
      on the 2024 checklist, but only as a migrant (M), coded NT globally and
      nationally. Its underwing coverts are black, not chestnut; the tail is
      shorter relative to the wing tips; the upperparts are often darker. Good
      light or a photograph of the underwing is needed. Oriental pratincole
      (Glareola maldivarum) is not on the 2024 Georgian confirmed list used
      here. True swallows and martins lack the wader bill and ground-running
      habit. This atlas does not yet have a separate black-winged pratincole
      page.
    - >-
      It is not a threat to people. A long-winged brown bird hawking insects
      over open wet ground in Georgia in the warm season may be this species or
      black-winged pratincole — check underwing colour.
facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1766 as Hirundo pratincola. It is now placed in
    Glareola (Brisson, 1760). The 2024 Georgian checklist follows the OSME
    Region List for names: order Charadriiformes, family Glareolidae, genus
    Glareola. English checklist name is Collared Pratincole.
  - >-
    Nominate G. p. pratincola is the form expected in the western Palearctic,
    including the Caucasus region in handbooks. African subspecies are not
    treated as field splits for Georgia on this page.
  - >-
    Georgian names: მდელოს მერცხალა is the usual species name; ჟღალფრთიანა
    მერცხალა appears in some checklists; მერცხალა is also the genus. English
    names include collared pratincole, common pratincole, and red-winged
    pratincole. Russian is луговая тиркушка. Black-winged pratincole is
    შავფრთიანა მერცხალა.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: BB; M, data quality A, global and national Red List
    LC, occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph). Older Georgian sources
    (for example NPLG / Soviet-era encyclopedia treatments) stated Georgia only
    on passage, with breeding in neighbouring Transcaucasia. An earlier SABUKO-
    style list coded the species as migrant (M) only. This page follows the
    2024 checklist for current national status and reports the older view as a
    disagreement. Country-level confirmation is not a regional map.
faq:
  - question: Is the collared pratincole found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia lists it as a
      confirmed breeding bird and migrant (BB; M). Older Georgian sources
      treated it only as a passage visitor.
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In the breeding and migration seasons on open ground near water — short
      grassland, mudflats, and wetland edges. The 2024 checklist does not publish
      a regional gazetteer, so this page does not colour regions. Do not assume
      it is statewide.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Swallow-like flight, forked tail, short bill, and a black throat collar
      in breeding plumage. Chestnut underwing coverts; black-winged pratincole
      has black underwings.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 24–28 cm long at species level, with a wingspan often given as
      about 60–70 cm. A separate measured series from Georgia is not given on
      this page.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Mainly insects, caught in flight or on the ground. Diet has not been
      studied separately in Georgia on this page.
  - question: When is it present in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      As a breeding migrant: present in the breeding season and on passage,
      absent in winter, according to the 2024 checklist. Exact dates for Georgia
      are not tabulated here.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      The 2024 checklist codes it BB (breeding bird). Older Georgian sources said
      passage only. Named nest sites inside Georgia are not listed on this page.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It is harmless. It is globally Least Concern and coded LC nationally on
      the 2024 checklist. It is an AEWA species. Do not disturb nests on open
