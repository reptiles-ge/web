overview: >-
  The little owl (Athene noctua) is a small owl (family Strigidae) confirmed in
  Georgia as a year-round resident. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of
  Georgia codes it YR-R: a breeder present throughout the year. Seasonality is
  classed as high-quality (code A). Occurrence quality is high (H), with
  photographic evidence. SABUKO lists the Georgian name ჭოტი. Adults are often
  about 21–23 cm long at species level, with a flat-topped head, yellow eyes and
  no ear tufts. It is often seen by day on posts, roofs or ruins. It is not a
  threat to people. IUCN lists the species as Least Concern. The 2024 checklist
  assigns the same category nationally.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia look in open and semi-open country — not in closed dense forest.
  This page does not colour a municipality-by-municipality map. The 2024
  checklist confirms country-level resident status but does not publish a
  regional gazetteer. NPLG treats it as a breeding bird in Georgia. Species-level
  habitat is farmland with trees or hedges, orchards, parks, rocky ground,
  steppes, semi-deserts, ruins, quarries and village edges. It often uses
  buildings and other human structures. Closed dense forest is less typical. Do
  not assume it evenly across the whole country, including high treeless alpine
  plateaus. A separate national elevation band for Georgia is not published
  here. The atlas map is left uncoloured until named Georgian localities are
  taken from sources that actually list them.
diet: >-
  There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page. Across the range the
  diet is mixed: insects (beetles, grasshoppers and others), earthworms and
  other invertebrates, plus small vertebrates — rodents, shrews, small birds,
  lizards and frogs. Prey is often taken from a perch by a short stoop, or by
  hunting on the ground. Surplus food may be cached in holes. That is
  species-level diet, not a measured Georgian menu.
behavior: >-
  It is active mainly at dusk and at night, but is also often seen by day on a
  prominent perch. It stands upright, bobbing when alert. The male territorial
  song is a repeated nasal call with a rising inflection; other yelping,
  chattering or barking notes are given near the nest. Nesting is in a cavity:
  a tree hole, rock crevice, wall cavity, ruin or nest box. Species-level
  clutches are often about three to five white eggs; the female incubates for
  about four weeks, while the male brings food. Young leave the nest after
  several weeks. Those figures are from the wider range, not a Georgian nest
  sample on this page. The 2024 checklist treats the species as a year-round
  resident in Georgia: it is not classed as a long-distance seasonal migrant.
  Do not handle it.
conservation: >-
  BirdLife International assesses Athene noctua as Least Concern on the IUCN Red
  List. The 2024 Georgian checklist assigns LC globally and nationally. It is
  not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List (Resolution No. 190); among owls that list
  includes barn owl (Tyto alba, EN D1). A Georgian population trend is not
  measured on this page. Documented pressures at species level in parts of the
  European range include loss of nest cavities, intensification of farmland and
  fewer insects after pesticide use; that is not a ranked national threat list
  for Georgia. It is listed on CITES Appendix II, so international trade is
  regulated. Do not catch wild birds.
interaction: >-
  It is not venomous and is harmless unless approached. Wild birds must not be
  taken.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A small compact owl with yellow eyes, a flat-topped head and no ear tufts.
    Confirmed in Georgia as a year-round resident on the 2024 checklist. Often
    seen by day; the usual confusion is Eurasian scops owl, which has ear tufts
    and is a breeding migrant.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length at species level is often about 21–23 cm; wingspan in the
      literature is often cited around 50–56 cm; mass is often about 105–260 g.
      Published figures differ slightly. Females are typically heavier than
      males. The head is round and flat-topped, without ear tufts. The eyes are
      yellow. Pale “eyebrow” lines give a frowning look. The legs are relatively
      long for an owl; the tail is short.
    - >-
      Upperparts are grey-brown to brown with whitish spots and bars. Underparts
      are paler with darker streaks or mottling. Juveniles are fluffier and less
      distinctly marked. Field identification is to species. By day it often
      perches openly on a post, roof, wire or ruin — unlike most forest owls.
      The repeated nasal song is unlike the single-note whistle of Eurasian
      scops owl.
    - >-
      Eurasian scops owl (Otus scops) is similar in size but has ear tufts and
      is a breeding migrant on the 2024 checklist, not a year-round resident;
      this atlas has a separate page. Tengmalm's owl (Aegolius funereus) is a
      small forest owl with no ear tufts and yellow eyes, but a larger facial
      disc and denser woodland habitat; this atlas has a separate page. Tawny
      owl (Strix aluco) is much larger, with dark eyes and no ear tufts; this
      atlas has a separate page. Long-eared owl (Asio otus) is larger, with long
      ear tufts and orange eyes. Eurasian eagle-owl (Bubo bubo) is much larger;
      in Georgian the name ჭოტი is sometimes wrongly used for it. Barn owl
      (Tyto alba) has a heart-shaped face and is pale; it is on the 2014 Red
      List.
    - >-
      It is not a threat to people. Do not handle it. A small yellow-eyed owl
      without ear tufts sitting openly by day in Georgian farmland or a village
      is this species, not eagle-owl.

facts:
  - >-
    Described by Scopoli in 1769. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows IOC and
    the OSME Region List for names: order Strigiformes, family Strigidae, genus
    Athene. The English name on the 2024 checklist and SABUKO list is Little
    Owl.
  - >-
    The subspecies expected in Georgia is A. n. indigena (Brehm, 1855). NPLG
    assigns this form to Georgia (“south-European little owl”), noting paler,
    greyer plumage than the nominate, with weaker belly contrast and weaker
    bars on the tail. Birds of the World places indigena from the Balkans and
    southern Ukraine through the Caucasus and Turkey. Field identification is
    to species.
  - >-
    The usual Georgian name is ჭოტი (SABUKO, NPLG). ბუკნაჭო is also used in some
    Georgian sources. English names are little owl, and historically owl of
    Athena or owl of Minerva. In Georgian, ჭოტი is sometimes wrongly used as a
    synonym of eagle-owl (ზარნაშო); those are different species.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: YR-R, data quality A, global and national Red List LC,
    occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photo). That is country-level
    confirmation, not a regional map.

  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      Small size, yellow eyes, no ear tufts, spotted brown plumage. Often sits
      openly by day. Eurasian scops owl has ear tufts and does not stay through
      the Georgian winter.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 21–23 cm long at species level; wingspan in the literature is often
      about 50–56 cm. There is no separate measured Georgian series on this
      page.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Insects, earthworms and other invertebrates, plus small rodents, birds,
      lizards and frogs. Diet in Georgia is not separately studied on this page.
  - question: When is it active in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Year-round, according to the 2024 checklist. It hunts mainly at dusk and
      at night, but is often visible by day on a perch.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. YR-R on the 2024 checklist means a breeding bird present throughout
      the year. The nest is a cavity in a tree, rock or building. Georgian
      clutch dates are not tabulated here.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It is not venomous. Do not approach. It is Least Concern globally and
      nationally on the 2024 checklist. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
