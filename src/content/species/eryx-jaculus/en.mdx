commonName: "Javelin Sand Boa"
location: South-eastern Georgia
description: >-
  The Javelin Sand Boa is a non-venomous sand boa. In Georgia it lives only in the semi-arid south-east and has been seen at Lake Kumisi and Lisi Lake.
overview: >-
  The javelin sand boa (Eryx jaculus) is a non-venomous burrowing boa found in
  Georgia only in the semi-arid south-east. The 2026 checklist treats it as
  biologically confirmed and lists the subspecies E. j. turcicus. The 2021
  Georgian snake survey places it in the same landscape as the Levantine viper
  and Urartian ratsnake — not across the whole country. A named barcode and a
  checklist figure are from Tbilisi. Photos on this atlas are from Lake
  Kumisi, June 2020 (Giorgi Iankoshvili) and Lisi Lake, 2 July 2023 (Ioane
  Rostiashvili). In Georgia it is usually under 50 cm;
  it has no venom and kills prey by constriction. Its burrowing look is easy
  to confuse with the European blind snake, but the build is different.
habitat: >-
  Look in dry open country with loose soil — sandy or loamy flats, semi-desert,
  sparse shrub, and stony slopes. It spends most of the day underground, in a
  burrow, or under a stone; it usually appears at dusk or at night. The 2021
  survey restricts it to south-eastern semi-arid landscape. This atlas maps
  Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli, and Tbilisi; that does not mean it is even across
  every corner of those regions. Named modern points are Tbilisi (CaBOL
  1027415; also the checklist figure) and photographs from Lake Kumisi (June
  2020) and Lisi Lake (2 July 2023). There is no confirmed range in western
  Georgia, Colchic forest, or the high mountains. A separate elevation band
  for Georgia is not published here.
diet: >-
  Elsewhere in its range, in Sicily, adult Javelin Sand Boas mainly eat small mammals. They also take lizard eggs and, less often, lizards. Juveniles forage more on lizard eggs. In Georgia it likely feeds on the same prey: small lizards and rodents. It does not kill with venom: it wraps its body around its prey and suffocates it.
behavior: >-
  The 2021 Georgian review lists it as nocturnal, together with the cat snake.
  It is rarely seen by day; encounters are more likely on a road, on freshly
  turned soil, or when a stone is lifted. It is live-bearing: eggs develop
  inside the female and young are born alive. Litter size and the exact season
  in Georgia are not published here. If you meet one, watch and leave it —
  do not pick it up. It is on the national Red List; removing it from the wild
  is restricted by law.
conservation: >-
  IUCN lists it globally as Least Concern — the range is wide. Georgia’s 2014
  Red List (Resolution No. 190) treats it as Vulnerable (VU), criterion B2a —
  a small fragmented range. The list cites Duméril & Bibron 1844 as authors;
  the accepted name is Eryx jaculus (Linnaeus, 1758). The 2026 checklist places
  it in Boidae. Local threats are conversion of dry habitat, roadkill, and
  killing after confusion with vipers. Do not collect it or kill it.
interaction: >-
  The Javelin Sand Boa is non-venomous and poses no medical risk to people. It bites in self-defence, and the bite carries no venom. Still, do not pick it up: it is easily injured, it is on the Red List, and it can be mistaken for a viper. If a snake has a broad triangular head and a vertical, slit-like pupil, it may be a viper, so give it room.

  summary: >-
    The Javelin Sand Boa is a short, stout, non-venomous boa with small eyes and a blunt tail. Two things set it apart from the European Blind Snake: it is larger, and its eyes are visible.
  traits:
    - >-
      The body is short and stout, almost cylindrical. The head is weakly set
      off from the neck and covered in small scales — not the large plates of
      most colubrids. The tail is short with a blunt tip; it can look like a
      second head. The 2021 Georgian review places it among small snakes to
      about 70 cm, usually under 50 cm.
    - >-
      The eyes are small but visible, and the pupil is a vertical slit. A large shield on the tip of the snout helps it push its way into the soil. The scales on the back are smooth toward the front and weakly ridged toward the rear. The belly scales are narrow.
    - >-
      The ground colour is grey, sandy, brownish or reddish, with darker irregular blotches or bars on the back. A dark streak often runs from the eye to the corner of the mouth, and the belly is pale. The pattern varies, so do not rely on colour alone.
    - >-
      The Javelin Sand Boa has small but clearly visible eyes and the stout body of a boa. The European Blind Snake (Xerotyphlops vermicularis), by contrast, is worm-like, with small eyes hidden under its scales, and is usually much shorter. The easiest way to tell the two apart is by their eyes and build.
    - >-
      The European cat snake (Telescopus fallax) also comes out at night and has a vertical pupil. But its eyes are large, its body is slender, and its head stands out more clearly from the neck. The Collared Dwarf Snake lives in the same dry zone, but it is a thin snake related to grass snakes, with a round pupil. The Eastern steppe viper has a broad triangular head and ridged scales. It is venomous.
faq:
  - question: Does the javelin sand boa occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2026 checklist lists it as a confirmed species in Georgia. It does not occur throughout the country, though: it lives only in the semi-arid south-east.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In south-eastern Georgia, in dry, open places with loose soil. The atlas map marks Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli and Tbilisi. It has been seen in Tbilisi, at Lake Kumisi and at Lisi Lake. The Lake Kumisi photo was taken in June 2020, and the Lisi Lake photo on 2 July 2023. It is rarely seen by day. Do not look for it in western Georgia or in the high mountains.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      No. The Javelin Sand Boa is a non-venomous boa and poses no threat to human health. It kills its prey by constriction, not with venom. Still, do not pick it up: it is on the Red List, and you may mistake it for a viper.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      The Javelin Sand Boa is a short, stout snake with small eyes, a blunt tail and a head covered in small scales. The European Blind Snake, by contrast, looks like a worm, and its eyes are barely visible. The European cat snake has large eyes and a slender body. So the easiest way to tell these three snakes apart is by their eyes and body shape.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      In Georgia it is usually under 50 cm; the 2021 review places it among
      small snakes up to about 70 cm.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Elsewhere in its range, it mainly eats small mammals. It also takes lizard eggs. It kills its prey by squeezing it.
  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      Mainly at night and at dusk. It spends most of the day underground.
  - question: Is it on the Georgian Red List?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Georgia's 2014 Red List includes the Javelin Sand Boa as a vulnerable species. The reason is that here it lives in a small area, split into patches cut off from one another. Globally, however, it faces little threat.
