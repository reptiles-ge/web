commonName: Darevsky's viper
location: Samtskhe — Javakheti
description: >-
  A critically endangered highland viper endemic to the Javakheti Ridge,
  Erusheti Mountains, and Akhaltsikhe Highland.
overview: >-
  Darevsky’s viper (Vipera darevskii) is a small venomous snake. It is found in northwestern Armenia, northeastern Turkey and southern Georgia — on the Javakheti Range, near Madatapa, in the Erusheti Mountains and on the Akhaltsikhe Upland. It lives among rocky debris in high mountain meadows, mainly at elevations of 2,300–3,000 m. It was named after Ilya Sergeyevich Darevsky, a researcher of reptiles. It faces an extremely high risk of extinction: its range covers less than 100 km², and it occurs in less than 10 km² of that range. Its separate groups are strongly isolated from one another.
habitat: >-
  In high, grassy mountain areas, it prefers stones and gravel left by glaciers and exposed rock, especially on warm south- and southeast-facing slopes. Among loose rocks, it shelters in winter, hides during the day, and finds food—rock lizards and rodents. In Georgia, it occurs on the Javakheti Range, at Madatapa, in Erusheti, and on the Akhaltsikhe Plateau, at about 2,300–3,000 m above sea level. These areas are cold and damp, with an average of about 1,000 mm of precipitation a year.
diet: >-
  It eats lizards (especially Darevskia valentini), small rodents (Microtus), and grasshoppers and related insects. Young ones eat newborn rock lizards and small insects. It hunts from ambush and uses venom to subdue its prey.
behavior: >-
  Diurnal; it moves within rocky deposits to keep an optimal body temperature
  of about 26–28 °C. Ovoviviparous: mating in May after the first spring shed;
  young are born in September to early October — usually 4–8 individuals,
  about 15–18 cm long. It hibernates deep in rock piles.
conservation: >-
  It is critically endangered (CR). Its range is smaller than 100 km², while the places where it is actually found cover less than 10 km² in total. Its groups are separated from one another, and the places suitable for it to live are shrinking. The main threats are overgrazing and grass mowing. In Armenia, much of the area where this species lives lies within Lake Artvi National Park.

faq:
  - question: Is Darevsky's viper dangerous to humans?
    answer: >-
      It is venomous but considered moderately dangerous. Its bite is painful and causes swelling around the bite. The bite is not usually considered life-threatening for an adult, but immediate medical attention is still needed.
  - question: Where is it found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Samtskhe–Javakheti, it is found on the Javakheti Range, near Madatapa, in the Erusheti Mountains and on the Akhaltsikhe Highlands, at about 2,300–3,000 metres. Its range is very limited and consists of separate areas.
  - question: How does it differ from Dinnik's viper?
    answer: >-
      Darevsky’s viper is smaller and is found in the southern highlands of the Lesser Caucasus (Javakheti–Erusheti). Dinnik’s viper is found in the Greater Caucasus. It occurs over a wider area, though the places where it lives are separated from one another.
  - question: Why is it critically endangered?
    answer: >-
      Its extent of occurrence is under 100 km² and area of occupancy under
      10 km²; the population is fragmented. Overgrazing and habitat
      degradation are reducing numbers.
  - question: How do I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Darevsky’s viper is a tiny highland venomous species. Size, zigzag
      pattern, and rocky habitat above 2,300 m separate it from the nose-horned
      and Dinnik’s vipers.
  - question: What should I do if bitten?
    answer: >-
      The bite is painful, and the bite area swells. Call 112 immediately.
