commonName: Red deer
location: Very rare in Georgia
description: >-
  A large deer. Males carry branched antlers; in Georgia natural populations are
  small and conservation-dependent.
overview: >-
  The red deer (Cervus elaphus) is an even-toed mammal in the deer family
  Cervidae. In Georgia the relevant animal is usually the Caucasian red deer,
  often called C. e. maral; current international databases treat it within
  Cervus elaphus. It is not a roe deer: it is much larger, longer-legged, and
  adult males carry large branched antlers. The species occurs in Georgia, but
  should not be treated as widespread. Modern reliable information is tied
  especially to protected areas and restoration work, including Algeti National
  Park and Tusheti records. It is not venomous or toxic to touch. IUCN assesses
  the species globally as Least Concern, but the Georgian population is on the
  national Red List and is locally threatened.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia red deer are forest-linked: deciduous and mixed woodland, forest
  edge, tall clearings near cover, and quieter parts of protected areas are the
  realistic habitats. Across the range the species uses woodland, forest-meadow
  mosaics, and foothills. It often comes into more open ground at dusk, night,
  or dawn.

  This page does not colour a regional distribution map. The sources confirm
  country-level occurrence, but that does not mean even presence throughout
  Georgia. UNDP's Algeti biodiversity indicator material lists red deer with a
  minimum of 74 individuals. In 2025 the Ministry and Agency of Wildlife wrote
  about Caucasian red deer restoration in Algeti. Caucasus Nature Fund published
  a Tusheti record after a long gap. These are named evidence points, not a full
  national atlas.
diet: >-
  It is a herbivore. It eats grasses and forbs, shoots, leaves, buds, fruit,
  acorns, and other tree mast depending on season and place. In winter it uses
  more woody browse and forest-edge food. This page does not use a separate
  quantitative diet study from Georgia, so the local menu should not be treated
  as measured.
behavior: >-
  It is a cautious animal and usually avoids people. Activity often increases at
  dusk and dawn. Males shed and regrow antlers each year. During the autumn rut,
  males roar, compete, and sometimes clash antlers. Calves are usually born in
  late spring or early summer; a small spotted calf may lie hidden in vegetation.
  A hidden calf should not be assumed to be abandoned.

  Ecologically it is a large browser and grazer: it shapes undergrowth, moves
  seeds, and is prey for large carnivores. In Georgia possible natural predators
  include wolf, lynx, and, where actually present, Caucasian leopard. Their diet
  shares in Georgia are not measured on this page.
conservation: >-
  Globally Cervus elaphus is widespread and assessed by IUCN as Least Concern.
  That does not describe the Georgian situation. Georgian sources treat the
  Caucasian red deer as a Red List species at high risk; the National Red List
  record shows Critically Endangered. Check the current legal national list
  before quoting a category in a legal context.

  Main local risks are poaching, disturbance, habitat fragmentation, free-ranging
  dogs, and demographic vulnerability in a small population. The Algeti
  restoration project is intended to strengthen a small, fragmented population.
  A national population trend is not quantified here.
interaction: >-
  It is not venomous and does not hunt people. Practical risks are close
  approach, road collision, chasing by dogs, and picking up calves. During the
  rut an adult male is a large, strong animal; antlers and front-leg strikes can
  injure. Watch from a distance, do not feed it, and do not approach calves.

identification:
  summary: >-
    One of Georgia's largest deer. Males have large branched antlers; females
    lack antlers. It is much larger and heavier than roe deer.
  traits:
    - >-
      The body is long-legged and heavy. Georgia's Agency of Wildlife text gives
      average body length around 240 cm and adult male mass around 300 kg. Those
      figures refer to adult males; females and young animals are smaller.
    - >-
      Summer coat is more reddish-brown; winter coat is darker and thicker. A
      pale rump area is visible. Calves are spotted.
    - >-
      Male antlers are shed and regrown every year and are much larger and more
      complex than the short antlers of roe deer. Bezoar goat and tur have
      permanent horns, not annually shed antlers.
    - >-
      Roe deer (Capreolus capreolus) is far smaller, with a short tail and a
      white rump patch. Bezoar goat is a goat; adult males have long backward
      curved permanent horns and a beard. Wild boar carries no antlers and has a
      heavier, lower outline.

faq:
  - question: Is the red deer found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes, but it is very rare. Reliable modern information is tied especially
      to protected areas and restoration work, including Algeti and Tusheti.
  - question: Is it the same as roe deer?
    answer: >-
      No. Roe deer is Capreolus capreolus and is much smaller. Red deer is
      Cervus elaphus.
  - question: Is it dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      It is not venomous and usually avoids people. Close approach, handling a
      calf, dogs chasing deer, road collisions, or standing near rutting males
      can be dangerous.
  - question: Why is it important to protect it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      The species is globally widespread, but Georgia's population is small and
      vulnerable. Poaching, disturbance, and habitat fragmentation are the main
      named pressures.
