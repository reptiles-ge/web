  A small, non-venomous, ground-dwelling forest Darevskia. The 2026 checklist

overview: |-
  The Artvin lizard (Darevskia derjugini) is a small, ground-dwelling forest lizard. It has no venom and is harmless to people. In Georgia, it occurs in the west and in the forest belt of the east. [Colour alone](/lizards/identifikacia) cannot reliably identify it.
  Its head and body, excluding the tail, are often about 4–6.5 cm long; the tail is often nearly twice as long as the body.
habitat: |-
  The Artvin lizard is found mainly in forests and at forest edges, on the ground among grass, fallen leaves and roots. It can also be seen beside forest roads, in cleared areas and along rivers and streams.
  The 2026 checklist names Lagodekhi, Sioni, Mulakhi, Racha, Baniskhevi and Kintrishi. These are individual places and do not imply continuous distribution between them.
diet: >-
  The Artvin lizard mainly eats small invertebrates, including insects and spiders.
behavior: |-
  The Artvin lizard is active during the day. In warm weather, it basks in the sun at forest edges and in open areas. When threatened, it runs away quickly and hides.
  It lays eggs.
conservation: |-
  In its 2009 global assessment, the IUCN classified the Artvin lizard as Near Threatened because habitat loss and fragmentation were thought to be causing a decline across much of its range.
  The species was not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
interaction: >-
  The Artvin lizard is not venomous and poses no risk to human health. If you catch one, it may bite or shed its tail to defend itself. Do not pick it up — take a photo and leave it where it is.

  summary: >-
    It is a small forest lizard that lives on the ground. Its head is less flat than those of [Darevskia species](/lizards/darevskia) that live on rocks. Colour alone makes it hard to identify; its body features, the kind of environment it lives in and where it is found also matter.
  traits:
    -  |-
      The Artvin lizard has a less flattened head than many Darevskia rock lizards. The scales on its back are rounded. They are mostly smooth, though some have a slightly raised ridge down the middle.
      Some distinguishing features, such as the tiny openings on its thighs, are visible only up close. Do not catch the lizard to count them.
    -  |-
      The back may be dark brown, olive green or brownish. Pale stripes often run along the sides, and some lizards also have faint pale spots there.
      Males often have a greenish belly and may have blue spots on their sides. Females more often have a yellowish or whitish belly. The rear part of the tail is often turquoise or blue in young lizards.
      Their colour varies, so the species cannot be identified with certainty by colour alone.
    -  |-
      The Artvin lizard may resemble the [meadow lizard](darevskia-praticola) (Darevskia praticola), the [Pontic meadow lizard](darevskia-pontica) (Darevskia pontica), and some rock-dwelling Darevskia in the same range.
      The Artvin lizard moves mainly on the forest floor. Rock-dwelling species, including [Darevskia brauneri](darevskia-brauneri) and [Darevskia obscura](darevskia-obscura), have flatter heads; they can occur in the same valley as the Artvin lizard. D. brauneri has several scales between the central temporal and tympanal scales, a feature visible only in a clear close-up. Colour alone does not identify these species.
    -  |-
      The species is listed as the “Caucasian forest lizard” in the 2026 Georgian list. It is also called the “Artvin lizard.” This name refers to Artvin in northeastern Turkey, which is considered the species’ type locality.
      Older sources also call the species Lacerta derjugini. Its current scientific name is Darevskia derjugini.
    -  |-
      The Artvin lizard occurs in western Georgia and in the forest belt of the east. It is less likely to be seen on dry plains or open steppes.
