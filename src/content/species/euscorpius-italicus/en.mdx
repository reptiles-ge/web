overview: >-
  The Italian scorpion (Euscorpius italicus) is a true scorpion and belongs to
  the arachnid class (Arachnida). Modern taxonomic databases recognize it as an
  accepted species in the family Euscorpiidae.

  Its presence in Georgia is confirmed by several sources. The Scorpion Files
  lists Georgia within the species range, while the Georgian Biodiversity
  Database treats it as a native species in Georgia and names several concrete
  localities from western Georgia: Batumi, Kobuleti, Ortabatumi, Ozurgeti,
  Kutaisi, Zestafoni, Zugdidi, and Gagra.

  These records confirm the species in western Georgia, but they do not provide
  a complete national range map.

  The Italian scorpion is a relatively large member of Euscorpius, and adults
  reach about 50 mm. Available data do not treat it as a species of high medical
  risk to people, and the sting effect is mainly local.
habitat: >-
  The Italian scorpion is associated with warm environments. Across its wider
  range it occurs in ruins, buildings and wall crevices, under stones, and in
  other protected places. It may sometimes be found in human living environments.

  The Georgian Biodiversity Database also lists human-modified environments as
  the habitat type in the Georgian records. Therefore, a Euscorpius-type scorpion
  found in a yard, wall crack, or near a building may be Euscorpius italicus,
  although exact identification from habitat alone is not possible.
diet: >-
  The diet of the Italian scorpion in Georgia has not been studied separately in
  detail. Like other scorpions, it is predatory and hunts small arthropods.

  It uses strong pincers to seize prey, and its sting and venom to subdue it.
  This is general scorpion biology, not the result of a separate Georgian diet
  study.
behavior: >-
  The Italian scorpion has a secretive way of life. During the day it often hides
  under stones, in wall or building cracks, and in other shelters. Activity is
  more likely in the evening and at night.

  Do not pick it up by hand. If it must be moved from a house or yard, use a
  container and stiff card instead of direct contact, or ask a specialist.

  For exact identification, sharp photos from above and the side, plus the exact
  discovery location, are especially useful.
conservation: >-
  In the Georgian Biodiversity Database 2021 record, Euscorpius italicus is
  listed as a native species of Georgia. The same database shows LC in the
  national assessment field, NE in the global IUCN field, and “Not defined” for
  protection status.

  These data should not automatically be equated with the legal status of
  Georgia's current Red List or a full national population assessment.
interaction: >-
  The Italian scorpion is venomous in the biological sense that it uses venom to
  subdue prey. Available medical data indicate that its sting effect is mainly
  local, and the species is not considered a scorpion of high medical risk to
  people.

  A sting can still be painful. Do not pick up the scorpion by hand, and do not
  let children or pets touch it. If pain is strong, a child is affected,
  allergic or other systemic symptoms develop, or the situation is unclear, call
  112 and seek medical care.

  traits:
    - >-
      According to the Scorpion Files, Euscorpius italicus is one of the
      relatively large species in the genus. The total length of adults reaches
      about 50 mm.
    - >-
      The body is usually dark brown, while the legs and the end of the tail may
      be lighter, yellow-brown. The pincers are strong and massive, and the tail
      is short and slender relative to the body.
    - >-
      Euscorpius and related Alpiscorpius species often cannot be separated
      exactly by colour or size alone. Reliable identification may require
      close-up morphological characters and specialist assessment.
    - >-
      The mottled scorpion (Mesobuthus eupeus) belongs to Buthidae and is linked
      in Georgia more with dry, open places. The Italian scorpion is a member of
      Euscorpiidae and is often linked in sources with warm places, buildings,
      wall cracks, and other shelters.
