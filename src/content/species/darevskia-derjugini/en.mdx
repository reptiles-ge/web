commonName: "Derjugin's Lizard"
location: "Western Georgia and the forest belt of eastern Georgia"
description: >-
  A small, non-venomous, ground-dwelling forest Darevskia. Confirmed in western
  Georgia and the eastern forest belt; colour does not identify the species.
overview: >-
  Derjugin's lizard (Darevskia derjugini) is a small, ground-dwelling forest
  lizard. In Georgia it occurs in the western part of the country and in the
  forest belt of the east. It belongs to the true lizards (Lacertidae) and is
  harmless to people.

  Body length is usually about 4–6.5 cm, and the tail is often nearly twice as
  long. The species does not occur throughout Georgia — forested and humid
  environments are more typical for it than dry plains and open steppe.

  IUCN lists Derjugin's lizard as Near Threatened.
habitat: >-
  Derjugin's lizard is found mainly in forest and at forest edges — on the
  ground, in grass, leaf litter, and among roots. It may also be found along
  forest roads, in clearings, and near rivers and streams.

  In Georgia it is distributed in the western part of the country and in the
  eastern forest belt. Records are known from different elevations, including
  lowlands to about 1,800 m. This does not mean the species is equally present
  everywhere across that range.
diet: >-
  The diet of Derjugin's lizard in Georgia has not been fully studied separately.
  Available data indicate that it feeds mainly on small invertebrates, including
  insects and spiders.
behavior: >-
  Derjugin's lizard is active by day. In warm weather it basks at forest edges
  and open patches, and when threatened it quickly runs away and hides in cover.

  The species lays eggs. Species-level accounts often mention 4–8 eggs, although
  this page does not have separate clutch data for Georgia.

  Data from other countries suggest sexual maturity after several years, but
  those figures should not be applied directly to Georgian populations.
conservation: >-
  IUCN lists Derjugin's lizard as Near Threatened. Loss and fragmentation of
  forest habitats are considered major threats.

  The species is not included in Georgia's 2014 Red List. Locally, it may be
  affected by logging, road construction, and changes to forest edges.

  Several subspecies have been described within the species, though their
  classification differs between sources. For this page, the main practical
  issue is accurate identification of Darevskia derjugini itself and confirmed
  distribution.
interaction: >-
  Derjugin's lizard has no venom and poses no medical danger to people. If
  caught, it may bite or shed its tail in defence. Do not pick it up — take a
  photo and leave it where it is.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A small, ground-dwelling forest lizard. Compared with rock-living Darevskia,
    its head is less flattened. Colour alone is difficult to use for
    identification — habitat, body traits, and locality matter.
  traits:
    - >-
      The head of Derjugin's lizard is less flattened than in many rock
      Darevskia. The dorsal scales are rounded and mostly smooth or slightly
      keeled.

      Some distinguishing characters, such as the number of femoral pores, require
      a close view. There is no need to catch the lizard to check them.
    - >-
      The back may be brown, olive, or reddish-brown, often with pale stripes
      along the sides. Some individuals also have faint pale spots on the flanks.

      Males often have a greenish belly and may have blue spots along the sides.
      Females more often have a yellowish or whitish belly. Young lizards often
      have a turquoise or blue rear part of the tail.

      Colour is variable and cannot reliably separate the species on its own.
    - >-
      Derjugin's lizard may resemble the meadow lizard (Darevskia praticola), the
      Pontic meadow lizard (Darevskia pontica), and several rock-living Darevskia
      species.

      Derjugin's lizard mainly moves on the ground in forest cover, while rock
      species are more often tied to rocky surfaces and have flatter heads. The
      sand lizard (Lacerta agilis) is larger, while the Colchic slow worm (Anguis
      colchica) has no legs at all.
    - >-
      In the 2026 Georgian checklist, the species appears under the Georgian name
      "Caucasian forest lizard." This atlas uses "Artvin lizard" in Georgian,
      linked to its type locality: Artvin in north-eastern Turkey.

      Older sources may use the name Lacerta derjugini. The currently accepted
      scientific name is Darevskia derjugini.
    - >-
      Derjugin's lizard is a confirmed species in Georgia. Modern records are
      known from Lagodekhi, Sioni, Mulakhi, Racha, Baniskhevi, and Kintrishi.

      The species is not distributed throughout the country. Encounters in dry
      plains and open steppes are less likely.

      not a reliable way to separate them.
