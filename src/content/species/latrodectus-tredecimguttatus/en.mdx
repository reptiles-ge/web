overview: >-
  Latrodectus tredecimguttatus is found in Georgia and is a [venomous spider](/spiders/shxamiani-obobebi). Its bite can cause severe pain and other symptoms; if it bites you, call 112. It has been recorded in [Tbilisi](/regions/tbilisi), [Shida Kartli](/regions/shida-kartli), [Kakheti](/regions/kakheti) and [Samtskhe-Javakheti](/regions/samtskhe-javakheti). Females are small, with a body length of 7–15 mm, excluding the legs. They often have red spots on the abdomen, although some adult females are entirely black. The species is also called the “black widow.” It was once considered a subspecies of Latrodectus mactans; today L. tredecimguttatus is a separate species.
habitat: >-
  This spider is found mainly in dry, open areas—steppes, semi-desert scrub, and sandy or pebbly places. It often builds its web close to the ground, among rocks and low plants.
diet: >-
  Qaraqurti mainly catches insects and other similar animals in a tangled web.
behavior: >-
  The female often hides in a shelter near her web. The karakurt does not seek out people; bites usually happen when someone accidentally touches the spider or it ends up in clothing or shoes. Do not pick up the spider with your hands. The female lays her eggs in a silk sac.
conservation: >-
  The species ყარაყურთი has not been assessed by the IUCN. It is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
interaction: |-
  A karakurt bite can cause severe pain, muscle spasms, sweating and nausea. Symptoms vary in severity; sometimes medical care is needed. A severe allergic reaction is also possible.

  If you are bitten, call 112 and seek medical care. Do not cut, suck or burn the bite. Do not pick up the spider with your hands. For more information, see the [guide to spider bites](/spiders/obobis-nakbeni).

identification:
  summary: >-
    The karakurt is a small, shiny black spider with an almost ball-shaped abdomen. Females often have red spots on their backs, though some spiders may be almost entirely black. Its web is irregular and tangled, not round.
  traits:
    - >-
      The female’s body is about 7–15 mm long, and the male’s is 4–7 mm long. These measurements do not include the legs.
    - >-
      The female has a shiny black front part of the body and black legs. Her abdomen is almost round like a ball. She often has red spots on her back, usually in three rows running along her body. The scientific name tredecimguttatus means “thirteen-spotted.” The spots are not always bright red; they may be orange or yellowish. Some have few or no spots, making the abdomen look entirely black. There may also be a red spot underneath the abdomen, but it does not always have the hourglass shape seen in the American black widow. The male is much smaller than the female and often has whitish spots.
    - >-
      In Georgia, the red spots on adult females often get smaller or disappear completely. Only a thin white outline may remain where the spots were. So the absence of red spots does not rule out ყარაყურთი.
    - >-
      The karakurt usually weaves an irregular, tangled web close to the ground. Its web is unlike [Argiope’s](argiope-bruennichi) round web with a white zigzag. Adult females often hide in a sheltered spot rather than sit in the middle of the web.
    - >-
      The presence of the pale-colored Latrodectus pallidus has also been confirmed in Georgia: females have a whitish or pale yellow abdomen. [Steatoda paykulliana](steatoda-paykulliana), which is also found in Georgia, resembles the karakurt. Like the karakurt, it has a black abdomen, but a red or yellow stripe often runs along its front edge. Color alone is not enough to identify the species reliably; do not touch the spider.
facts:
  - >-
    The accepted name is Latrodectus tredecimguttatus (Rossi, 1790). The World
    Spider Catalog treats the species as accepted and as the type species of the
    genus. The original combination was Aranea 13-guttata. Range: Mediterranean,
    Ukraine, the Caucasus, Russia (Europe to southern Siberia), Kazakhstan, the
    Middle East, Iran, Central Asia, China.
  - >-
    Latrodectus mactans (Fabricius, 1775) is a separate species. WSC: probably
    native to North America only; introduced elsewhere. The sources on this page
    do not record L. mactans in Georgia’s fauna.
  - >-
    Georgian names: ყარაყურთი and შავი ქვრივი. English: Mediterranean black
    widow / European black widow. Russian: каракурт. Turkish: karakurt.
  - >-
    Published records place karakurt in Tbilisi, near Gori and Aspindza, and in
    the Shiraki and Eldari steppes. This does not mean it is equally common
    throughout Georgia.
faq:
  - question: Is the Mediterranean black widow found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Latrodectus tredecimguttatus has been confirmed in Georgia. That does not mean it is equally widespread in every region of the country.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Its presence has been confirmed in Georgia, including in Tbilisi, Shida Kartli, Kakheti and Samtskhe-Javakheti. It is found mainly in dry, open areas, often among rocks and low vegetation.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      ყარაყურთი is a small black spider with a round abdomen. It may have no red spots, and its web is tangled. Color alone is not a reliable way to identify it—do not touch the spider.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      The female’s body is about 7–15 mm long, and the male’s is 4–7 mm long. These measurements do not include the legs.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Karakurt venom is dangerous to people, and a bite can cause severe pain and other symptoms. Do not pick up the spider with your hands. If bitten, call 112 and seek medical care.
  - question: Is this Latrodectus mactans?
    answer: >-
      No. Latrodectus mactans is a North American species, while the presence of Latrodectus tredecimguttatus has been confirmed in Georgia. Older literature sometimes confused the two names.

    answer: >-
