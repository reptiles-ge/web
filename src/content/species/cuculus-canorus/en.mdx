commonName: Common cuckoo
location: Breeding migrant in Georgia
description: >-
  A grey bird that looks like a sparrowhawk and has a two-note call. 2024 data
  confirm that it breeds in Georgia; it does not stay for the winter.
overview: >-
  The common cuckoo (Cuculus canorus) is a medium-sized bird in the cuckoo
  family (Cuculidae) that lays its eggs in other birds’ nests. An adult is about
  32–34 cm long. It is slate grey above and barred below. Its breeding in
  Georgia is confirmed, but it does not stay once the breeding season is over.
  It is also seen during migration. Its presence in Georgia has been confirmed
  with photographic evidence, and the information about its seasonal movements
  is reliable. It poses no direct threat to people. The IUCN assesses it as
  Least Concern (LC) worldwide. Georgia’s 2024 national assessment places it in
  the same category.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, look for the cuckoo during the breeding season and on migration,
  not in winter. It is a bird of open and partly open country: forest edges,
  farmland with trees, river valleys, reed beds, gardens and similar places
  where the songbirds in whose nests it lays its eggs breed. 2024 data confirm
  that it breeds in Georgia and passes through on migration. The map on this
  page shades Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti, Tbilisi, Adjara and
  Samtskhe-Javakheti as regions with records: regions where public iNaturalist
  photo observations reach the table threshold. Guria, Abkhazia, Imereti and
  Shida Kartli have only individual records, which do not yet count as regional
  distribution. Few or no records do not mean the species is absent, and record
  counts reflect observation effort, not density, so do not assume it occurs
  evenly across the country, including on high, treeless plateaus or in dense
  forests.
diet: >-
  Adult cuckoos eat various insects, especially hairy caterpillars that other
  birds often avoid. When a female visits another bird’s nest, she may also eat
  the owner’s egg or chick. This information comes from other parts of the
  cuckoo’s range and describes the species in general, not cuckoos in Georgia
  specifically.
behavior: >-
  The cuckoo is active during the day. Its call is often heard before the bird
  itself is seen. The male usually sits on a conspicuous perch and sings a
  two-note “cuck-oo”. The female has a different, bubbling call. The cuckoo does
  not build its own nest: the female lays her eggs in other birds’ nests and
  often removes one of the owner’s eggs. It is known to use the nests of many
  bird species. In Europe, these often include meadow pipits, reed warblers,
  dunnocks and wagtails. Some of these birds also nest in Georgia, but that does
  not mean the cuckoo lays its eggs in their nests there. The cuckoo chick
  usually pushes any remaining eggs or other chicks out of the nest. In general,
  the eggs hatch after about 11–13 days. Over one season, a female can lay many
  eggs in different nests; a commonly given figure is about 9–22. Adults leave
  the breeding areas before the young birds do. According to 2024 data, the
  cuckoo breeds in Georgia and migrates, but does not winter there.
conservation: >-
  Cuculus canorus is assessed as Least Concern (LC) worldwide. It occurs across
  a very large part of the world. Its numbers have fallen in some rural parts of
  Europe, and the decline is well documented in parts of the United Kingdom.
  Globally, however, the decline is not considered fast enough to place the
  species in a higher threat category. The cuckoo is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red
  List (Decree No. 190). In Georgia’s 2024 national assessment, it is listed as
  LC both globally and for Georgia. The EU Birds Directive does not apply in
  Georgia. Do not catch wild birds.
interaction: >-
  The cuckoo is not dangerous to people. It is a wild bird, so do not catch it
  or pick it up.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A medium-sized grey bird with a long tail and a barred belly. Its call has
    two notes. According to 2024 data, it is a migrant that nests in Georgia;
    the 2024 checklist gives it no wintering status. It is often mistaken for a
    [Eurasian
    sparrowhawk](accipiter-nisus).
  traits:
    - >-
      The cuckoo is about 32–34 cm long, with a wingspan of about 55–60 cm. It
      often weighs around 110–130 g; on average, males are slightly heavier than
      females. It has a slim body, pointed wings and a long tail whose feathers
      are arranged in steps. The adult male is dark grey with a slate tint on
      the head, back and chest. The black-and-white bars on the belly begin at a
      sharp edge. The eye, the ring around it, the base of the bill and the feet
      are yellow. A grey adult female looks like the male but often has a
      yellowish or brownish tint on the chest and the sides of the neck.
    - >-
      Some adult females are reddish; this colouring is more common in young
      birds. Such birds have a reddish-brown back with dark bars. Young birds
      show scale-like markings or bars on their feathers more clearly. They
      often have a white patch on the back of the head and pale edges to their
      feathers. In flight, a cuckoo can look like a sparrowhawk. The call is a
      reliable way to tell them apart: the male cuckoo sings “cuck-oo”, while a
      sparrowhawk does not make that sound.
    - >-
      According to 2024 data, the [Eurasian sparrowhawk](accipiter-nisus)
      (Accipiter nisus) is both a year-round resident and a migrant in Georgia.
      It has broader, rounded wings and a shorter, barred tail. Its bill is
      hooked, and its eyes are bright yellow or orange. It does not make a
      “cuck-oo” call. The Levant sparrowhawk (Accipiter brevipes) also nests in
      Georgia and is migratory; it, too, is a hawk, not a cuckoo. According to
      2024 data, the great spotted cuckoo (Clamator glandarius) is an occasional
      visitor to Georgia, and its presence is confirmed by photographs. It has a
      crest and white spots and is sandy brown rather than grey, so it does not
      resemble a grey sparrowhawk. The Oriental cuckoo (Cuculus optatus) is not
      recorded in Georgia in the 2024 data. The cuckoo is sometimes also
      confused with the [common kestrel](falco-tinnunculus). A kestrel is often
      seen hovering in one spot in the air, and its pale underside has dark
      speckles, not bars.
    - >-
      If a grey, barred bird is calling “cuck-oo” from a treetop in Georgia in
      late spring, it is a cuckoo, not a sparrowhawk. The cuckoo poses no direct
      threat to people. Do not pick it up.
  coloration: >-
    The adult male is grey on the head, back, wings, chest and tail. Its belly
    is white with sharp black crossbars. Its eyes and legs are yellow. A grey
    female has a yellowish-brown tinge on the chest, and her barring is slightly
    weaker and starts right at the throat. Some females have a rarer
    reddish-brown colouring; these birds are barred across the back and wings as
    well. A young bird is brown above and has a white patch on the back of the
    head. A reddish-brown female looks like a young bird but has no white patch
    on the back of the head.

facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758. Order Cuculiformes, family Cuculidae, genus
    Cuculus.
  - >-
    Widely recognised subspecies include C. c. canorus (Europe east through
    Turkey and much of northern Asia), C. c. bangsi (Iberia and north-west
    Africa), C. c. subtelephonus (Central Asia), and C. c. bakeri (Himalayan
    region and nearby). The nominate form is the one whose mapped range includes
    Turkey and therefore the expected form for the Caucasus. Field
    identification is to species. A measured Georgian specimen series assigned
    to subspecies is not given on this page.
  - >-
    Georgian is გუგული; ჩვეულებრივი გუგული is also used. The Georgian name of
    the family is გუგულისებრნი. English names in use are common cuckoo, European cuckoo,
    and Eurasian cuckoo. The 2024 checklist uses Common Cuckoo.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: BB; M, data quality A, global and national Red List
    LC, occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph). That is country-level
    confirmation, not a regional map. Great spotted cuckoo is listed separately
    as casual (Cas), with photographs, and national Red List NE.
faq:
  - question: Is the common cuckoo found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The common cuckoo is confirmed to breed in Georgia. It is a migrant:
      it is found here in the warmer months, during the breeding season and on
      migration, and it does not stay for the winter.
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      During the warmer months, look in open and partly open areas with trees or
      reeds, where small songbirds nest. You will often hear its “cuck-oo”
      before you see the bird. Regions with enough photo observations are
      Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti, Tbilisi, Adjara and
      Samtskhe-Javakheti; Guria, Abkhazia, Imereti and Shida Kartli have
      individual records so far. See the map on this page.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Its back is dark grey and its belly is barred. It has a long tail, pointed
      wings and a yellow ring around each eye. The male sings a two-note
      “cuck-oo”. To tell it from a Eurasian sparrowhawk: the sparrowhawk has a
      hooked bill and broader wings, and it does not make this call.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: 'The cuckoo is about 32–34 cm long, with a wingspan of about 55–60 cm.'
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      It feeds mainly on insects, especially hairy caterpillars that other birds
      often avoid. A female may also eat an egg or chick from another bird’s
      nest.
  - question: When is it present in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      It is in Georgia during the warmer months. According to 2024 data, it
      breeds here and is also seen on migration, but not in winter. The map
      includes one iNaturalist photo observation from Kakheti dated 29 January
      2022. A single record does not make the cuckoo a wintering species.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. According to 2024 data, its breeding in Georgia is confirmed. The
      cuckoo does not build its own nest: it lays its eggs in other birds’
      nests. Its chick usually pushes any remaining eggs or other chicks out of
      the nest.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      The cuckoo is harmless to people. It is assessed as Least Concern (LC)
      worldwide, and Georgia’s 2024 national assessment puts it in the same
      category. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. It is a wild bird, so do
      not catch it.
