overview: >-
  The Caucasian agama (Paralaudakia caucasia) is Georgia’s only agamid lizard.
  It is a diurnal, rock-associated species and is harmless to people. In Georgia
  it occurs mainly in the eastern and southern parts of the country, with only
  local occurrence in the west. Confirmed records are known from Aspindza and
  Dedoplistskaro municipalities. It is not distributed in humid Colchic forests.
habitat: >-
  The Caucasian agama inhabits rocky slopes and dry or semi-dry open places. It
  uses rock crevices, spaces under stones, and burrows as shelter. In Georgia it
  is found mainly in the eastern and southern parts of the country, while in the
  west it occurs locally. Confirmed records are known from Aspindza Municipality
  — Pia and Tmogvi — and from Dedoplistskaro Municipality, around Datviskhevi and
  Pantishara. Other sources also name the Iori Plateau, Kartli, Borjomi
  Municipality, and Meskheti. Exact western Georgian municipalities are not given
  in the available checklist.
diet: >-
  The Caucasian agama feeds mainly on insects and other small arthropods,
  including arachnids. Some sources also mention plant material, but its diet in
  Georgia has not been studied in detail.
behavior: >-
  The Caucasian agama is active by day. It often basks on stones and rock ledges
  and quickly retreats into a crevice when threatened. In defence it may also
  inflate its body. It is an egg-laying species, although the exact laying period
  and clutch size in Georgia are not given in the available data. Do not handle
  wild animals.
conservation: >-
  The Caucasian agama is a biologically confirmed species in Georgia. IUCN
  assesses it globally as Least Concern, and it is not listed on Georgia’s Red
  List. Older sources may use the name Laudakia caucasia, but the modern Georgian
  checklist uses Paralaudakia caucasia.
