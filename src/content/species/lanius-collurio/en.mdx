overview: >-
  The Red-backed Shrike (Lanius collurio) is a small bird in the shrike family,
  Laniidae. Georgia's 2024 bird checklist lists it as a breeding bird and a
  passage migrant. It also appears in a 2000–2004 list from Lagodekhi Reserve.
  Public iNaturalist photo observations are recorded in other parts of the country too.

  It is about 17 cm long. An adult male has a grey head, black eye mask and
  chestnut back; females and juveniles are much browner. The species uses open
  ground with shrubs and lookout perches. It spends the non-breeding season in
  Africa. Its global assessment is Least Concern (LC).
habitat: >-
  In Georgia the species occurs during the breeding season and on passage.
  One named historical record is the 2000–2004 bird list for Lagodekhi Reserve.
  All 12 regions have confirmed status in the map's records-by-region table:
  each has at least one retained iNaturalist observation. Shading does not mean
  the bird occurs throughout every part of a region.

  Typical habitat across the species' range is open or semi-open ground with
  shrubs, hedges and woodland edges. It needs a raised hunting perch, such as
  a bush top or wire, with open ground nearby.
diet: >-
  It mainly catches large insects and other invertebrates. It sometimes takes
  small vertebrates, including lizards and rodents. It often spots prey from a
  perch and may fix a catch onto a thorn to eat later.
behavior: >-
  It commonly hunts from the top of a bush, a branch or a wire. Its calls are
  often sharp and harsh, while its song is quieter and can mimic other birds.

  It is a long-distance migrant that spends the non-breeding season in
  sub-Saharan Africa. It should not be treated as a typical winter bird in Georgia.
conservation: >-

  describe its population trend in Georgia. Loss of open, shrub-rich habitat
  and reduced insect availability are documented threats elsewhere in its
  range; the cited sources do not assess a resulting decline in Georgia separately.
