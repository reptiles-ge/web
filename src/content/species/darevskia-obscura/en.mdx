overview: >-
  The obscure rock lizard (Darevskia obscura) occurs in Georgia. The 2026 Georgian checklist places local lizards formerly called D. rudis in the broad sense under this name, so older sources may call them D. rudis. Its separation from D. rudis rests mainly on mitochondrial DNA; possible gene flow where the two forms meet has not yet been studied. The checklist therefore treats it as a candidate species: the species boundary needs further testing, not its presence in Georgia. The records-by-region table on this page’s map combines public iNaturalist observations filed as Darevskia rudis obscura with additional localities that have no observer. That table confirms distribution in Samtskhe — Javakheti and Adjara. Shida Kartli has a single record and is not treated as part of the range.
habitat: >-
  The 2026 paper names specific Georgian localities: the Meskheti Range in Akhaltsikhe Municipality, Rveli in Baniskhevi Gorge, Mount Saaghdgomo near Akhalkalaki, and Borjomi Gorge. Another study collected specimens by the Kirkhbulakhi River for research on reproduction. The 2026 checklist also includes parts of Trialeti, Meskheti, Javakheti and the Enguri basin in its broad treatment of D. obscura; this is a generalised range, not a list of specimens from every part of it. This page’s map does not treat that generalised range as distribution: a region counts only when the records table marks its status as confirmed.
behavior: >-
  Thirteen females collected by the Kirkhbulakhi River in Georgia each laid 2–5 eggs per clutch while temporarily held under laboratory conditions. This small sample from one place cannot establish a species-wide clutch size or the breeding season elsewhere in Georgia.
conservation: >-
  “Candidate” in the 2026 checklist is a taxonomic status, not a threat category. This page does not give an IUCN or Georgian national threat assessment for D. obscura.
