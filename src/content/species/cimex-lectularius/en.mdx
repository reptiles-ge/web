overview: >-
  The common bed bug, Cimex lectularius, is a blood-feeding insect in the family
  Cimicidae. Adults are roughly 5–7 mm long, wingless and flattened before
  feeding. Their bites may itch, but they are not considered effective vectors
  of human disease.

  Georgia has confirmed specimens. A 2023 study reported
  bugs collected on 30 May 2022 at Gremi Church in Kvareli Municipality and
  at a church near Pichkhovani village. Both sites were associated with bat
  colonies in old buildings. These records cannot establish how common bed
  bugs are in Georgian homes. The study also cites older literature for
  human-associated occurrence in Georgia without mapping household prevalence.
habitat: >-
  Human-associated bed bugs generally hide close to places where people sleep
  or rest: mattress seams, bed frames, headboards, furniture joints and cracks.
  This is general biology, not a Georgian household survey.

  The published Georgian specimens came from two old churches with bat colonies.
  The researchers searched caves as well but found no bed bugs there. Available
  evidence does not establish an elevation range or a household distribution
  map for Georgia.
diet: >-
  Adults and all nymphal stages feed on the blood of warm-blooded hosts.
  Human-associated populations commonly feed on sleeping people; other mammals
  and birds can also be hosts. The Georgian specimens were associated with bat
  colonies, though the precise bat host at Gremi remains unresolved.
behavior: >-
  Bed bugs are mainly active at night, but may seek a host in daylight. After
  hatching, a pale nymph passes through five stages and needs a blood meal
  before each molt. Eggs are about 1 mm long. These are general life-cycle
  facts; no Georgia-specific seasonal calendar has been established.
conservation: >-
  This species is a parasite of people and other warm-blooded hosts and is
  treated as a household pest. The consulted evidence does not support assigning
  it an invasive or conservation category in Georgia; national status needs
  separate verification.
interaction: |-
  Bites can cause itching and disrupt sleep. In rare cases, they can cause a severe allergic reaction. A skin rash alone does not confirm the presence of bedbugs. Check gaps in the mattress and bed for live insects, shed skins, eggs or small dark spots. A clean home does not necessarily mean there are no bedbugs.
  If you find a specimen, keep it for identification and contact a pest control specialist. Using chemicals at random is no substitute for identifying the insect accurately. Call 112 if you have a severe allergic reaction or trouble breathing.

identification:
  summary: >-
    An adult has six legs, two antennae and a brownish abdomen with clearly visible segments. Before feeding, its body is flattened from top to bottom. After feeding on blood, it swells and darkens. You cannot identify the insect species from the bite alone.
  traits:
    - >-
      An adult is usually 5–7 mm in size and cannot fly. The small, immature insects are yellowish-white and can be hard to spot. The eggs are light-colored and about 1 mm in size.
    - >-
      Check the mattress seams, the joints of the bed frame, and nearby cracks. Look for the insect itself or signs of it. Dark spots, shed skins, and eggs add to the suspicion; marks on the skin alone are not a reliable sign.
    - >-
      The [brown marmorated stink bug](halyomorpha-halys) is a different insect: it is larger, has a shield-shaped body, feeds on plants and does not suck human blood. An adult tick has eight legs; a bed bug has six. Closely related Cimicidae species look very similar, so identifying the exact species sometimes requires a specialist.

    answer: No. They can occur in well-kept places; cleanliness does not rule them out.
