overview: >-
  The European glass lizard (Pseudopus apodus) is a large legless lizard that
  people in Georgia often mistake for a snake. In fact, it is a lizard in the
  family Anguidae. Moving eyelids, ear openings, and the clear fold along the
  sides of the body are especially helpful for telling it apart from snakes.

  According to the 2026 Georgian checklist, the species is biologically
  confirmed in Georgia and is listed only from the eastern part of the country.
  The atlas map marks Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli, Shida Kartli, Mtskheta–Mtianeti,
  and Tbilisi.

  The European glass lizard is non-venomous and does not pose a danger to
  people. It reaches about one metre in length, and large individuals sometimes
  grow to 1.35 m. Its global IUCN status is Least Concern (LC), and the species
  is not listed in Georgia's Red List.

  If you meet one, leave it where it is and do not handle it. For
  identification, take a photo from a safe distance.

  help it eat shelled prey. That makes a glass lizard seen in a garden a useful
  neighbour more often than a threat.
behavior: >-
  The European glass lizard is a ground-dwelling, mostly day-active legless
  lizard. Moving eyelids, ear openings, and the deep fold along the sides of the
  body help tell it apart from snakes. A small rudimentary limb may sometimes be
  visible near the base of the tail.

  When frightened, it may hiss or bite, but this is a defensive reaction and is
  not connected with venom. It can shed its tail, though this is rare in this
  species. Do not touch wild animals by hand — use a photo for identification.
