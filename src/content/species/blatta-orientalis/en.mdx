overview: >-
  The Oriental cockroach, Blatta orientalis, is an insect in the order Blattodea
  and family Blattidae. Adults are about 22–27 mm long and dark brown to nearly
  black. Males have wings shorter than the abdomen; females have only short wing
  pads. Neither sex flies. It is also called the black cockroach; the older
  combination Periplaneta orientalis refers to the same species.

  Ilia State University's Georgian Biodiversity Database lists the species.
  The map shows 75 public iNaturalist observations from Georgia and five
  georeferenced photo records from the Reptiles.ge gallery. In the regional
  table, distribution status is confirmed for Tbilisi, Kakheti, Abkhazia and
  Adjara. Records from Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samtskhe-Javakheti and Racha-Lechkhumi
  are shown as recorded-only, not as distribution. This status reflects the
  map's record-count criterion, not a systematic population survey. The National
  Library of Georgia's Terminology Dictionary lists Blatta orientalis as
  “შავი ტარაკანი”.

behavior: >-
  They are mainly active at night. A female encloses eggs in a dark case called
  an ootheca and deposits it in a sheltered place. The hatchling nymphs lack
  wings and molt repeatedly before adulthood. Development time depends on
  temperature and conditions; seasonality reported elsewhere should not be
  treated as a calendar for Georgia.

interaction: |-
  The black cockroach is not venomous. There are other risks: tiny pieces of cockroaches’ bodies and their droppings can make allergies or asthma symptoms worse in sensitive people. Cockroaches can also contaminate food. These are general household risks; this passage does not describe cases of illness linked to this species in Georgia.
  If you find a cockroach at home, first identify the species. Check damp places where it may hide, fix water leaks, keep food covered, and seal cracks through which it could enter. If cockroaches appear again, contact a pest control professional. Use chemical products only as directed on the label.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The adult has a flattened, shiny body that is dark brown or almost black, and long feelers. Wing length varies by sex, so color or size alone is not enough to identify the species.
  traits:
    - >-
      It is about 22–27 mm long. The male’s wings do not cover the end of its abdomen, while the female has very short, undeveloped wings. Neither can fly. In a photo, wing shape and the upper side of the body are more useful for identification than dark color alone.
    - >-
      The young insect has no wings. It is hard to tell it apart from an adult female without looking at its size and body details. The eggs are enclosed in a single dark capsule. Do not rush to identify it from a damaged or blurry photo.
    - >-
      Adult German cockroaches (Blattella germanica) are generally smaller and
      tan-brown, with two dark lengthwise stripes behind the head. Oriental
      cockroaches are larger and more uniformly dark, though neither feature
      is conclusive alone. The [common bed bug](cimex-lectularius) is another
      indoor insect: the adult is much smaller and oval, lacks functional wings,
      and feeds on blood.

  - question: How can I tell it from a German cockroach?
    answer: >-
      An Oriental cockroach is usually larger and very dark, with short wings
      on males and only wing pads on females. A German cockroach is smaller and
      has two dark stripes behind its head.

    answer: Around 22–27 mm long; this is a general species measurement.
  - question: Can it fly?
    answer: >-

      reduced wings. Neither sex flies.
