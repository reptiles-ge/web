overview: |-
  The Mingrelian scorpion (Euscorpius mingrelicus) is a true scorpion. It belongs to the arachnid class (Arachnida) — it is neither an insect nor a spider.
  This page uses Euscorpius mingrelicus as its main scientific name. A 2015 paper linked Euscorpius mingrelicus, in the narrow sense, to Georgia and Turkey. In 2019, Alpiscorpius was treated as a separate genus. That is why some modern databases also list this species as Alpiscorpius mingrelicus.
  Reliable information places this species in western Georgia. The map marks Adjara, Samegrelo–Zemo Svaneti, Guria and Imereti.
  The Mingrelian scorpion is small and dark-colored. An adult reaches a length of up to about 38 mm. It has venom, but based on current information, it is not considered a serious threat to human health.
habitat: |-
  According to available information, the Mingrelian scorpion is found mainly in damp, mountainous and wooded places.
  During the day, it may hide under stones, logs or the bark of dead trees, or in other natural hiding places. Dry, open places are more typical of the mottled scorpion.
  If you find a scorpion in a damp, wooded valley in western Georgia, it could be Euscorpius mingrelicus, but its location alone is not enough to identify the species with certainty.
