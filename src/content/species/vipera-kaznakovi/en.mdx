commonName: Caucasus viper
location: western Georgia
description: >-
  The Caucasus viper (Vipera kaznakovi) is a venomous snake found in Georgia
  mainly in the humid Colchic forests of the west and in zones near the Black
  Sea coast. Adults are usually about 45–60 cm long. It has a relatively stout
  body, a vertical pupil, and often bright reddish-orange colouring.
overview: |-
  The Caucasian viper (Vipera kaznakovi) is a venomous snake. In Georgia, it is found mainly in the damp Colchic forests of the west and near the Black Sea coast. Adults are usually about 45–60 cm long. They have a fairly thick body, vertically elongated pupils and often a vivid red-orange colour.
  The specimen used to describe the species came from Tsebelda, near Sukhumi in Abkhazia. Outside Georgia, this snake is also found in northeastern Turkey and along part of Russia’s Black Sea coast.
  A 2026 list treats the Caucasian viper as a possible separate species within the Vipera kaznakovi group. The available genetic data have not yet fully settled whether it and Dinnik’s viper should be considered separate species.
  The IUCN classifies the Caucasian viper as Endangered (EN). It is also listed with the same status in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
  In the “Records by Region” table on this page’s map, its range is confirmed in Adjara and Shida Kartli. For Samegrelo–Zemo Svaneti, Samtskhe–Javakheti, Racha–Lechkhumi–Kvemo Svaneti and Imereti, there are currently only isolated records, and these regions are not counted as part of its range.
habitat: >-
  The Caucasus viper is mainly associated with the humid Colchic environments of
  western Georgia. It occurs at forest edges, in ravines, in beech and chestnut
  groves, on river terraces and in cleared meadows. The dry eastern plains and
  the Greater Caucasus subalpine zone are not typical habitat for it.

  From the map’s regional table, only regions with status “distribution
  confirmed” count as distribution. At present that is Adjara and Shida Kartli.
  Samegrelo — Zemo Svaneti, Samtskhe — Javakheti, Racha — Lechkhumi — Kvemo
  Svaneti, and Imereti appear only as scattered records and are not counted as
  distribution.

  Vipers from the Borjomi gorge and Baniskhevi were historically assigned to the
  Caucasus viper, but their species assignment within the Vipera kaznakovi
  complex is not fully clear. Some studies treat populations on the left bank of
  the middle Kura as a separate lineage, while the 2026 checklist links the
  forest form of central Georgia to Vipera dinniki tuniyevi. The map table
  status reflects record density and does not resolve species boundaries in the
  complex.

  The Caucasus viper is mainly found up to about 1,000 m above sea level. It
  rarely enters deep, closed coniferous forest.
diet: >-
  Adult Caucasus vipers mainly take rodents, lizards and small birds. Juveniles
  more often feed on small lizards and insects, including grasshoppers.

  When hunting, it often waits in ambush and subdues prey with venom. These data
  are based on observations from the western Caucasus; the diet of Georgia’s
  population has not been fully studied separately.
behavior: >-
  The Caucasus viper is a secretive species. In coastal zones it often becomes
  active after winter in March, while at about 600–800 m it appears from the
  second half of April or in early May. It can be seen by day, especially in cool
  weather; in heat it more often shelters in shade.

  The species is viviparous — the young are born alive. Mating takes place in
  spring, and the young are usually born in August–September. A female may give
  birth to about 5–10 young at a time.

  In coastal zones it often begins overwintering in November; at higher sites it
  may do so from late September or early October. This seasonal calendar is based
  mainly on western Caucasus data and has not been studied in the same detail for
  every Georgian population.

  If you meet a Caucasus viper, keep your distance and do not try to pick it up.
  It does not seek people out and mainly reacts defensively when it feels
  threatened.
conservation: >-
  The IUCN assesses the Caucasus viper as Endangered (EN). Its area of occupancy
  is under 500 km², its distribution is strongly fragmented, and the population
  continues to decline.

  In Georgia, the species is also included in the 2014 Red List as EN. Main
  threats include habitat loss from tourism, urbanisation and agricultural
  expansion, as well as illegal collection and killing by people. Some coastal
  populations have already disappeared.

  In the 2026 checklist, the Caucasus viper is treated as a candidate species
  because boundaries between species in the Vipera kaznakovi complex have not
  yet been fully confirmed with genomic data.

  summary: |-
    The Caucasian viper (Vipera kaznakovi) is a relatively small, thick-bodied snake. Its pupils are vertical, and its scales have a raised ridge down the middle. Its main body color is often bright red-orange, though its coloring can vary.
    Where it is found is especially helpful in identifying it: in Georgia, it is found mainly in the damp lowlands of Colchis in the west.
  traits:
    -  |-
      Its body is relatively short and thick. Its triangular head is clearly set apart from its neck, and it has no horn-like projection on its snout.
      An adult Caucasian viper is usually about 45–60 cm long, though it rarely reaches 65–70 cm. Females are generally larger than males.
      In one group of measured snakes, the largest female was 60 cm long, while the largest male was 47.5 cm.
    -  |-
      The pupil looks like a vertical slit. Each scale on the back has a sharp, raised ridge down its center.
      The body's main color is often brick red, orange or yellowish brown, with a dark zigzag stripe along the back. Black individuals are also common, though they usually have a red or yellow tinge around the mouth.
    -  |-
      The [grass snake](natrix-natrix) (Natrix natrix) has round pupils and a slimmer body. Its yellow “collar” is often clearly visible. It is often found near water and is not venomous.
      The cat snake (Telescopus fallax) has vertical pupils, but a slimmer body and large eyes. The scales on its back are smooth, with no raised ridge down the middle.
    -  |-
      [Dinnik’s viper](vipera-dinniki) is found mainly high in the Greater Caucasus Mountains, at about 1,500–2,800 m. It is often grayer or browner and has a narrower head than the Caucasian viper.
      [The nose-horned viper](vipera-transcaucasiana) has a clearly visible, horn-like growth on its nose. In Georgia, it is found in dry mountain areas of the Lesser Caucasus.
      [The gyurza](macrovipera-lebetina) is a much larger snake — adults are often about 1–1.5 m long. In Georgia, it is found mainly on dry plains and in the foothills in the east.
    - >-
      The Caucasian viper is venomous. It poses a moderate risk to people. If it bites you, you need medical help. Do not approach, touch or try to pick up the snake.
faq:
  - question: Is it venomous and dangerous to humans?
    answer: >-
      Yes, the Caucasus viper is venomous. The risk to humans is moderate —
      lower than for the Levantine viper, but a bite still
      requires medical care.

      The snake does not chase people and usually reacts defensively only when it
      feels threatened. Keep your distance and do not try to pick it up or catch
      it.
  - question: What should I do if bitten?
    answer: >-
      Call 112 immediately. Keep the person calm, have them lie down,
      and limit movement as much as possible.

      Do not cut the bite site, do not suck out venom, and do not use a tight
      tourniquet. The person needs hospital transport and medical treatment.
  - question: How can it be told from the grass snake?
    answer: >-
      The Caucasus viper is a small, stout snake with a vertical pupil, keeled
      scales and often a reddish-orange ground colour. A dark zigzag stripe
      usually runs along the back. It has no horn-like projection on the snout.

      The grass snake has a round pupil, a slimmer body and often a yellow
      “collar”. It is not venomous.
  - question: Is the Caucasus viper found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The Caucasus viper is confirmed in Georgia, and the species’ type
      locality is in Abkhazia.

      In Georgia it is found mainly in the humid Colchic zones of the west. It
      is not distributed throughout the whole country, and the dry eastern plains
      are not typical habitat for it.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Georgia, the Caucasus viper is found mainly in the humid Colchic zones
      of the west — at forest edges, in ravines and in other moist wooded
      habitats, mostly up to about 1,000 m above sea level.

      In the map’s records-by-region table, Adjara and Shida Kartli have status

      The species assignment of Borjomi gorge and Shida Kartli records within the

  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      Adult Caucasus vipers are usually about 45–60 cm long, though rarely they
      may reach 65–70 cm. Females are usually larger than males.

      Compared with the Levantine viper, it is much smaller and shorter.
  - question: How does it differ from Dinnik’s viper?
    answer: >-
      The Caucasus viper is mainly associated with low, humid Colchic forests
      and is often found up to about 1,000 m above sea level. It has a relatively
      broad head and often reddish-orange colouring.

      Dinnik’s viper is found in the high mountains of the Greater Caucasus, at
      about 1,500–2,800 m, and is often greyer or browner.

      In the 2026 checklist, the species boundaries between these two forms are
      not yet fully resolved.
  - question: Why is it a candidate and Endangered?
    answer: >-
      In the 2026 checklist, the Caucasus viper is treated as a candidate species
      because its genetic separation within the Vipera kaznakovi complex has
      not yet been fully confirmed.

      According to the IUCN, the species is Endangered (EN): its range is narrow
      and strongly fragmented, and the population is declining. In Georgia, the
      Caucasus viper is also included in the Red List as EN.
