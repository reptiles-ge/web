commonName: Caucasus viper
location: western Georgia
description: >-
  The Caucasus viper (Vipera kaznakovi) is a venomous snake threatened with extinction. In Georgia it lives mainly in the humid Colchic forests of the west and near the Black Sea coast. Adults are usually about 45–60 cm long, with a relatively stout body, a vertical pupil and often bright reddish-orange colouring.
overview: |-
  Vipera kaznakovi is a venomous snake that is moderately dangerous to people. It is also endangered: it was assessed this way globally in 2008 and in Georgia’s 2014 Red List. In Georgia, it is found mainly in the humid Colchic forests of the west and near the Black Sea coast. Adults are usually about 45–60 cm long. The body is relatively thick, the pupils are vertically elongated, and it is often bright red-orange. If you see a snake like this, do not go near it.
  The species was described from a snake collected in Tsebelda, near Sukhumi in Abkhazia. [As of 2026, Vipera kaznakovi is recognized as a confirmed species](/news/georgia-herpetofauna-checklist-2026). Dinnik's viper and some other vipers in the same group are considered possible separate species, because it is not yet fully clear where one species ends and the next begins.
habitat: |-
  Vipera kaznakovi is found mainly in the damp parts of Colchis in western Georgia. It lives at forest edges, in ravines, in beech and chestnut groves, on flat, raised ground beside rivers, and in cleared meadows. It occurs up to about 1,000 m above sea level and rarely ventures deep into dense coniferous forest. It does not usually live on the dry plains of the east or in the mountain belt above the forests of the Greater Caucasus.
  Vipers living in the Borjomi Gorge and Banishkhevi were previously classified as Vipera kaznakovi. It is still not entirely clear which form in the Vipera kaznakovi group these vipers belong to. A 2026 list assigns vipers living in the forests of central Georgia to Vipera dinniki tuniyevi.
diet: |-
  Adult Caucasus vipers catch rodents, lizards and small birds. Young snakes also eat insects, including grasshoppers.
behavior: |-
  The Caucasian viper lives out of sight, but it also comes out during the day, especially in cool weather. In hot weather, it keeps to the shade more. It gives birth to live young and may have 5–10 at a time.
  It does not seek out people. It usually defends itself when it feels threatened. So if you come across one, keep your distance and do not try to pick it up.
conservation: |-
  The Caucasus viper is at risk of extinction. That was the finding of a global assessment in 2008 and of Georgia's assessment in 2014. According to the 2008 assessment, all the places where this snake lives cover less than 500 km² in total. These places are sharply separated from one another, and the number of vipers is falling. The assessment was made before it was clarified which species belong to this group.
  People are the main threat. As tourism, cities and agriculture expand, the viper is losing the places where it lives. It is also captured illegally and killed by people. Georgia's 2026 checklist lists the Caucasus viper as a "candidate species". This label is about how snakes are grouped into species, not about the risk of extinction.

  traits:
    -  |-
      Its body is relatively short and thick, and its triangular head is clearly distinct from the neck. It has no horn-like growth on its snout. An adult Caucasian viper is usually about 45–60 cm long and rarely grows to 65–70 cm. Females are generally larger than males: in one group of measured snakes, the largest female was 60 cm long and the largest male 47.5 cm.
    -  |-
      The main body color is often brick red, orange or yellowish brown, with a dark zigzag stripe along the back. Black vipers are also common, but they too usually have a red or yellow tinge around the mouth. The pupil looks like a vertical slit. Each scale on the back has a distinct raised line down its center.
    -  |-
      In the west, there are also almost black [grass snakes](natrix-natrix) (Natrix natrix), which look like a black Vipera kaznakovi from a distance. The grass snake is not venomous and has round pupils. Its pale “collar” is often visible, but it does not always show on an almost black grass snake.
      The brownish [smooth snake](coronella-austriaca) (Coronella austriaca) has dark spots on its back. If you cannot see the snake clearly, you may mistake these spots for the dark pattern on the back of Vipera kaznakovi. The smooth snake has round pupils and smooth back scales, with no raised ridge down the middle. But do not go up to an unknown snake to look at its pupils or scales.
    -  |-
      [Dinnik's viper](vipera-dinniki) is often grayer or browner, and its head is narrower than the Caucasian viper's. It lives mainly in the high mountains of the Greater Caucasus, at about 1500–2800 m. The [nose-horned viper](vipera-transcaucasiana), by contrast, has a clearly visible, horn-like projection on its nose. In Georgia, it is found in dry mountain areas of the Lesser Caucasus.
    - >-
      The Caucasus viper is venomous and moderately dangerous to people. If it bites you, you will need medical help. So do not approach the snake, touch it or try to pick it up.
faq:
  - question: Is it venomous and dangerous to humans?
    answer: |-
      Yes, the Caucasus viper is venomous and poses a moderate risk to people. If you are bitten, you will need medical help. The snake does not chase people and usually defends itself only when it feels threatened. So keep your distance and do not try to pick it up or catch it.
  - question: What should I do if bitten?
    answer: |-
      Call 112 immediately. Reassure the person who was bitten, have them lie down, and try to keep them as still as possible.
      Do not cut the bite site, try to suck out the venom, or apply a tight tourniquet. The person must be taken to a hospital for treatment. Read more in the [snakebite guide](/snakes/gvelis-nakbeni).
  - question: How can it be told from the grass snake?
    answer: |-
      The Caucasian viper is a small, thick-bodied snake with vertically elongated pupils. It is often reddish-orange, and a dark zigzag stripe usually runs along its back. Its scales have a raised line down the middle, and it has no horn-like growth on its nose.
      The grass snake is slimmer and has round pupils. It often shows a yellow “collar.” The grass snake is not venomous. Still, do not approach an unknown snake to look at its pupils or scales.
  - question: Is the Caucasus viper found in Georgia?
    answer: |-
      Yes, the Caucasus viper is confirmed to occur in Georgia. The species was first described from a snake taken in Abkhazia. It lives mainly in the humid Colchian areas of western Georgia and is not found throughout the country. It does not usually live on the dry plains of the east.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: |-
      The Caucasian viper is found in Georgia mainly in damp parts of Colchis. It lives at forest edges, in ravines and in other damp, wooded places, up to about 1,000 m above sea level. Vipers of the Vipera kaznakovi group have also been seen in the Borjomi Gorge and Shida Kartli. However, it is still not fully clear exactly which species they belong to.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: |-
      An adult Caucasian viper is usually 45–60 cm long. Only rarely does one reach 65–70 cm. Females tend to grow larger than males. Compared with the [Levantine viper](macrovipera-lebetina), the Caucasian viper is a much smaller and shorter snake.
  - question: How does it differ from Dinnik’s viper?
    answer: |-
      These two vipers differ in where they live, their colour and the width of the head. The Caucasus viper lives mainly in the damp lowland forests of Colchis, often at elevations up to about 1000 m. Its head is relatively broad, and it is often red-orange.
      Dinnik's viper lives in the high mountains of the Greater Caucasus, at about 1500–2800 m above sea level. It is often more grey or brownish. Still, do not go near a snake to check its head or colour.
  - question: Why is it Endangered?
    answer: |-
      The Caucasian viper is endangered. A global assessment reached this conclusion in 2008. The snake lives in a small area split into many separate parts, and its numbers are falling. Georgia’s 2014 Red List gives it the same status.
