commonName: Smooth snake
location: Georgia — widespread
description: >-
  A harmless, smooth-scaled snake of Georgia — secretive and often
  mistaken for a viper.
overview: >-
  The smooth snake (Coronella austriaca) is a non-venomous snake found in
  Georgia, often confused with vipers because of its colour. When identifying
  it, do not rely on colour alone — check the smooth scales, round pupil and
  head pattern. Georgian sources describe the smooth snake as usually under
  50 cm, though it may grow to about 70 cm. Globally it is listed as Least
  Concern by the IUCN and is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
habitat: >-
  The smooth snake is treated as a widespread species in Georgia. It occurs
  along forest edges, in open woodland, scrub, rocky slopes and foothills. It
  often hides under stones, logs and other cover. It is associated more with
  humid and montane environments than with the very dry semi-desert of the
  south-east.
diet: >-
  The smooth snake mainly hunts lizards. In adults, the diet may also include
  small mammals and, rarely, other small snakes. Its diet has not been studied
  separately in Georgia in full, so these notes reflect the species’ general
  ecology.
behavior: >-
  The smooth snake is quite secretive and often hides under stones or other
  cover. By day it may come out briefly to warm itself. When threatened, it
  flattens its body and can look more like a viper. If handled, it may bite,
  but it has no venom. The species is ovoviviparous — the young are born live.
  At species level, litters of 2–15 young are described, though this number
  has not been studied separately for Georgia.
conservation: >-
  The IUCN lists the smooth snake globally as Least Concern. It is not included
  in Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Locally, the species may be affected by habitat
  change and by people killing it, especially when it is mistaken for a viper.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The smooth snake is a colubrid harmless to humans, though it can sometimes
    resemble a viper. For identification, consider scale structure, pupil shape
    and head pattern. There is no need to pick up a snake to count scales.
  traits:
    - >-
      The dorsal scales are smooth and have no keel. Viper dorsal scales are
      keeled, with a small raised line along the middle of each scale. The
      smooth snake’s head is relatively narrow and less clearly set off from
      the neck than in vipers.
    - >-
      The pupil is round. Georgian vipers have a vertical, slit-like pupil.
      A dark stripe often runs from the smooth snake’s eye toward the back of
      the head. The top of the head may have a dark crown- or heart-shaped
      marking.
    - >-
      Colour can be coppery, brownish or grey. The back often has two rows of
      small dark spots rather than one continuous zigzag. The belly is often
      dark grey or reddish. Juveniles may have a more contrasting pattern.
      Georgian sources describe the smooth snake as usually under 50 cm, with
      a maximum of about 70 cm.
    - >-
      Several vipers may look similar to the smooth snake. The Caucasus viper
      has keeled scales and a vertical pupil. Dinnik’s viper is mainly linked
      to high mountain zones of the Greater Caucasus, while the nose-horned
      viper has a distinct horn on the snout. The grass snake often has a
      yellow or whitish collar and is more often found near water.
    - >-
      The smooth snake is not venomous. If you meet one, keep your distance
      and let it move away. Because it can be confused with a viper, do not
      pick up an unidentified snake.

    answer: >-

  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to humans?
    answer: >-
      No. The smooth snake has no venom and is not dangerous to humans. If

  - question: How do I tell it from a viper?
    answer: >-
      Look for smooth scales, a round pupil, a narrow head and two rows of
      small dark spots on the back. Vipers have keeled scales and a vertical
      pupil. Colour alone is not a reliable way to tell them apart.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
