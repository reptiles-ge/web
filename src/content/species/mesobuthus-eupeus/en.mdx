overview: >-
  The mottled scorpion (Mesobuthus eupeus) is a buthid scorpion. This page
  treats the species in the modern, narrow taxonomic sense. According to the
  2022 revision, Mesobuthus eupeus sensu stricto is linked to the Caucasus
  region, and older subspecies are no longer recognized separately.

  The species is confirmed for Georgia. This page maps Tbilisi, Kakheti, Kvemo
  Kartli, Mtskheta — Mtianeti, Shida Kartli, and Samtskhe — Javakheti. MCZbase
  lists a specimen as Georgia, Caucasus, Tiflis (= Tbilisi), and older checklist
  literature also names Tbilisi.

  It uses venom to subdue prey. A sting can be painful and may sometimes cause
  systemic symptoms, so handling a scorpion is not safe.
habitat: >-
  This page does not have detailed local habitat data for Georgia. In the
  Caucasus and neighbouring parts of its range, Mesobuthus eupeus is associated
  mostly with warm, dry, rocky or open grassy environments. During the day it
  often hides in shelter.

  The mapped regions do not mean the species occurs evenly in every habitat
  within each region. Other regions are not added without additional
  confirmation.
diet: >-
  The diet of the mottled scorpion in Georgia has not been fully studied
  separately. Like other scorpions, it is predatory and mainly hunts small
  arthropods, including various insects and other small invertebrates.

  It uses its pincers to catch prey, then the sting and venom to subdue it.
behavior: >-
  Scorpions mostly have a secretive way of life. During the day they often hide
  under stones, in cracks, or in other shelters, while activity increases in the
  evening and at night.

  Do not catch or move a scorpion by hand. If it must be moved safely from a
  house or yard, avoid direct contact and use a suitable container and a stiff
  card, or ask a specialist.
conservation: >-
  This page does not have a species-specific IUCN or Georgian Red List status

  Conservation claims are therefore not invented here: the page confirms

interaction: >-
  The mottled scorpion is venomous, but that does not mean it seeks out people
  or tries to attack. Stings are mostly accidental, when a scorpion is touched,
  pressed, or disturbed in its shelter.

  Do not pick it up. If pain after a sting is strong, the affected person is a
  child, allergic or other systemic symptoms develop, or the situation is
  unclear, call 112 and seek medical care.

  summary: >-
    A small or medium-sized, yellowish-brown scorpion. It has two pincers and a
    segmented tail ending in a venomous sting. Exact species identification from
    a photo alone is not always reliable and often needs specialist assessment.
  traits:
    - >-
      A scorpion has two pincers and an upcurved segmented tail. The tail ends in
      a sting. This is one of the key signs separating scorpions from
      pseudoscorpions, which have pincers but no tail or sting.
    - >-
      The body of Mesobuthus eupeus is often yellowish or yellowish brown. Dark
      spots or stripes may also be visible on the back. Colour alone is not
      enough for exact species identification.
    - >-
      Many scorpions in the family Buthidae have relatively slender pincers and a
      well-developed tail. These general signs are not enough to determine the
      exact species. A good close-up photo or specialist assessment is more
      reliable.
    - >-
      When comparing it with Georgia's Euscorpiidae scorpions, the [Italian
      scorpion](euscorpius-italicus) and [Mingrelian
      scorpion](euscorpius-mingrelicus), family, pincer build, tail proportions,
      and habitat context matter; colour alone is not enough.
    - >-
      Older literature may use the names Buthus eupeus or Mesobuthus eupeus in a
      broader taxonomic sense. After the 2022 revision, such older records should
      not automatically be equated with Mesobuthus eupeus as defined in the
      modern narrow sense.
facts:
  - >-
    The accepted name is Mesobuthus eupeus (C. L. Koch, 1839). The protonym is
    Androctonus eupeus C. L. Koch, 1839; older sources may also use Buthus
    eupeus.
  - >-
    Kovařík et al. 2022 restrict Mesobuthus eupeus sensu stricto to the Caucasus
    region and recognize no subspecies.
  - >-

    country-level “Georgia” record.
  - >-
    The Georgian name used here is ჭრელი მორიელი. English sources often use
    mottled scorpion; older broad usage also used lesser Asian scorpion.

      Yes. Mesobuthus eupeus is confirmed in Georgia. This page maps Tbilisi,

  - question: Is it a scorpion, spider, or insect?
    answer: >-
      The mottled scorpion is a scorpion and belongs to the arachnid class
      (Arachnida). Spiders are also arachnids, but scorpions are not spiders.
      Insects belong to a completely different group.
  - question: How risky is it to people?
    answer: >-
      A sting from the mottled scorpion can be painful and may sometimes cause
      systemic symptoms. Do not pick it up.

      If pain is strong, the affected person is a child, an allergic reaction or
      other systemic symptoms develop, or the situation is unclear, call 112 and
      seek medical care.

    answer: >-
