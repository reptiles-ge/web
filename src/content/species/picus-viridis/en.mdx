commonName: European green woodpecker
location: Year-round resident in Georgia
description: >-
  A medium-sized green woodpecker with a red crown, black mask, and yellow rump.
  A year-round resident and breeder in Georgia.
overview: |-
  Picus viridis is found in Georgia throughout the year and nests here. It is not dangerous to people. It often searches for food at forest edges or in nearby meadows.
  An adult bird is about 30–36 cm long. Its back is olive green, the area above its tail is bright yellowish, and the top of its head is red.
  Its occurrence is confirmed in Kvemo Kartli, Tbilisi and Kakheti. There are only isolated records in Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Abkhazia, Shida Kartli, Racha-Lechkhumi and Kvemo Svaneti, and Adjara; it is not considered widespread in these regions.
  Globally, this species faces a low level of threat.
habitat: |-
  In 2022, the green woodpecker was also recorded in Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park and the protected areas of Pshav-Khevsureti. There, it was mostly seen in forests with open patches and meadows. Pshav-Khevsureti is in Mtskheta-Mtianeti. Records of the green woodpecker in this region do not establish that it is widespread there.
  In general, the green woodpecker is found where there are both trees and open areas: in deciduous and mixed forests, at forest edges, in floodplain forests, gardens and parks. Open areas with short grass are often nearby, as it frequently searches for food on the ground, especially ants. Dense forest with no open areas is more typical of the black woodpecker than of the green woodpecker.
diet: |-
  The green woodpecker mainly eats ants. It usually looks for food on the ground, finding ant nests and eating both adult ants and their larvae.
  It also eats other insects.
  It is normal for the large green woodpecker to look for food on a lawn or in a meadow. Unlike spotted woodpeckers, searching for larvae in tree trunks is not its main way of finding food each day.
behavior: |-
  The woodpecker known in Georgian as მწვანე კოდალა is active during the day. We often hear it before we see it. It marks its territory with a loud, rapid series of laughter-like sounds. Compared with other woodpeckers, it rarely taps rapidly on trees with its beak.
  Its flight follows a wave-like path. The bright yellow feathers above its tail are especially easy to see in flight. It is wary and avoids getting close to people. If you find one, do not pick it up.
  It makes its nest in a hollow dug into a tree trunk. The female usually lays 4–6 eggs.
  In Georgia, it nests and stays throughout the year.
conservation: |-
  Globally, Picus viridis is assessed as facing a low level of threat. Georgia’s 2024 bird list gives it the same assessment at the national level, though that assessment comes from a project to update the national Red List. Picus viridis is not on the Red List approved by a 2014 government decree. The 2024 assessment does not give it legal protection.
  Surveys in Borjomi-Kharagauli and Pshav-Khevsureti are not enough to determine whether the number of Picus viridis is rising or falling across Georgia.
  Old trees with cavities suitable for nesting are important for Picus viridis. So are meadows with many ants and other open places where it finds food. Losing these places could harm the species.
  Do not catch wild Picus viridis or take it from the wild.
interaction: >-
  It is not dangerous to people; do not catch wild birds.

identification:
  summary: |-
    The green woodpecker is large and easy to spot. It has a red cap, a black “mask”, and bright yellow coloring above its tail.
    It is most often confused with the grey woodpecker. The main difference is on the head: red covers more of the top of the green woodpecker’s head, while the top of the grey woodpecker’s head is not entirely red.
  traits:
    -  |-
      The woodpecker called მწვანე კოდალა is about 30–36 cm long, with a wingspan of 45–51 cm. Its wing measures 150–170 mm, its tail 105–116 mm, and it weighs 185–250 g.
      Its back is olive green, the feathers above its tail are bright yellow, and its underside is a lighter yellowish green. Both males and females have red on the tops and backs of their heads. A distinct black patch around the pale eye looks like a mask.
      Like other woodpeckers, it has a straight, strong, chisel-shaped beak.
    -  |-
      Adult green woodpeckers can be told apart by the moustache-like stripe on their cheek: in males, the middle of the black stripe is red, while in females the whole stripe is black.
      Young birds look more mottled. They have many stripes and spots on their bodies, giving their feathers a scaly look. Their green colour and the red feathers on their heads are still visible.
    -  |-
      The green woodpecker is most often confused with Picus canus. Picus canus is also found in Georgia year-round, but its head is greyer and the top of its head is not entirely red. The male has only a small red patch on its forehead; the female has none.
      Dryocopus martius is much larger and almost entirely black. The male has a broad red area on its head, while the female’s red colouring is mainly visible on the back of its head.
      The [large spotted woodpecker](dendrocopos-major), along with the Syrian, medium spotted, white-backed and small spotted woodpeckers, is mainly black and white with red markings. These birds lack the green woodpecker’s olive-green colouring.
      The [wryneck](jynx-torquilla) is much smaller, and its brown colouring helps it blend into its surroundings. It nests in Georgia but, unlike the green woodpecker, does not stay all year.
    - >-
      In Georgia, a large, green woodpecker seen on the ground, especially at a forest edge or in a meadow, is often the species called the green woodpecker. Unlike spotted woodpeckers, it often comes down to the ground to look for food.
facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows the
    OSME Region List for names: order Piciformes, family Picidae, genus Picus.
    IOC World Bird List treats Picus viridis as a species separate from Iberian
    green woodpecker (Picus sharpei) and Levaillant’s woodpecker (Picus
    vaillantii).
  - >-
    Widely recognised subspecies include P. v. viridis, P. v. karelini
    (Italy and the Balkans east through Asia Minor and the Caucasus to
    northern Iran and south-west Turkmenistan), and P. v. innominatus in the
    Zagros. Georgian dictionaries name the form in the country as southern
    green woodpecker, P. v. karelini (Brandt, 1841). Field identification is to
    species. Some genetic work has questioned whether karelini is distinct from
    nominate viridis; handbooks still list it.
  - >-
    Georgian is მწვანე კოდალა. English names in use are European green
    woodpecker (2024 checklist), Eurasian green woodpecker (BirdLife), and
    green woodpecker. Older English includes yaffle, from the call. An older
    Georgian dictionary statement that only one Picus species occurs in Georgia
    is out of date: the 2024 checklist also confirms grey-headed woodpecker
    (Picus canus).
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: YR-R, data quality A, global LC and proposed national
    LC, occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph) and SR (sound
    recording). That is country-level confirmation. Regional distribution on this
    page is only the map table's confirmed status: Kvemo Kartli, Tbilisi, and Kakheti.
