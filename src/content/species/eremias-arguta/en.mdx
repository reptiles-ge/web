commonName: "Steppe-Runner"
location: "Southeast Georgia — sporadic"
description: >-
  The Steppe-Runner (Eremias arguta) is a non-venomous steppe lizard that lives in Georgia only in the southeast, and only in scattered spots. You may mistake it for the Rapid Racerunner.
overview: >-
  The steppe-runner (Eremias arguta) is a steppe lizard that in Georgia lives only in the southeast, and even there only in scattered places. It has been reliably confirmed in the Samukhi Valley, in Dedoplistskaro Municipality. It belongs to the true lizard family (Lacertidae), and the subspecies found in Georgia is E. a. transcaucasica. Globally it faces little risk of extinction, and it is not on Georgia's 2014 Red List. It has no venom, so it poses no danger to people.
habitat: |-
  The Steppe-Runner is a lizard of open, dry ground: steppe, semi-desert, and sandy or clay soil where the grass is sparse. It chooses places like these across its whole range, so don't look for it in forest. In Georgia it lives in the southeast, although its exact distribution here is not fully known. A specimen found in Samukhi Valley, Dedoplistskaro Municipality, has also been studied genetically. It occurs in Kakheti, but that does not mean it lives throughout Kakheti, and not every dry valley has been checked. Older records from Georgia also exist, from 1913 and 1970. No locality is known from western Georgia, Colchis or high Javakheti.
  Samukhi and the dry Dedoplistskaro lowland lie at low elevation. The subspecies living in Georgia was first described in Armenia, at about 2,100 m, but that should not be taken as a Georgian elevation. The species' full range is wide, stretching from eastern Europe to Central Asia. The South Caucasus lies near the southern edge of that range.
diet: >-
  The steppe-runner feeds mainly on small invertebrates. Its diet includes beetles, other insects and spiders. Some sources also mention small snails.
behavior: |-
  The steppe-runner is a fast lizard that is active by day. It often basks on open ground. It has been seen in Dedoplistskaro in late September, but that is a single observation and does not show the whole season. It lays eggs: in Europe, clutches of 3–11 eggs have been reported.
  When threatened, it quickly runs off and hides in a burrow, among grass roots or under other cover. If caught, it may bite or shed its tail. So leave it where it is.
conservation: >-
  Globally, the Steppe-Runner faces a low risk of extinction, and its population is assessed as stable. It is not on Georgia's 2014 Red List, even though in Georgia it occurs only in patches in the southeast. Locally, it may be threatened by the conversion of dry steppe land.

  summary: >-
    The Steppe-Runner is a relatively large lizard of open, dry places. Its back often has pale eyespots or dark blotches. In Georgia it may most closely resemble the Rapid Racerunner and the Snake-Eyed Lizard. Colour alone is not enough to identify it, so look at other features too.
  traits:
    - >-
      Compared with the Rapid Racerunner, the Steppe-Runner is stockier, with relatively shorter legs. Its head is pointed, and its body without the tail is often about 6–8 cm long. The lower eyelid is movable. This is the feature that helps you tell it apart from the Snake-Eyed Lizard.
    - >-
      The back may be grey, sandy or olive, with pale eyespots or irregular dark spots. Young lizards often have a bolder pattern. In adults, the stripes may be faint or not visible at all. The Rapid Racerunner often has clearer stripes running along the body and blue eyespots on its sides. Colour varies quite a lot, so do not rely on colour alone.
    - >-
      The scale under the eye also helps you tell the Steppe-Runner from the Rapid Racerunner. In the Steppe-Runner, this scale does not reach the edge of the mouth, while in the Rapid Racerunner it usually does. You need a close look to see this feature, but there is no need to catch the lizard.
    - >-
      The Rapid Racerunner (Eremias velox) is slimmer and has a relatively longer tail. It is more common in southeast Georgia. The Snake-Eyed Lizard (Ophisops elegans) is small, and its eyelids are fused, so its eye looks more like a snake's. The green lizard (Lacerta strigata) is larger and often green. The Asian snake-eyed skink (Ablepharus pannonicus) is not a racerunner at all but a slender skink.
    - >-
      The Steppe-Runner does occur in Georgia, but only in scattered spots in the southeast. It has been reliably recorded in the Samukhi Valley, in Dedoplistskaro Municipality. So don't assume it is found all across Georgia.
faq:
  - question: Does the steppe-runner occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The Steppe-Runner definitely occurs in Georgia. It lives in the southeast, though even there only in scattered spots. It has been reliably confirmed in the Samukhi Valley, in Dedoplistskaro Municipality.
  - question: Where might I find it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In southeast Georgia. There, the Steppe-Runner lives in dry, open steppe. One such place is Samukhi Valley in Dedoplistskaro Municipality. There are no confirmed sightings from western Georgia or Colchis.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      No. The Steppe-Runner has no venom and poses no threat to human health. If you catch one, it may bite or shed its tail to defend itself. So don't pick it up. Just watch it and leave it where it is.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Look for three features: a relatively robust body, a back with pale eyespots or spots, and a movable eyelid. The Rapid Racerunner is slimmer and often striped. The Snake-Eyed Lizard, by contrast, has fused eyelids. Colour alone is not enough to identify it, so check these features together.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      Not counting the tail, the Steppe-Runner's body is often about 6–8 cm long. This figure applies to the species as a whole, not specifically to lizards in Georgia.
  - question: How does it differ from the rapid racerunner?
    answer: >-
      The steppe-runner has a more robust body and often a pattern of eye-spots. The rapid racerunner is slimmer, has a longer tail and is more common. Both belong to the genus Eremias and may occur in the same places in southeast Georgia. The scale beneath the eye also helps tell them apart: in the steppe-runner, this scale does not reach the edge of the mouth. You do not need to catch the lizard to check this feature.
  - question: What does it eat, and does it lay eggs?
    answer: >-
      The Steppe-Runner lays eggs. In Europe, clutches of 3–11 eggs have been recorded. It feeds mainly on small invertebrates.
  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      By day, in the warm season. At that time, the Steppe-Runner is active in open places. It has also been photographed in Dedoplistskaro in late September. But this is only one observation and cannot show the whole season.
