commonName: Common blackbird
location: Year-round resident in Georgia
description: >-
  A medium thrush: males are black with a yellow-orange bill; females are dark
  brown. Confirmed in Georgia as a year-round resident on the 2024 national bird
  checklist.
overview: >-
  The common blackbird (Turdus merula) lives and breeds in Georgia year-round.
  Adult males have black plumage, a yellow-orange bill and a yellow eye-ring;
  females are dark brown. It poses no threat to people. Look for it in woodland,
  scrub, parks and gardens.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, common blackbirds occur in woodland, riverside woods, scrub, parks
  and gardens, including in settlements. Places with tree and shrub cover are
  especially suitable.
diet: >-
  It searches the ground for earthworms, insects, snails and other
  invertebrates; it also eats berries and fruit in season. While foraging, it
  often turns over leaves or probes the soil with its bill.
behavior: >-
  Males sing from high perches, such as treetops or rooftops, especially in the
  breeding season. The song is melodious and fluting; alarm calls are sharper.
  It builds a nest in a shrub or tree. In Georgia it breeds and remains
  present throughout the year.
conservation: >-
  Globally, the common blackbird is assessed as Least Concern. The 2024
  scientific checklist of Georgia's birds also gives it this national category,
  but that checklist is not the official Red List. The species was not included
  in Georgia's 2014 Red List.
interaction: >-
  It poses no threat to people. Do not catch wild birds or approach their nests.

identification:
  summary: >-
    An adult male is black with a yellow-orange bill and a yellow eye-ring.
    Females and young birds are brown and may have spots on the breast.
    So not every common blackbird is black.
  traits:
    - >-
      The bird is about 23.5–29 cm long and often moves across the
      ground in hops.
    - >-
      Unlike the adult male, the female has dark brown plumage and a duller bill.
      Young birds have breast spots, so colour alone can make identification
      difficult.
    - >-
      Adult male ring ouzels (Turdus torquatus) have a white breast band.
      Song thrushes (Turdus philomelos) are smaller, with brown backs and
      pale, spotted breasts. Mistle thrushes (Turdus viscivorus) are larger
      and greyer, with large breast spots. Fieldfares (Turdus pilaris) have
      grey heads and chestnut backs; in Georgia they are migrants and winter
      visitors.
