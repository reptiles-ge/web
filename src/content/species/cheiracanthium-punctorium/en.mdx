overview: >-
  Cheiracanthium punctorium is a spider in the family Cheiracanthiidae. A reliable,
  established Georgian common name is not confirmed for this page, so the scientific
  name is used. In English it is often called the European yellow sac spider or
  yellow sac spider.

  The species is confirmed in Georgia. In the Caucasian Spiders database, records
  are known from Tbilisi, Betania, Chokhatauri, Sighnaghi, Baghdati, Bakuriani,
  south of Kutaisi, Poti, and other places. The iNaturalist import adds 6 public
  observations for this page, and the map also includes 9 added localities without
  an observer. Because the species is known from these localities, this page
  treats one record as enough for the region to count as confirmed distribution.

  Female body length is about 10–15 mm; male body length is about 7.5–12 mm,
  excluding the legs. The spider is usually pale yellowish green or yellowish
  brown; the prosoma may be orange or reddish, and the chelicerae have dark tips.

  Bites are uncommon, but they can be painful and may cause local redness or
  swelling. According to modern European data, necrosis is not considered a typical
  outcome.
habitat: >-
  Cheiracanthium punctorium is mainly associated with warm and open habitats.
  European data place it in tall grass, shrub layer, dry and semi-dry meadows,
  and vegetation above the ground. It does not build a large orb web for catching
  prey.

  In Georgia, sources name localities from the west, east, and south of the
  country, including Poti, Chokhatauri, Baghdati, south of Kutaisi, Bakuriani,
  Sighnaghi, Tbilisi, and Betania.

  These data do not allow a full Georgian distribution or exact elevation range
  to be defined. The map records are shown as points, and the regional table
  treats every region with at least one record as distribution.
diet: >-
  Cheiracanthium punctorium is a predatory spider. It feeds mainly on insects
  and other small arthropods. It actively searches for prey and uses venom to
  subdue it.

  The diet of the Georgian population has not been studied separately in full.
  This description concerns the species' general biology, not the result of a
  special diet study carried out in Georgia.
behavior: >-
  The species is more active at night. During the day it often hides in a silk
  retreat built in vegetation.

  Females may be more defensive while guarding eggs or young, especially if their
  silk retreat or egg sac is touched.

  Cheiracanthium punctorium does not seek out or attack people. Contact is usually
  accidental, for example when someone touches the spider by hand, disturbs it
  while cutting grass or shrubs, or finds it in clothing.

  European data indicate that adults are more noticeable in the warm season, but
  the sources used on this page do not establish a precise seasonal calendar for
  Georgia.
conservation: >-
  The current accepted scientific name of the species is Cheiracanthium punctorium.
  Its range includes Europe, Turkey, the Caucasus, parts of Russia, Iran, and
  Central Asia.

  The species is not included in Georgia's 2014 Red List. The sources used here
  do not define a quantified population trend, specific threats, or special
  protection status for Georgia.

  There is no need to kill or catch the spider by hand. If it is found indoors
  and removal is necessary, act without direct contact.
interaction: >-
  Cheiracanthium punctorium is a venomous spider in the sense that it uses venom
  to subdue prey. An accidental bite to a person can be painful and may cause a
  burning sensation, redness, and local swelling. Nausea, headache, mild fever,
  or other general symptoms have sometimes been reported.

  Modern verified European cases do not show necrosis as a typical or expected
  result of bites from this genus. Even so, reactions can differ between people.

  Do not touch the spider by hand and do not open its silk retreat. If a bite
  occurs, wash the area with soap and water and monitor your condition. If pain
  is strong or increasing, swelling spreads quickly, breathing becomes difficult,
  the face or throat swells, fever appears, or your general condition worsens,
  call 112.

    A medium-sized, pale yellowish or yellow-green spider with long front legs
    and relatively large, dark-tipped chelicerae. It does not build a large round
    web and is often found in tall grass and shrubs.
  traits:
    - >-
      Female body length is about 10–15 mm; male body length is about 7.5–12 mm.
      Legs are not included, so a live individual can look much larger than its
      actual body length.
    - >-
      The abdomen is usually pale yellowish green. The prosoma may be greenish
      brown, orange, or reddish. The chelicerae are relatively large and have
      dark tips.
    - >-
      The front legs are long and conspicuous. The species lacks the bold
      yellow-and-black bands of [the wasp spider](argiope-bruennichi) and the
      white abdominal cross of [the European garden spider](araneus-diadematus).
    - >-
      [Wasp spider](argiope-bruennichi) and [lobed argiope](argiope-lobata) sit
      in large orb webs and have strong body patterns. Cheiracanthium punctorium
      is not a spider sitting in the centre of that kind of large round web.
    - >-
      [Mediterranean black widow](latrodectus-tredecimguttatus) and [false black widow](steatoda-paykulliana)
      are dark Theridiidae spiders with a more rounded abdomen. Cheiracanthium
      punctorium is paler, more elongated, and longer-legged, so it looks clearly
      different.
