overview: >-
  The Caucasian rock lizard (Darevskia caucasica) is found in rocky areas of the Greater Caucasus in Georgia. A 2026 Georgian list includes records of the lizard and DNA data from Zakagori Fortress, Mount Tbatana and Lebarde. This atlas has not yet settled whether to treat it as a separate species: researchers are still studying which lizards belong to the caucasica group and how members of that group in Georgia differ genetically. The lizard is not venomous and poses no risk to human health. To identify it in the wild, the location, the rocks immediately around it and the features of its scales all matter. Green or brown colouring alone is not enough to identify the species.
habitat: >-
  A Georgian source names the Truso Valley, Zaqagori Fortress, Mount Tbathana and Lebarde. The closest genetic data used for comparison also comes from Truso. These records do not show that the species occurs throughout Georgia. A region is marked on the map only when a source names it or gives a location that can reliably be placed within it. Look for the species on sunny rocks, stony slopes, roadside rock debris or rocky parts of a valley. It quickly hides in cracks.

behavior: >-
  This is a day-active rock lizard: it basks on stone or rock and retreats into
  crevices when disturbed. The Georgian sources used on this profile do not give
  a separate hourly calendar, exact emergence from winter shelters, or breeding
  months. For a useful observation, photograph the locality, elevation, back,
  side, and head scalation; catching the lizard is unnecessary.
conservation: >-
  Globally, the IUCN Red List assesses the species as Least Concern, but that
  older assessment covers the whole range, not Georgia alone. The 2026 checklist

  populations, and the 2025 genomic study also supports several distant units
  inside the species. A separate Georgian population trend, abundance estimate,
  and verified national Red List status are not given on this profile.

  summary: >-
    The Caucasus lizard is a small, fast rock lizard (Darevskia). Its color varies, so color alone cannot reliably distinguish it from closely related species. More reliable clues are where it was found, its rocky habitat and, when possible, the features of the scales on its head and just in front of the opening at the rear of its body.
  traits:
    - >-
      It has a slender body suited to life on rocks. Its tail is long, though after an injury it may look shorter or as if it has grown back. It can still be identified despite this.
    - >-
      It may be greenish, yellowish-brown, gray or brown, with pale stripes and dark spots along its sides. These features help describe its appearance, but they are not enough to identify the species: several Darevskia species look alike.
    - >-
      The 2026 images of the Zakagori/Truso material show the scales on the head and the plates in front of the opening at the rear of the body. These details are needed to identify a lizard in a photo among similar rock lizards.
    - >-
      The [Dagestani lizard](darevskia-daghestanica) is hardest to distinguish in the upper reaches of the Terek and near Tbatana: colour does not help, and sources also mention possible crossbreeding. In these places, one photo is often not enough.
    - >-
      The [Colchian rock lizard](darevskia-mixta) is associated with the damp valleys of western Georgia. In the Greater Caucasus, it comes into contact with caucasica only in a small area. The [Abkhazian rock lizard](darevskia-alpina) is also considered a candidate for this atlas; colour alone is not a reliable way to identify it.
