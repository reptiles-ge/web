overview: >-
  The raven (Corvus corax), known in English as the Northern Raven or Common Raven, is the largest member of the crow family in Georgia and one of the heaviest perching birds in the world. According to Georgia’s 2024 bird list, it nests in Georgia and stays there year-round. Its presence in Georgia is also confirmed by a photograph. The same list identifies its subspecies as C. c. corax. Ravens generally measure about 54–71 cm long, with a wingspan of about 116–153 cm. They pose no danger to people. Its risk of extinction is assessed as low both worldwide and in Georgia.
habitat: >-
  This page does not mark municipalities in different colors on a map. A 2024 list confirms that the species lives in Georgia year-round, but gives no list of records by region. Do not assume it is equally common in every settlement or on every alpine ridge. The species lives among cliffs, in open mountains, at forest edges, in river valleys and in rural areas where different kinds of land lie side by side. It often nests on a cliff or in a tall tree, and sometimes on a tall structure. According to an entry in a Georgian dictionary, it is widespread in Georgia, is more numerous near the upper edge of the forest and occurs up to about 2,500 m; it goes higher to look for food. This is a summary based on other information, not new field observations made for this page. Places where it has been reported include birdwatching sites in the Greater Caucasus near Kazbegi, Svaneti (including a 2016 entry in a Mestia photo gallery) and Tusheti National Park (in regional lists). These reports come from tour lists, lists of protected areas and photo locations; they do not form a complete map of the country. A densely populated city center is less suitable for it than cliffs, valleys and mountain pastures.
diet: >-
  This page does not cite a separate study of its diet in Georgia. In other places where it lives, it eats what it can find: carrion, small mammals, birds and their eggs, animals without backbones, plant fruit and human waste. It may also eat a weak or dying animal, but it does not usually hunt large animals. Do not put out poisoned bait or carcasses. It is not considered food for people.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. Flight is strong and often soaring; the tail looks
  diamond- or wedge-shaped in flight, not squared like a typical crow. Pairs
  hold territories; young birds may gather in loose groups. The usual call is a
  deep, resonant croak, not the higher caw of hooded crow. Species-level nests
  are large stick platforms on cliffs or trees; clutch size is often about
  four to six eggs, with incubation of roughly three weeks — those figures are
  from the wider range, not a Georgian nest sample on this page. The 2024
  checklist treats the species as a year-round resident breeder in Georgia, not
  as a passage migrant. Local birds may make short seasonal movements for food;
  that is not the same as long-distance migration. Voice on this page: xeno-canto
  XC727695 (Johannes Worona); no Georgian recording is included here. Do not
  handle wild birds or approach nests closely.
conservation: >-
  BirdLife International assesses Corvus corax as Least Concern on the IUCN Red
  List, with a very large range and an increasing global trend in the last
  published assessment used here. The 2024 Georgian bird checklist assigns
  global and national Red List category LC. A current national population census
  is not given on this page. The species does not appear as ყორანი / Corvus
  corax on the 2014 Georgian Red List (Government Decree No. 190). At species
  level, historical persecution, poisoned baits, and habitat change can reduce
  local numbers; those pressures are not quantified for Georgia on this page.
  Do not catch or kill wild birds without a legal basis.
interaction: >-
  It poses no medical danger to people. If you pick it up or corner it, its large beak and strong legs could injure you. Leave its nests alone. Do not keep a wild bird as a pet. It feeds on dead animals, so it may appear on roads or near the carcass of a farm animal. It is a scavenger; seeing it there does not mean it is attacking people.

identification:
  summary: >-
    It is a very large black bird of the crow family. It has a thick bill and shaggy feathers on its throat. In flight, its tail is wedge-shaped. It has a deep, croaking call. In Georgia, it is most often confused with the hooded crow when judged by size and colour. In the mountains, it may be confused with crow-family birds that have red or yellow bills.
  traits:
    - >-
      Size and shape: The species is about 54–71 cm long, with a wingspan of about 116–153 cm. It is much heavier than a hooded crow or a rook, and its beak is much larger. In flight, its tail is wedge-shaped or diamond-shaped, without a straight edge. It often glides without flapping its wings. This page does not give separate measurements from birds measured in Georgia.
    - >-
      Plumage: adults are glossy black with blue or purple iridescence in good
      light. Throat feathers are long and pointed (hackles). The bill is deep
      and slightly curved; the nasal bristles cover much of the base. Juveniles
      are duller, with a less glossy plumage and a pinkish gape when very young.
      Sexes look alike in the field.
    - >-
      The hooded crow (Corvus cornix) lives here year-round, according to the 2024 list. It has a grey body and a black head, wings and tail; it is not entirely black. The rook (Corvus frugilegus) is also confirmed on that list: some live here year-round, while others are seen during migration or in winter. Adult rooks have a bare, grey face around the base of the bill and a more pointed crest. Their tails are not sharply wedge-shaped. Red-billed and yellow-billed choughs (Pyrrhocorax pyrrhocorax, P. graculus) are smaller members of the crow family that live in high mountains. They have slim, curved red or yellow bills, not black ones. The carrion crow (Corvus corone) is not listed as a confirmed species on the 2024 Georgian list. Not every black bird in the crow family is a raven. These similar species do not yet have separate pages in this atlas.
    - >-
      It is harmless to people in an ordinary encounter. A large black bird gliding over the cliffs of the Caucasus, with a low, rough call and a wedge-shaped tail, is usually this species.

facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae. Order Passeriformes,
    family Corvidae, genus Corvus. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows the OSME
    Region List and uses the English name Northern Raven and the trinomial
    Corvus corax corax. IOC World Bird List uses Common Raven for the species.
    Field identification is to species; subspecies is not usually separable in
    Georgian field conditions beyond the checklist assignment.
  - >-
    Handbooks place Caucasus birds in the nominate subspecies C. c. corax
    (northern and central Europe east to Lake Baikal, south to the Caucasus and
    northern Iran). Other subspecies occur elsewhere in the Holarctic range and
    are not expected as Georgian breeders.
  - >-
    Georgian ყორანი is this species. რუხი ყვავი is hooded crow (Corvus cornix).
    English names in use are Northern Raven (2024 checklist / OSME) and Common
    Raven (IOC / many field guides). Russian is ворон.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: YR-R, data quality A, global and national Red List LC,
    occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph). That is country-level
    confirmation plus the named localities above, not a complete regional map.
faq:
  - question: Is the northern raven found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia lists it as a
      confirmed year-round resident breeder (YR-R), with high-quality
      photographic evidence.
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Often over cliffs, mountain pastures, gorges, and open country away from
      the densest city centres. Named encounter contexts include Kazbegi-area
      birding routes, Svaneti (including Mestia photo records), and Tusheti
      National Park checklists. This page does not map every municipality.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Very large all-black crow, heavy bill, shaggy throat hackles, wedge-shaped
      tail in flight, deep croak. Hooded crow is grey and black. Alpine choughs
      have red or yellow bills.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 54–71 cm long at species level, wingspan about 116–153 cm. A
      separate measured series from Georgia is not given on this page.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Carrion and a wide mix of animal and plant food. Diet has not been studied
      separately for Georgia on this page.
  - question: When is it present in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      All year as a resident breeder, according to the 2024 checklist (YR-R). It
      is not coded as a passage migrant there.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. YR-R on the 2024 checklist means resident breeder present through the
      year.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It is harmless to people on ordinary encounters. Globally and nationally
      it is coded LC on the 2024 checklist. It is not on the 2014 Georgian Red
      List. Do not catch wild birds or use poisoned baits.
