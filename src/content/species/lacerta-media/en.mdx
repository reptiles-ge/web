commonName: "Caucasian Green Lizard"
location: "Eastern Georgia — Tmogvi, Pantishara; sporadically the northwest"
description: >-
  The Caucasian green lizard is a large, non-venomous lizard of semi-arid eastern Georgia. Juveniles often have three stripes, and the Georgian checklist lists it as a candidate species.
overview: >-
  The Caucasian green lizard (Lacerta media) is a large, non-venomous lizard that lives in Georgia, mainly in the semi-dry east. It belongs to the true lizard family (Lacertidae) and is not dangerous to people. The species name is based on a specimen taken in Georgia, in the Kvabishevi valley near Borjomi, at about 1,025 m. The subspecies that carries the species name is L. m. media. The 2026 Georgian checklist lists it as a separate species, but only as a “candidate”. The zone where it meets lizards of the L. trilineata group has not yet been studied well enough, so its status as a separate species is not final. Globally it is considered “least concern”, and it is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Its English names include Caucasian green lizard, medium lizard, three-lined lizard and Levant green lizard.
habitat: |-
  The Caucasian Green Lizard lives in bushy, semi-arid places: riversides and the edges of gardens and fields. Bare desert is not its home. According to the 2026 checklist, it is widespread in the semi-arid east of Georgia and occurs only here and there in the northwest. Its distribution is not yet fully clear. An older 2009 assessment mentioned only northeastern Georgia, a narrower area. Do not expect it across the whole country or in the humid Colchic forest.

  The atlas map marks two regions with exact records. From Tmogvi village (Aspindza, Samtskhe-Javakheti) there is both a picture of the animal and DNA samples. DNA samples were also taken in Pantishara Gorge, in the Dedoplistskaro area of Kakheti. This does not mean it lives only in these two regions.

  In Iran it avoids dry desert and chooses grassy, shrubby or wooded valleys, field edges and gardens. Its habitats are also said to include woodland with scrub, steppe, stony ground and farmland. In the southern part of its range it is tied to moist places. For the species as a whole, the elevation is often given as about 600–2,000 m. In Dagestan it has been recorded at 840–2,250 m, but that is a Russian measurement, not a limit for Georgia.

  The species was described in 1920 from “green lizards” collected in the Caucasus. The specimens for the description were collected in the Chorokhi valley between Batumi and Artvin, in Borjomi, around Tbilisi, in the Araks valley and elsewhere. According to 2019 data, the main reference specimen for the name comes from the Kvabishevi valley.
diet: >-
  The Caucasian Green Lizard feeds on small invertebrates, mainly arthropods, meaning insects and their relatives. Rarely, it also eats a small vertebrate. This picture comes from other parts of its range and is not an exact menu for every place in Georgia.
behavior: >-
  Active by day, it is a large, fast lizard that lives on the ground. It often basks by bushes, stones or walls. At one highland site in Turkey it is active from mid-May to mid-September. That is the season of that one place only, not a calendar for Georgia. For the species, females are described as laying eggs twice a year, 9–18 at a time. Two clutches a year have also been noted in Armenia. It can breed from about two years of age. If you catch it, its tail may break off, and it may bite you too. So watch it, leave it where it is and do not pick it up.
conservation: >-
  IUCN assessed it as Least Concern in 2009 because of a wide range and a
  presumably large population. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List
  (Resolution No. 190). Georgia's 2026 checklist treats it as a candidate
  taxon: separate status is supported indirectly by phylogeny and parapatry in
  western Anatolia, but the contact zone with the L. trilineata group remains
  unexplored (2013 study). Cytochrome b difference from L. trilineata reaches
  6.5% — that does not confirm species rank in this atlas. It was raised to
  species in 1933; later it was often kept as a subspecies of L. trilineata
  until species rank was restored in 1986. Local threats in Georgia are not
  measured on this page; habitat conversion is an obvious pressure. It is not
  listed on CITES. Do not catch wild lizards.
interaction: >-
  The Caucasian Green Lizard is not venomous and poses no risk to human health. If you pick it up, it may bite, and its tail may break off. So leave it where it is: a wild animal should not be kept at home.

  summary: >-
    A large lizard that lives on the ground. Juveniles often have three stripes running along the body, while adults are often green. It is a candidate species: its recognition as a separate species is not yet final. Don't rely on colour alone to identify it.
  traits:
    - >-
      A robust lizard of the true lizard family, with well-developed legs. Adults are often plain green or olive-green. Females may be more olive-brown, with spots on the sides that fade with age. Adult males studied in Iran no longer have pale lines or spots on the back. Across the species, head-and-body length is often about 10–12 cm, and with the tail it may reach about 40–50 cm. It is neither a rock lizard (Darevskia) nor a skink.
    - >-
      In the group of lizards closely related to L. trilineata, the young often have three stripes running along the body. That is where the English name three-lined lizard comes from. Young Striped Lizards (Lacerta strigata), by contrast, usually have five narrow pale stripes. The stripes fade with age, and green adults are easy to mix up. So do not identify it by colour alone.
    - >-
      The Caucasian Green Lizard is larger than the Striped Lizard (Lacerta strigata). The Striped Lizard is confirmed in Georgia and has been described from semi-arid areas across the country. The Caucasian Green Lizard lives mainly in the semi-arid east and occurs only here and there in the northwest. It is a candidate species: its recognition as a separate species is not yet final. Where both occur, compare their size and the number of stripes on the young.
    - >-
      The Sand Lizard (Lacerta agilis) is smaller than the Caucasian Green Lizard, although its young can also be striped. Still, it does not belong to the group of large green lizards that includes the Caucasian Green Lizard. It is widespread in western Georgia, but in the east it lives only above about 600–800 m. So do not rely on a juvenile's stripes alone, and look at its size too.
    - >-
      The Snake-Eyed Lizard (Ophisops elegans) is smaller than the Caucasian Green Lizard, and its eyelids are fused, so its eye looks lidless. It lives on open, dry ground in the southeast. Rock lizards (Darevskia) are also smaller and are usually found on rocks or walls. None of these lizards is venomous.

  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      It is a large lizard that lives on the ground. Juveniles often have three stripes, and adults are often green. Compare it with the Striped Lizard, whose young usually have five stripes. Colour alone is not enough to identify it.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      No. The Caucasian Green Lizard has no venom and is not dangerous to people. If you try to catch it, it may bite you, and its tail can break off. So don't pick it up; leave it where it is.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      Head and body length is often about 10–12 cm. Including the tail, it can reach about 40–50 cm. These measurements are given for the species as a whole.
  - question: Why is it a candidate species?
    answer: >-
      The Caucasian Green Lizard belongs to a group of lizards closely related to L. trilineata. The zone where it meets other lizards of this group has not yet been studied. In this case, the difference in DNA does not confirm that it is a separate species. It keeps a separate name in the checklist until that zone is studied. In other words, its recognition as a separate species is not yet final.
  - question: How does it differ from the striped lizard?
    answer: >-
      By size and by the stripes on the young. The Caucasian Green Lizard (L. media) is larger and lives mainly in the east. It is a candidate species: its status as a separate species is not yet settled. The Striped Lizard (L. strigata) is confirmed in Georgia and has been recorded in semi-dry places across the country. Young Caucasian Green Lizards have three stripes, while young Striped Lizards have five. Green adults of the two look alike, so do not tell them apart by colour alone.
  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      It is active by day. In other regions, it has been described as active from spring to autumn. That season is not an exact calendar for every part of Georgia.
