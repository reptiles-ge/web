overview: |-
  The European glass lizard (Pseudopus apodus) is not a snake but a large legless lizard of the Anguidae family. It has no venom and poses no danger to people, yet in Georgia it is often mistaken for a snake. It can be [told apart from a snake](/lizards/xvlikis-da-gvelxokeras-gansxvaveba) by its movable eyelids, ear openings and a noticeable fold along the sides of its body. It reaches about one meter in length, and some large ones grow up to 1.35 m.
  A 2026 scientific list confirms that the European glass lizard occurs in Georgia, but lists it only in eastern Georgia. The atlas map also shows publicly reported sightings from Abkhazia and Adjara. However, the list does not confirm that it occurs in western Georgia. Worldwide, its risk of extinction has been assessed as low, and a separate 2021 assessment found the risk low in Georgia too. It is not on the country’s Red List. If you come across one, leave it where it is and do not pick it up: to identify it, take a photo from a safe distance.
habitat: |-
  The European glass lizard lives in warm, open places: fields, sparse woodland, scrub, rocky slopes and river valleys. In eastern Georgia it also lives in the dry and semi-dry parts of the Mtkvari, Iori and Alazani valleys. It is also found in gardens and along the edges of village yards.
diet: >-
  The European glass lizard often eats snails and slugs. Its strong jaws especially help it eat animals with shells. It also catches insects, and sometimes small lizards and other small animals. Because it eats snails and slugs, it may be useful in a garden.
behavior: |-
  In eastern Georgia, the European glass lizard is most active in spring and early summer. It lives on the ground and moves about more by day. The female lays eggs and sometimes stays by the nest to guard them.
  When frightened, it can hiss, bite or shed its tail. This is how it defends itself, and it has nothing to do with venom. Don’t touch a wild animal with your hands — take a photo to identify it.
conservation: |-
  A global assessment found that the European glass lizard faces little threat. A 2021 national assessment found the same for Georgia. It is not on the country’s Red List.
interaction: >-
  It is harmless to people and has no venom. If you see it in your yard or garden, leave it where it is. Do not kill it just because you think it is a snake. If you are unsure what animal it is, keep a safe distance and take a photo without touching it. Call 112 only if there is real danger.

  - question: How is it different from the slow worm?
    answer: >-

The Colchis slow worm is smaller and has a smooth body. Both are nonvenomous lizards, and neither is a snake.
