commonName: Lobed argiope
location: Shida Kartli and Kakheti
description: >-
  A silvery orb-weaver with a lobed abdomen. It is confirmed in Georgia and is
  not a medical risk to people.
overview: >-
  The lobed argiope (Argiope lobata) is an orb-weaving spider in the family
  Araneidae. The accepted name is Argiope lobata (Pallas, 1772); the original
  combination was Aranea lobata. The World Spider Catalog treats the species as
  accepted and as the type species of Argiope. There is no widely established
  Georgian vernacular; this page uses the descriptive name ლობებიანი არგიოპა,
  from the lateral lobes on the abdomen. English names include lobed argiope and
  black-lobed garden orb-web spider. At species level Nentwig et al. give female
  body length as 16.8–25.5 mm and male as 5.2–7.5 mm. Otto 2022 lists the
  species for Georgia. That is country-level confirmation, not even density in
  all twelve regions. It has venom for insect prey; it is not medically
  significant for people. IUCN has not assessed the species. It is not on
  Georgia’s 2014 Red List; the arachnid on that list is the fen raft spider
  (Dolomedes plantarius).
habitat: >-
  At species level Nentwig et al. give habitat as grass in very warm places.
  Otto links it in the Caucasus to dry shrubland and grassland — the more
  continental, low-rainfall east, not the humid Colchic lowland. That is an
  ecoregion-level description, not a measured Georgian elevation belt. This
  atlas colours regions only from named records. Seropian et al. 2023 publish
  one female south of Lake Nadarbazevi, east of Gori (N41.97820°, E44.28510°;
  950 m; 13 September 2020; Iankoshvili; CaBOL-ID 1004216) — Shida Kartli on
  this atlas map. Two specimens in the Zoological Research Museum Alexander
  Koenig (ZFMK), identified by Krammer, are labelled Vashlovani National Park
  entrance, Kakheti (GBIF; no date on those records). Other regions are not
  coloured on this page. GBIF also aggregates citizen-science points elsewhere;
  this atlas does not colour regions from those points. Photos on this page were
  not taken in Georgia.
diet: >-
  There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page. At species level the
  female takes flying and jumping insects in the orb web. Prey is wrapped, then
  bitten. That is the general trophic niche, not a measured Georgian menu. Do
  not treat it as biological control in a garden.
behavior: >-
  By day the female often sits in the web centre, head down. In the genus
  Argiope the hub often has a white zigzag of silk — a stabilimentum; its
  function is not fully settled. At species level adult females are obvious in
  the warm season. A named Georgian date is 13 September 2020 at Nadarbazevi. A
  full national calendar is not measured here. The male is much smaller and the
  abdomen is not lobed laterally (Nentwig et al.). Do not pick it up.
conservation: >-
  IUCN has not assessed the species. It is not on Government Resolution No. 190
  of 2014; the official Red List arachnid is Dolomedes plantarius (VU). Otto
  2015 treated Caucasus records as abundant (28 records across the North
  Caucasus, Georgia and Azerbaijan together); that is not a Georgian population
  trend. In Armenia the genus Argiope was first confirmed by Kosyan 2022 — a
  neighbouring country, not a Georgian range. Do not catch wild spiders.
interaction: >-
  The spider has venom for prey. It is not a medical risk to people: bites are
  rare and usually only local pain or redness. This is not a viper bite and not
  a widow (Latrodectus) bite. An allergic reaction to any bite is possible; if
  breathing, the face, or general condition worsens, call 112. Do not handle it.
  A web in dry grass or low shrub in summer–autumn can be typical; destroy it
  only if it blocks a path — the spider is not a pest.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A large orb-weaver with a silvery abdomen that has lateral lobes. The female
    is much larger than the male; the male abdomen is not lobed.
  traits:
    - >-
      At species level female body length is 16.8–25.5 mm (Nentwig et al.); male
      5.2–7.5 mm. Legs are not included. There is no separate Georgian
      measurement series on this page.
    - >-
      The female cephalothorax (prosoma) is shiny silver. The abdomen
      (opisthosoma) is dorsally silver-grey, with lateral lobes — the main field
      character. Around the lobes there may be brownish contrast. The underside
      is darker. Legs are yellowish-brown with black rings. The male is more
      uniformly yellowish, and the abdomen is not lobed at the sides.
    - >-
      The web is an orb, often in grass or low shrub. The hub may have a white
      zigzag (stabilimentum). An adult female often sits head-down in the
      centre.
    - >-
      The same genus in Georgia includes the wasp spider (Argiope bruennichi).
      That species has yellow-and-black cross-bands and no lateral lobes. Garden
      orb-weavers such as Araneus have a different mark, often a cross, not
      these lobes. Dry shrubland may also hold widow spiders (Latrodectus) —
      smaller, with a rounded abdomen and no lobes; this atlas has no widow
      profile yet.
facts:

    The accepted name is Argiope lobata (Pallas, 1772). The World Spider Catalog
    treats the species as accepted and as the type species of the genus. Range:
    southern Europe to Central Asia and China, northern Africa, Tanzania, South
    Africa, the Middle East, Pakistan, India, Myanmar.

    that source. Otto 2015 counted 28 Caucasus records (Russia / Georgia /
    Azerbaijan; Armenia was not then included). Seropian et al. 2023 publish the
    Nadarbazevi specimen; ZFMK holds Vashlovani specimens.

faq:
  - question: Is the lobed argiope found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Otto 2022 lists the species for Georgia. That is country-level
      confirmation.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Named records are south of Lake Nadarbazevi east of Gori (Shida Kartli,
      950 m, 13 September 2020) and the Vashlovani park entrance (Kakheti; ZFMK
      specimens). Look in warm, dry grass and shrub. Other regions are not
      coloured on this page.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      The female has a silvery abdomen with lateral lobes. The male is much
      smaller and not lobed. In Georgia the similar species is the wasp spider
      (Argiope bruennichi), which has yellow-and-black bands and no lobes.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      At species level the female is about 17–26 mm, the male about 5–8 mm. Legs
      are not included. There is no Georgian series on this page.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      It has venom for insects. It is not a medical risk to people. Bites are
      rare and usually local. Do not handle it. Call 112 if serious symptoms
      appear.
  - question: When is it visible?
    answer: >-
      Adult females are obvious in the warm season at species level. A named
      Georgian record is September at Nadarbazevi. A full national calendar is
      not on this page.

    answer: >-
