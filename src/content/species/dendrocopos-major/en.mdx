commonName: Great spotted woodpecker
location: Year-round resident in Georgia
description: >-
  A pied black, white and red woodpecker with large white shoulder patches.
  Confirmed in Georgia as a year-round resident and breeder on the 2024 national
  bird checklist.
overview: >-
  The great spotted woodpecker (Dendrocopos major) is a black-and-white bird with
  large white shoulder patches and a red vent. It breeds in Georgia and is
  present year-round, as confirmed by the 2024 national bird checklist. Look
  for it in woodland, parks and gardens with trees. It is not a threat to people.
habitat: >-
  Great spotted woodpeckers live in deciduous, coniferous and mixed woodland;
  they also occur in parks and gardens with trees. A 2022 report on a survey in
  Georgia recorded the species in Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park and Pshav-Khevsureti
  Protected Areas. It was the most frequently recorded woodpecker in both areas.
  The study found no association with one particular forest type, though the
  birds avoided areas with sparse vegetation.
diet: >-
  It feeds mainly on insects and their larvae, searching for them beneath tree
  bark. It also eats nuts and seeds, particularly in winter; to extract seeds
  from a conifer cone, it often wedges the cone into a tree crevice and prises it
  open with its bill. It sometimes eats other birds' eggs and chicks.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day, and its sharp “kik” or “tchick” is often heard before the
  bird is seen. In spring it also drums rapidly on trees to mark its territory
  and attract a mate. Its flight rises and dips. It excavates its own nest
  cavity in a tree. Do not handle the bird.
conservation: >-
  The species is assessed globally as Least Concern (LC) by IUCN. The 2024
  Georgian bird checklist also gives it a national LC category; it is not on
  Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Old and dead trees provide places to feed and
  excavate nest cavities. Do not catch wild birds.
interaction: >-
  It is not a threat to people; wild birds must not be caught.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A pied woodpecker with large white shoulder patches and a red vent. Adult
    males have a red nape patch; adult females do not. It most closely resembles
    the Syrian woodpecker.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length about 22–23 cm and wingspan 34–39 cm. The back is black with large
      white shoulder patches; the wings and tail sides are
      barred black and white. The underparts are white with a red vent. The bill
      is straight and chisel-like. A black cheek stripe links the bill base,
      throat and nape.
    - >-
      Sex is marked on the head: adult males have a red nape patch; adult females
      have an all-black hindcrown. Juveniles have a red crown, more barred
      shoulder patches and a pinker vent.
    - >-
      Syrian woodpecker (Dendrocopos syriacus) is also a year-round resident in
      Georgia on the 2024 checklist and looks most similar. Its black cheek
      stripe does not join the nape, so the white cheek extends onto the neck.
      In young great spotted woodpeckers this black stripe can be incomplete;
      do not rely on that mark alone. Middle
      spotted woodpecker (Dendrocoptes medius) has a red crown as an adult and
      lacks the large white shoulder patches. Lesser spotted woodpecker
      (Dryobates minor) is much smaller and has no red
      vent. This atlas does not yet have separate pages for those species.
    - >-
      [European green woodpecker](picus-viridis) is a year-round resident in
      Georgia on the 2024 checklist: larger, olive-green, with a red crown. This
      atlas has a page for that species. Grey-headed woodpecker (Picus canus) is
      also a resident: grey-headed and green-backed. Black woodpecker (Dryocopus
      martius) is large and black. [Eurasian wryneck](jynx-torquilla) is small,
      cryptically brown, and a breeding migrant, not a pied resident; this atlas
      has a page for that species.
facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758 as Picus major. The 2024 Georgian checklist
    follows the OSME Region List for names: order Piciformes, family Picidae,
    genus Dendrocopos. IOC World Bird List treats Dendrocopos major as a
    species.
  - >-
    NPLG names the form in Georgia as Caucasian D. m. tenuirostris Buturlin,
    1906; Avibase gives the type locality as Kutaisi and Akhaltsikhe. IOC treats
    tenuirostris as the Caucasian subspecies. Birds of the World / Winkler often
    lumps tenuirostris into pinetorum, whose range includes the Caucasus. Field
    identification is to species.
  - >-
    Georgian is დიდი ჭრელი კოდალა. English names in use are Great Spotted
    Woodpecker (2024 checklist) and Greater Spotted Woodpecker (Ilia State
    University SPA checklist). Russian is большой пёстрый дятел.
faq:
  - question: Is the great spotted woodpecker found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia lists it as a
      confirmed year-round resident (YR-R): a breeder present throughout the
      year, with photographs, sound recordings and ringing data.
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In woodland, parks and gardens with trees. A survey in Georgia recorded it
      in Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park and Pshav-Khevsureti Protected Areas.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      A pied woodpecker with large white shoulder patches and a red vent. Males
      have a red nape patch; females do not. Juveniles have a red crown. In
      Syrian woodpecker the black cheek stripe does not join the nape, though
      this mark alone is unreliable in young great spotted woodpeckers.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 22–23 cm long, with a wingspan of 34–39 cm.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Mainly insects and their larvae. It also eats seeds, including those it
      extracts from conifer cones in winter.
  - question: When is it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      All year, according to the 2024 checklist. It is not treated there as a
      passage migrant or winter visitor only.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 Georgian bird checklist lists it as a breeder present
      year-round.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It is harmless. Globally it is Least Concern, and the 2024 checklist codes
      it LC nationally. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Do not catch wild
      birds.
