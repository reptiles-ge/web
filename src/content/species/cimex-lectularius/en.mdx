overview: |-
  The bed bug, also called the common bed bug, is a blood-feeding insect in the Cimicidae family. An adult is about 5–7 mm long. It has no wings, and its body is flat and oval before feeding. Its bite may cause itching, but current evidence does not indicate that it effectively transmits diseases to humans.
  Its presence in Georgia has been confirmed. A 2023 paper describes specimens collected on 30 May 2022 from the Gremi church in Kvareli Municipality and a church near the village of Pichkhovani. Both records concern old buildings associated with bat colonies. These two sites cannot show how bed bugs are distributed in homes across the country. Citing older sources, the paper also notes that bed bugs occur in human dwellings in Georgia, but it does not establish how common they are in different regions.
habitat: |-
  Groups living close to people usually hide near places where people sleep or rest: in mattress seams, bed frames and headboards, furniture joints, and cracks in walls. This is generally characteristic of the species; it is not a finding from a new study of homes in Georgia.
  Specimens described in a study published in 2022 were found near bat colonies in old churches in Georgia. One site was Gremi Church in Kvareli Municipality; the other was a church near the village of Pichkhovani. The researchers also searched caves but found no specimens there. These sources do not establish the range of elevations where the species occurs in Georgia or how widespread it is in residential buildings.
diet: >-
  Adults and young insects at every stage of development feed on the blood of warm-blooded animals. Groups associated with humans often feed on sleeping people. This insect can also feed on the blood of other mammals and birds. Insects described in reports published in Georgia were associated with groups of bats living together, but it is not yet known which bat they feed on in Gremi.
behavior: >-
  It usually comes out of hiding at night, though it may also be active during the day. The pale insect that emerges from the egg goes through five stages before becoming an adult. It needs to feed on blood to move on to each next stage. The egg is about 1 mm. This describes its development in general; it has not been established when it goes through these stages during the year in Georgia.
conservation: >-
  This species is a parasite of people and other warm-blooded hosts and is
  treated as a household pest. The consulted evidence does not support assigning
  it an invasive or conservation category in Georgia; national status needs
  separate verification.
interaction: |-
  Bites can cause itching and disrupt sleep. In rare cases, they can cause a severe allergic reaction. A skin rash alone does not confirm the presence of bedbugs. Check gaps in the mattress and bed for live insects, shed skins, eggs or small dark spots. A clean home does not necessarily mean there are no bedbugs.
  If you find a specimen, keep it for identification and contact a pest control specialist. Using chemicals at random is no substitute for identifying the insect accurately. Call 112 if you have a severe allergic reaction or trouble breathing.

identification:
  summary: >-
    An adult has six legs, two antennae and a brownish abdomen with clearly visible segments. Before feeding, its body is flattened from top to bottom. After feeding on blood, it swells and darkens. You cannot identify the insect species from the bite alone.
  traits:
    - >-
      An adult is usually 5–7 mm in size and cannot fly. The small, immature insects are yellowish-white and can be hard to spot. The eggs are light-colored and about 1 mm in size.
    - >-
      Check the mattress seams, the joints of the bed frame, and nearby cracks. Look for the insect itself or signs of it. Dark spots, shed skins, and eggs add to the suspicion; marks on the skin alone are not a reliable sign.
    - >-
      The [brown marmorated stink bug](halyomorpha-halys) is a different insect: it is larger, has a shield-shaped body, feeds on plants and does not suck human blood. An adult tick has eight legs; a bed bug has six. Closely related Cimicidae species look very similar, so identifying the exact species sometimes requires a specialist.
