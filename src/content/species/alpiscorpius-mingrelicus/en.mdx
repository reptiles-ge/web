overview: >-
  The Mingrelian scorpion (Alpiscorpius mingrelicus) is a true scorpion, not an
  insect and not a spider. It belongs to the arachnid class (Arachnida), the
  order Scorpiones, and the family Euscorpiidae. Older sources often list it as
  Euscorpius mingrelicus.

  In modern taxonomy, this name no longer means the old broad “Euscorpius
  mingrelicus” complex. The 2015 revision restricted Euscorpius mingrelicus
  sensu stricto to Georgia and Turkey, while the Scorpion Files revision summary
  links the range to western Georgia, northern Turkey, and the adjacent
  south-western Russian area. The 2019 paper elevated Alpiscorpius to genus
  rank, so the accepted name used here is Alpiscorpius mingrelicus.

  For Georgia, the defensible takeaway is presence in western Georgia. Public
  GBIF/iNaturalist records also point to places in western Georgia, but such
  points should not automatically be used to fill an administrative distribution
  map. This page therefore does not expand the mapped range from unverified
  occurrence points.

  It is a small, dark scorpion. The Scorpion Files gives adult length up to
  about 38 mm. It has venom, but available information does not treat it as a
  high medical-risk scorpion for people; a sting may still be painful, so there
  is no reason to handle it.
habitat: >-
  Detailed, region-by-region habitat data for Georgia are still insufficient.
  The Scorpion Files associates the species with humid, mountainous and forest
  habitat; scorpions often hide by day under stones, logs, dead bark, or other
  cover.

  This differs from the dry, open and rocky settings associated with the mottled
  scorpion (Mesobuthus eupeus). If a scorpion is found in a humid forested
  valley in western Georgia, Alpiscorpius mingrelicus is a more plausible
  candidate, but habitat alone is not an identification.

behavior: >-
  The Mingrelian scorpion is associated with a secretive way of life. Encounters
  are more likely when stones, logs, bark, or other natural cover are disturbed,
  especially in humid forested places.

  Do not pick it up by hand. If it must be moved from a house or yard, avoid
  direct contact and use a container and stiff card, or ask a specialist. For
  identification, sharp photos from above and the side, plus the exact locality,
  are most useful.

interaction: >-
  The Mingrelian scorpion is venomous in the biological sense that it uses venom
  on prey. According to the Scorpion Files, expected effects in
  Euscorpius/Alpiscorpius-type scorpions are mainly local, and the species is
  not treated as a high-risk scorpion for people.

  Even so, a sting can be painful. Do not handle the scorpion and do not let
  children or pets play with it. If pain is strong, the affected person is a
  child, allergic or other systemic symptoms develop, or the situation is
  unclear, call 112 and seek medical care.

  - >-
