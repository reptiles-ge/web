commonName: European pied flycatcher
location: Passage migrant in Georgia
description: >-
  A small black-and-white flycatcher with a bold white wing patch. Confirmed in
  Georgia as a passage migrant on the 2024 national bird checklist; it is not
  listed as a breeding bird. Named photographs on this page are from
  Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park, 31 May 2015.
overview: >-
  The European pied flycatcher (Ficedula hypoleuca) is a small Old World
  flycatcher (family Muscicapidae) confirmed in Georgia as a passage migrant.
  The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia codes it M: a bird of
  passage, present mainly in spring and autumn. Seasonality is classed as
  high-quality (code A). Occurrence quality is high (H), with photographs. It is
  not coded BB (breeding bird). Adults are about 12–13.5 cm long at species
  level. It is not a threat to people. IUCN lists the species as Least Concern.
  The 2024 checklist assigns the same category nationally.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia look for it on spring and autumn passage, not as a summer nesting
  bird and not in winter. Named record on this page: Borjomi-Kharagauli National
  Park, 31 May 2015 (photographs by ზაური ხაჩიძე). The 2024 checklist confirms
  country-level migrant status but does not publish a regional gazetteer. Do not
  assume it occurs evenly across Georgia. Species-level habitat on migration is
  woodland and edge: deciduous or mixed forest, parks, gardens, scrub, and
  similar trees where flying insects can be taken from a perch. A separate
  national elevation band for Georgia is not published here.
diet: >-
  There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page. Across the range it
  takes insects and other small invertebrates, often by sallying from a perch
  into the air or foliage. Caterpillars and other prey are taken in the breeding
  range; that is species-level feeding, not a measured Georgian menu.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. The male’s song, on the breeding grounds, is a short
  whistled phrase of repeated notes; calls include a sharp “whit” or “witt”.
  Full song is less the expected cue on Georgian passage than a small flycatcher
  hawking insects from a tree. It is a long-distance migrant: it breeds across
  much of Europe and western Siberia and winters mainly in tropical Africa.
  Georgia lies on passage, not on the mapped breeding core of the species.
  Cavity nesting, nest boxes, and typical clutches of about five to seven eggs
  with incubation of about 13–15 days are species-level facts from the breeding
  range. The 2024 checklist does not treat the species as a breeder in Georgia.
  The late-May photographs from Borjomi-Kharagauli fall on spring passage.
conservation: >-
  BirdLife International assesses Ficedula hypoleuca as Least Concern on the
  IUCN Red List. Parts of the European population have declined, but the global
  range is large and the species does not meet a higher threat category.
  EUNIS reports Least Concern in Europe and notes EU Birds Directive coverage;
  that instrument does not apply in Georgia. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red
  List (Government Resolution No. 190). The 2024 Georgian bird checklist assigns
  global and national Red List category LC. A Georgia-specific population trend
  is not quantified on this page. Do not catch or keep wild birds.
interaction: >-
  It is not a threat to people; wild birds must not be captured.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A sparrow-sized flycatcher with a large white wing patch. Breeding males are
    black above and white below, without a full white collar. In Georgia it is a
    passage migrant on the 2024 checklist. The usual confusion is semi-collared
    and collared flycatcher.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length about 12–13.5 cm and wingspan about 21–24 cm at species level. Mass
      is often given around 10–15 g. The bill is fine and dark. Both sexes show
      a conspicuous white patch on the closed wing. Breeding males are typically
      black on the head and back, white below, with a white forehead spot; they
      lack a complete white neck-collar. The rump is dark, not a broad white
      saddle. Some males are brownish rather than jet-black; that is a known
      plumage variant, not a different species.
    - >-
      Adult females and many autumn birds are olive-brown or grey-brown above
      and paler below, still with a clear white wing patch and no rusty breast.
      Juveniles are similar to females, often with paler spotting. In Georgia,
      most encounters are likely to be brown birds on passage, not black-and-white
      spring males. The call is a short, sharp note; this page does not treat
      call as a standalone identification in mixed Ficedula flocks.
    - >-
      Semi-collared flycatcher (Ficedula semitorquata) is the black-and-white
      Ficedula coded as a breeding bird and migrant (BB; M) on the 2024 Georgian
      checklist. Breeding males show a broken or incomplete white collar and
      usually a second pale wing-bar of white-tipped median coverts; they also
      tend to show more white on the tail. Collared flycatcher (Ficedula
      albicollis) is also a passage migrant (M) in Georgia: breeding males have
      a complete white collar and a large white rump. Females and autumn birds
      of all three are difficult; a brown flycatcher on passage in Georgia should
      not be named to species from a poor view. Red-breasted flycatcher
      (Ficedula parva) is a breeding migrant (BB; M): adult males have a rusty
      throat, not a pied back. Spotted flycatcher (Muscicapa striata) is also a
      breeding migrant: streaked grey-brown, without a large white wing patch.
      This atlas has a page for semi-collared flycatcher; it does not yet have
      separate pages for collared, red-breasted, or spotted flycatcher.
    - >-
      It is not a threat to people. Leave it alone. A black-and-white flycatcher
      in Georgian woodland in June is more likely semi-collared than this
      species, on the 2024 checklist.
facts:
  - >-
    Described by Pallas in 1764 (as Motacilla hypoleuca). The 2024 Georgian
    checklist follows the OSME Region List for names: order Passeriformes,
    family Muscicapidae, genus Ficedula. IOC World Bird List treats Atlas
    flycatcher (Ficedula speculigera) as a separate species; it is not on the
    Georgian list.
  - >-
    Widely recognised subspecies include F. h. hypoleuca (much of Europe),
    F. h. iberiae (Iberian Peninsula), and F. h. sibirica (western and
    south-central Siberia). Field identification in Georgia is to species. A
    measured Georgian specimen series assigned to subspecies is not given on
    this page.
  - >-
    Georgian names in use are ჭრელი მემატლია and ჭრელი ბუზიჭერია. English names
    include European pied flycatcher (2024 checklist: European Pied Flycatcher),
    pied flycatcher, and western pied flycatcher. Older scientific synonyms
    include Muscicapa hypoleuca and Ficedula atricapilla.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: M, data quality A, global and national Red List LC,
    occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph). That is country-level
    confirmation of passage, not a regional map and not a breeding record.
    Named photographic evidence on this page is Borjomi-Kharagauli National
    Park, 31 May 2015. Some Georgian popular sources list it among breeders;
    that conflicts with
    the 2024 checklist, which codes breeding to semi-collared flycatcher and
    codes this species only as M.
faq:
  - question: Is the European pied flycatcher found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia lists it as a
      confirmed passage migrant (M), with photographs. It is not listed there as
      a breeding bird.
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      On spring and autumn passage, in woodland, parks, and other trees with
      flying insects. Named photographs on this page are from
      Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park on 31 May 2015. This page does not map
      every region.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Small flycatcher with a large white wing patch. Breeding males are black
      above and white below, without a full white collar. Females and autumn
      birds are brown with the same wing patch. Semi-collared and collared
      flycatchers are the usual confusion; both also occur in Georgia.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 12–13.5 cm long at species level, with a wingspan of about 21–24 cm
      and mass often around 10–15 g. A separate measured series from Georgia is
      not given on this page.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Insects and other small invertebrates, often caught in short flights from
      a perch. Diet has not been studied separately in Georgia on this page.
  - question: When is it present in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Mainly in spring and autumn, according to the 2024 checklist definition of
      M. It is not treated there as a winter visitor or as a summer breeder.
      Exact arrival and departure dates for Georgia are not tabulated on this
      page. The photographs here are from 31 May.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Not on the 2024 checklist, which codes it only as a migrant (M). The
      black-and-white Ficedula listed as a breeding bird in Georgia on that
      list is semi-collared flycatcher (Ficedula semitorquata).
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It is harmless. It is globally Least Concern and is coded LC nationally
      on the 2024 checklist. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Do not catch
      wild birds.
