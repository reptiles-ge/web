commonName: Griffon vulture
location: Year-round resident and migrant in Georgia
description: >-
  A large pale-bodied vulture with dark flight feathers and a short tail. Confirmed
  in Georgia as a breeding resident and a migrant on the 2024 national bird
  checklist. Named colonies are documented in Vashlovani and Kazbegi.
overview: >-
  The griffon vulture (Gyps fulvus) is a large scavenging bird that nests in
  colonies on cliffs. It breeds in Georgia and is present year-round; migrants
  are also recorded. Its pale brown body contrasts with dark flight feathers;
  it also has a short tail. It poses no threat to people in an ordinary encounter, but
  keep away from nests and colonies. It is globally Least Concern according to
  IUCN and Vulnerable on Georgia’s current Red List.
habitat: >-
  Breeding colonies in Georgia are documented in Vashlovani Protected Areas,
  Kakheti, and Kazbegi National Park, Mtskheta–Mtianeti. Documented sites in
  Vashlovani include Eagle Canyon and the Khornabuji cliffs; in Kazbegi they
  include Dariali Gorge near Gveleti and the area around Mount Kabarjini.
  Griffon vultures nest on cliffs and forage over open ground. A Caucasian
  study found that occupied nesting cliffs were associated with less rainfall,
  more open ground and more dead livestock nearby. Autumn passage has also been
  recorded near Batumi; this does not indicate breeding there.
diet: >-
  Griffon vultures feed mainly on carcasses of large domestic and wild mammals;
  they do not normally hunt large live animals. Poisoned carcasses endanger
  them and other scavengers. Do not lure wild birds with food.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day and nests in small colonies on cliffs. Adults stay near
  colonies year-round; young birds mostly move south after breeding and return
  in spring. Courtship is mainly in December–January, and eggs are laid from
  February to April. A pair usually has one egg, which both parents incubate.
  Published incubation periods range from 48 to 65 days; young leave the nest
  97–136 days after hatching. It is rarely heard in flight, but hisses and low calls
  can be heard at carcasses and nests. Keep away from colonies.
conservation: >-
  Globally, the griffon vulture was assessed as Least Concern (LC) by IUCN in
  2021. Georgia’s current 2014 Red List classifies it as Vulnerable (VU, D1,
  for a small population). The 2024 bird checklist gives it a national category
  of NT, but this comes from a draft update that has not been officially
  adopted and does not replace the current list. An annex to the European Red
  List of Birds estimated 120–1,300 breeding pairs in Georgia for 2013–2017.
  This wide range was based on limited data, not a complete census. Poisoning
  and disturbance at nests are potential threats to vultures. Do not catch or
  kill them.
interaction: >-
  It poses no threat to people in an ordinary encounter, but do not handle the
  bird. Keep away from nests and colonies. The griffon vulture is on Georgia’s
  Red List; do not catch or kill it. Poisoned bait endangers scavengers.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A large pale-bodied vulture with contrasting dark flight feathers, a short
    tail and a pale ruff in adults. Confirmed in Georgia as a breeding resident
    and a migrant on the 2024 checklist. Usual confusion is with cinereous,
    bearded and Egyptian vultures and with golden eagle.
  traits:
    - >-
      Body length is about 93–122 cm and wingspan 2.3–2.8 m. The head and neck
      are covered in short down, and adults have a pale neck ruff. The body is
      pale sandy brown, while flight and tail feathers are dark. In flight,
      long, broad wings with spread tips and a short tail stand out. The wings
      are often held in a shallow V. Males and females look alike; females are
      generally larger.
    - >-
      Young birds have a browner ruff and more mottled upperparts. Adult plumage
      develops over several years.
    - >-
      Cinereous vulture (Aegypius monachus) is uniformly dark,
      with a short wedge-shaped tail, and often soars with flatter wings. Bearded
      vulture (Gypaetus barbatus) has narrower wings, a long diamond-shaped
      tail, and a rusty body with a black “beard” in adults. Egyptian vulture
      (Neophron percnopterus) is much smaller; adults are white with black
      flight feathers and a yellow face. Golden eagle (Aquila chrysaetos) has a
      longer tail, golden nape and feathered legs; it hunts live prey.
    - >-
      A large pale bird with dark flight feathers near a cliff colony is more
      likely a griffon vulture than an eagle. Keep away from colonies.
facts:
  - >-
    Hablizl described the species as Vultur fulvus in 1783; its accepted name
    is now Gyps fulvus. The National Parliamentary Library of Georgia identifies
    the form found in Georgia as G. f. fulvus. Appearance permits identification
    to species level.
  - >-
    Georgia’s Red List names the species ორბი. ჩვეულებრივი ორბი also appears in
    dictionaries from the National Parliamentary Library and Ilia State
    University. English names include Griffon Vulture and Eurasian Griffon
    Vulture.
