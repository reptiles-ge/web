commonName: Grass snake
location: Abkhazia, Samegrelo — Zemo Svaneti, Guria, Adjara, Imereti, Samtskhe — Javakheti, Mtskheta — Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti, Tbilisi
description: >-
  The grass snake is non-venomous and is not a viper. You can recognise it by the yellow or pale collar behind its head, and in Georgia it has been seen from Abkhazia to Kakheti.
overview: |-
  The grass snake (Natrix natrix) is a non-venomous snake found both in water and on land. You can recognise it most easily by the yellow, white or cream “collar” behind its head, though in dark snakes the collar may be faint. Its presence in Georgia is confirmed in the 2026 checklist: it has been seen in many regions from Abkhazia to Kakheti, and in Tbilisi too. Its risk of extinction is considered low, and it is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. In western Georgia the grass snake is often mistaken for the [Caucasus viper](vipera-kaznakovi), and near water for the [dice snake](natrix-tessellata) (Natrix tessellata).

  Grass snakes living in Georgia belong to one group within the species, Natrix natrix scutata. Striped grass snakes, once called N. n. persa, are now included in the same group. The “large-headed grass snake” (Natrix megalocephala) was once considered a separate species, but it no longer is.
habitat: |-
  The grass snake most often lives by marshes, lakes and streams, in riverside floodplain woods and meadows, and in damp forests. Compared with the dice snake, it more often chooses cool, shady places. In Colchic forest it has also been seen up in trees, including while warming itself in the sun or hunting a tree frog. It may also live in gardens, parks and farmland if there are damp hiding places and enough animals that live both in water and on land.

  The atlas map marks Abkhazia, Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti, Guria, Adjara, Imereti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti and Tbilisi. One record is enough to mark a region, so this does not mean it lives everywhere across each region. In eastern Georgia, one place named in the 2026 checklist is the village of Tushurebi in Tianeti Municipality, Mtskheta-Mtianeti.
diet: >-
  It mainly eats frogs and toads. It also catches fish, but relies on fish less than the dice snake does. It hunts both in water and on land, and when looking for toads it sometimes goes quite far from water. It does not strangle its prey but swallows it alive.
behavior: |-
  It is active in the warm season and moves mostly during the day. In very hot weather, it may also move in the evening or at night. In the western lowlands, its active season lasts longer than in high mountains.

  In general, it mates in spring. In summer, the female usually lays about 8–40 eggs in a warm, moist place, such as rotting plants or compost. The young hatch in late summer or early autumn.

  When threatened, it may release a strong-smelling liquid from the opening under the tail, flatten its head or play dead. It does not chase people and usually avoids them. Even so, do not pick it up.
conservation: >-
  The grass snake is assessed as being at low risk of extinction. In the humid west, it is common in some areas. It is not on Georgia's 2014 Red List, but it is listed in Appendix III of the Bern Convention. In some places, it may be threatened by the loss of marshes and other damp areas, water pollution and deaths on roads. Another possible threat is that people mistake it for a venomous snake and kill it.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The grass snake is a non-venomous snake that spends time both in water and on land. You can recognize it most easily by the yellow or light-colored “collar” behind its head and its round pupils. In western Georgia it may be mistaken for the Caucasus viper, and near water for the dice snake.
  traits:
    - >-
      One of the easiest ways to recognize this snake is the “collar” that often shows behind its head. The collar is made up of two pale spots. They are half-moon shaped and yellow, white or cream, with a dark area just behind them. In very dark grass snakes, the collar may look faint or may not be visible at all. So if the snake is very dark, do not rely on this one feature alone to identify it.
    - >-
      The back may be greenish, gray or brownish. In Colchis, very dark, almost black grass snakes also occur. Each back scale has a small raised line down the middle, and the pupil is round. Some have two light stripes running lengthwise along the back. These striped snakes were once known as “persa”. They have also been seen around Paliastomi.
    - >-
      In Georgia, it is a medium-sized snake. It is usually about 70–120 cm long, and females are larger than males. In other parts of Europe, grass snakes longer than 1.5 m are rarely found, but in Georgia such a length is not typical for this snake. The head stands out only slightly from the body and is not broad and sharply triangular like a viper's.
    - >-
      The dice snake (Natrix tessellata) has no light-colored “collar” behind its head, and its back often shows spots arranged like a chessboard. It is more often found in open water or near water and feeds mainly on fish. In Georgia, it may be easier to spot in semi-dry areas. The grass snake more often hunts frogs and similar animals that live both in water and on land. It is also found in damp, shady places. If a snake has a light collar behind its head, it is not a dice snake.
    - >-
      The Caucasus viper is venomous. Its body is shorter and thicker, and its pupil is elongated from top to bottom. It lacks the pale “collar” near the neck that is typical of the grass snake. Young [Aesculapian snakes](zamenis-longissimus) may have pale spots near the neck, but a dark stripe runs back from the eye and they have dark markings on the head. The [smooth snake](coronella-austriaca) has smooth scales and no such mark near the neck. The [European glass lizard](pseudopus-apodus) is [not a snake at all](/lizards/xvlikis-da-gvelxokeras-gansxvaveba): it has movable eyelids and ear openings. Do not pick up an unknown snake.

faq:
  - question: Is the grass snake venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. This snake is not venomous and is not dangerous to people. When threatened, it may release a strong-smelling liquid, flatten its head or play dead. Still, do not pick it up, especially in western Georgia. There, from a distance, it is not always easy to tell apart from the Caucasus viper.
  - question: How does it differ from the dice snake?
    answer: >-
      First, look for a “collar”. The grass snake often has a light-colored “collar” behind its head, while the dice snake has no such “collar”. Instead, the dice snake often has spots on its back that look like a chessboard pattern. The two snakes also differ in where they live and what they eat. The grass snake prefers damp, shady places, and its main food is animals that live both in water and on land. The dice snake prefers to stay in or near water, and its main food is fish. So if a snake has a light “collar” behind its head, it is not a dice snake.
  - question: How can I tell it from a viper?
    answer: >-
      Look at three things: the “collar,” the pupil and the body. The grass snake has a yellow, white or cream-colored “collar” behind the head, a round pupil and a relatively slender body. In Colchis it can be very dark, even almost black. The Caucasus viper has no such “collar.” Its body is shorter and thicker, and its pupil is elongated from top to bottom. [When identifying an unknown snake](/snakes/shxamiani-gvelis-amocnoba), do not rely on just one feature.
  - question: Is the grass snake found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes, the grass snake occurs in Georgia. The 2026 Georgian list records it in Abkhazia, Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti, Guria, Adjara, Imereti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti and Tbilisi.
  - question: Where might I encounter it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Georgia, the grass snake has been recorded in Abkhazia, Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti, Guria, Adjara, Imereti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti and Tbilisi. You may see it near marshes, lakes, streams and rivers, and in damp forests.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      The grass snake mostly eats frogs and toads. It also catches fish, but less often than the dice snake. It swallows its prey alive.
  - question: Does it lay eggs or give live birth?
    answer: >-
      The grass snake reproduces by laying eggs. It generally mates in spring. In summer, the female usually lays about 8–40 eggs in a warm, damp place. The young hatch in late summer or early autumn.
  - question: What should I do if one comes into the yard?
    answer: >-
      If you [find a snake in your yard](/snakes-in-the-yard), keep your distance and do not pick it up. The grass snake is non-venomous and is not dangerous to people. If you do not know which snake it is, do not go near it; calmly let it move away on its own.
