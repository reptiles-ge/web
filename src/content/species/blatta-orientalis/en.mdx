overview: |-
  The black cockroach (Blatta orientalis) is an insect in the cockroach order (Blattodea) and the Blattidae family. Adults are about 22–27 mm long and range from dark brown to almost black. Males have short wings, while females have only small wing buds. Neither males nor females can fly. Older sources may also call it Periplaneta orientalis; its currently accepted scientific name is Blatta orientalis.
  The black cockroach has been recorded in Georgia. The map shows 75 observations from Georgia posted on iNaturalist and five photo records from the Reptiles.ge gallery that include location coordinates. According to the regional table, its presence is confirmed in Tbilisi, Kakheti, Abkhazia and Adjara. There are also records from Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, and Racha-Lechkhumi and Kvemo Svaneti, but the table does not count its presence in those regions as confirmed. This distinction is based on the number of records on the map, not on a systematic study of the species’ population.

interaction: |-
  The black cockroach is not venomous. There are other risks: tiny pieces of cockroaches’ bodies and their droppings can make allergies or asthma symptoms worse in sensitive people. Cockroaches can also contaminate food. These are general household risks; this passage does not describe cases of illness linked to this species in Georgia.
  If you find a cockroach at home, first identify the species. Check damp places where it may hide, fix water leaks, keep food covered, and seal cracks through which it could enter. If cockroaches appear again, contact a pest control professional. Use chemical products only as directed on the label.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The adult has a flattened, shiny body that is dark brown or almost black, and long feelers. Wing length varies by sex, so color or size alone is not enough to identify the species.
  traits:
    - >-
      It is about 22–27 mm long. The male’s wings do not cover the end of its abdomen, while the female has very short, undeveloped wings. Neither can fly. In a photo, wing shape and the upper side of the body are more useful for identification than dark color alone.
    - >-
      The young insect has no wings. It is hard to tell it apart from an adult female without looking at its size and body details. The eggs are enclosed in a single dark capsule. Do not rush to identify it from a damaged or blurry photo.
    - >-
      An adult red cockroach (Blattella germanica) is usually smaller and brownish, with two dark stripes behind its head that run lengthwise along its body. A black cockroach is larger and uniformly dark, though one feature alone is not enough to tell them apart. The [bed bug](cimex-lectularius) is a different insect: the adult is smaller and oval, has no wings it can use to fly, and feeds on blood.
