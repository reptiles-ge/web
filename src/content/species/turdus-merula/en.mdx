commonName: Common blackbird
location: Year-round resident in Georgia
description: >-
  A medium thrush: males are black with a yellow-orange bill; females are dark
  brown. Confirmed in Georgia as a year-round resident on the 2024 national bird
  checklist.
overview: >-
  Turdus merula lives in Georgia year-round and nests there. Adult males have black feathers, a yellow-orange beak and a yellow ring around the eye; females are dark brown. It is not dangerous to people. You can see it in forests, among bushes, and in parks and gardens.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the blackbird is found in forests, floodplain woodlands, shrubland, parks and gardens, including in settlements. Places where trees and shrubs provide cover are especially suitable for it.
diet: >-
  It looks for earthworms, insects, snails and other animals without backbones on the ground. It also eats berries and fruit in season. When looking for food, it often turns over leaves or scratches at the soil with its beak.
behavior: >-
  The male sings especially during the nesting season, from a high place such as a treetop or a roof. Its song is melodic and sounds like a flute; its alarm call is sharper. It builds its nest in a bush or tree. It nests in Georgia and stays here year-round.
conservation: >-
  Worldwide, the common blackbird is assessed as facing relatively little threat. Georgia’s 2024 scientific bird list gives it the same assessment for the country, though this list is not an official Red List. The species was not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
interaction: >-
  This bird is not dangerous to people. Do not catch a wild bird or approach its nest.

  summary: >-
    An adult male is black, with a yellow-orange beak and a yellow ring around the eye. Females and young birds are brown and may also have spots on their chests. So not every bird of this species is black.
  traits:
    - >-
      The bird is about 23.5–29 cm long. It often hops on the ground.
    - >-
      Unlike the adult male, the female has dark brown feathers and a paler beak. A young blackbird has spots on its chest, so it can be hard to identify by color alone. [A young robin](erithacus-rubecula) is also brown and speckled: when briefly seen in the bushes, it may look like a young blackbird. The robin is much smaller, and a young robin has a light brown stripe on its wing.
    - >-
      An adult male Turdus torquatus has a white band on its chest. Turdus philomelos is smaller, with a brown back and a pale, spotted chest. Turdus viscivorus is larger and grayer, with large spots on its chest. Turdus pilaris has a gray head and a chestnut-colored back; it is seen in Georgia during migration and also spends the winter there.
