overview: >-
  The Oriental cockroach, Blatta orientalis, is an insect in the order Blattodea
  and family Blattidae. Adults are about 22–27 mm long and dark brown to nearly
  black. Males have wings shorter than the abdomen; females have only short wing
  pads. Neither sex flies. It is also called the black cockroach; the older
  combination Periplaneta orientalis refers to the same species.

  Ilia State University's Georgian Biodiversity Database lists the species.
  Photo-backed iNaturalist observations document it in Tbilisi (2021), Batumi
  (2022), Khornabuji (2024) and Lentekhi (2025). These are individual records,
  not a systematic survey of range or abundance. The Georgian name შავი ტარაკანა
  (“black cockroach”) is recorded in the National Library's terminology dictionary.
habitat: >-
  Those named records establish occurrence in Georgia but do not define local
  habitat or elevation limits. International references describe refuges in
  cool, dark, damp places: basements, around pipes and drains, near building
  foundations, and beneath outdoor debris or leaf litter. These are species-level
  habitat descriptions, not microhabitat measurements at every Georgian site.
diet: >-
  Oriental cockroaches feed on a variety of foods and organic waste. International
  references also associate them with refuse, sewage and decaying matter.
  This is general species biology; the cited sources do not include a separate
  study of diet in Georgia.
behavior: >-
  They are mainly active at night. A female encloses eggs in a dark case called
  an ootheca and deposits it in a sheltered place. The hatchling nymphs lack
  wings and molt repeatedly before adulthood. Development time depends on
  temperature and conditions; seasonality reported elsewhere should not be
  treated as a calendar for Georgia.
conservation: >-
  This species also lives in human-associated environments and is treated as a

interaction: >-
  The Oriental cockroach is not venomous. The practical household concerns are
  different: cockroach fragments and droppings can worsen allergies or asthma
  in sensitive people, and food may be contaminated. These are general indoor
  risks, not a report of disease cases caused by this species in Georgia.

  If you find one indoors, first confirm the identification and inspect damp
  refuges. Repair leaks, keep food sealed and close entry gaps. For repeated
  appearances, seek qualified pest-control advice; use any pesticide only
  according to its label.

identification:
  summary: >-
    Adults are flattened, glossy and dark brown to almost black, with long
    antennae. Wing length differs between sexes, so colour or size alone
    cannot settle the identification.
  traits:
    - >-
      Adults measure about 22–27 mm. Male wings do not reach the abdominal tip;
      females have only very short wing pads. Both are flightless. A clear view
      of the wings and upper body is more useful than darkness alone.
    - >-
      Nymphs have no wings and may be hard to distinguish from adult females
      without their size and other body details. Eggs are held in one dark case.
      Do not identify a blurred or damaged specimen from a single trait.
    - >-
      Adult German cockroaches (Blattella germanica) are generally smaller and
      tan-brown, with two dark lengthwise stripes behind the head. Oriental
      cockroaches are larger and more uniformly dark, though neither feature
      is conclusive alone. The [common bed bug](cimex-lectularius) is another
      indoor insect: the adult is much smaller and oval, lacks functional wings,
      and feeds on blood.

    answer: >-
      An Oriental cockroach is usually larger and very dark, with short wings
      on males and only wing pads on females. A German cockroach is smaller and
      has two dark stripes behind its head.
