commonName: False black widow
location: Georgia, including Tbilisi, Kakheti and Adjara
description: >-
  A cobweb spider with a globular abdomen, often mistaken for a true widow. It
  is confirmed in Georgia; a bite can be painful, but this is not the karakurt.
overview: >-
  Steatoda paykulliana occurs in Georgia, including Tbilisi, Kakheti and Adjara. The female has a dark, rounded abdomen, often with a red or yellow stripe along its front edge. It resembles the [karakurt](latrodectus-tredecimguttatus), but belongs to a different genus; color alone cannot always tell them apart. Steatoda paykulliana [is venomous](/spiders/shxamiani-obobebi), and its bite can be painful. Do not touch an unfamiliar spider.
habitat: >-
  The species is found mainly in dry and semi-dry places: dry meadows, rocky slopes and crevices in rocks. It is also occasionally found in untended gardens. In Georgia, its presence has been confirmed in Tbilisi, Sighnaghi, Kodiani and Kveda Chkhutuneti.
diet: >-
  It catches insects and other small prey in an irregular web.
behavior: >-
  The web is often found under stones, in rock crevices, in dry grass and close to the ground. The spider does not spin a large, round web.
conservation: >-
  The IUCN has not assessed this species. It is not listed in Georgia’s 2014 Red List. There are many records of it across the Caucasus.
interaction: >-
  Steatoda paykulliana uses venom to subdue its prey. It may bite a person if it is accidentally crushed or picked up; [the bite](/spiders/obobis-nakbeni) may be painful. See a doctor if the pain is severe or symptoms get worse. Call 112 immediately if the face or throat swells severely, breathing becomes difficult, or the person’s condition worsens sharply. Do not touch or pick up the spider. There is no need to remove its web just because the spider appears, unless the web blocks a space you need to use.

identification:
  summary: >-
    It is a small, dark spider with a rounded abdomen. It often has a red or yellow stripe on the front of its abdomen. The female is larger than the male. It looks like a karakurt, but it is not one.
  traits:
    - >-
      The female’s body is about 8–13 mm long, while the male’s is 4.5–8.4 mm long. These measurements do not include the legs.
    - >-
      The front part of the female’s body ranges from dark brown to almost black. Her abdomen is black, with a red or yellow band at the front. In some females, this band forms a more complex pattern. Her legs are brown. The male is smaller. The band at the front of his abdomen is often whitish, and he may also have an additional lengthwise pattern.
    - >-
      This spider’s irregular web is often found under a stone, in a crevice or close to the ground. A large, symmetrical, round web with a white zigzag in the middle is more typical of [Argiope](argiope-bruennichi).
    - >-
      The spider called ცრუ ყარაყურთი may resemble Latrodectus tredecimguttatus. Females of Latrodectus tredecimguttatus often have red spots on their abdomens, but sometimes the spots are not visible at all. Color alone is not a reliable way to tell the two apart. Other Steatoda species are also found in Georgia. If you are unsure which spider it is, do not touch or pick it up.
facts:
  - >-
    The accepted name is Steatoda paykulliana (Walckenaer, 1806). The World
    Spider Catalog treats the species as accepted. Range: Europe, Mediterranean
    to Central Asia, India.
  - >-
    Otto 2022 lists it on the Georgia checklist. Nentwig et al. cite Georgia
    from that source. ZFMK specimens are from Sighnaghi, Kodiani (1,850 m), and
    Kveda Chkhutuneti.
  - >-
    IUCN has not assessed it. It is not on the 2014 Red List.
