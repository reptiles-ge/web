commonName: "Pond Slider"
location: "Tbilisi, Batumi, and Abkhazia — introduced"
description: >-
  The pond slider (Trachemys scripta) is an introduced water turtle that breeds in the wild around Tbilisi and Batumi. Look for the red stripe behind its eye, and do not release it into the wild.
overview: >-
  The pond slider (Trachemys scripta) is a popular pet freshwater turtle, but it is not native to Georgia. It turns up in the wild when people release it or when it escapes. Around Tbilisi and Batumi it already breeds in the wild, but that does not mean it is established across the whole country. The form known in Georgia is T. s. elegans. The easiest way to recognise it is the broad red stripe behind the eye. It is not venomous to people, but it must not be released into the wild.
habitat: >-
  The pond slider lives in fresh water and may be found in lakes, ponds and other bodies of water. In Georgia, it is confirmed to breed in the wild around Tbilisi and Batumi. On this page's map, Tbilisi, Adjara and Abkhazia have enough records, while Imereti, Kvemo Kartli and Guria have only one or two each. A record shows that a turtle was seen there, not that it breeds there. Seeing a single turtle in another body of water does not by itself mean these turtles have already settled there.

behavior: >-
  The pond slider is a water turtle, so it is natural to see it in the water or on the bank. Its seasonal activity, breeding time and daily routine in Georgia have not been described separately. If you come across one in the wild, do not move it to another lake or pond. Moving it can help this introduced turtle settle in a new place.
conservation: >-
  In Georgia, the pond slider is a conservation concern as an introduced species, not as a native turtle threatened with extinction. Releasing it into the wild raises the risk that it will take hold in new places. Such releases also do not help protect native water turtles.
interaction: >-
  The pond slider (Trachemys scripta) is not venomous to people and is usually not dangerous. If you disturb it or pick it up, it may bite in self-defence. Wash a bite with soap and clean water, and seek medical help if there is heavy bleeding, a deep wound or signs of infection. Do not move a pond slider you find in the wild to another body of water. In Georgia this is an introduced species, and a new population may affect local nature. Most importantly, never release a pet pond slider into the wild.

  - question: Does the pond slider occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes, but it is not a native species: it was introduced to Georgia. Around Tbilisi and Batumi, it has been confirmed to breed in the wild, so Pond Sliders have already taken hold in these two places. The map also has enough records from Abkhazia.
  - question: Is it native to Georgia?
    answer: >-
      No. The pond slider is not native to Georgia. It is a pet freshwater turtle that ended up in the wild after being released or escaping, and became established there. Georgia's native water turtles are the European pond turtle and the Caspian turtle.
  - question: Where is it confirmed?
    answer: >-
      Around Tbilisi and Batumi, where the Pond Slider is confirmed to breed in the wild. On the map, Tbilisi, Adjara and Abkhazia have enough records, while Imereti, Kvemo Kartli and Guria have only one or two each. This does not mean it occurs throughout the country.
  - question: How do I identify a pond slider?
    answer: >-
      By the broad red stripe behind the eye, the fairly flat shell and the webbed feet. For T. s. elegans, the form known in Georgia, the red stripe is an especially good identification mark. So when you see the turtle, look for this stripe first.
  - question: How do I tell it from native water turtles?
    answer: >-
      The pond slider has a broad red stripe behind the eye. The European pond turtle and the Caspian turtle have no such stripe. The land tortoise (Testudo graeca) stands out in other ways: it has a high, domed shell and legs made for walking on land. So when you try to identify a turtle, look behind the eye first, then check how high the shell is.
  - question: Can I release a pet turtle into a lake?
    answer: >-
      No. The pond slider is an introduced species in Georgia, not a native one. If you release a pet turtle into a lake or pond, pond sliders may settle there and form a new group. So do not release a pet turtle into the wild.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. The pond slider has no venom and is not considered dangerous to people. If you catch one, it may bite to defend itself. So do not pick it up.
