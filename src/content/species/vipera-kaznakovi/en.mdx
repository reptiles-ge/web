location: western Georgia
description: >-
  The Caucasus viper (Vipera kaznakovi) is a venomous snake found in Georgia
  mainly in the humid Colchic forests of the west and in zones near the Black
  Sea coast. Adults are usually about 45–60 cm long. It has a relatively stout
  body, a vertical pupil, and often bright reddish-orange colouring.
overview: |-
  The Caucasian viper (Vipera kaznakovi) is a venomous snake. In Georgia, it is found mainly in the damp Colchic forests of the west and near the Black Sea coast. Adults are usually about 45–60 cm long. They have a fairly thick body, vertically elongated pupils and often a vivid red-orange colour.
  The specimen used to describe the species came from Tsebelda, near Sukhumi in Abkhazia.
  The [2026 checklist](/news/georgia-herpetofauna-checklist-2026) treats the Caucasus viper as a biologically confirmed species. Dinnik’s viper and some other forms in the same group are treated as candidate species because their boundaries remain unresolved.
  The IUCN’s 2008 global assessment lists the Caucasus viper as Endangered (EN). It also has EN status on Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
habitat: |-
  The Caucasian viper is found mainly in the damp Colchic areas of western Georgia. It lives along forest edges, in ravines, beech and chestnut groves, on raised flat ground beside rivers, and in cleared meadows. The dry plains of the east and the subalpine zone above the forests of the Greater Caucasus are not its usual habitats.
  Vipers from the Borjomi Gorge and Baniskhevi were previously classified as Caucasus vipers. It is still not entirely clear which form in the Vipera kaznakovi group they belong to. The 2026 checklist associates vipers in central Georgia’s forests with Vipera dinniki tuniyevi.
  The Caucasian viper is found mainly up to about 1,000 metres above sea level. It rarely enters the depths of dense coniferous forests.
diet: |-
  Adult Caucasian vipers catch rodents, lizards and small birds. Young vipers also eat insects, including grasshoppers.
behavior: |-
  The Caucasian viper is elusive. It can also be seen during the day, especially in cool weather. In hot weather, it more often seeks shade.
  It gives birth to live young, sometimes about 5–10 at a time.
  If you encounter a Caucasian viper, keep your distance and do not try to pick it up. It does not seek out people and generally defends itself only when it feels threatened.
conservation: |-
  The IUCN’s 2008 global assessment lists the Caucasus viper as Endangered (EN). Its area of occupancy was estimated at less than 500 km²; its range is severely fragmented and its population trend is decreasing. That assessment predates later work on the boundaries within this viper group.
  It also has EN status on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Its main threats are the loss of habitat as tourism, cities and agriculture expand, as well as illegal capture and being killed by people.

  traits:
    -  |-
      Its body is relatively short and thick. Its triangular head is clearly set apart from its neck, and it has no horn-like projection on its snout.
      An adult Caucasian viper is usually about 45–60 cm long, though it rarely reaches 65–70 cm. Females are generally larger than males.
      In one group of measured snakes, the largest female was 60 cm long, while the largest male was 47.5 cm.
    -  |-
      The pupil looks like a vertical slit. Each scale on the back has a sharp, raised ridge down its center.
      The body's main color is often brick red, orange or yellowish brown, with a dark zigzag stripe along the back. Black individuals are also common, though they usually have a red or yellow tinge around the mouth.
    -  |-
      In western Georgia, the [grass snake](natrix-natrix) (Natrix natrix) can be nearly black and resemble a black Caucasus viper from a distance. The grass snake has round pupils; a pale “collar” is often visible but may be absent from view in black forms. It is not venomous.
      A brown [smooth snake](coronella-austriaca) (Coronella austriaca) has dark blotches along its back; in a partial view, these can be mistaken for the Caucasus viper’s dark pattern. The smooth snake has round pupils and smooth dorsal scales without a raised ridge down the middle.
    -  |-
      [Dinnik’s viper](vipera-dinniki) is found mainly high in the Greater Caucasus Mountains, at about 1,500–2,800 m. It is often grayer or browner and has a narrower head than the Caucasian viper.
      [The nose-horned viper](vipera-transcaucasiana) has a clearly visible, horn-like growth on its nose. In Georgia, it is found in dry mountain areas of the Lesser Caucasus.
    - >-
      The Caucasian viper is venomous. It poses a moderate risk to people. If it bites you, you need medical help. Do not approach, touch or try to pick up the snake.
faq:
  - question: Is it venomous and dangerous to humans?
    answer: |-
      Yes, the Caucasus viper is venomous. It poses a moderate risk to people, but a bite still requires medical attention.
      The snake does not chase people and usually defends itself when it feels threatened. Keep your distance and do not try to pick it up or catch it.
  - question: What should I do if bitten?
    answer: |-
      Call 112 immediately. Calm the person who was bitten, have them lie down, and try to keep them moving as little as possible.
      Do not cut the bite, try to suck out the venom, or apply a tight tourniquet. The person needs to be taken to a hospital for treatment. For more information, see the [snakebite guide](/snakes/gvelis-nakbeni).
  - question: How can it be told from the grass snake?
    answer: |-
      The Caucasian viper is a small, stocky snake. Its pupils are vertically elongated, and its scales have a raised ridge down the middle. Its main body color is often red-orange. It usually has a dark zigzag stripe along its back. It has no hornlike growth on its snout.
      The common grass snake is slimmer. It has round pupils and often a yellow “collar.” It is not venomous.
  - question: Is the Caucasus viper found in Georgia?
    answer: |-
      Yes. The Caucasian viper has been confirmed in Georgia. The specimen used to first describe the species came from Abkhazia.
      It is found mainly in the damp Colchic areas of western Georgia. It is not found throughout the country, and the dry plains in the east are not its usual habitat.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: |-
      In Georgia, the Caucasian viper is found mainly in the wet parts of Colchis in the west, along forest edges, in ravines and in other damp wooded places. It usually lives at elevations of up to about 1,000 m above sea level.
      The precise identity of vipers from this group recorded in the Borjomi Gorge and Shida Kartli remains unresolved.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: |-
      An adult Caucasian viper is usually about 45–60 cm long. It rarely reaches 65–70 cm. Females are usually larger than males.
      This snake is much smaller and shorter than a [gyurza](macrovipera-lebetina).
  - question: How does it differ from Dinnik’s viper?
    answer: |-
      The Caucasian viper is found mainly in the low-lying, damp forests of Colchis, often up to about 1,000 m above sea level. It has a relatively broad head and is often red-orange in color.
      Dinnik’s viper is found in the high mountains of the Greater Caucasus, at about 1,500–2,800 m above sea level. It is often grayer or more brownish.
  - question: Why is it Endangered?
    answer: |-
      The IUCN’s 2008 global assessment lists the Caucasus viper as Endangered (EN): its area of occupancy is small and severely fragmented, and its population trend is decreasing. It also has EN status on Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
