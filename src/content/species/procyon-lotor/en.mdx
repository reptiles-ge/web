commonName: Raccoon
location: Introduced in Georgia
description: >-
  A medium-sized procyonid with a black facial mask and a ringed tail. It is
  not native to Georgia; it is introduced and established.
overview: >-
  Procyon lotor is a mammal in the raccoon family. It was introduced to Georgia and has become established there. It is especially well known in Kakheti; its main features are a black “mask” near the eyes and a ringed tail. It is not venomous, but it may bite people, so do not touch it. Nyctereutes procyonoides is a different species.
habitat: >-
  Raccoons often live in forests and riverside woodland near water, but also enter gardens and the edges of settlements. There is no document recording their release in Georgia; they probably spread from Azerbaijan. Records from around Lagodekhi date back to the 1950s. In Kakheti, they are known from Lagodekhi, the Alazani Valley and Gombori. They also appear in photographs and camera recordings from Kvemo Kartli and the area around Tbilisi National Park. There are also reports from the coast of Abkhazia; possible traces found in Adjara have not yet been confirmed as raccoon traces.
diet: >-
  It eats both animal and plant foods: animals without backbones, frogs, fish, birds’ eggs and chicks, fruit and hazelnuts. It also eats food scraps near settlements. In Georgia, local residents say it damages crops and poultry, though the extent of the damage has not been assessed.
behavior: >-
  It is mainly active at night and at dusk, though it may also appear during the day. It swims well and climbs trees. Its five-toed front paw prints look like small handprints. It shelters in tree hollows, burrows, rock crevices or building attics. It does not truly hibernate: in cold weather, it moves around less and may stay in its den for a long time. Pregnancy lasts about 63 days. Young animals seen in Lagodekhi and three cubs photographed with a female in Shuamta confirm that it breeds in Georgia.
conservation: >-
  A 2016 assessment found that the raccoon faces a low risk of extinction worldwide. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Raccoons were introduced to Georgia and have become established there. They are considered a threat to local wildlife. Raccoons can eat birds’ eggs and chicks, as well as amphibians. Do not take in a wild raccoon or release a pet raccoon into the wild.
interaction: >-
  A raccoon has no venom, but it may bite, especially if cornered or protecting its young. Rabies can spread to a person if an infected animal bites them or the animal’s saliva gets onto broken skin or a mucous membrane. Do not touch or feed raccoons. If you are bitten, wash the wound immediately with soap and running water and seek medical care urgently. In an unprotected yard, a raccoon may take a chicken, eggs or fruit.

    - >-
      It is grayish brown or yellowish gray, often with a pale belly. It has a black “mask” with a white border around its eyes. Its tail usually has 4–7 dark rings and a dark tip. Each front paw has five long toes. Its claws do not retract. Its ears are relatively small, and its snout is pointed.
    - >-
      [Meles canescens](meles-canescens) is native to Georgia. It has a heavier build and lengthwise black and white stripes on its head rather than a mask-like marking. Its tail is short and has no rings. Nyctereutes procyonoides was introduced to Georgia. It has shorter legs and a mask-like marking on its face, but no rings on its tail.

    answer: >-

  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
