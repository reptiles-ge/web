overview: >-
  The European glass lizard (Pseudopus apodus) is a large legless lizard — not
  a snake. It has no legs, so people often fear it, but it has no venom and
  poses no real danger to people. In Georgia it occurs in the east: Kakheti,
  Kartli, Mtskheta–Mtianeti, and around Tbilisi. It is not confirmed in the
  west on the checklist we follow. Adults are roughly a metre long, sometimes a
  little more.
habitat: >-
  It likes open places: fields, open woodland, scrub, rocky slopes, and river
  valleys. It often turns up in gardens or near village yards. The map shows
  Kakheti, Kvemo and Shida Kartli, Mtskheta–Mtianeti, and Tbilisi. Western
  Georgia is not on the map — we do not have confirmed atlas records there.
diet: >-
  It mostly eats snails and slugs. It also takes insects. Sometimes a small
  lizard or other small prey. Strong jaws crack snail shells easily. It shows
  up more often in wet weather.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. Tell it from a snake by eyelids, ear openings, and a
  deep fold along the side. A tiny limb stub may show near the base of the
  tail. If scared it may hiss or bite — defence, not venom. The tail can break
  off, though that is uncommon in this species. It lays eggs. Do not handle
  wild animals.
conservation: >-
  Globally it is Least Concern. It is not on Georgia’s Red List. The main local
  problems are habitat loss and people killing it after mistaking it for a
  snake. In fact it helps farms by eating pest insects and slugs.

  - question: How is it different from the slow worm?
    answer: >-

      clear side fold, and often a yellow belly. Both are harmless; neither is a
      snake.

    answer: >-
