overview: >-
  Jojo (Paralaudakia caucasia) is the only lizard in the agama family found in Georgia. It is active during the day, lives in rocky areas and is not venomous to humans. It is found mainly in the east and south of the country, and only in a few places in the west. Its presence has been confirmed in the municipalities of Aspindza and Dedoplistskaro. Jojo is not widespread in the humid forests of Colchis.
habitat: >-
  The jojo lives on rocky slopes and in dry or semi-dry open areas. It shelters in cracks in rocks, under stones and in burrows. In Georgia, it is found mainly in the east and south, and in some places in the west. Its presence has been confirmed in the Aspindza municipality, in Pia and Tmogvi, and in the Dedoplistskaro municipality, around Datviskhevi and Fantishara. Other sources also mention the Iori Plateau, Kartli, the Borjomi municipality and Meskheti. The available list does not specify which municipalities in western Georgia it inhabits.
diet: >-
  The lizard mainly eats insects, spiders and other small animals like them. Some sources say it also eats plant food, but what it eats in Georgia has not been studied in detail.
behavior: >-
  The Caucasian agama is active by day. It often basks on stones and rock ledges
  and quickly retreats into a crevice when threatened. In defence it may also
  inflate its body. It is an egg-laying species, although the exact laying period
  and clutch size in Georgia are not given in the available data. Do not handle
  wild animals.
conservation: >-
  This lizard’s presence in Georgia has been confirmed. Worldwide, its risk of extinction is low (Least Concern), and it is not on Georgia’s Red List. In older sources, you may find it under the name Laudakia caucasia; the current Georgian list uses Paralaudakia caucasia.

    The jojo is a sturdy lizard with a flattened body, a triangular head and a spiny tail. The scales on its tail form distinct rings. These features help you tell it apart from rock lizards, the Caspian gecko and the long-legged skink.

      The scales on the back are not all alike: they are relatively large along the spine and spikier on the sides of the body.
    - >-
      The tail is about twice as long as the body and is covered with spiny scales arranged in rings. This is one of the easiest ways to recognize an agama.
    - >-
      Its back is usually olive green or gray. It may have yellowish spots or a dark, netlike pattern on its body. During the breeding season, the male’s belly sometimes gets darker.
    - >-
      Rock lizards (Darevskia) have slimmer bodies and lack the spiny tail rings seen on the jojo lizard. The Caspian gecko has a vertical pupil and broad toes. It is mostly active after dusk.
