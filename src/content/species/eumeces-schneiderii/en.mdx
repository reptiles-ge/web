overview: >-
  Schneider's skink (Eumeces schneiderii) is a large, harmless skink confirmed
  in Georgia from the far south-east of Kakheti. Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026 list
  it as biologically confirmed: the figures are from Vashlovani National Park,
  and the barcoded specimens are from Chachuna Managed Reserve. Georgian
  material belongs to the Caucasus/Iran form E. s. princeps; some authors treat
  that form at species rank as Novoeumeces princeps, but the Georgian checklist
  keeps it as a subspecies of Eumeces schneiderii. It is not a snake, has no
  venom, and is not dangerous to people. The sources used here do not publish a
  separate Georgian elevation range, seasonal calendar, or population estimate.
habitat: >-
  Reliable named Georgian material comes from dry south-eastern Kakheti. The
  2026 checklist places the species in the extreme south-east of Georgia; its
  figures are from Vashlovani National Park and the barcodes from Chachuna
  Managed Reserve. The atlas map marks Kakheti because those localities are in
  Kakheti, not because the species should be expected across the whole region
  or across Georgia. The realistic search area is the Vashlovani-Chachuna zone
  of dry, semi-desert and rocky-shrubby landscapes. No other Georgian region or
  elevation belt is assigned on this page without a named source.
diet: >-
  No published diet study from Georgian material is cited for this profile.
  Like many skinks, the species is expected to take small invertebrates, but
  that is a general biological expectation, not a measured Georgian diet. A
  precise local prey list should wait for stomach-content or direct feeding
  records from Georgia.
behavior: >-
  A Georgian activity calendar has not been published in the sources used here.
  For field identification, focus on the strong elongated body, smooth shiny
  scales, well-developed limbs, and visible eyelids. Do not handle wild
  animals: a dorsal and side photograph plus the locality is more useful than
  capture. The Reptile Database lists the species as oviparous; clutch size and
  breeding dates in Georgia are not confirmed here.
conservation: >-
  The 2026 Georgian checklist treats Schneider's skink as biologically
  confirmed. The same paper notes that Georgian E. s. princeps COI sequences
  differ strongly from E. s. pavimentatus; future taxonomic revision is
  possible, but this page does not split the species. This profile does not
  assign a Georgian Red List status or a directly verified IUCN category.
  Because the confirmed Georgian range is small and south-eastern, habitat
  change, fragmentation of dry open landscapes, and accidental killing are
  practical concerns, but a quantitative threat assessment for Georgia is not
  available.
interaction: >-
  Harmless to people: it has no venom and does not attack. Do not catch it or
  move it elsewhere. If photographed, the most useful views are the side of the
  body, the dorsal pattern, the head, and an exact locality.

  traits:
    - >-
      The body is elongated and strong, with smooth, shiny scales. The limbs are
      fully developed and the toes are visible, so it is neither a legless
      lizard nor a snake.
    - >-
      Older species descriptions describe olive-brown upperparts, sometimes
      with golden-yellow spots or streaks, and often a pale yellowish lateral
      stripe. Colour varies, so do not identify it from colour alone.
    - >-
      Species-level descriptions give a total length of roughly 35–37 cm
      including the tail. That is a broad-range figure, not a separate Georgian
      measurement series.
    - >-
      The [snake-eyed skink](ablepharus-pannonicus) is much smaller and has
      fused transparent eyelids, so it appears unable to blink. Schneider's
      skink is far larger and has a visible eyelid ring.
    - >-
      The [snake-eyed lizard](ophisops-elegans) can also occur in dry eastern
      Georgia and may confuse observers because of its snake-like eye, but it is
      a slender, fast lacertid with different scales. The [Caucasian agama](paralaudakia-caucasia)
      is a broader, flatter-headed rock lizard, not a smooth cylindrical skink.
    - >-
      Georgian confirmation is linked to Vashlovani and Chachuna. A similar
      animal from western Georgia, high mountains, or humid Colchic forest
      should be checked especially carefully.
