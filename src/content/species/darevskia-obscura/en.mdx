overview: >-
  The Meskhetian rock lizard (Darevskia obscura) belongs to the Darevskia group, which is often mentioned in accounts of Georgia’s wildlife and whose species are difficult to tell apart. It is a harmless rock lizard. Records from Georgia concerning it are linked to the upper Mtkvari River, Trialeti, Meskheti, Javakheti and the Enguri basin. In a 2026 paper by Tarkhnishvili and coauthors, its recognition as a separate species remains unsettled. That paper groups Georgian lizards previously assigned to D. rudis in a broad sense under D. obscura, rather than with the D. rudis of Anatolia. This is why similar lizards may also appear under the name D. rudis in older or more general sources. The case for treating it as a separate species rests mainly on DNA passed down through the maternal line. Body features and DNA in the cell nucleus do not yet provide a complete picture. It is known to come into contact with related species, including D. portschinskii and D. valentini, and to exchange genes with them through interbreeding. So colour, a single photo or an older name is not enough for a reliable identification. The location where the lizard was seen and detailed photos are needed, and the evidence must be assessed carefully.
