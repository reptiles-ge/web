description: >-
  Pond slider (Trachemys scripta) in Georgia — identification, freshwater
  habitats, Tbilisi and Batumi records, and why pet turtles must not be released
  into the wild.
overview: >-
  The pond slider (Trachemys scripta) is not native to Georgia. It is a popular
  pet freshwater turtle that appears in the wild after being released or
  escaping. The form recorded in Georgia is T. s. elegans. Reproducing
  populations are known from the vicinities of Tbilisi and Batumi, but that does
  not mean the species is established across the whole country. The most visible
  identification mark is the broad red stripe behind the eye. The species is not
  venomous to people, but it must not be released into the wild.
habitat: >-
  The pond slider is tied to freshwater habitats and may be seen in lakes,
  ponds, and other water bodies. In Georgia, confirmed reproducing populations
  are known from the vicinities of Tbilisi and Batumi. Seeing one individual in
  another water body does not automatically mean an established population is
  already present there. In the records-by-region table of this page's map,
  Tbilisi, Adjara, and Abkhazia have enough records. Imereti, Kvemo Kartli, and
  Guria have only single records. A record shows that a turtle was seen there,
  not that a reproducing population lives there.
diet: >-
  The sources used on this profile do not separately describe the diet of pond
  sliders in Georgia. For that reason, no specific food list is added here as
  confirmed information.
behavior: >-
  The pond slider is an aquatic species, so seeing it in water or near the bank
  is expected. Seasonal activity, breeding period, and daily routine for the
  Georgian populations are not separately described in the sources used on this
  profile. If you see a pond slider in the wild, do not move it to another lake
  or pond. Such movement can help create a new introduced population.
conservation: >-
  In Georgia, the pond slider is a conservation issue as an introduced species,
  not as a native turtle at risk of extinction. Releasing it into the wild
  increases the risk of creating new populations and does not help native water
  turtles. This profile does not cite its global IUCN status or Georgian
  national Red List category.
interaction: >-
  The pond slider (Trachemys scripta) is not venomous to people and is usually
  not dangerous. If disturbed or picked up, it may bite defensively. If a turtle
  bites you, wash the injured area with soap and clean water. Seek medical help
  for heavy bleeding, a deep wound, or signs of infection. Do not move a pond
  slider seen in the wild to another water body. It is especially important
  never to release a pet pond slider into the wild — this is an introduced
  species in Georgia, and a new population can affect the local ecosystem.

identification:
  summary: >-
    An introduced water turtle. In Georgia, one of the main marks of T. s.
    elegans is the broad red stripe behind the eye. Also consider shell shape,
    webbed feet, and the aquatic setting.
  traits:
    - >-
      The pond slider is a water turtle with a relatively flat shell. A high,
      strongly domed shell points more toward the land tortoise (Testudo
      graeca).
    - >-
      The T. s. elegans form recorded in Georgia has a broad red stripe behind
      the eye. This is one of the clearest marks, but other traits should also
      be considered.
    - >-
      It has webbed feet. A water body setting and a relatively flat shell are
      additional signs typical of a water turtle.
    - >-
      Georgia's native pond turtle (Emys orbicularis) and Caspian turtle
      (Mauremys caspica) do not have such a broad red stripe behind the eye.
    - >-
      In Georgia, reproducing populations are confirmed in the vicinities of
      Tbilisi and Batumi. The map also has enough records from Abkhazia. These
      are separate foci, not a countrywide range.
    - >-
      A pet-like appearance and proximity to settlements are important clues,
      because the pond slider is introduced in Georgia.

    answer: >-
      Yes, but as an introduced species. In Georgia, reproducing populations are

  - question: Is it native to Georgia?
    answer: >-
      No. The pond slider is not native to Georgia. It is a pet freshwater turtle
      that became established after releases or escapes. Georgia's native water
      turtles are the pond turtle and the Caspian turtle.
  - question: Where is it confirmed?
    answer: >-
      Known reproducing populations in Georgia are in the vicinities of Tbilisi

  - question: How do I identify a pond slider?
    answer: >-
      Look for the broad red stripe behind the eye, a relatively flat shell, and
      webbed feet. For T. s. elegans in Georgia, the red stripe is an especially
      useful identification mark.
  - question: How do I tell it from native water turtles?
    answer: >-
      The pond slider has a broad red stripe behind the eye. Pond and Caspian
      turtles lack this mark. The land tortoise (Testudo graeca) has a high,
      domed shell and land-adapted legs.
  - question: Can I release a pet turtle into a lake?
    answer: >-
      No. The pond slider is introduced in Georgia. Releasing a pet turtle into
      a lake or pond can create a new population.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. The pond slider has no venom and is not considered dangerous to
      people. It may bite defensively if caught, so do not pick it up.
