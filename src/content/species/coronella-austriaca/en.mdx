commonName: Smooth snake
location: Adjara, Mtskheta — Mtianeti, Samtskhe — Javakheti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli, Samegrelo — Zemo Svaneti
description: >-
  A harmless, smooth-scaled snake of Georgia — secretive and often
  mistaken for a viper.
overview: >-
  The smooth snake (Coronella austriaca) is a nonvenomous snake found in Georgia. Because of its coloring, people often mistake it for a viper. To identify it, don’t rely on color alone: look at its smooth scales, round pupils and the pattern on its head. According to Georgian sources, it is usually less than 50 cm long, though it can reach about 70 cm. The IUCN assesses it as facing little threat worldwide. It is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
habitat: >-
  The records table confirms its distribution in Adjara, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli, and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. This page counts only these regions as part of its range. It has also been recorded in Guria, Shida Kartli, Imereti, and Abkhazia, but these regions are not counted as part of its range. It occurs at forest edges, in open woodland and shrubby areas, on rocky slopes, and in foothills. It often hides under rocks, stumps, and other cover. It is more common in damp, mountainous places than in the very dry semi-desert of the southeast.
diet: >-
  The smooth snake mainly hunts lizards. Adults may also eat small mammals and, rarely, other small snakes. What it eats specifically in Georgia has not been fully studied, so this information describes the species’ diet in general.
behavior: >-
  The smooth snake is quite secretive and often hides under stones or other
  cover. By day it may come out briefly to warm itself. When threatened, it
  flattens its body and can look more like a viper. If handled, it may bite,
  but it has no venom. The species is ovoviviparous — the young are born live.
  At species level, litters of 2–15 young are described, though this number
  has not been studied separately for Georgia.
conservation: >-
  According to the IUCN, the smooth snake faces relatively little threat worldwide (Least Concern). It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. In some places, changes to where it lives and people killing it may pose a threat, especially when people mistake it for a viper.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The smooth snake belongs to the grass snake family and is harmless to people, though it sometimes looks like a viper. To tell it apart, look at its scales, the shape of its pupils, and the pattern on its head. You do not need to pick up the snake to count its scales.
  traits:
    - >-
      The smooth snake has smooth scales on its back, with no raised line down the middle. Vipers have a small raised line down the middle of each scale on their backs. The smooth snake’s head is narrower than a viper’s and less clearly set off from its neck.
    - >-
      The smooth snake has round pupils. Vipers in Georgia have vertical, slit-like pupils. A dark stripe often runs from the smooth snake’s eye toward the back of its head. It may also have a dark crown- or heart-shaped marking on top of its head.
    - >-
      Its color may be coppery, brownish or gray. It often has two rows of small dark spots on its back rather than one continuous zigzag. Its belly is often dark gray or reddish. The markings on young copper snakes may be more distinct. According to Georgian sources, the copper snake is usually less than 50 cm long and reaches a maximum length of about 70 cm.
    - >-
      The smooth snake can be mistaken for several vipers. The Caucasian viper has scales with a raised ridge down the middle and vertically elongated pupils. Dinnik’s viper is found mainly in the high mountains of the Greater Caucasus, while the nose-horned viper has a prominent horn on its nose. The common grass snake often has a yellow or whitish ring around its neck and is more often found near water.
    - >-
      The smooth snake is not venomous. If you encounter one, keep your distance and let it pass. It can be mistaken for a viper, so do not pick up a snake you cannot identify.

    answer: >-
      No. The smooth snake has no venom and is not dangerous to humans. If
      handled, it may bite, so do not pick it up.
  - question: How do I tell it from a viper?
    answer: >-
      Look for smooth scales, a round pupil, a narrow head and two rows of
      small dark spots on the back. Vipers have keeled scales and a vertical
      pupil. Colour alone is not a reliable way to tell them apart.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      Georgian sources describe the smooth snake as usually under 50 cm. Its
      maximum size is about 70 cm.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      It mainly eats lizards. It may also feed on small mammals and, rarely,
      other small snakes. Its diet has not been studied separately in Georgia
      in full.
  - question: Does it lay eggs or give live birth?
    answer: >-
      The smooth snake is ovoviviparous — the young are born live. At species
      level, litters of 2–15 young are described, though this number has not
      been measured separately for Georgia.
  - question: What should I do if I meet one?
    answer: >-
      If you meet a smooth snake, keep your distance and let it move away. Do
      not kill it or pick it up. An unidentified snake may be a viper, so
      touching it is not needed for identification.
