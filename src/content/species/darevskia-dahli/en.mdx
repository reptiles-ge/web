description: >-
  A harmless rock lizard of mountain forest. Confirmed in Georgia; not
  countrywide. Colour will not separate it from other Darevskia.
overview: >-
  Dahl’s rock lizard (Darevskia dahli) is a nonvenomous lizard found mainly in wooded, rocky parts of the Trialeti Mountains in Georgia. It is harmless to people. Females reproduce without males and lay eggs. It resembles other rock lizards, so colour alone cannot identify it.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, Dahl’s rock lizard lives on rocky ground, including in mountain forest, meadows and above the tree line. Sites where it was found in a 2008–2009 field survey lay at 830–1,960 m above sea level, mainly in the Trialeti Mountains. In some places it lives alongside the Kura lizard. At one rocky site near Tbilisi, Dahl’s rock lizard was found closer to a permanent water source than the Kura lizard.
diet: >-
  Rock lizards generally catch small invertebrates, such as insects and spiders. A 2010 study found that Dahl’s rock lizard competes with the Kura lizard for food and shelter.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day, basking on rocks and hiding in crevices. Dahl’s rock lizard arose from a cross between the [Colchian rock lizard](darevskia-mixta) (D. mixta) and the Kura lizard (D. portschinskii); today females reproduce without males and lay eggs. Females collected in Georgia laid 1–4 eggs per clutch while in captivity.
conservation: >-
  Georgia’s 2014 Red List lists Dahl’s rock lizard as Vulnerable (VU) because of its small, fragmented range. The 2009 global IUCN assessment lists it as Near Threatened (NT). That assessment names urban expansion and road construction as threats.
interaction: >-
  It has no venom and is harmless to people. Do not pick it up: its tail breaks off easily. If you see one, observe it from a distance, photograph its head and side, and leave it where it is. Colour alone cannot establish the species.

identification:
  summary: >-
    It is a small rock lizard. Back colour cannot separate it from [other Darevskia](/lizards/darevskia); location and the scales on the head and just in front of the vent matter more. Do not pick it up to inspect its scales.
  traits:
    - >-
      It has a small, slender body, a flattened head and well-developed legs. In adult lizards found in Georgia, body length is usually about 5.4–5.8 cm. The greatest reported body length is about 63 mm. Its tail is longer than its body and breaks off easily. Do not pick up the lizard to count its scales.
    - >-
      The back is gray, brownish or olive-colored, often with dark spots or a netlike pattern. The belly may be yellowish. Other rock lizards have similar coloring, so color alone cannot identify the species. The 2026 images from Kojori show the scales on the head and the plates in front of the anus.
    - >-
      Dahl’s rock lizard has two plates just in front of the vent; the [Armenian rock lizard](darevskia-armeniaca) (D. armeniaca) has one broad plate. The Armenian rock lizard is larger (body length reaches about 74 mm) and also occurs in southern Georgia (Ninotsminda / Saghamo, Akhalkalaki). Do not pick up a lizard to check this feature.
    - >-
      The [Kura lizard](darevskia-portschinskii) (D. portschinskii) is often found on the same rocks as dahli. Individuals found in Georgia are similar in size. The Kura lizard has both males and females, while almost all dahli lizards are female. To tell them apart reliably, you need to look at the scales on their heads and the area around the opening under their tails; colour alone is not enough.

faq:

    answer: >-
      Yes. Its presence in Georgia is confirmed, including in the Trialeti Mountains and at other rocky sites such as Kojori.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      It lives on rocky ground in Georgia, in mountain forest and above it. Confirmed sites include Trialeti, Kojori, Tedzami, Ateni and Zedazeni; sites in the 2008–2009 survey lay at 830–1,960 m.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to humans?
    answer: >-
      No. It has no venom and is harmless to people. Do not pick it up: its tail breaks off easily.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      Colour alone cannot identify it. One feature that separates it from the Armenian rock lizard is the pair of plates in front of the vent; that species has one broad plate there. Do not pick up a lizard to inspect its scales.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      Adults studied in Georgia have a body length of about 5.4–5.8 cm, excluding the tail. The reported maximum is 63 mm, also excluding the tail.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Rock lizards generally catch small invertebrates such as insects and spiders. The specific prey of Dahl’s rock lizard in Georgia has not been established.
  - question: When is it active, and how does it reproduce?
    answer: >-
      It is active by day. Females lay eggs without males; females collected in Georgia laid 1–4 eggs per clutch while in captivity.
  - question: What should I do if I encounter one?
    answer: >-
      Observe it from a distance and, if possible, photograph its head and side. Do not pick it up; leave it where it is. Colour alone cannot establish the species.
