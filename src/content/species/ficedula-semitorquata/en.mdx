commonName: Semi-collared flycatcher
location: Breeding migrant in Georgia
description: >-
  A small black-and-white flycatcher with a broken white neck-collar. Confirmed
  in Georgia as a breeding migrant on the 2024 national bird checklist.
overview: >-
  The semi-collared flycatcher (Ficedula semitorquata) is a small Old World
  flycatcher (family Muscicapidae) confirmed in Georgia as a breeding migrant.
  The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia codes it BB; M: a breeder
  that is absent in the non-breeding season, and also a bird of passage.
  Seasonality is classed as high-quality (code A). Occurrence quality is high
  (H), with photographs and ringing data. Adults are about 12–13.5 cm long at
  species level. It is not a threat to people. IUCN currently lists the species
  as Least Concern. It was previously assessed as Near Threatened. The 2024
  checklist assigns LC globally and nationally.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia look for it in the breeding season and on passage, not in winter.
  The 2024 checklist confirms country-level breeding-migrant status but does not
  publish a municipality gazetteer. Do not assume it occurs evenly across
  Georgia, including treeless high plateaus. Species-level habitat is mature
  deciduous or mixed woodland, especially oak and other cavity-rich forest,
  riverine woodland, and similar edges. A separate national elevation band for
  Georgia is not published here.
diet: >-
  There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page. Across the range it
  takes insects and other small invertebrates, mainly by sallying from a perch.
  That is species-level feeding, not a measured Georgian menu.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. The song is a short whistled phrase, intermediate between
  pied and collared flycatcher; calls include sharp notes similar to other
  Ficedula. It is a long-distance migrant: it breeds from the Balkans and Turkey
  through the Caucasus to northern Iran and winters in eastern and central
  Africa. Georgia is in the breeding range. Nesting is in a tree hole or nest
  box. Species-level clutches are typically about five to seven eggs; those
  figures are from the wider range, not a Georgian nest sample on this page.
  The 2024 checklist treats the species as a breeder in Georgia.
conservation: >-
  BirdLife International currently assesses Ficedula semitorquata as Least
  Concern on the IUCN Red List. Older assessments treated it as Near Threatened;
  being on the Red List is not the same as a current threatened category.
  EUNIS reports Least Concern at world and Europe scales and notes EU Birds
  Directive coverage; that instrument does not apply in Georgia. It is not on
  Georgia’s 2014 Red List (Government Resolution No. 190). The 2024 Georgian
  bird checklist assigns global and national Red List category LC. A
  Georgia-specific population trend is not quantified on this page. Loss of
  mature cavity trees is the biologically relevant pressure at species level.
  Do not catch or keep wild birds.
interaction: >-
  It is not a threat to people; wild birds must not be captured.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A sparrow-sized flycatcher. Breeding males are black above and white below,
    with a broken white collar, not a full neck-ring. In Georgia it is a
    breeding migrant on the 2024 checklist. The usual confusion is European
    pied flycatcher and collared flycatcher.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length about 12–13.5 cm at species level. The bill is fine and dark.
      Breeding males are black on the head and back and white below, with a
      white forehead patch. The white on the neck sides forms a half-collar: it
      does not meet as a complete ring. The closed wing usually shows a large
      white patch plus a second pale bar of white-tipped median coverts. The
      outer tail often shows more white than in European pied flycatcher. A
      pale grey rump may be visible; it is not the broad white saddle of
      collared flycatcher.
    - >-
      Adult females and many autumn birds are brown above and paler below, with
      a white wing patch. The second wing-bar is often still the best clue, but
      brown Ficedula in Georgia should not be named from a poor view. Juveniles
      resemble females. Breeding males in late May are in full pied plumage.
    - >-
      European pied flycatcher (Ficedula hypoleuca) is a passage migrant (M) in
      Georgia on the 2024 checklist, not a breeder. Breeding males lack a
      half-collar and usually lack the second median-covert bar. Collared
      flycatcher (Ficedula albicollis) is also a passage migrant (M): breeding
      males have a complete white collar and a large white rump. Red-breasted
      flycatcher (Ficedula parva) is a breeding migrant with a rusty throat in
      adult males. Spotted flycatcher (Muscicapa striata) is streaked grey-brown
      without a large white wing patch. This atlas has a page for European pied
      flycatcher; it does not yet have separate pages for the others.
    - >-
      It is not a threat to people. Leave it alone. A black-and-white flycatcher
      in Georgian woodland in late May is more likely this species than
      European pied flycatcher, on the 2024 checklist.
facts:
  - >-
    Described by Homeyer in 1885. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows the OSME
    Region List for names: order Passeriformes, family Muscicapidae, genus
    Ficedula. The species is treated as monotypic. It has sometimes been lumped
    with collared flycatcher; current lists treat it as a full species.
  - >-
    Georgian names in use are ნახევარსაყელოიანი მემატლია, ნახევართეთრყელა
    მემატლია, and ნახევართეთრყელა ბუზიჭერია. English names include
    semi-collared flycatcher (2024 checklist: Semi-collared Flycatcher) and
    semicollared flycatcher. An older scientific name is Muscicapa
    semitorquata.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: BB; M, data quality A, global and national Red List
    LC, occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph) and RD (ringing data).
    That is country-level confirmation, not a complete regional map.
faq:
  - question: Is the semi-collared flycatcher found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia lists it as a
      confirmed breeding bird and migrant (BB; M). It is absent in winter.
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In mature woodland in the warmer months. The 2024 checklist confirms
      country-level occurrence; this page does not map every region.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Breeding males are black above and white below, with a broken white
      collar and usually a second pale wing-bar. European pied flycatcher
      lacks the half-collar. Collared flycatcher has a complete white collar.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 12–13.5 cm long at species level. A separate measured series from
      Georgia is not given on this page.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Insects and other small invertebrates, often caught in short flights from
      a perch. Diet has not been studied separately in Georgia on this page.
  - question: When is it present in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In the breeding season and on passage, not in winter, according to the
      2024 checklist. Exact arrival and departure dates for Georgia are not
      tabulated on this page.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. BB on the 2024 checklist means a confirmed breeder.
  - question: Is it on the IUCN Red List or dangerous?
    answer: >-
      It is on the IUCN Red List as Least Concern, not as a current threatened
      category. It was previously Near Threatened. The 2024 Georgian checklist
      also codes LC nationally. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. It is
      harmless. Do not catch wild birds.
