commonName: "Caspian Turtle"
location: "Southeastern Georgia"
description: >-
  The Caspian Turtle is a freshwater turtle native to Georgia and lives in the country's southeast. Unlike the Pond Slider, it has no broad red stripe behind the eye.
overview: >-
  The Caspian turtle (Mauremys caspica) is a native freshwater turtle of Georgia — neither a land tortoise nor the introduced pond slider. Its presence in Georgia is confirmed, and it is linked to the southeast of the country. The atlas map marks Tbilisi, Kvemo Kartli and Kakheti, and one turtle was also found at Lisi Lake. These places only show where it was seen. They do not show the species' full range, and they do not mean the turtle lives everywhere in these regions. [When identifying a turtle](/turtles/identifikacia), note whether you saw it by the water, what shape its shell is and whether stripes run along its neck. The Caspian turtle has no broad red stripe behind the eye.
habitat: >-
  The Caspian Turtle is a turtle tied to water: it lives in ponds, lakes and slow-flowing fresh water. In Georgia, it is linked to the southeast of the country. If you see a turtle on dry land with a high, dome-shaped shell, it is more likely to be another species.
diet: >-
  The Caspian Turtle eats both plant and animal food. Its diet can include aquatic plants, insects and young fish. Young turtles usually eat more animal food than adults.
behavior: >-
  In warmer parts of Georgia, the Caspian turtle is sometimes active even in February. This does not mean it stays active all winter across the country. It is a freshwater turtle and may often be seen in the water or near the bank. If you see one, do not pick it up: it will not help you identify it and causes the animal unnecessary stress.
conservation: >-
  The Caspian turtle is native to Georgia. Across parts of its range, changes to water bodies and pollution are documented threats to it.
interaction: >-
  The Caspian turtle is not venomous. When it meets people, it usually tries to escape or hide. If you pick it up or disturb it, it may bite to defend itself, and the bite can become infected. If a turtle bites you, wash the bite with soap and clean water. Seek medical help if there is heavy bleeding, a deep wound or signs of infection. Do not take a wild turtle home or move it to another body of water. If you come across one in nature, it is best to leave it calmly where it is.

  - question: Where does it occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-

  - question: How do I identify a Caspian turtle?
    answer: >-
      Look for the signs of a water turtle. The Caspian Turtle has a relatively flat shell and webbed feet. Pale stripes run lengthwise along its neck, and there is no broad red stripe behind the eye.
  - question: Is this a land tortoise?
    answer: >-
      No. The Caspian turtle (Mauremys caspica) is a freshwater turtle. Georgia's land tortoise, Testudo graeca, has a higher, more domed shell. So to tell the two apart, look at the shape of the shell first.
  - question: How do I tell it from a red-eared slider?
    answer: >-
      The pond slider (Trachemys scripta) was brought to Georgia from elsewhere, while the Caspian turtle is native. The pond slider's well-known form, T. s. elegans, has a broad red stripe behind the eye, and the Caspian turtle has no such stripe. So to tell the two apart, look behind the eye.
  - question: How do I tell it from a European pond turtle?
    answer: >-
      The shell, the neck and the place help you tell these two turtles apart. The European Pond Turtle (Emys orbicularis) often has a dark shell with yellow speckles. The Caspian Turtle, in turn, often has pale stripes running lengthwise along its neck. Their ranges differ too: the European Pond Turtle occurs in western and eastern Georgia, while the Caspian Turtle is linked to southeastern Georgia. So look at the shell, the neck and where you saw the turtle.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No, the Caspian Turtle is not venomous. But if you catch it, it may bite, and the bite can become infected. So do not pick it up.
  - question: What does it eat?
