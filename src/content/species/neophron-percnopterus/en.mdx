commonName: Egyptian vulture
location: Breeder and migrant in Georgia; nesting confirmed in Kakheti
description: >-
  A breeding and migratory scavenging bird in Georgia. Nesting was confirmed
  by field monitoring in Eagle Canyon, Vashlovani. Adults are white with black
  flight feathers; young birds are dark. Globally Endangered (EN).
overview: >-
  Neophron percnopterus is a small bird in the eagle family that feeds on carrion. It nests in Georgia and is also seen during migration; nesting has been confirmed in Eagle Gorge in Vashlovani, Kakheti. Adults have a white body, black flight feathers, a yellow face and a tail that narrows toward the tip; young birds are darker. Its wingspan is about 155–180 cm. It is at risk of extinction worldwide. Georgia’s 2024 national scientific assessment also places it at this level of risk, although the country’s current legal Red List places it in a lower risk category.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the species has been recorded in Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samtskhe-Javakheti and Shida Kartli. Nesting has been confirmed only in Eagle Gorge Natural Monument, within the Vashlovani Protected Areas in Kakheti. In 2023, three pairs were recorded in this gorge; two of them each fledged one chick. The species looks for food in open, often dry areas and nests on cliffs. It has also been recorded during migration in western Georgia, including near Batumi in autumn.
diet: >-
  It mainly eats carrion. The species’ diet also includes small animals with backbones and eggs.
behavior: >-
  The Egyptian vulture is migratory. In the northern part of the areas where it nests, it is found mainly from March to September. It usually starts nesting in April. It sits on its eggs for about 42 days, and the chick leaves the nest about 70–90 days after hatching. In Ardzivi Gorge, two pairs were sitting on eggs in May 2023, and each pair raised one chick that left the nest in July.
conservation: >-
  Globally, this bird is assessed as endangered. Georgia’s 2014 legal Red List places it in category VU, D1 because of its small population. A 2024 scientific assessment considers it endangered in Georgia. The legal and scientific assessments differ, but the newer scientific assessment does not automatically change the legal Red List. Threats documented internationally include poisoning, deaths on power transmission lines, and illegal killing.
interaction: >-
  The bird poses no danger to people when you simply observe it. Do not approach its nest or try to pick it up. Watch it from a distance, especially during nesting season. Poisoned bait is dangerous to birds that feed on carrion.

identification:
  summary: >-
    From below, an adult of this species clearly shows a white body and black flight feathers. Up close, its bare yellow facial skin is also visible. A young bird is brown and much darker than an adult. Both adults and young birds are small compared with other vultures and have wedge-shaped tails.
  traits:
    - >-
      An adult has a white body, black flight feathers, a yellow face, and a slender, wedge-shaped tail.
    - >-
      A young bird has dark brown feathers. It does not yet have the adult’s striking black and white coloring. Its feathers gradually lighten as it approaches adulthood. Color alone is not enough to identify a young bird—look at its body shape and tail, which narrows toward the tip.
    - >-
      [Gyps fulvus](gyps-fulvus) is much larger, with a pale sandy body and broad wings. An adult Egyptian vulture has a white body, and black feathers stand out on its wings. [Aegypius monachus](aegypius-monachus) is entirely dark and also much larger. [Gypaetus barbatus](gypaetus-barbatus) has a long, diamond-shaped tail and narrow wings; adults often have a rust-colored body and a black “beard.” To identify a dark young Egyptian vulture, look at its size and tail shape as well as its color.

faq:
  - question: Does the Egyptian vulture occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      According to 2024 data, this species breeds in Georgia and is also seen during migration. Its breeding in Eagle Gorge in Vashlovani has been directly confirmed.
  - question: Where is breeding confirmed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Eagle Gorge, in the Vashlovani Protected Areas, Kakheti.
  - question: How can I identify an adult in flight?
    answer: >-
      Its black flight feathers stand out against its white body. Its tail is wedge-shaped. Up close, its bare yellow face is also visible.
  - question: Are young Egyptian vultures white too?
    answer: >-
      No. A young bird is mostly dark brown and gradually becomes lighter as it ages. Do not identify a dark bird by its color alone.
  - question: What is its wingspan?
    answer: >-
      The wingspan of this species is approximately 155–180 cm.
  - question: When might I see one?
    answer: >-
      From spring to autumn. It nests in Georgia and is also seen during migration. It has also been recorded migrating near Batumi in autumn.
  - question: What is its status on Georgia's Red List?
    answer: >-
      The 2014 legal list records VU, D1. A 2024 scientific list assesses the national status as EN, but this does not automatically change the legal listing.
