description: >-
  The common kestrel (Falco tinnunculus) is a small falcon that breeds and occurs
  year-round in Georgia. It often hovers while hunting over open ground; its long
  tail is another useful identification clue.
overview: >-
  The common kestrel (Falco tinnunculus) is a small falcon in the family
  Falconidae. It breeds in Georgia, occurs throughout the year and is also
  recorded on passage. It is not dangerous to people in an ordinary encounter.
  Hovering over open ground, a long tail and a spotted rufous back help identify
  it.
habitat: >-
  The species is also recorded in the Lagodekhi reserve in Georgia. It
  hunts over grassland, fields and other open ground, including grassy road
  verges.
diet: >-
  It feeds mainly on small mammals, especially voles and mice. It also takes
  insects, small birds and other small animals. While hovering, it watches the
  ground and then drops towards prey.
behavior: >-
  Active by day, the kestrel hunts from a post or another high perch. It also
  hovers with rapid wingbeats and a spread tail while keeping its head nearly
  still. A sharp, repeated “kee-kee-kee” may be heard, especially near a nest.
  It nests on ledges, in building cavities or in old nests of other birds; it
  does not usually build a twig nest of its own.
conservation: >-
  Georgia's 2024 bird checklist gives the common kestrel a Least Concern
  assessment both globally and nationally. Its 2021 global assessment reports a
  decreasing population trend; this does not describe the trend in Georgia.
  The lesser kestrel (Falco naumanni) is a different species assessed as
  Critically Endangered nationally in the same checklist.
interaction: >-
  It is not dangerous to people in an ordinary encounter. Do not handle a wild
  bird or approach its nest; a disturbed bird can defend itself with its talons.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A small falcon with pointed wings and a long tail. The back is rufous with
    dark spots, the underside pale and spotted, and a dark mark sits below the
    eye. Hovering over open ground is a good clue, but not conclusive alone.
  traits:
    - >-
      Adult males have a grey head and tail, with one broad black band near the
      tail tip and a spotted rufous back. Females are browner, with several
      bars across the tail; juveniles resemble females.
    - >-
      The lesser kestrel (Falco naumanni) also occurs in Georgia. Its adult
      male has an unspotted rufous back and pale claws, whereas the common
      kestrel's claws are black. Claw colour needs a close view or a clear photo.
      Females and juveniles are often difficult to separate; hovering or distant
      colour alone is insufficient.
    - >-
      In a brief, distant view, both the common kestrel and the [peregrine falcon](falco-peregrinus)
      (Falco peregrinus) show pointed wings and dark facial marks; juvenile
      peregrines are brownish too. Peregrines are stockier, with a shorter tail,
      dark hood and broad moustache; the kestrel's tail is longer. The
      [common buzzard](buteo-buteo) (Buteo buteo) has broader wings and a
      different silhouette.

faq:
  - question: Does the common kestrel occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. It breeds in Georgia, occurs throughout the year and is also
      recorded on passage.
  - question: Where might I see one in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Look for it over open grassland and fields. It is also recorded in the
      Lagodekhi reserve.
  - question: How can I identify a common kestrel?
    answer: >-
      Look for pointed wings, a long tail, a spotted rufous back and hovering.
      Males have grey heads and tails; females and young are browner. Hovering
      alone is not a conclusive identification.
  - question: How is it different from a lesser kestrel?
    answer: >-
      Adult male lesser kestrels have unspotted backs and pale claws; common
      kestrels have black claws. Females and juveniles are often harder to
      separate.
  - question: How large is a common kestrel?
    answer: >-
      Typical species-level measurements are about 32–35 cm long with a
      71–80 cm wingspan; these are not measurements from Georgia.
  - question: What does it eat, and is it dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      Small mammals are main prey, with other small animals also taken. It
      presents no danger in an ordinary encounter. Leave wild birds and nests
      undisturbed.
