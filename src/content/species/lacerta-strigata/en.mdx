commonName: "Striped Lizard"
location: "Georgia — mainly semiarid areas"
description: >-
  The Striped Lizard (Lacerta strigata) is non-venomous and lives in semi-arid places in Georgia. Young lizards have five pale stripes, and adult males are often green.
overview: >-
  The Striped Lizard (Lacerta strigata) is harmless to people, and its presence in Georgia has been confirmed. It lives mainly in semi-arid areas. Young lizards have five light stripes along the back and sides, while adult males often have a green front part of the body. [By colour alone, you will find it hard to tell apart from other green lizards](/lizards/identifikacia).
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the Striped Lizard is found in semi-dry areas. It lives on dry grassy plains, in semi-deserts, in open areas along rivers, at the edges of meadows and fields, in windbreaks and among shrubs. It shelters near shrubs, in thick grass, in rodent burrows and among piles of stones. One lizard found in Kakheti was also checked by DNA analysis and turned out to be a Striped Lizard.
diet: >-
  This lizard mainly eats small animals without backbones, including beetles, spiders, snails, ants, flies and grasshoppers.
behavior: |-
  The Striped Lizard is active during the day. In the Caucasus, many adults come out in March after winter. In Georgia, they may appear as early as February if the winter is mild. From September, a large share of adults begin their winter rest, while some young lizards stay active in October and November too.
  Mating begins in spring. Egg laying has been observed from late spring to early summer. A female can lay eggs twice in one season, about 6–11 eggs each time.
conservation: >-
  A 2008 global assessment found that the Striped Lizard faces a low level of threat worldwide, although its overall population is declining. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. The destruction of the places where it lives is considered a threat to it.
interaction: >-
  The Striped Lizard is not venomous and is not dangerous to people. If you pick it up, it may bite to defend itself or shed its tail. In the wild, it is best to leave it where it is and not disturb it. There is no need to take a wild lizard home.

  summary: >-
    The Striped Lizard is a medium-sized lizard with well-developed legs. Young lizards are usually brownish, with five pale stripes along the body. Adult males often turn bright green, especially toward the front of the body, while the rear may stay olive-brown. Do not rely on colour alone to tell it apart from other green lizards.
  traits:
    -  |-
      The Striped Lizard has a sturdy body and well-developed legs. A distinct “collar” of scales shows on its neck, and several lengthwise rows of larger plates run along its belly. These small features are hard to see from a distance. So instead of picking the lizard up, it is better to look at its overall size, color, body pattern and the place where you saw it.
    -  |-
      Young lizards of this species are olive brown, with five narrow, pale stripes along the back and sides. Some also have small dark spots between the stripes. The stripes gradually fade as they grow.
      In adult males, the front of the body is often bright green, while in some the rear and tail stay olive brown. During the mating season, the sides of the head, throat and neck sometimes turn bluish. Females are usually paler, and spots or traces of the stripes often stay with them for longer.
    -  |-
      The [Sand Lizard](lacerta-agilis) (Lacerta agilis) can have stripes too, so it resembles the Striped Lizard, especially when young. The Sand Lizard is widespread in western Georgia, while in the east it is known mainly from places above about 600–800 m. The Striped Lizard, by contrast, is more typical of semi-arid places. You cannot reliably tell these two species apart by colour alone.
    -  |-
      [Caucasian Green Lizard](lacerta-media) (Lacerta media) is another large green lizard that may resemble the Striped Lizard. In Georgia it is found mainly in semi-arid areas in the east, and there are also isolated sightings from the northwest. It is listed as a candidate on the Georgian checklist.
      Young lizards of both species may have five pale stripes, and adult males are green. So stripe count or green colour alone will not tell them apart. An adult green Striped Lizard often keeps brownish hind legs and tail. The described adult male Caucasian Green Lizard, by contrast, has an entirely green back. Compare clear photos that show the whole lizard.
    - >-
      In Kakheti’s dry, open areas, the [Rapid Racerunner](eremias-velox) (Eremias velox) can be mistaken from a distance for a young Striped Lizard, especially in a photo taken from behind. Both have light stripes running along the body over a brownish background. The racerunner is slimmer, and young racerunners described in Georgia had distinctly reddish tails. Judge these features only from a clear photo, and do not rely on a single feature.
    -  |-
      The [Snake-Eyed Lizard](ophisops-elegans) (Ophisops elegans) lives in open, dry places in the southeast. From a distance, a brownish one with light stripes may be mistaken for a young Striped Lizard. It is smaller than the Striped Lizard. Its eyelids are fused, so in a close-up photo its eye looks as though it is always open.
      [Darevskia lizards](/lizards/darevskia) are usually smaller than the Striped Lizard and are often found on rocks and walls. They lack the five stripes of a young Striped Lizard. None of these lizards is venomous.
faq:
  - question: Does the striped lizard occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The Striped Lizard (Lacerta strigata) is a confirmed species in Georgia.
  - question: Where might I find it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Georgia's semi-arid areas. It lives in steppes and semi-deserts, in open places along rivers and near fields with shrubs. One lizard found in Kakheti was also checked with DNA analysis, and it did turn out to be a Striped Lizard.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      The Striped Lizard is medium-sized. Young lizards are usually brownish, with five light stripes running along the body. Adult males often have a green front half, while the rear stays brownish or olive. During the mating season, the throat may turn bluish. Do not rely on color alone to tell it apart from other green lizards.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      No. The striped lizard is not venomous and is not dangerous to people. If you pick it up, it may bite to defend itself or shed its tail. So if you see one in the wild, it is best to leave it where it is.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      Including the tail, an adult Striped Lizard grows to about 30 cm. Without the tail, the head and body are usually about 7–11 cm long.
  - question: How does it differ from the sand lizard?
    answer: >-
      Elevation or body pattern alone is not enough to tell the Sand Lizard from the Striped Lizard. In eastern Georgia, the Sand Lizard (Lacerta agilis) is found mainly higher up, above roughly 600–800 m, while the Striped Lizard (Lacerta strigata) occurs in semi-arid areas. Stripes won't help either: young lizards of both species may have them.
  - question: How does it differ from the Caucasian green lizard?
    answer: >-
      The Caucasian green lizard (Lacerta media) is a green lizard, larger than the striped lizard. In Georgia, it is found mainly in semi-arid areas in the east. Adults of the two species may look alike in colour, so do not rely on green alone to tell them apart.
  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      It is active during the day, mostly in the warm season. In the Caucasus, many adult lizards appear in March. In Georgia, when the winter is mild, adults may appear as early as February. Some young lizards stay active later into autumn. So if you hope to see one, choose daytime hours in the warmer months.
