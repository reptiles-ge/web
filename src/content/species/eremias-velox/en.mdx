commonName: "Rapid Racerunner"
location: "Kakheti, south-eastern Georgia"
description: >-
  The Rapid Racerunner is a fast, long-tailed lizard of Kakheti's dry steppes and semi-deserts. It has no venom and is harmless to people.
overview: >-
  The Rapid Racerunner (Eremias velox) is a fast, non-venomous lizard that has been reliably confirmed in Georgia only in the dry south of Kakheti. Georgia's 2026 species checklist lists it as a confirmed species and considers it fairly common in the country's south-east. It has been recorded in Vashlovani National Park, and lizards found in Pantishara Canyon have also been checked by DNA. Its presence in western Georgia or across the whole country has not been confirmed, so do not automatically assume that a lizard seen outside Kakheti is a Rapid Racerunner. Globally it is not threatened, but this is a worldwide assessment, not Georgia's national status. Lizards from Georgia are assigned to the subspecies E. v. caucasia. Data from one stretch of DNA suggest that the classification of the Caucasian form may be revised in future, but Georgia's current checklist still keeps it as Eremias velox.
habitat: >-
  The Rapid Racerunner is a lizard of open, warm and dry places. It probably lives in steppe, semi-desert and low shrubland, on stony or sandy ground where bare strips of earth remain between low plants. In Georgia, reliable records come from the dry south of Kakheti. Confirmed sites include Vashlovani National Park and Pantishara Canyon.
diet: >-
  It feeds on small insects and other small invertebrates. Like other fast lizards of its kind, it searches for food on open ground.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. It is most likely to be seen in sunny, dry, open places where patches of bare ground alternate with low plants. When disturbed, it quickly runs off into low vegetation or a nearby shelter.
conservation: >-
  Worldwide, the Rapid Racerunner is not under threat: it is assessed as Least Concern. Georgia's 2026 species checklist includes it as a confirmed species. Damage to dry steppes and semi-deserts is a possible threat to it, but no decline in its numbers has been confirmed in Georgia. There is no need to catch these lizards or move them elsewhere.
interaction: >-
  The Rapid Racerunner is harmless to people: it has no venom and poses no health risk. Still, do not catch it. A small lizard held in the hand may bite, drop its tail or get hurt. It is better to photograph it from a distance and leave it where you found it.

  summary: >-
    The Rapid Racerunner is a slender, long-tailed lizard of the genus Eremias that lives in dry, open places in Kakheti. Three things help most in recognising it: its body build, stripes or eye-like spots on its sides, and comparison with the Steppe-Runner. Don't rely on a single feature; look at several together.
  traits:
    - >-
      It has a slim body, fairly long legs and a tail that looks much longer than the body. This build helps it run fast across open ground. But speed alone won't tell you the species: a lizard that darts away quickly may be a different one.
    - >-
      Colour and pattern vary. Often the back is sandy, greyish or brownish with pale lengthwise stripes, and pale spots or eye-like markings show along the sides. Young lizards, as in many racerunners, may look more sharply striped.
    - >-
      Up close, the scales can help too. The large scale below the eye borders the mouth, and the fourth toe has no distinct sand fringe along its sides. Check these features on photos or on a lizard found dead. There is no need to catch a live one for this.
    - >-
      The most useful comparison is with the [Steppe-Runner](eremias-arguta). The Steppe-Runner lives in the same south-eastern region too, but it has been seen there less often and only here and there. The Rapid Racerunner usually looks slimmer, longer-tailed and faster. So when comparing the two lizards, watch the body, the tail and the way it moves.
    - >-
      The [Snake-Eyed Lizard](ophisops-elegans) may live in similar dry places, but its eye looks different, more like a snake's. The [Striped Lizard](lacerta-strigata) and other Lacerta species often have a larger or greener body, or a different build. So don't call a lizard a Rapid Racerunner just because you saw it somewhere dry: look at its eye, size, colour and build.
    - >-
      In Georgia, location is an important clue too. The Rapid Racerunner has been reliably recorded in dry southern Kakheti. So do not automatically take a lizard photographed in western Georgia, in forest or in high mountains for a Rapid Racerunner.
    - >-
      It is not venomous to people, but it is still better not to catch it for a photo. For identification, take several clear shots: from the side, from above and with its surroundings in view.
