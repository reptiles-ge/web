overview: >-
  Jojo (Paralaudakia caucasia) is the only lizard in the agama family found in Georgia. It is not venomous to humans. On this page’s records-by-region table, distribution is confirmed in Kakheti, Shida Kartli, Kvemo Kartli, Tbilisi, Mtskheta — Mtianeti, and Samtskhe — Javakheti. Imereti and Samegrelo — Zemo Svaneti have only isolated records and are not treated as distribution. It has a flattened body and a spiny tail with distinct rings, and is active during the day.
habitat: >-
  The jojo lives on dry, rocky slopes and cliffs. Its flattened body helps it slip into narrow crevices; it also shelters under stones and in burrows. In Georgia, it has been confirmed in Pia and Tmogvi in Aspindza Municipality, and around Datviskhevi and Fantishara in Dedoplistskaro Municipality. It is also known from the Iori Plateau, Kartli, Borjomi Municipality and Meskheti.
diet: >-
  The jojo eats insects, spiders and other small arthropods, as well as plant food.
behavior: >-
  The agama is active during the day. It often warms itself on rocks and cliff ledges, but quickly hides in a crevice when threatened. It may also puff up its body to defend itself. It lays eggs. Do not touch wild animals with your hands.
conservation: >-
  The 2017 global assessment places the jojo at low risk of extinction. It is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The jojo is a sturdy lizard with a flattened body, a triangular head and a spiny tail. The scales on its tail form distinct rings. A young jojo and a [Caspian gecko](tenuidactylus-caspius) may look alike in a distant photo when the eye and tail scales are unclear.
  traits:
    - >-
      Its head is flattened and triangular. Its ear opening is clearly visible.
    - >-
      The scales on the back are not all alike: they are relatively large along the spine and spikier on the sides of the body.
    - >-
      The tail is about twice as long as the body and is covered with spiny scales arranged in rings. This is one of the easiest ways to recognize an agama.
    - >-
      Its back is usually olive green or gray. It may have yellowish spots or a dark, netlike pattern on its body. During the breeding season, the male’s chest and the sides of its belly may darken.
    - >-
      Young jojo lizards may have dark spots or crossbars on the back; the Caspian gecko has a similar pattern. In a close photo, check for the gecko's vertical pupil and the jojo's spiny tail rings. Time of day alone is unreliable: the gecko is mainly nocturnal, but it has also been recorded by day.
faq:
  - question: Does the Caucasian agama occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Jojo (Paralaudakia caucasia) has been confirmed in Georgia. It is the country’s only lizard in the agama family.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Distribution is confirmed in Kakheti, Shida Kartli, Kvemo Kartli, Tbilisi, Mtskheta — Mtianeti, and Samtskhe — Javakheti, including Aspindza and Dedoplistskaro municipalities. Imereti and Samegrelo — Zemo Svaneti have only isolated records and are not treated as distribution.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous?
    answer: >-
      The agama is not venomous. It may bite if picked up, so leave it in the wild and do not touch it.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      The jojo’s body reaches up to 15 cm from the tip of its snout to the base of its tail. Its tail is about twice as long as its body.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      To identify a jojo, look for its firm, flattened body, triangular head, uneven spiny scales, and tail with spines arranged in rings. [To separate it from a gecko](/lizards/identifikacia), check the pupil and tail scales; color or time of day alone is insufficient.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Insects, spiders and other small arthropods; it also eats plant food.
  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      During the day, especially in warm weather. The jojo is often seen on cliffs and rocks.
