commonName: Eurasian wryneck
location: Kakheti, Adjara, Mtskheta — Mtianeti
description: >-
  A sparrow-sized, bark-coloured woodpecker. It breeds in Georgia and also
  occurs on passage; it usually searches for ants on the ground.
overview: >-
  The Eurasian wryneck (Jynx torquilla) is a small member of the woodpecker
  family. Unlike most woodpeckers, it often searches for food on the ground.
  According to the 2024 Georgian bird checklist, it breeds here and also passes
  through in spring and autumn. It is not dangerous to people. Look for mottled
  bark-coloured plumage and a dark stripe running from the nape down the back.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the wryneck occurs in the breeding season and on spring and autumn
  passage; the 2024 checklist does not list it as a winter visitor. The species
  favours semi-open places such as woodland edges, orchards, and parks, with
  cavities for nesting and short, ant-rich ground for feeding.
diet: >-
  It eats mainly ants, their larvae and pupae, taken from the ground with a long,
  sticky tongue. It also eats other small invertebrates. It does not normally
  peck into tree trunks to find food.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. Its voice is a repeated series of loud, nasal notes;
  drumming on wood is not typical. When threatened, it may twist its neck like a
  snake and hiss. It does not usually excavate its own nest: it uses an existing
  tree cavity or nest box and lays white eggs.
conservation: >-
  The IUCN assesses the species globally as Least Concern (LC). It is absent
  from Georgia’s official 2014 Red List. The national LC shown in the 2024 bird
  checklist comes from a revised assessment that had not yet been adopted at
  the time; it is not a status in the official 2014 list. Do not catch or keep
  wild birds.
interaction: >-
  It is not a threat to people; wild birds must not be captured.
danger: Harmless

identification:
  summary: >-
    A sparrow-sized, bark-coloured woodpecker with a dark stripe down the nape
    and back, a short bill, and a rounded tail. It does not look like the green
    or pied woodpeckers.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length about 16–18 cm at species level. NPLG gives wing 80–95 mm, tail
      60–75 mm, bill 15–16 mm, and mass 30–45 g. Wingspan is often given around
      25–30 cm. The bill is short and pointed, not a woodpecker chisel. The
      tail is rounded and relatively soft, not a stiff prop. Upperparts are
      grey-brown, mottled and barred like bark, with a darker band from the nape
      onto the back. The throat is buff with fine bars; the underparts are pale
      with small arrow-shaped marks. Legs and bill are brown. Sexes are alike
      in the field; NPLG notes the male as only slightly larger.
    - >-
      Juveniles are similar to adults, with slightly duller contrast. Flight is
      undulating. It often feeds on the ground and may cling to trunks, but it
      does not hitch up trees the way spotted woodpeckers do. The call is a
      carrying series of nasal notes.
    - >-
      [European green woodpecker](picus-viridis) is much larger, with a green
      back and red crown. [Great spotted woodpecker](dendrocopos-major) is
      black and white with red markings. The wryneck is bark-brown and often
      searches for food on the ground.
facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows the OSME
    Region List for names: order Piciformes, family Picidae, genus Jynx. IOC
    World Bird List treats two living Jynx species: this one and red-throated
    wryneck (Jynx ruficollis) of sub-Saharan Africa. The African species is
    not on the Georgian list.
  - >-
    The Georgian name is მაქცია; dictionaries also record ჩვეულებრივი მაქცია.
faq:
  - question: Is the Eurasian wryneck found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia lists it as a
      confirmed breeding bird and migrant (BB; M), with photographs and ringing
      data.
  - question: Where is it confirmed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Kakheti, Adjara, and Mtskheta-Mtianeti. There are also isolated records
      from Tbilisi, Samtskhe-Javakheti, and Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti; these alone
      do not establish distribution across each whole region.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Sparrow-sized, bark-brown and barred, with a dark stripe down the nape
      and back and a short bill. It often feeds on the ground. Green and pied
      woodpeckers in Georgia look very different. The song is a nasal
      “quee-quee-quee” series.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 16–18 cm long. NPLG gives a mass of 30–45 g.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Mainly ants and their larvae, which it searches for on the ground.
  - question: When is it present in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In the breeding season and on spring and autumn passage. The 2024 checklist
      does not list it as a winter visitor in Georgia.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 checklist lists it as a breeder. It usually uses an existing
      tree cavity.
  - question: Is it dangerous or on Georgia’s Red List?
    answer: >-
      It is not dangerous to people. It is absent from Georgia’s official 2014
      Red List and has a global LC status. Do not catch wild birds.
