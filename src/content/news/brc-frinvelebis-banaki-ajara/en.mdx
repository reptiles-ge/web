## Three days with birds

Every autumn hundreds of thousands of raptors pass over the Batumi bottleneck, and counters from all over the world count them. This year Batumi Raptor Count brought local teenagers closer to that work. Its first bird camp brought together 25 students chosen from schools in <Region id="adjara">Adjara</Region>, in two groups of 13 and 12. The camp builds on the team’s education work with local schools.

Each group’s camp lasted three days. Students heard talks about birds from experts. They watched documentaries about conservation work in other parts of the world and about poaching threats in the region. They also played team games meant to build teamwork, critical thinking and a sense of responsibility for nature.

## At the Sakhalvasho station

The liveliest part of the camp was the Sakhalvasho station. It is one of the count’s two main stations, where the team counts migrating raptors. Students watched kettles and streams of birds of prey in the sky. Through binoculars they picked out plumage details and the shape of each bird.

They also tried spotting scopes and hand counters, or “clickers”, themselves. That is how they learned how the count works. The big days of this autumn’s count are in the <NewsIndex>news</NewsIndex>.

## After the camp: two school clubs

On the last day, students split into small groups and presented what they had learned in three days. Each group did it in its own way: some made a poster, others wrote messages about protecting nature and birds.

<Figure src="https://cdn.reptiles.ge/news-brc-camp-2026-shoshitashvili-poster.jpg" />

<Figure src="https://cdn.reptiles.ge/news-brc-camp-2026-shoshitashvili-messages.jpg" />

Feedback from the students was very positive: the camp sparked their interest in birds. Two students started bird clubs at their own schools. Between the two camps, the education team met more than 25 teachers from schools around the Batumi bottleneck.
