commonName: Dice snake
location: Georgia — near water
description: >-
  The dice snake (Natrix tessellata) is a nonvenomous snake that is harmless to people. In Georgia it lives mostly near water, and its back often has dark spots in a checkerboard pattern.
overview: >-
  The dice snake (Natrix tessellata) is non-venomous and harmless to people. It lives both in water and on land and is found especially near rivers, lakes, reservoirs and other wet places. Its back often has dark, square-shaped spots. It usually lacks the yellow “collar” that sets the [grass snake](natrix-natrix) (Natrix natrix) apart. Adults are about 70–110 cm long, and a large female may grow longer than 1.2 m. The 2026 Georgian list confirms that it occurs in Georgia. The atlas lists as its [distribution areas](/snakes/gavrtseleba) only the regions with enough records: Abkhazia, Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti, Guria, Adjara, Imereti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti and Tbilisi. There are also records from Shida Kartli and Racha-Lechkhumi-Kvemo Svaneti, but these two regions are not yet counted as part of its distribution area. It is assessed as not facing a high threat and is not on Georgia’s Red List. In the semi-dry east, the dice snake is often easier to spot than the grass snake, while in some damp places in the west it is the other way around.
habitat: >-
  On this page, the water snake is listed as occurring only in the regions with enough records: Abkhazia, Samegrelo — Zemo Svaneti, Guria, Adjara, Imereti, Samtskhe — Javakheti, Mtskheta — Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti and Tbilisi. There are also sighting records from Shida Kartli and Racha — Lechkhumi — Kvemo Svaneti, but here these regions are not listed as places where it occurs. In Georgia, the water snake is usually found near water: by rivers, streams, lakes, reservoirs, canals and pools. It is often seen on stones, muddy banks or branches hanging over the water. It may also be found on farmland or in other places changed by people, if there is enough water and food nearby. In western Georgia, it also occurs in the Colchis wetlands and around water bodies on the Black Sea coast. It has been seen in Kolkheti National Park, in the Charnali Valley of Adjara, and in Tbilisi, near Lisi Lake. Compared with the common grass snake, the water snake more often chooses open, rocky and semi-dry banks. According to records from Georgia, in western Georgia it is usually found up to about 600 meters above sea level, and in eastern Georgia up to 900 meters. Rarely, it has also been seen at about 1,100 meters.
diet: >-
  It feeds mainly on fish. On the Caucasus coast, goby-like fish are an important part of its prey. It may also eat very young fish. Besides fish, it catches animals that live both in water and on land, including [frogs and toads](/amphibians/bayayi). It hunts in the water and along the shore, and swallows its prey alive.
behavior: |-
  It is active in the warm season and mostly moves around during the day. It often swims or basks in the sun on banks, on stones and on branches hanging over the water. It spends the winter near water, in dry burrows and crevices. When its winter rest begins and ends depends on the local climate and elevation.

  It usually mates in spring. It lays its eggs in summer in warm, damp places, for example in soil, rotting plants or cracks in stone walls. The young hatch in late summer or early autumn.

  When threatened, it may release a foul-smelling liquid from the rear of its body or play dead. It has no venom. If you come across one, watch it from a distance and leave it where it is.
conservation: >-
  According to an international assessment, the dice snake currently has a low risk of extinction. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List, but it is listed in Appendix II of the Bern Convention. In regions where it has been seen, it is sometimes common in suitable wet places. It may be threatened by water pollution, changes to riverbanks and shores, and killing by people, because it is often mistaken for a venomous snake.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The dice snake is a non-venomous snake that lives both in water and on land. You can recognize it by the dark, square spots on its back and by the fact that it is often seen close to water. As a rule, it has no yellow “collar” at the neck, the mark that sets the grass snake apart.
  traits:
    - >-
      Its back is olive, gray or brownish. It often has dark spots arranged in squares or in a checkerboard pattern. Behind the head there is usually no yellow “collar”, the mark that sets the grass snake apart. In some snakes the spots are faint, so you cannot identify it reliably by color alone.
    - >-
      The head is relatively narrow and elongated, and the pupil is round. A small raised line runs along the middle of the scales on the back. The belly is often yellow or orange, with black spots or dots.
    - >-
      An adult Dice snake is usually about 70–110 cm long, and a large female may be longer than 1.2 m. Females are often bigger than males. Young snakes show their body pattern more clearly.
    - >-
      The dice snake is often near water. It often swims or basks in the sun on the shore, on rocks and on branches hanging over the water. But being near water is not enough to identify it reliably. So also look for the checkerboard-like pattern on its back and the lack of a yellow “collar.”
    - >-
      The dice snake usually has no yellow or light-colored “collar” behind its head. The grass snake (Natrix natrix) often does. Do not pick up an unknown snake, not even to check for the collar.

faq:
  - question: Is the dice snake venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. The dice snake has no venom and is not dangerous to people. If you pick it up, it may release a foul-smelling liquid or go still as if it were dead. Don't pick it up: it is better to watch it from a distance and leave it where it is.
  - question: How does it differ from the grass snake?
    answer: >-
      The dice snake often has dark, chessboard-like spots on its back; the grass snake, a yellow or light-colored “collar” behind its head. Near water, the dice snake is more common than the grass snake. In the humid west, the grass snake is especially common. Their food differs too: the dice snake mainly eats fish, while the grass snake eats more animals that live both in water and on land. The spots and the collar are not always visible, so don't rely on a single feature to tell them apart.
  - question: Does the dice snake occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The dice snake is confirmed to occur in Georgia, and the 2026 Georgian species list also records it as confirmed. Only regions with enough records count as part of its range. A single sighting somewhere else does not yet make that area part of its range.
  - question: Where can I find it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      The dice snake has enough records in Abkhazia, Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti, Guria, Adjara, Imereti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Kvemo Kartli, Kakheti and Tbilisi. In Shida Kartli and Racha-Lechkhumi-Kvemo Svaneti, all that has been confirmed is that it occurs there too. It is found near rivers, lakes, streams, canals, reservoirs and other places with water.
  - question: How can I tell it from a viper?
    answer: >-
      Start with the back: the dark spots are arranged like a chessboard. There is no yellow “collar” on the neck, the head is fairly narrow, and the pupils are round. It is often in the water or near the shore. Vipers have a thicker body and vertical pupils. Still, when [identifying an unknown snake](/snakes/shxamiani-gvelis-amocnoba), do not rely on just one sign.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      An adult dice snake is usually about 70–110 cm long. A large female may be even longer than 1.2 metres.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      It mainly eats fish. Tiny young fish may also end up in its diet. Besides fish, it catches frogs and toads. It swallows its prey alive.
  - question: What should I do if I find one?
    answer: >-
      Watch it from a distance and leave it where it is. The dice snake has no venom, so there is no need to catch or kill it. If you don't know which snake it is, don't go near it, not even to look at its pupils or the shape of its head.
