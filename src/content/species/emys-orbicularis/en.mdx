commonName: "European Pond Turtle"
location: "Western and eastern Georgia"
description: >-
  A native water turtle with a low, often dark shell and yellow speckling. A high
  domed shell points more to a land tortoise; a broad red stripe behind the eye
  points to the introduced red-eared slider.
overview: >-
  The European pond turtle (Emys orbicularis) is Georgia’s native freshwater
  turtle. It is neither a land tortoise nor the introduced red-eared slider. The
  species is confirmed in Georgia; different subspecies are listed for the
  western and eastern parts of the country. It has no venom. In Caucasian
  populations the shell is often under 16 cm. IUCN lists the species globally as
  Near Threatened. It is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
habitat: >-
  The European pond turtle lives in still or slow-moving fresh water — ponds,
  lakes, marshes, oxbows, and slow rivers. It especially favours vegetated
  banks. Females move onto land to lay eggs and often nest in sunny, sandy
  places. Confirmed western Georgian localities include Poti and the Colchic
  coastal wetlands. The shore and dunes by Churia Fen are also described as
  nesting areas. The species is also confirmed in eastern Georgia, though this
  page does not have a full municipality list for the east. These sources do not
  give an exact elevation range for Georgia.
diet: >-
  The European pond turtle is omnivorous and finds much of its food in water. It
  eats invertebrates, small fish, amphibians, and plant material. At species
  level, juveniles are known to eat more animal food, while adults take
  relatively more plants. This page does not cite a detailed diet study for the
  Georgian population.
behavior: >-
  By day the European pond turtle often leaves the water and basks on a bank,
  log, or aquatic plants. When threatened, it quickly enters the water. It is
  mainly aquatic, though females come onto land to lay eggs and turtles may move
  over land between waterbodies. At species level it often spends winter on the
  bottom or in mud, but exact seasonal periods for Georgia are not established on
  this page. Females lay eggs in a terrestrial nest. Broad European data give
  one to three clutches a year, with about six to nine eggs in each; those data
  are not specific to Georgia.
conservation: >-
  The European pond turtle is native to Georgia. IUCN lists it globally as Near
  Threatened, though that assessment is old and needs updating. The 2024 European
  regional assessment also gives the species NT status. The European pond turtle
  is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Important pressures include loss
  of marshes and other aquatic habitats, shoreline change, and road mortality,
  especially when turtles move over land. The national population trend is not
  assessed on this page. Different subspecies are listed for western and eastern
  Georgia, but sources do not fully agree on Caucasian subspecies classification.
  A field photo is therefore not a reliable way to assign an individual to a
  subspecies.
interaction: >-
  The European pond turtle is not dangerous to people and has no venom. It does
  not normally attack and tries to flee into water when threatened. If picked up,
  disturbed, or cornered, however, it may bite in self-defence. The bite is
  usually not a serious danger, but it can cause pain or skin damage. European
  pond turtles often leave the water to bask, so you may see one on a bank,
  stone, or log. If you meet one in nature, leave it where it is and do not take
  it home or move it to another waterbody. If a turtle bites you, wash the wound
  well with soap and clean water. Seek medical help for heavy bleeding, a deep
  wound, or signs of infection. This is educational information and does not
  replace a medical protocol.

  traits:
    - >-
      The European pond turtle’s shell is low and oval, not high and domed. The
      colour is usually dark olive, brown, or black, often with yellow dots or
      short streaks on the surface. The lower shell is paler. One feature that
      helps separate it from the Caspian turtle is the movable plastron hinge
      found in Emys.
    - >-
      The head, neck, and legs are dark and often covered with yellowish dots or
      short streaks. There is no broad red stripe behind the eye. The feet are
      webbed. In Caucasian Kura-basin forms, shell length is usually under
      16 cm, with a described maximum of about 18 cm. This page does not have
      full separate measurements for the western Georgian population. Males are
      often smaller than females, with a longer tail and a more concave lower
      shell.
    - >-
      The European pond turtle can resemble several other turtles. The
      Mediterranean tortoise (Testudo graeca) has a high domed shell and legs
      adapted for walking on land. The introduced red-eared slider (Trachemys
      scripta) often shows a broad red stripe behind the eye. The Caspian turtle
      (Mauremys caspica), found in southeastern Georgia, often has pale
      lengthwise stripes on the neck, and its shell is usually more striped than
      speckled.
    - >-
      The European pond turtle is not venomous. If you see one in nature, watch
      it and leave it in place; there is no need to pick it up. In city ponds it
      is especially important to rule out the introduced red-eared slider,
      including in Tbilisi and Batumi.
    - >-
      The European pond turtle is native and confirmed in Georgia. Western
      Georgia is listed for Emys orbicularis orbicularis, while eastern Georgia
      is listed for E. o. persica. Named western localities include Poti and the
      Colchic coastal wetlands. The species is confirmed in eastern Georgia too,
      though the map used on this page does not cover specific eastern regions.

      absence of a red stripe behind the eye. A high domed shell points more to a
      land tortoise, while a broad red stripe behind the eye points to a
      red-eared slider.

  - question: How do I tell it from a red-eared slider?
    answer: >-
      The red-eared slider is an introduced species. Its best-known form has a
      broad red stripe behind the eye. The European pond turtle is native to
      Georgia and does not have that stripe.
  - question: How do I tell it from a Caspian turtle?
    answer: >-
      The Caspian turtle is found mainly in southeastern Georgia and often has
      pale lengthwise stripes on the neck. The European pond turtle’s shell is
      usually darker and often covered with yellow dots or short streaks.

    answer: >-
      The European pond turtle is not venomous. If picked up or disturbed, it may
      bite in self-defence. In nature, it is best not to touch it. If bitten,
      wash the wound well with soap and clean water.

    answer: >-
