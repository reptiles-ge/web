description: >-
  The Mediterranean black widow. It is recorded in Georgia; a bite is medically
  significant.
overview: >-
  The Mediterranean black widow (Latrodectus tredecimguttatus) is a venomous
  spider found in Georgia. Its bite can cause severe pain and other symptoms; if
  bitten, call 112. Published records include [Tbilisi](/regions/tbilisi),
  [Shida Kartli](/regions/shida-kartli), [Kakheti](/regions/kakheti) and
  [Samtskhe-Javakheti](/regions/samtskhe-javakheti). The female is small, with a
  body length of 7–15 mm excluding the legs. It often has red spots on its
  abdomen, though some adult females are entirely black. The species is also
  called the European black widow. Older literature sometimes treated it as a
  subspecies of Latrodectus mactans; L. tredecimguttatus is now recognised as a
  separate species.
habitat: >-
  Karakurt mainly lives in dry, open places, including steppe, semi-desert
  shrubland, and sandy or gravelly ground. It often builds its web near the ground,
  among stones and low vegetation.
diet: >-
  Karakurt mainly catches insects and other arthropods in its tangled web.
behavior: >-
  The female often hides in a retreat beside its web. Karakurt does not seek out
  people; bites usually happen when the spider is touched accidentally or trapped
  in clothing or a shoe. Do not handle it. The eggs are enclosed in silk sacs.
conservation: >-
  Karakurt has not been assessed by IUCN. It is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red
  List.
interaction: >-
  A karakurt bite can cause severe pain, muscle spasms, sweating and nausea.
  Symptoms vary in severity and may require medical care. A severe allergic
  reaction is also possible. If bitten, call 112 and seek medical care. Do not
  cut, suck or burn the bite. Do not handle the spider. For more information, see
  the [spider bite guide](/spiders/obobis-nakbeni).

identification:
  summary: >-
    Karakurt is a small, glossy black spider with a rounded, almost globose
    abdomen. The female often has red dorsal spots, although some individuals may
    be almost entirely black. The web is irregular and tangled, not a round orb.
  traits:
    - >-
      Female body length is about 7–15 mm, while males measure 4–7 mm. These
      measurements exclude the legs.
    - >-
      The female has a black, glossy cephalothorax and black legs. The abdomen is
      rounded, almost globose. Red spots often appear on the back, usually arranged
      in three longitudinal rows. The scientific name tredecimguttatus means
      “thirteen-spotted”. The spots are not always bright red; they may be orange
      or yellowish. In some individuals they are reduced or absent, and the abdomen
      can look entirely black. A red mark may also be present on the underside, but
      it does not always resemble the hourglass mark known from the American black
      widow. The male is much smaller than the female and often has whitish spots.
    - >-
      In Georgia, adult females often have reduced or completely lost red spots.
      In that case, only a thin white margin may remain where the spots were. A
      lack of red spots therefore does not rule out karakurt.
    - >-
      Karakurt spins an irregular, tangled web, usually near the ground. Its web
      does not resemble the round Argiope orb with a white zigzag. An adult female
      often hides in a retreat rather than sitting in the centre of the web.
    - >-
      The pale Latrodectus pallidus is also confirmed in Georgia: the female has
      a whitish or pale yellow abdomen. Karakurt can resemble the false black
      widow (Steatoda paykulliana), another Georgian species. It also has a black
      abdomen, but often bears a red or yellow band along its front edge. Colour
      alone is not enough for a reliable identification; do not touch the spider.
facts:
  - >-
    The accepted name is Latrodectus tredecimguttatus (Rossi, 1790). The World
    Spider Catalog treats the species as accepted and as the type species of the
    genus. The original combination was Aranea 13-guttata. Range: Mediterranean,
    Ukraine, the Caucasus, Russia (Europe to southern Siberia), Kazakhstan, the
    Middle East, Iran, Central Asia, China.
  - >-
    Latrodectus mactans (Fabricius, 1775) is a separate species. WSC: probably
    native to North America only; introduced elsewhere. The sources on this page
    do not record L. mactans in Georgia’s fauna.
  - >-
    Georgian names: ყარაყურთი and შავი ქვრივი. English: Mediterranean black
    widow / European black widow. Russian: каракурт. Turkish: karakurt.
  - >-
    Published records place karakurt in Tbilisi, near Gori and Aspindza, and in
    the Shiraki and Eldari steppes. This does not mean it is equally common
    throughout Georgia.
faq:
  - question: Is the Mediterranean black widow found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Karakurt (Latrodectus tredecimguttatus) is a confirmed species in
      Georgia. That does not mean it is equally distributed across every region of
      the country.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Published records include Tbilisi, Shida Kartli, Kakheti and
      Samtskhe-Javakheti. It mainly lives in dry, open places, often among stones
      and low vegetation.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      Karakurt is a small black spider with a rounded abdomen; red spots may be
      absent, and its web is tangled. Colour alone is not reliable for
      identification; do not touch the spider.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      Female body length is about 7–15 mm, while males are about 4–7 mm. These
      measurements do not include the legs.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Karakurt venom is medically significant, and a bite can cause severe
      pain and other symptoms. Do not handle the spider; if bitten, call 112 and
      seek medical care.
  - question: Is this Latrodectus mactans?
    answer: >-
      No. Latrodectus mactans is a North American species, while the species
      confirmed in Georgia is Latrodectus tredecimguttatus. Older literature
      sometimes confused these names.
  - question: Is it on Georgia’s 2014 Red List?
    answer: >-
      No. Karakurt is not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
