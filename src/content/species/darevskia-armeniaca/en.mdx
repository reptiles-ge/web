overview: >-
  The Armenian Rock Lizard (Darevskia armeniaca) is a harmless lizard whose females can reproduce without males. In Georgia, it lives in rocky places in the south. Its main locations are Lake Saghamo in Ninotsminda Municipality and Akhalkalaki. A 2026 study lists it as a separate species rather than a possible one. A similarly coloured lizard in these places is not always D. armeniaca, because Darevskia species often look alike. This lizard's origins are linked to D. valentini and D. mixta, while its DNA line passed down from the mother also matches D. mixta and D. dahli. These links offer no simple rule for identification: colour alone, a single photo or one feature is often not enough. To identify it reliably, consider where you saw the lizard, take a detailed photo and check the features of its scales.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the Armenian Rock Lizard is found in a small area in the south of the country. Across its range it lives in rocky mountain areas and high steppes, at about 1,500–2,500 m. The 2026 checklist names exactly two places: the lizard shown in the image was found at Lake Saghamo in Ninotsminda Municipality, and the DNA data come from Akhalkalaki. Most photo observations on the map are also from Samtskhe-Javakheti, where both places lie. Kvemo Kartli, including Tsalka Municipality, has only a few records, so it is not counted as part of the range. Similar surroundings or colour cannot, on their own, confirm that it lives anywhere else. If you see one in the wild, note the exact place and date, and take clear photos that show its back, sides and head scales well. A note like “I saw a green/brown rock lizard in southern Georgia” is not enough to identify the species.
