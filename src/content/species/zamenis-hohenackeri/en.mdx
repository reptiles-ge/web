description: >-
  A harmless, blotched ratsnake of dry rocky and grassland habitats in Georgia.
overview: >-
  The Transcaucasian ratsnake (Zamenis hohenackeri) is a nonvenomous, blotched snake confirmed in Georgia. The subspecies here is Z. h. hohenackeri. It lives mainly in dry, rocky and grassy places, and adults are often less than a metre long. Its blotches can lead to confusion with other snakes, including vipers.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the species is associated mainly with dry, rocky or grassy places in the east, but a 2021 study also confirmed it in suitable western habitats. Named records include Gomaro village in Adigeni Municipality and Boshuri; Tbilisi appears historically in the material used for the original description. This atlas maps Imereti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, Shida Kartli, Kvemo Kartli and Tbilisi. The map is still incomplete, and these records do not imply even distribution throughout Georgia.
diet: >-
  It preys mainly on rodents and birds, and also eats lizards. It restrains prey with its body. Its diet has not been separately documented in Georgia in the sources used here.
behavior: >-
  It is secretive and often active at dusk or at night, though it can also appear by day. It lays eggs; species accounts report 3–7 eggs per clutch. The sources used here do not establish breeding dates for Georgia.
interaction: >-
  It is nonvenomous. Do not pick up a wild snake: its spots can make it easy to mistake for a viper. Do not approach to inspect its pupils or head shape. If you are bitten and are unsure of the species, call 112 in Georgia.
conservation: >-
  The IUCN assessed the species globally as Least Concern (LC) in 2009. It is absent from Georgia's 2014 Red List. Neither statement describes a population trend in Georgia.

  summary: >-
    A relatively slender ratsnake. Its back usually shows two rows of dark blotches separated by a pale line; the blotches remain in adults.
  traits:
    - >-
      Its pupil is round, it has no nose horn, and its dorsal scales are mostly smooth. Pupil shape alone is not enough to identify a snake.
    - >-
      The subspecies found in Georgia usually has two rows of brownish blotches with a pale line between them; the blotches sometimes merge. A dark stripe is visible behind the eye. Color and pattern can vary among individuals.
    - >-
      Adult Aesculapian snakes (Zamenis longissimus) are often more uniform in color, while this species retains its blotches. The nose-horned viper has a nose horn and keeled scales; it is venomous. These features can be compared in photos, but do not approach a snake to inspect them.
facts:
  - >-
    Strauch described the species in 1873 as Coluber hohenackeri. Older Georgian
    and Soviet books also use Elaphe hohenackeri.
  - >-
    The 2026 checklist suggests an old, unconfirmed Tbilisi report of Elaphe
    situla may instead refer to this snake or Elaphe dione.

    answer: >-
      Yes. According to the 2026 list, biological evidence confirms its presence. The same list names the subspecies Z. h. hohenackeri for the country.
  - question: Where can it be found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Mainly in dry, rocky or grassy places. It is confirmed in the east and in suitable western habitats. Named records include Gomaro (Adigeni Municipality) and Boshuri. The atlas map is incomplete.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. This is a nonvenomous snake in the colubrid family. Do not pick it up, as it could be confused with another snake.
  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-
      Its pupils are round. Two rows of dark spots run along its back, with a pale line between them. A dark stripe runs from the eye to the corner of the mouth. The spots remain in adults.
  - question: How does it differ from the Aesculapian snake?
    answer: >-
      As an adult, the Aesculapian snake often has a more uniform color and is associated with wetter forests. The Transcaucasian snake keeps its spots into adulthood and prefers dry, rocky places. Neither is venomous.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-

  - question: Is it on Georgia's Red List?
