commonName: "Dahl's Rock Lizard"
location: "Trialeti — Kojori"
description: >-
  Dahl's Rock Lizard is non-venomous, and its females reproduce without males. In Georgia it lives mainly in forested, rocky places in the Trialeti mountains, and its colour does not set it apart from other Darevskia.
overview: >-
  Dahl's Rock Lizard (Darevskia dahli) is a nonvenomous rock lizard whose females reproduce without males and lay eggs. In Georgia it lives mainly in the wooded and rocky areas of the Trialeti Mountains. It is not dangerous to people. It looks like other rock lizards, so you cannot identify it by color alone.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, Dahl's Rock Lizard is found in rocky places, including mountain forests, meadows and even above the treeline. During a 2008–2009 survey, it was found at 830–1960 m above sea level, mainly in the Trialeti Mountains. In some places it lives alongside Portschinsky's Lizard. At one rocky site near Tbilisi, Dahl's Rock Lizard was found closer to permanent water than Portschinsky's Lizard.
diet: >-
  Rock lizards usually catch insects, spiders and other small animals without backbones. Dahl's Rock Lizard competes with Portschinsky's Lizard for food and shelter.
behavior: >-
  It is active during the day: it basks on rocks and hides in crevices. Dahl's Rock Lizard arose from a cross between the [Mixed Rock Lizard](darevskia-mixta) (D. mixta) and Portschinsky's Lizard (D. portschinskii). Its females now reproduce without males and lay eggs. Females caught in Georgia laid 1–4 eggs at a time in captivity.
conservation: >-
  Georgia’s 2014 Red List classifies Dahl's Rock Lizard as vulnerable because it lives in a small range made up of separate, scattered sites. A global assessment in 2009 found it close to becoming threatened. The same assessment names the growth of cities and road building as threats to it.
interaction: >-
  It has no venom and is not dangerous to people. Still, do not pick it up: its tail breaks off easily. If you come across one, watch it from a distance and leave it where it is. You cannot identify the species by color alone, so take photos of its head and side.

identification:
  summary: >-
    Dahl's Rock Lizard is a small rock lizard with a slender body and a flattened head. You cannot tell it apart from [other Darevskia species](/lizards/darevskia) by the color of its back. Two things matter for identifying it: where you saw it, and what the scales on its head and in front of its anus look like. But do not pick it up to check its scales.
  traits:
    - >-
      It has a small, slender body, a flattened head and well-developed legs. Adult lizards found in Georgia usually have a body length of about 5.4–5.8 cm. The largest recorded length is about 63 mm. The tail is longer than the body and breaks off easily, so do not pick the lizard up, not even to count its scales.
    - >-
      The back is gray, brownish or olive, often with dark spots or a netlike pattern. The belly may be yellowish. Other rock lizards have similar coloring, so you cannot tell the species by color alone. Photos taken in Kojori in 2026, however, also show the head scales and the plates in front of the anus.
    - >-
      Dahl's Rock Lizard has two plates in front of the anus, while the [Armenian Rock Lizard](darevskia-armeniaca) (D. armeniaca) has one broad plate. The Armenian Rock Lizard is larger: its body length reaches about 74 mm. It also occurs in southern Georgia, around Ninotsminda, Saghamo and Akhalkalaki. Do not pick the lizard up to look at these plates.
    - >-
      [Portschinsky's Lizard](darevskia-portschinskii) (D. portschinskii) is often found on the same rock as Dahl's Rock Lizard. Individuals of both species found in Georgia are similar in size. Portschinsky's Lizards include both males and females, while almost all Dahl's Rock Lizards are female. To tell them apart reliably, people look at the scales on the head and features around the anus. You cannot tell them apart by colour alone.

  - >-
    The name dahli honours the zoologist Sergei Konstantinovich Dahl. The
    Georgian name დალის ხვლიკი uses the same eponym.
