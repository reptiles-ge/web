overview: >-
  The red fox (Vulpes vulpes) is a mammal in the dog family (Canidae). It is the
  largest species of the true fox genus (Vulpes) and one of the world's most
  widely distributed predatory mammals. In Georgia it is a native and year-round
  resident species.

  At species level, head–body length is about 45–90 cm, the tail is 30–55 cm,
  and mass often ranges between 3–14 kg.

  It has no venom. The main possible risk to people is a bite and infectious
  disease risk, including rabies. IUCN assesses the red fox globally as Least
  Concern, and it is not included in Georgia's 2014 Red List.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, red foxes occur in many kinds of places: woodland edges, scrub,
  grasslands, agricultural landscapes, foothills, and the edges of settlements.

  The species' global habitat is very broad and includes forests, open
  landscapes, mountains, coasts, and city outskirts. IUCN notes occurrence up to
  about 4,500 m across the whole range, but a Georgia-specific elevation range
  is not defined by the sources used on this page.

  The named Georgian locality on this page is the Iori floodplain. A complete
  municipality list and regional map are not presented here, so do not assume
  the same density in every part of the country.
diet: >-
  The red fox is an opportunistic omnivore. Small mammals, especially rodents,
  make up an important part of its diet. It also eats birds, insects, fruit, and
  carrion, and near settlements it may use food waste.

  In unprotected yards it may sometimes take poultry. A separate quantitative
  diet study for the Georgian population is not presented by the sources used on
  this page, so the exact local diet should not be treated as measured here.
behavior: >-
  The red fox is mainly active at dusk and at night, though it can also appear
  by day, especially in quiet places or when food is abundant. Daytime activity
  does not by itself indicate rabies.

  It may use its own earth or settle in another animal's abandoned burrow; rock
  crevices can also serve as shelter. A family group forms during the breeding
  period, while at other times red foxes often hunt alone.

  At species level, gestation lasts about 51–53 days and often 4–6 cubs are
  born, mostly in spring. Those figures refer to the broad range, not a separate
  study of the Georgian population.

  Fox tracks resemble dog tracks, though they are often more compact. A white
  tail tip is typical, but not every individual has one.
conservation: >-
  IUCN assesses the red fox globally as Least Concern, and the overall
  population trend is assessed as stable. The species is not included in
  Georgia's 2014 Red List.

  In Georgia, the red fox is also included on the list of hunting species. The
  current hunting season and other conditions are defined by official rules and
  are not copied on this page as live data.

  Local pressures can include hunting, road mortality, and conflict involving
  poultry. A modern quantitative trend for the Georgian population is not
  separately assessed by the sources used on this page.
interaction: >-
  The red fox is not venomous, but it can bite. In Georgia it is named, together
  with other wild predators, as a possible source of rabies. Do not feed a wild
  fox or pick it up. Rabies vaccination for domestic dogs is important.

  traits:
    - >-
      At species level, head–body length is about 45–90 cm, tail 30–55 cm,
      shoulder height about 35–50 cm, and mass often ranges between 3–14 kg.
      Males are usually slightly larger than females.

      A separate broad measurement series for the Georgian population is not
      presented by the sources used on this page. The red fox has a slender
      muzzle, erect ears, and vertically oval pupils.
    - >-
      Typical colour is rusty-red on the back, while the throat and belly are
      paler. The backs of the ears and the lower legs are usually black.

      The tail is long and bushy. A white tip is a good identification mark
      when visible, but not every individual has one; some foxes have a dark
      tail tip. Darker and other colour forms also occur.
    - >-
      The golden jackal (Canis aureus) is taller and heavier than a red fox, and
      its colour is usually more uniformly yellowish-grey. The tail is
      relatively shorter and lacks the white tip typical of many red foxes.

      The wolf (Canis lupus) is much larger and longer-legged. Compared with a
      dog, a fox usually has a finer muzzle, smaller tracks, and a straighter
      line of travel.

      The corsac fox (Vulpes corsac) is recorded from dry areas of the northern
      Caucasus, but by the data used on this page it is not treated as part of
      Georgia's fauna.
    - >-
      The red fox is not venomous. However, a bite or dangerous contact with
      saliva can be associated with rabies risk. Do not touch a wild fox by hand,
      especially if it is unusually fearless, disoriented, or aggressive.

      Seeing a fox during the day does not by itself mean disease; a healthy fox
      may also be active during daylight hours.
facts:
  - >-
    Linnaeus described it in 1758, originally as Canis vulpes. The accepted
    name is Vulpes vulpes. Order Carnivora, suborder Caniformia, family
    Canidae, genus Vulpes.
  - >-
    Caucasus literature historically names several subspecies, including
    V. v. caucasica (Dinnik, 1914) and V. v. alpherakyi (Satunin, 1906).
    Subspecies counts and boundaries are disputed; do not assign a Georgian
    individual to a subspecies in the field with confidence.
  - >-
    In Georgian the usual names are მელა and ჩვეულებრივი მელა; some texts use
    წითელი მელა. In English: red fox; older literature also says common fox.
    Cross fox and silver fox are colour morphs of the same species.
  - >-
    IUCN lists Georgia as native. It is in Bukhnikashvili and Kandaurov’s 2002
    annotated mammal list. Eliava et al. 2007 list it as LC. That is
    country-level confirmation, not a regional map.

      shorter-tailed, while the wolf is much larger and longer-legged than a red
      fox.
