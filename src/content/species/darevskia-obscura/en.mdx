description: >-
  A rock lizard recorded in Georgia and treated as a candidate species in the 2026 checklist; colour alone cannot identify it.
overview: >-
  The Meskhetian rock lizard (Darevskia obscura) occurs in Georgia. A 2026 Georgian list places local lizards previously included in a broader D. rudis group under the name Meskhetian rock lizard. This is why older sources may also use the name D. rudis. Treating it as a separate species from D. rudis rests mainly on data from mitochondrial DNA, one type of DNA. Whether genes can pass between the two groups has not yet been studied. The list therefore treats it as a possible separate species: its separation from D. rudis needs checking, not its presence in Georgia. The “Records by Region” table on this page’s map brings together public observations recorded under the name Darevskia rudis obscura and locations added without naming an observer. According to the table, its presence is confirmed in Samtskhe–Javakheti and Adjara. There is one record from Shida Kartli, but its presence across that region is not considered confirmed.
habitat: >-
  The 2026 study names several specific places in Georgia: the Meskheti Range in Akhaltsikhe Municipality, Rveli in the Baniskhevi Valley, Mount Saaghdgomo near Akhalkalaki, and the Borjomi Valley. Samples for another study of reproduction were collected near the Kirkhbulakhi River. A 2026 list treats D. obscura in a broad sense and includes parts of Trialeti, Meskheti, Javakheti, and the Enguri basin in its range. This is a general area; it does not mean samples were collected at every one of those places. The map on this page does not treat that general area as confirmed distribution: it counts a region as part of the species’ range only when its status is confirmed in the records table.
behavior: >-
  In Georgia, 13 females caught near the Kirkhbulakhi River laid 2–5 eggs at a time while temporarily kept in a laboratory. This small group came from just one location, so it cannot tell us how many eggs the species generally lays at a time or what time of year it breeds elsewhere in Georgia.
conservation: >-
  In the 2026 list, “candidate” refers to how D. obscura is identified as a species. It does not say whether the species is at risk.

[To identify the lizard](/lizards/identifikacia), note exactly where you saw it and photograph it from several angles, especially its head and scales. If these features are not visible, label it “Darevskia sp.” (the exact species is uncertain) or “probably D. obscura.”

faq:
  - question: Does it occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2026 Georgian list includes records and specimens of it. “Candidate” means further checking is needed to determine whether it is a separate species.
  - question: Where has it been recorded?
    answer: >-
      The places named are the Meskheti Range in Akhaltsikhe Municipality, the Rveli/Baniskhevi Valley, Saagdgomo Mountain near Akhalkalaki, and the Borjomi Valley. According to the records on this page, its presence is confirmed in Samtskhe-Javakheti and Adjara. A single record from Shida Kartli and the general mention of the Enguri basin in the list are not counted as evidence of its range on this map.
  - question: Why is it a candidate species?
    answer: >-
      It is distinguished from D. rudis mainly by differences in mitochondrial DNA. Where the two forms meet, it has not yet been studied whether they can exchange genes. It has therefore not been conclusively confirmed that they are separate forms.
  - question: How do I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Do not rely on color or a single partial photo. Note exactly where you saw the animal, and photograph its head and scales from several angles. If you are unsure of the species, record it as Darevskia sp. or probable D. obscura.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      No. This atlas labels the species as harmless and does not consider it venomous.
