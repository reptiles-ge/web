commonName: Darevsky's viper
location: Samtskhe — Javakheti
description: >-
  Darevsky's viper is a small venomous snake. It lives in the high mountains of southern Georgia, in Javakheti, Erusheti and western Trialeti.
overview: >-
  Darevsky's viper (Vipera darevskii) is a small venomous snake. In Georgia, it lives in the high mountains of the Lesser Caucasus, on stony, grassy slopes above 2,000 metres. It is found on the Javakheti Range, in Erusheti and the Akhaltsikhe Highlands, and on the Abul-Samsari Range and in western Trialeti. Under the current assessment, it faces an extremely high risk of extinction. That assessment is based on data from a time when the snake was known from a much smaller area. It has since been found in new places, but the assessment has not yet changed.
habitat: >-
  On high mountain meadows, it shelters in gaps between rocks. In Georgia, it has been found in piles of small stones, and on the Akhaltsikhe upland also in a rocky ravine. At Madatapa and Gumbati, it has also been found among piles of large volcanic rocks. In these two places it has been seen at 2,200–2,600 metres above sea level, but this does not mean it occurs only at these elevations.
diet: >-
  Adult vipers eat [rock lizards](/lizards/darevskia) and sometimes small rodents.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day and shelters among rocks. It spends the winter among rocks too, but deep down. It gives birth to live young.
conservation: >-
  Darevsky's viper has been assessed as facing an extremely high risk of extinction. That assessment rests on old, scarce information about where the snake lives. A study published in 2025 confirmed that it also occurs in other places in Georgia. So the assessment's figure that the snake lives in an area of less than 100 km² is outdated. The assessment itself has not changed yet. Overgrazing remains a threat: when the grass is damaged, the snake loses shelter, and the animals it feeds on are harmed too.

faq:
  - question: Is Darevsky's viper dangerous to humans?
    answer: >-
      Yes, it is venomous. You cannot tell in advance how serious a bite will be. So if it bites you, call 112 immediately.
  - question: Where is it found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Georgia, it lives in the mountains above 2,000 metres. In Samtskhe-Javakheti, it has been found on the Javakheti Range, near Madatapa, in Erusheti and on the Akhaltsikhe Upland. More recent reports also place it on the Abul-Samsari Range and in western Trialeti.
  - question: How does it differ from Dinnik's viper?
    answer: >-
      These two vipers live in different mountains. Darevsky's viper is found in the high mountains of the Lesser Caucasus, while [Dinnik's viper](vipera-dinniki) lives in the Greater Caucasus. Small size and body markings alone are not enough to identify the species. So pay attention to where you saw the snake as well.
  - question: Why is it critically endangered?
    answer: >-
      Under the current assessment, Darevsky's viper faces an extremely high risk of extinction. That assessment relies on information from a time when only a small area where the snake occurs was known. The places known by 2025 cover a much larger area. Even so, overgrazing still damages the places where it lives. Its level of threat therefore needs to be reassessed.
  - question: How do I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Three features can help you recognize it: its small size, a dark zigzag and the place where it lives, the rocky meadows high in the Lesser Caucasus. One feature alone is not enough to tell it apart from similar vipers, so take all three into account together. And do not approach a snake you do not recognize.
  - question: What should I do if bitten?
    answer: >-
      Call 112 immediately and seek medical attention. You can read more in the [snakebite guide](/snakes/gvelis-nakbeni).
