commonName: Darevsky's viper
location: Samtskhe — Javakheti
description: >-
  A highland viper of southern Georgia, also recorded in the Abul-Samsari and western Trialeti ranges.
overview: >-
  Darevsky’s viper (Vipera darevskii) is a small venomous snake. In Georgia, it occurs in the highlands of the Lesser Caucasus: on the Javakheti Range, in Erusheti and on the Akhaltsikhe Highland, as well as in the Abul-Samsari Range and western Trialeti. It lives on rocky, grassy slopes above 2,000 m. The IUCN still lists it as Critically Endangered (CR), but that assessment used a much smaller known range before the newer records were found.
habitat: >-
  Gaps between rocks provide shelter in high mountain meadows. In Georgia, this viper has been found beyond fine scree: among large volcanic rocks at Madatapa and Gumbati, and in a rocky ravine on the Akhaltsikhe Highland. Records from Madatapa and Gumbati span 2,200–2,600 m above sea level; this is not the full elevation range of the species.
diet: >-
  Adult vipers eat rock lizards and, sometimes, small rodents.
behavior: >-
  It is active during the day and shelters among rocks. It gives birth to live young. It spends the winter deep among rocks.
conservation: >-
  The IUCN’s Critically Endangered (CR) assessment rests on older, limited range data. A study published in 2025 confirmed further sites in Georgia and found the assessment’s range estimate of under 100 km² outdated. The status has not yet changed. Overgrazing remains a threat: damage to grass cover removes shelter for the viper and harms its prey.

faq:

    answer: >-
      It is venomous. The severity of a bite cannot be predicted in advance; call 112 immediately.
  - question: Where is it found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In Samtskhe–Javakheti, it is confirmed on the Javakheti Range near Madatapa, in Erusheti and on the Akhaltsikhe Highland. Newer records also include the Abul-Samsari Range and western Trialeti. In Georgia it occurs above 2,000 m.
  - question: How does it differ from Dinnik's viper?
    answer: >-
      Darevsky’s viper occurs in the Lesser Caucasus highlands, while Dinnik’s viper is found in the Greater Caucasus. Small size and pattern alone do not establish the species.
  - question: Why is it critically endangered?
    answer: >-
      The current IUCN CR assessment used an older, narrow range estimate. New research shows a much wider known range, while overgrazing still damages its habitat. The status needs reassessment.

    answer: >-
