overview: >-
  The Colchis slow worm (Anguis colchica) is a legless anguid lizard. In Georgia
  it is often mistaken for a snake or the European glass lizard, although it is
  harmless to people. Georgia's 2026 checklist of amphibians and reptiles treats
  it as a candidate species in the widespread western Eurasian A. fragilis
  complex. In 2010 the Caucasian form (A. f. colchica) was raised to species
  rank, but the
  checklist does not treat that rank as settled. According to the checklist, the
  Colchis slow worm probably occurs apart from A. graeca and A. fragilis. Its
  cytochrome b DNA sequence differs from those relatives by 6.5–8%. The
  checklist figure comes from Nigoeti; DNA barcodes come from the Mtisdziri
  environs, Baniskhevi and Lanchkhuti.

behavior: >-

  - question: Is the slow worm found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. In this atlas the slow worm is on Georgia's herpetofauna checklist,
      but as a candidate species. The sources name Nigoeti, the Mtisdziri area,
      Baniskhevi and Lanchkhuti. These localities are not a full range list:

  - question: Is the slow worm a snake?
    answer: >-
      No — it is a legless lizard. Unlike a snake, it has movable eyelids. If
      you are not sure, there is no need to handle it to check.
  - question: How do I identify the slow worm?
    answer: >-
      Look for movable eyelids, a smooth legless body and no clear fold along
      the side. Colour alone is not enough for identification.
  - question: How does it differ from the glass lizard?
    answer: >-
      The European glass lizard (Pseudopus apodus) is a large legless lizard
      with a clear side fold and often a yellow belly. The slow worm is smaller
      and smoother. Both are harmless.
