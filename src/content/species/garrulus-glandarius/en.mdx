commonName: Eurasian jay
location: Year-round resident and breeder in Georgia
description: >-
  A medium-sized woodland corvid with a black crown in the Caucasus form and a
  bright blue wing panel. Confirmed in Georgia as a year-round resident and
  breeder on the 2024 national bird checklist.
overview: >-
  The Eurasian jay (Garrulus glandarius) is a crow-family bird that lives in
  Georgia year-round and breeds there. Look for its blue, black-barred wing
  patch, black moustache and white rump. It is not dangerous to people. The
  bird is also called ჯაფარა in Georgian. The Caucasian form found in Georgia
  has a mostly black crown.
habitat: >-
  The jay is a woodland bird found in deciduous and mixed forest, especially
  oak woods, as well as forest edges, parks and wooded settlements. In Georgia,
  its presence is documented in Algeti National Park and Tbilisi, among other
  places. Treeless highlands are not typical habitat.
diet: >-
  Jays eat acorns, other seeds, fruit and insects; they sometimes take the eggs
  and young of smaller birds. In autumn they hide acorns in soil and fallen
  leaves and later retrieve some of them. Acorns left behind may sprout,
  helping oaks spread.
behavior: >-
  Jays are active by day. Their call is loud and harsh, and they also mimic
  other birds. In flight, the white rump and blue wing patch are easy to see.
  They nest in trees or tall shrubs.
conservation: >-
  The species was assessed globally as Least Concern by IUCN in 2017. The
  2024 scientific checklist of Georgia's birds gives the same assessment for
  Georgia; this does not establish its legal protection status.
interaction: >-
  The Eurasian jay is not dangerous to people. Do not catch wild birds.

identification:
  summary: >-
    The jay has a pinkish-brown body, black moustache, white rump and a blue,
    black-barred wing patch. In Georgia its crown is mostly black. Magpies and
    ravens lack that wing patch.
  traits:
    - >-
      Body length, from bill to tail tip, is about 35 cm; wingspan is
      52–58 cm and mass is about 140–190 g. The body is pinkish-brown, with a
      black moustache mark beside the pale throat. In flight, the white rump
      and blue, black-barred wing
      patch stand out. The patch is the most useful identification mark when
      the wing can be seen clearly.
    - >-
      The Caucasian form found in Georgia, G. g. krynicki, has a mostly black
      crown. Crown colour alone is not enough to identify the subspecies.
      Males and females look alike.
    - >-
      The Eurasian magpie (Pica pica) is black and white with a long tail. The
      northern raven (Corvus corax) is much larger and all dark. Neither has
      the jay's blue, black-barred wing patch.
