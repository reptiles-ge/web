  The Colchis slow worm (Anguis colchica) is a legless lizard, harmless to people, that is often mistaken for a snake or a European glass lizard. It is known from Georgia and remains a candidate species in the 2026 checklist.
overview: >-
  The Colchis slow worm (Anguis colchica) looks like a snake, but it is a legless lizard and harmless to people. In Georgia it is often mistaken for a snake or a European glass lizard, so if you see one, don't take it for a snake. It belongs to the anguid family and is part of a group of close relatives of A. fragilis that is widespread in western Eurasia. The Caucasian form (A. f. colchica) was split off as a separate species in 2010. Georgia's 2026 checklist, however, still lists it as a candidate species: its status as a separate species is not yet confirmed. It probably lives apart from A. graeca and A. fragilis, and one section of its DNA differs from theirs by 6.5–8%. In Georgia it has been photographed in Nigoeti, and DNA samples were taken from the Mtisdziri area, Baniskhevi and Lanchkhuti.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the Colchis Slow Worm is known from Nigoeti, the Mtisdziri area, Baniskhevi and Lanchkhuti. It has also been photographed in Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park. These places are not a complete list of its range. And a sighting in one place does not mean it lives throughout the whole region. So if you see a small, smooth, snake-like legless lizard in Georgia, do not identify it by the place alone: check its body features too.

behavior: >-
  The Colchis Slow Worm is not a snake. Unlike snakes, it has movable eyelids. It differs from the European glass lizard by its smoother body and the lack of a visible fold along its side. If you see one on a road, a path or near a yard, do not catch it or try to check by hand whether it is a snake.
conservation: >-

"Candidate" is not a threat category: it only concerns whether the animal is a distinct species. So this status says nothing about whether the Colchis slow worm is under threat.
