commonName: Common buzzard
location: Year-round resident, migrant and winter visitor in Georgia
description: >-
  The common buzzard is a medium-sized, broad-winged raptor with a fairly short
  tail. Plumage is highly variable: some birds are almost entirely dark brown,
  while others are much paler.

  In Georgia it is a year-round resident, though additional birds occur during
  migration and winter. It is most often confused with European honey-buzzard,
  long-legged buzzard, or rough-legged buzzard. It is not an eagle.
overview: >-
  The common buzzard (Buteo buteo) is a medium-sized bird of prey in the hawk
  family (Accipitridae). In Georgia it is a year-round resident and also occurs
  during passage migration and winter.

  At species level body length is about 40–58 cm, with a wingspan of about 109–140
  cm. It is not a direct threat to people.

  IUCN lists the common buzzard as Least Concern. The 2024 checklist of the birds
  of Georgia also gives it national LC status.

  People sometimes call it a “common eagle”, but a buzzard is not an eagle: it
  belongs to the genus Buteo.
habitat: >-
  The common buzzard occurs in Georgia throughout the year, also takes part in
  seasonal migration, and remains in winter. It prefers semi-open habitats:
  woodland edges, tree-covered slopes, meadows, farmland, and foothills where it
  can hunt over open ground.

  In autumn, especially impressive passage is documented in the Batumi migration
  corridor. Large numbers of steppe buzzards (B. b. vulpinus) are counted each
  year from the Sakhalvasho and Shuamta watchpoints. Long-term data show hundreds
  of thousands of individuals passing in an average season, with the peak mainly
  in late September and early October.

  These numbers refer to the migratory stream, not to the size of Georgia’s
  breeding population. This page does not have a complete distribution map by
  region or municipality.
diet: >-
  The common buzzard is an opportunistic predator. Its main food is often small
  mammals, especially mouse-like rodents, though it also catches birds, reptiles,
  amphibians, and large insects. It sometimes feeds on carrion too.

  When hunting, it often sits in a tree, on a pole, or on another raised perch and
  watches the ground. Over open slopes it often circles on broad wings.
behavior: >-
  The common buzzard is active by day. Its typical call is sharp, drawn-out, and
  sometimes like a cat-like “pee-ow”.

  In flight it sometimes holds its broad wings in a shallow V. When hunting, it
  often sits for a long time in one place and then drops quickly onto prey; it
  may also hover in the air.

  It builds a stick nest in a tree or sometimes on a cliff. At species level
  clutches often contain 2–4 eggs, though those data come from the broader range,
  not specifically from Georgian nest studies.

  In Georgia the species is a breeding resident. In autumn especially large
  numbers of the steppe form pass through the Batumi corridor, and winter presence
  in the country is also confirmed.
conservation: >-
  IUCN lists the common buzzard globally as Least Concern. The 2024 checklist of
  the birds of Georgia also gives it national LC status. The common buzzard is not
  included on Georgia’s 2014 Red List.

  The overall trend of the Georgian population is not assessed separately on this
  page. Long-term counts in the Batumi migration corridor describe migratory birds,
  not the state of Georgia’s breeding population.

  Illegal shooting of raptors in the Batumi corridor is a documented pressure. Do
  not catch wild buzzards.
interaction: >-
  The common buzzard is not a threat to people if you do not disturb or pick it
  up. It has strong talons and may injure you if captured. Do not catch wild birds
  of prey.

  traits:
    - >-
      The common buzzard is about 40–58 cm long, with a wingspan of about 109–140
      cm. Mass is often about 800–1200 g. Females are usually larger than males.

      The head is rounded, the bill relatively short, the wings broad, and the
      tail medium or short. The underwing often shows a clear dark carpal patch.

      Plumage is especially variable, from almost dark brown to very pale forms.
      Male and female are difficult to separate by colour alone.
    - >-
      The common buzzard has several forms. The steppe buzzard (Buteo buteo
      vulpinus) is usually smaller and more agile and is often more rufous. This
      is the form that passes through the Batumi migration corridor in large
      numbers in autumn.

      The Caucasian form, often referred to as B. b. menetriesi, is described in
      some guides as a larger and relatively paler bird. Young birds are often more
      barred or scaly below.

      Exact subspecies identification in nature is not always simple, so species-level
      field identification is more reliable.
    - >-
      European honey-buzzard (Pernis apivorus) can look similar to common buzzard,
      but it has a longer tail, a relatively smaller pigeon-like head, and flatter
      wings in flight.

      Long-legged buzzard (Buteo rufinus) is often paler or more rufous, with
      longer legs and a tail that is often pale with a dark terminal band.

      Rough-legged buzzard (Buteo lagopus) occurs in Georgia mainly during migration
      and winter. It often has a white tail with a dark terminal band and feathered
      legs.

      Golden eagle (Aquila chrysaetos) is much larger than common buzzard, while
      Eurasian sparrowhawk (Accipiter nisus) is much smaller, with a long tail and
      short broad wings.
    - >-
      The common buzzard is not a direct threat to people. Do not pick it up: its
      strong talons may injure you.

      In Georgia, a medium-sized brown raptor seen by a road, over a meadow, or at
      a woodland edge is often a buzzard rather than an eagle.

facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758 as Falco buteo. The 2024 Georgian checklist
    follows the OSME Region List for names: order Accipitriformes, family
    Accipitridae, genus Buteo. IOC World Bird List treats Buteo buteo as this
    species; Japanese and Himalayan forms are often split and are not on the
    Georgian list.
  - >-
    Two forms matter in Georgia: B. b. vulpinus (steppe buzzard; long-distance
    migrant, the Batumi autumn mass) and B. b. menetriesi (Caucasian; often
    treated as resident from Crimea to northern Iran). NPLG and Georgian
    dictionaries use ჩვეულებრივი კაკაჩა. Subspecies identification in the field
    needs experience and is not required on this page.
  - >-
    Georgian is ჩვეულებრივი კაკაჩა, also კაკაჩა. English names include Common
    Buzzard (2024 checklist) and Eurasian Buzzard (BirdLife). The folk name
    ჩვეულებრივი არწივი appears in searches; this page is the buzzard, not an
    eagle.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: YR-R; M; WV, data quality A, global and national Red
    List LC, occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph). That is
    country-level confirmation plus the named Batumi counts, not a complete
    regional map.
faq:
  - question: Is the common buzzard found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The common buzzard (Buteo buteo) is a confirmed species in Georgia.
      According to the 2024 checklist, it is a year-round resident and also occurs
      during migration and winter.
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      The common buzzard may be seen throughout the year near woodland edges,
      tree-covered slopes, meadows, and farmland.

      In autumn, especially large numbers pass through the Batumi migration corridor.
      One of the main watch areas is around Sakhalvasho and Shuamta.
  - question: Is it an eagle? Why do people call it ჩვეულებრივი არწივი?
    answer: >-
      No. The common buzzard (Buteo buteo) is not an eagle. It belongs to the genus
      Buteo, while eagles belong to other genera, including Aquila.

      The official Georgian name is ჩვეულებრივი კაკაჩა. The folk name “common
      eagle” is often used because of its size and soaring silhouette, but it is
      taxonomically incorrect.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Look for medium size, broad wings, a fairly short tail, and often a dark
      carpal patch on the underwing. Plumage is very variable, so colour alone is
      not reliable.

      European honey-buzzard has a longer tail and a relatively smaller head, while
      eagles are usually much larger.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      The common buzzard is about 40–58 cm long, with a wingspan of about 109–140
      cm. Mass is often 800–1200 g. The steppe form is usually smaller.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      The common buzzard mainly feeds on small mammals, though it also catches
      other small vertebrates and invertebrates.
  - question: When is it present in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      The common buzzard occurs in Georgia throughout the year. In autumn, especially

    answer: >-
