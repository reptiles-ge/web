description: >-
  A slim, long-tailed black, white and grey wagtail. Confirmed in Georgia as a
  year-round resident on the 2024 national bird checklist.
overview: >-
  The white wagtail (Motacilla alba) is a slim, long-tailed wagtail confirmed in
  Georgia as a year-round resident on the 2024 national bird checklist. The 2024
  annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia (Sandgrouse) codes it YR-R: a
  breeder present throughout the year, and lists the taxon as Motacilla alba
  alba. Seasonality is classed as high-quality (code A). Occurrence quality is
  high (H), with photographs. SABUKO’s public checklist and the Iliauni SPA
  country list also code it YR-R, M. This page follows the peer-reviewed 2024
  appendix for country status (YR-R) and reports the extra migrant code as a
  disagreement. Adults are about 16.5–19 cm long at species level. It is not a
  threat to people. IUCN lists the species as Least Concern. The 2024 checklist
  assigns LC globally and nationally. The usual Georgian name is თეთრი
  ბოლოქანქარა.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia look for it in open or semi-open ground, often near water or
  buildings: river edges, roadsides, yards, parks, farms, roofs, and car parks.
  At species level it feeds on bare or short-cover surfaces where it can see and
  chase insects. Dense closed forest is not typical. A named Georgian locality
  is the Chorokhi Delta south of Batumi (Adjara): SABUKO’s 2018 spring survey
  counted the species on several dates. Iliauni SPA N 16 (Batumi) lists it as
  BB, M; SPA N 17 (Kolkheti) lists it as YR-R. The 2024 checklist confirms
  country-level status but does not publish a municipality gazetteer. Do not
  assume it occurs evenly across Georgia. A separate national elevation band for
  Georgia is not published here; the atlas map colours only administrative units
  with a named locality in a cited source.
diet: >-
  There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page. Across the range it is
  insectivorous: insects and their larvae, spiders, and other small
  invertebrates on or near the ground. It often runs, wags the tail, then makes
  a short dash at prey. That is species-level feeding, not a measured Georgian
  menu.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. On the ground it walks and runs; after landing and when
  hunting it pumps the long tail up and down. Flight is undulating; in flight it
  often gives a short, sharp contact call, roughly “tsilip” or “chissick”. The
  recording on this page is xeno-canto XC501786 (Hannu Varkki, Helsinki;
  CC BY-SA 4.0). The nest is a rough cup in a crevice or cavity — riverbank,
  wall, bridge, or building. Species-level clutches are often about four to six
  eggs; incubation is about 12–16 days, and young leave the nest at about 12–15
  days. Those figures are from the wider range, not a Georgian nest sample on
  this page. The 2024 checklist treats the species as a breeding resident in
  Georgia. Large winter roosts in towns are described elsewhere; they are not
  measured here for Georgia.
conservation: >-
  BirdLife International / IUCN assess Motacilla alba as Least Concern. The
  range and population are very large. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List
  (Government Resolution No. 190). The 2024 Georgian bird checklist assigns
  global and national Red List category LC. A Georgia-specific population trend
  is not quantified on this page. At species level it uses human buildings and
  open ground well; that does not mean it is equally successful in every
  habitat. Do not catch or keep wild birds.
interaction: >-
  It is not venomous, poisonous, or a threat to people. Wild birds must not be
  captured. Keep a distance from nests in wall crevices and on buildings.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A slim, long-tailed black, white and grey bird that walks on the ground and
    wags its tail. In Georgia it is a year-round resident on the 2024 checklist.
    The usual confusion is other wagtails, especially grey wagtail.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length about 16.5–19 cm at species level; mass commonly about 18–25 g. The
      body is slim and the tail is long. The bill is thin and black. Flight is
      undulating. The 2024 checklist ties the Georgian form to Motacilla alba
      alba: the back is grey, not solid black.
    - >-
      In breeding plumage the crown and throat are black, the forehead and face
      white, the back grey, the underparts white, with a white wing panel.
      Outside breeding the throat and breast are paler, with less black.
      Juveniles are duller grey-brown, with weaker contrast. The British and
      Irish form M. a. yarrellii has a black back; that form is not listed for
      Georgia on the 2024 checklist.
    - >-
      Grey wagtail (Motacilla cinerea) is YR-R; M in Georgia on the 2024
      checklist; it has yellow underparts, a grey back, a longer tail, and
      usually streams or valleys. Western yellow wagtail (Motacilla flava) is
      BB; M: olive-green back, yellow underparts, a shorter tail. Citrine
      wagtail (Motacilla citreola) is BB; M: breeding males have a yellow head.
      This atlas does not yet have separate pages for those wagtails.
    - >-
      It is not a threat to people. Leave it alone. A black-and-white bird
      wagging its tail and running on a yard or roadside in Georgia is usually
      this species.

facts:
  - >-
    Described by Linnaeus in 1758. The 2024 Georgian checklist follows IOC /
    OSME Region List names: order Passeriformes, family Motacillidae, genus
    Motacilla. English is white wagtail; pied wagtail mainly names the British
    black-backed form (M. a. yarrellii).
  - >-
    The 2024 appendix links the Georgian record to Motacilla alba alba.
    Handbooks recognise several subspecies; assigning them in the field,
    especially in non-breeding plumage, is hard. Treat birds in Georgia to
    species unless a specialist source assigns a form.
  - >-
    The usual Georgian name is თეთრი ბოლოქანქარა. The family in Georgian is
    ბოლოქანქარასებრნი; the genus is ბოლოქანქარა. Russian is белая трясогузка;
    Turkish is ak kuyruksallayan.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: YR-R, data quality A, global and national Red List LC,
    occurrence quality H, evidence PH (photograph). SABUKO and Iliauni public
    lists also add M. That is country-level confirmation, not a full regional
    map.
faq:
  - question: Is the white wagtail found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia lists it as a
      confirmed year-round resident (breeder present throughout the year).
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      In open ground, often near water or buildings — yards, parks, roadsides,
      river edges. A named locality is the Chorokhi Delta (Adjara). This page
      has no complete municipality list.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      It is a slim bird with a long tail that it wags on the ground. In
      breeding plumage the head and throat are black, the face white, the back
      grey. Grey wagtail has yellow underparts. Yellow wagtail has an olive back
      and yellow underparts, with a shorter tail.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 16.5–19 cm long at species level, mass about 18–25 g. A separate
      measured series from Georgia is not given on this page.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Insects, larvae, spiders and other small invertebrates on the ground.
      Diet has not been studied separately in Georgia on this page.
  - question: When is it present in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Year-round, according to the 2024 checklist (YR-R). Some public checklists
      also code it as a migrant; it was counted at the Chorokhi Delta in spring
      2018.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Year-round resident on the 2024 checklist means it is treated as a
      breeder present throughout the year.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It is harmless. It is globally Least Concern and is not on Georgia’s 2014
      Red List. Do not catch wild birds.
