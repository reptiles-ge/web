commonName: False black widow
location: Kakheti, Samtskhe–Javakheti and Adjara
description: >-
  A cobweb spider with a globular abdomen, often mistaken for a true widow. It
  is confirmed in Georgia; a bite can be painful, but this is not the karakurt.
overview: >-
  The false black widow (Steatoda paykulliana) is a cobweb-weaving spider in the
  family Theridiidae and spins an irregular cobweb. Because of its appearance,
  Georgian names include “false karakurt” and “false black widow”, but it belongs
  to Steatoda, a different genus from the karakurt. The species is confirmed in
  Georgia. At species level, female body length is about 8–13 mm and male body
  length is 4.5–8.4 mm. Legs are not included in that size. It uses venom to
  subdue prey. A bite to a person can be painful, but it should not be treated as
  a karakurt (Latrodectus) bite. IUCN has not assessed the species, and it is not
  on Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
habitat: >-
  The false black widow is mainly associated with dry and semi-dry environments:
  dry grassland, stony slopes, rock crevices, and similar places. It can also be
  found in less disturbed gardens and cracks in buildings. On this page, sourced
  Georgian records are Sighnaghi in Kakheti, the Kodiani area in
  Samtskhe–Javakheti, and Kveda Chkhutuneti in Adjara. These records do not mean
  the species is evenly distributed across the country.
diet: >-
  The diet of the false black widow in Georgia has not been separately fully
  studied. At species level, it catches insects and other small prey in an
  irregular web. It first wraps prey in silk and then bites it.
behavior: >-
  The web is often found under stones, in rock or building cracks, in dry grass,
  and close to the ground. The spider does not build a large orb web. At species
  level, females can appear at different times of year, but this page does not
  have a full seasonal calendar for Georgia. Seasonal data described from other
  countries should not be transferred directly to Georgian populations.
conservation: >-
  IUCN has not assessed the false black widow. The species is not on Georgia’s
  2014 Red List. Many records are known across the Caucasus, but that does not
  separately show the size or trend of the Georgian population.
interaction: >-
  The false black widow (Steatoda paykulliana) uses venom to subdue prey. Bites
  to people are rare and mainly may happen when the spider is accidentally
  touched, pressed, or picked up. Bites by Steatoda spiders can cause local pain.
  Nausea, headache, weakness, and other general symptoms are described more
  rarely. Available data suggest such reactions are usually milder than with a
  karakurt (Latrodectus) bite, although those data mainly concern other Steatoda
  species and not a separate Georgian study. If bitten, watch your condition. If
  the face or throat swells significantly, breathing becomes difficult, or the
  general condition worsens sharply, seek medical help immediately and call 112.
  Do not pick up the spider. Its presence is not a danger by itself, and the web
  does not need to be destroyed unless it directly blocks a needed space.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A small spider with a dark, rounded abdomen. It often has a red or yellow
    band on the front of the abdomen. The female is larger than the male. Despite
    the visual similarity, this is not a karakurt.
  traits:
    - >-
      At species level, female body length is about 8–13 mm and male body length
      is 4.5–8.4 mm. Legs are not included in that size. This page does not have
      a complete separate Georgian measurement series.
    - >-
      The female carapace is dark brown to almost black. The abdomen is black and
      has a red or yellow band at the front, which forms a more complex pattern
      in some individuals. The legs are brown. The male is smaller; the front
      abdominal band is often whitish and may have an additional lengthwise
      pattern.
    - >-
      The false black widow spins an irregular cobweb. This kind of web is often
      found under stones, in crevices, or close to the ground. A large,
      symmetrical orb web with a white zigzag in the middle is more typical of
      Argiope, not this species.
    - >-
      In Georgia, the false black widow can most resemble the karakurt
      (Latrodectus tredecimguttatus). The karakurt often has red spots or another
      distinct abdominal pattern, but field separation is not always simple.
      Other Steatoda species also occur in Georgia. If you are unsure of the
      identification, do not pick up or touch the spider.
facts:
  - >-
    The accepted name is Steatoda paykulliana (Walckenaer, 1806). The World
    Spider Catalog treats the species as accepted. Range: Europe, Mediterranean
    to Central Asia, India.
  - >-
    Otto 2022 lists it on the Georgia checklist. Nentwig et al. cite Georgia
    from that source. Otto 2015 counted 72 Caucasian records (Russia / Georgia /
    Azerbaijan). ZFMK specimens are from Sighnaghi, Kodiani (1,850 m), and Kveda
    Chkhutuneti.

    answer: >-

      visible near the front. The karakurt (Latrodectus) belongs to another genus
      and often has a different red pattern. They are not always easy to separate
      in the field, so do not pick up an unknown spider.
