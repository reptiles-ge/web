overview: >-
  Valentin's Lizard (Darevskia valentini) is a rock lizard found in Georgia in the highlands of Samtskhe-Javakheti. It is one of the parent species of the Armenian Rock Lizard, whose females reproduce without males. It has no venom and is not dangerous to people. In Ninotsminda Municipality it has been seen at Lake Saghamo and by the Kirkhbulaki River, but this does not mean it lives only in those two places. A 2026 Georgian list includes it as a confirmed species, not merely a possible one. A global assessment lists it as “Least Concern”, and it is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. It belongs to the true lizard family (Lacertidae), and its English names are Valentin's lizard and Caucasian rock lizard. The species was described from a lizard found at Basarchay / Bazarkent in Armenia, not in Georgia. Its subspecies D. v. valentini bears the same name.
habitat: |-
  Valentin's Lizard lives in high mountain meadows and steppes with rocky hills and cliffs. It also occurs by cliffs beside roads or rivers and along lake shores. You may see it on stones, where it warms itself and takes shelter. It does not live in barren desert or wet Colchic forest. Do not expect it in the Colchic forests of western Georgia or in the eastern lowlands.

  In Georgia, it occurs in Ninotsminda Municipality, where it has been found at Lake Saghamo and the Kirkhbulak River. The lizard pictured in a 2026 study is from Lake Saghamo, and DNA data come from both sites. Confidence in its distribution data is moderate. The Armenian Rock Lizard (Darevskia armeniaca) also occurs at Lake Saghamo. It is a separate species that reproduces without males, and Valentin's Lizard is one of its parent species. Both lizards have been recorded by the lake at about 2,015 metres.

  In the Lesser Caucasus, it has often been recorded at 1,900–3,110 metres; other data, including records from Turkey, give 1,300–3,000 metres. These figures do not tell us exactly what elevations it lives at in Georgia. One prediction places its range in several separate patches of high mountain meadow and steppe, including in southern Georgia. This is a prediction, not another place where it has been found in Georgia. It is not yet known from any other region of Georgia, but that does not mean every suitable mountain has been checked.
diet: >-
  In the highlands of Armenia, Valentin's Lizard eats mainly beetles and flies. Other insects, spiders and plant parts turn up in its diet only rarely. These findings come from the Armenian mountains, so they do not mean it feeds exactly the same way everywhere in Georgia.
behavior: |-
  It is active during the day and lives on rocks. It warms itself on stone and hides in crevices when threatened. In Armenia, it emerges after winter between late April and mid-May. It mates soon afterward and lays eggs in the second half of June. It has also been seen there through the end of September. These dates apply to Armenia and may differ in Georgia.

  In Georgia, a lizard specimen was collected near Lake Saghamo in May–June. There, it laid 2–6 eggs at a time; in Armenia, the average was about six, with a maximum of 12. In both places, it usually lays eggs only once per season. If you catch it, it may lose its tail or bite you. Watch it, but do not pick it up — leave it where it is.
conservation: >-
  A 2009 global assessment rated Valentin's Lizard as of "least concern", because it is widespread and its numbers are probably large. That assessment is now old. It is not on Georgia's 2014 Red List (Resolution No. 190), nor on the CITES list. Georgia's 2026 checklist includes it as a species confirmed for the country. Where it meets the Obscure Rock Lizard and Portschinsky's Lizard in a narrow strip, gene exchange between them has been described. This is not a threat category, but it makes it harder to tell where one species ends and the next begins. Do not catch wild lizards.

  summary: >-
    Valentin's Lizard is a relatively large rock lizard found in the Javakheti highlands. Its back is often greenish-brown or bright green, but colour alone won't tell it apart from other rock lizards of the genus Darevskia. To identify it, also look at its scales, its size and the place where you saw it.
  traits:
    - >-
      This is a relatively large lizard of the genus Darevskia, with well-developed legs. It lives on cliffs and rocks and is not a large ground-dwelling Lacerta. Its back is often greenish-brown or bright green, and its belly is yellow or white. A stripe runs near the temple, marked with dark rings that have pale centers. This look is typical for it, but you cannot tell these species apart by color alone.
    - >-
      The head and body, not counting the tail, often measure about 6–7 cm. Among lizards measured in Armenia, one female reached 7.9 cm. Unusually for the genus Darevskia, females are often larger than males.
    - >-
      At Lake Saghamo, the Armenian Rock Lizard (D. armeniaca) occurs alongside Valentin's Lizard. Its females reproduce without males. One of its parent species is Valentin's Lizard itself; the other is D. mixta. The Armenian Rock Lizard often has a duller green back than Valentin's Lizard, with pale spots on its sides. Between the plate by the ear and the scale in the middle of the temple, it usually has one row of scales, while Valentin's Lizard has several. You cannot tell them apart by color alone.
    - >-
      Portschinsky's Lizard (D. portschinskii) is much smaller than Valentin's Lizard and lives only in the central Lesser Caucasus, for example in Kojori. The Obscure Rock Lizard (D. obscura) is named in the checklist as a possible candidate from the rudis group. In one study, photos of a lizard from Saghamo Lake show the head scales and the plates just in front of the anal opening. Valentin's Lizard, Portschinsky's Lizard and the Obscure Rock Lizard have been reported to meet in one small area, where their genes mix widely. So in that area, they cannot be told apart by color alone.
    - >-
      The 2026 list includes Valentin's Lizard as a confirmed species, not a possible one. In Ninotsminda Municipality, it has been seen at Lake Saghamo and the Kirkhbulak River. This does not mean it occurs throughout Georgia. The Armenian Rock Lizard (D. armeniaca), which reproduces without males, also occurs right there at Lake Saghamo. You cannot tell these two lizards apart by colour alone.

    answer: >-

  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Not by color alone. Valentin's Lizard is a relatively large rock lizard. Compare it with the Armenian Rock Lizard (armeniaca), which reproduces without males and lives alongside Valentin's Lizard at Lake Saghamo. Also compare it with the smaller Portschinsky's Lizard (portschinskii) and Obscure Rock Lizard (obscura). The rows of scales near the ear and the place where you saw the lizard are more reliable clues than the color of its back.

Colour alone will not reliably tell them apart.
