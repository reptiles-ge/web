location: Year-round in Georgia
description: >-
  A small, elongated predator with a brown back, white belly, and short tail. The
  tail has no black tip, which is especially useful for separating it from the
  stoat. It lives in Georgia year-round.
overview: >-
  The least weasel (Mustela nivalis) is a small predatory mammal in the weasel
  family. It is a native, year-round species in Georgia. It is considered the
  smallest member of the order Carnivora. Head–body length is about 13–26 cm, and
  the tail is relatively short. It has no venom; the main human risk is a bite if
  handled. IUCN assesses it globally as Least Concern.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the least weasel may be found in grassy places, scrub, farmland,
  woodland edges, rocky slopes, and mountains, especially where small rodents are
  present. The species can use different habitat types, but often avoids fully
  open places where raptors can spot it easily.

  In the Caucasus it also occurs in high mountain areas, and some sources give
  about 3,660 m as an upper elevation. That is a Caucasus-wide figure, not a
  limit measured only for Georgia.

  Georgian records named on this page are Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park,
  Lagodekhi, the Kvesheti–Kobi road corridor, and Telavi. These places do not
  show the full range of the species and do not mean that the least weasel occurs
  at the same density in every part of the country.
diet: >-
  The least weasel mainly hunts small rodents. Its small, narrow body lets it
  follow prey into burrows. In other parts of the range it has also been recorded
  eating birds’ eggs and nestlings, young lagomorphs, lizards, frogs, insects,
  and carrion. Its diet in Georgia has not been fully studied separately, so the
  exact local diet is unknown.
behavior: >-
  The least weasel is active both by day and at night. It moves quickly and often
  stands on its hind legs to watch its surroundings. It rarely digs its own den
  and often uses rodent burrows, rock piles, or other natural cover.

  At species level, gestation lasts about 34–37 days. In some populations females
  have one or two litters a year, usually with several young. These are wider-range
  figures, not measurements established separately for Georgia.

  In some northern populations the coat turns white in winter, while farther
  south it often remains brown. On this page, a white winter coat is not confirmed
  as a usual Georgian trait.

  Least weasel calls are rarely heard. The audio recording on this page is from
  Spain, and the recordist marked the identification as uncertain; there is no
  Georgian audio recording here.
conservation: >-
  IUCN assesses the least weasel globally as Least Concern, and it has a wide
  range. One national Georgian assessment also lists the species as LC. It is not
  treated as a threatened taxon on Georgia’s 2014 Red List and is not included in
  CITES appendices.

  Local pressures on the species may include secondary poisoning from rodenticides,
  habitat loss, and road mortality. This page does not have a full assessment of
  the population trend across Georgia.
interaction: >-
  It has no venom. It can bite if handled. Like other wild mammals, the least
  weasel theoretically carries a rabies-transmission risk, although in Georgia
  fox, jackal, wolf, and dog are more often named as the main rabies sources. Do
  not feed or handle it.

  summary: >-
    A small, elongated predator with a brown back, white belly, and short tail.
    The tail has no black tip, which is especially useful for separating it from
    the stoat. It lives in Georgia year-round.
  traits:
    - >-
      At species level, head–body length is about 13–26 cm, and the tail is about
      1.5–9 cm. Mass can range from a few tens of grams to about 250 g. Males
      described from the Caucasus are about 17.6–26.5 cm long and are usually
      larger than females. A complete set of separate Georgian measurements is
      not available on this page. The head is narrow and fairly flat, the neck is
      long, the legs are short, and the ears are small and rounded.
    - >-
      The back, flanks, outer legs, and tail are brown, with colour varying from
      dark chocolate to pale sandy. The belly, throat, and inner legs are white.
      The boundary between the brown and white areas is almost straight in some
      individuals and more uneven in others. The tail tip may be slightly darker
      but is not black. This is one of the main ways to separate it from the
      stoat.
    - >-
      The least weasel is most similar to the stoat (Mustela erminea). The stoat
      is larger and always has a black tail tip, including in its white winter
      coat. Beech marten (Martes foina) and pine marten (Martes martes) are much
      larger and have bushier tails. A red fox is clearly different in body
      shape.
    - >-
      It is not venomous. If you see a small, very fast, elongated brown animal
      with a white belly and short tail in a garden, near a stone wall, or in
      grass, it may be a least weasel. Do not pick it up; watch from a distance.
facts:
  - >-
    Linnaeus described it in 1766. The accepted name is Mustela nivalis. Order
    Carnivora, suborder Caniformia, family Mustelidae, genus Mustela.
  - >-
    Caucasus literature historically used two names: M. n. caucasica
    (Barrett-Hamilton, 1900) and M. n. dinniki (Satunin, 1907; type from
    Stavropol, held in the Museum of Georgia). Abramov and Baryshnikov 2000 treat
    dinniki as a synonym of caucasica and find little difference between the
    former “large” and “small” Caucasian forms. Georgian encyclopaedias still
    name two subspecies. Field identification is to species.
  - >-
    In Georgian the usual names are დედოფალა and სინდიოფალა. In English: least
    weasel; in Europe often weasel or common weasel. In ethnographic
    dictionaries დედოფალა can also mean a doll; this page means only Mustela
    nivalis.
  - >-
    IUCN lists Georgia as native. It is in Bukhnikashvili and Kandaurov’s 2002
    annotated mammal list. Eliava et al. 2007 list it as LC. That is
    country-level confirmation plus the named points above, not a regional map.
