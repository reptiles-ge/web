overview: >-
  Radde's Lizard (Darevskia raddei) is a nonvenomous rock lizard whose presence in Georgia has been confirmed along the upper Mtkvari River in Samtskhe-Javakheti. Georgia's 2026 list treats it as a confirmed species, not just a possible one. Confirmed records in Georgia come from Aspindza Municipality: Pia, Tmogvi and the Mtkvari valley near the Turkish border. This does not mean it occurs throughout Georgia. Worldwide, the species faces a low risk of extinction. D. raddei is hard to tell apart from D. nairensis. The 2026 list does not treat them as clearly separate species. According to the same list, the lizards living in Georgia are closer to the nairensis lineage, although nairensis remains a subspecies.
habitat: |-
  In Georgia, Radde's Lizard has been confirmed along the upper Kura River in Samtskhe-Javakheti, near the Turkish border. It has been recorded in Fia (Aspindza) and Tmogvi. The one lizard shown in the images was found in Fia, in the Kura Valley. Not every suitable rocky site there has been checked.

  Beyond Georgia, it is known from Armenia, southern Azerbaijan, northwestern Iran and northeastern Turkey. Across this whole area it lives at 600–2,500 m above sea level, in wooded, shrubby, grassy and rocky places. These elevation limits were not measured in Georgia.

  - question: How can I identify it from a field photo?
    answer: >-
      First, check where you saw the lizard. In Georgia, reliable records come from the Pia–Tmogvi area. Then see whether its body shape matches that of Darevskia rock lizards. To tell it apart from them, you often need a close look at the scales on its head and just in front of the anus.
  - question: How is it different from the Obscure Rock Lizard?
    answer: >-
      The Obscure Rock Lizard (Darevskia obscura) has been seen in more places within the same broad basin. You cannot reliably tell the two lizards apart by colour. What helps more is exactly where you saw the lizard, how big it is and what its scales look like.
  - question: Is D. nairensis a separate species in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      No. The 2026 Georgian list does not treat D. raddei and D. nairensis as clearly distinct species. In that list, nairensis remains a subspecies.
  - question: What does it eat and when is it active?
    answer: >-
      What it eats in Georgia has not yet been studied. It is active during the day, in rocky places. That is where you may most often see Radde's Lizard, since it can hide in the crevices there. So watch rocks with cracks and crevices during daylight hours.
