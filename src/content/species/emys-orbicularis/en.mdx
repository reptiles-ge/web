description: >-
  A native freshwater turtle — not a land tortoise and not the introduced slider.
overview: >-
  The European pond turtle (Emys orbicularis) is a native freshwater turtle.
  Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026 list it as biologically confirmed. In western
  Georgia — E. o. orbicularis; in the east — E. o. persica. E. o. colchica and
  E. o. iberica, described for the southern Caucasus, are treated as synonyms
  (of orbicularis and persica respectively). The checklist figure specimen is
  from Poti — a locality, not the whole of Samegrelo.
habitat: >-
  Ponds, lakes, slow rivers, and wetlands. West: E. o. orbicularis; east:
  E. o. persica. Poti is on the Samegrelo coast — a specimen locality, not a
  regional map.
diet: >-
  An omnivorous freshwater turtle — invertebrates, plant material, and small
  vertebrates.
behavior: >-
  Swims and often basks on banks or logs. Not a land tortoise.
conservation: >-
  A native, biologically confirmed species. This profile does not cite a
  national Red List entry.

  summary: >-
    A native water turtle with a flatter shell. Domed shell means tortoise; a
    red ear stripe means the introduced slider.
  traits:
    - Flatter shell than a land tortoise’s dome.
    - Dark shell often with yellow dots or streaks.
    - Webbed feet; often basks on the bank.
    - No red stripe behind the eye — that mark belongs to the slider.
faq:
  - question: Is this a land tortoise?
    answer: >-
      No. Emys orbicularis is a freshwater turtle. Georgia’s only land tortoise
      is Testudo graeca, with a high-domed shell.
  - question: Where does it occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026: E. o. orbicularis in the west, E. o. persica in
      the east. Poti is a figure-specimen locality, not all of Samegrelo.
  - question: How do I tell it from a red-eared slider?
    answer: >-
      The slider (Trachemys scripta) is introduced; T. s. elegans has a broad
      red stripe behind the eye. The pond turtle is native and lacks that stripe.
  - question: How do I tell it from a Caspian turtle?
    answer: >-
      The Caspian turtle (Mauremys caspica) is recorded from southeastern
      Georgia and often has pale lengthwise neck stripes. The pond turtle’s
      shell is usually darker with yellow speckling.
  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-
      Site classification: Harmless. It has no venom.
