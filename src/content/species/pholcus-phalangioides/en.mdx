overview: >-
  The long-bodied cellar spider (Pholcus phalangioides), also called the
  cosmopolitan cellar spider, is a pholcid spider in the family Pholcidae. The
  name daddy long-legs spider is also used, but it can be confused with
  harvestmen and crane flies, so the scientific name is clearer.

  The species is confirmed in Georgia. Caucasian Spiders lists it in the Georgia
  country checklist and gives many Georgian records, including Tbilisi, Kutaisi,
  Batumi, Borjomi-Kharagauli, Lagodekhi, Gori, Mtskheta, Kazbegi, and other
  localities.

  It is a pale, thin-bodied spider with very long legs. It often hangs upside
  down in a loose, irregular web in ceilings, corners, cellars, garages, caves,
  and other quiet dark places. Body length is usually about 6-10 mm; the legs
  are much longer, so the spider looks larger than the body measurement suggests.

  It is not considered dangerous to people. It has venom for subduing prey, but
  its bite is not treated as a medically significant risk. The species has not
  been globally assessed by the IUCN and is not included in Georgia's 2014 Red
  List.
habitat: >-
  The long-bodied cellar spider is synanthropic: it often lives in habitats
  connected with people. In Georgia, realistic places to encounter it include
  houses, cellars, stairwells, storage rooms, garages, under bridges and
  buildings, and natural or artificial caves.

  The Caucasian Spiders datasheet names many Georgian records, including
  Tbilisi, Kutaisi, Batumi, Borjomi-Kharagauli, Lagodekhi, Gori, Mtskheta,
  Kazbegi, Sighnaghi, Zestaponi, Oni, Kobuleti, Akhaltsikhe, Chiatura, and other
  places. This indicates broad occurrence, especially in settled or sheltered
  habitats.

  Even with many records, this is not a complete administrative distribution
  map. This page does not claim that the species is confirmed in every Georgian
  municipality; it reports the evidence named by the sources used here.
diet: >-
  This spider is a predator and catches small arthropods in or near its web.
  Typical prey includes small insects, other spiders, and small invertebrates
  living indoors.

  It often waits in one place and detects prey through vibrations in the web.
  It can catch other spiders, so its presence indoors should not be treated as a
  sign of a harmful pest.
behavior: >-
  The web is loose, irregular, and three-dimensional, not a round orb web. The
  spider often hangs upside down in it. When disturbed, it may rapidly vibrate
  its body and web; this is a defensive behaviour that can confuse a predator or
  disturbance.

  The species is most associated with dark sheltered places, but in buildings it
  may be seen at any time of year. A separate Georgian seasonal calendar is not
  established by the sources used here.

  Females carry eggs in a silk sac, often near the mouthparts. Spiderlings
  disperse after hatching. This is general species biology, not a separate study
  of the Georgian population.
conservation: >-
  World Spider Catalog treats Pholcus phalangioides as the accepted name. The
  species belongs to Pholcidae. Older names and combinations include Aranea
  phalangoides and several other synonyms, but current usage accepts Pholcus
  phalangioides.

  According to WSC, the native range is associated with western Asia, while the
  species has been introduced to Europe, both Americas, Africa, other parts of
  Asia, Australia, New Zealand, and many islands. In Georgia it is confirmed by
  the Caucasian Spiders checklist and species datasheet.

  The IUCN has not globally assessed the species, and it is not included in
  Georgia's 2014 Red List. The sources used here do not define a special
  Georgian legal protection status or quantified population trend.
interaction: >-
  The long-bodied cellar spider is treated as harmless to people. Its presence
  in a house is not a danger by itself, and it does not attack people. As with
  any wild spider, there is no need to handle it.

  If an accidental bite happens, wash the area with soap and water and observe
  your condition. If strong pain, increasing swelling, breathing difficulty, or
  worsening general condition occurs, call 112. This is educational information,
  not a medical protocol.

    legs. It usually hangs in a loose irregular web in corners, ceilings, or

  traits:
    - >-
      Body length is about 6-10 mm, but the very long legs make the spider look
      much larger. The body is narrow and pale brownish to greyish.
    - >-
      The web is not round. It is a loose, irregular web often placed in a
      ceiling corner, cellar, stairwell, or garage.
    - >-
      When disturbed, it often vibrates rapidly in the web. This behaviour helps
      separate it from many other long-legged house spiders.
    - >-
      Do not confuse it with harvestmen. Harvestmen have a body that appears
      single and compact and they do not spin webs; Pholcus is a true spider
      with two main body parts and silk webs.
    - >-
      The [European garden spider](araneus-diadematus) builds a large round orb
      web and often has a white cross mark on the abdomen. Pholcus has an
      irregular web and much longer, thinner legs.
