overview: >-
  The Lebanon lizard (Phoenicolacerta laevis) is confirmed in Georgia only from
  the area of Anaklia Castle and the adjacent meadow; it is a probably
  unintentionally introduced lacertid and is harmless to people. The 2026
  Georgian checklist treats the species as biologically valid and introduced in
  Georgia. The 2017 study linked the Anaklia population genetically with
  populations from southern Türkiye, so records from other parts of Georgia
  should not be treated as established without evidence.
habitat: >-
  The confirmed Georgian habitat is very specific: the old stone walls of
  Anaklia Castle and the adjoining grassy area with Rubus shrubs, near the
  mouth of the Enguri and the Black Sea coast. The 2017 authors described an
  area of about one hectare where lizards moved on the walls and nearby meadow.
  This record does not mean the species occurs throughout western Georgia or in
  every coastal habitat.
diet: >-
  The diet of the Anaklia population has not been described in detail in the
  published sources. Lacertid lizards, including Phoenicolacerta, usually feed
  on small invertebrates, but the diet of the Georgian population still needs
  verification.
behavior: >-
  This is a fast, diurnal lacertid. At Anaklia, on 6 May 2017 around midday, the
  authors saw more than 100 adult and young lizards during a one-hour
  observation, indicating an established but spatially restricted population.
  Full seasonal activity in Georgia has not been published; lowland Turkish
  populations are described as active from March to September.
conservation: >-
  Globally, the species occurs across the eastern Mediterranean region, and The
  Reptile Database shows its IUCN category as Least Concern. In Georgia it is
  not treated as native fauna and is known only from one small, probably
  human-introduced population. Changes to the Anaklia Castle walls and adjacent
  vegetation could damage this isolated population; a Georgian national
  protection status is not verified on this page.

  summary: >-
    In Georgia, the most important clues for identifying the Lebanon lizard are
    place, behavior, and the typical lacertid body shape. Colour alone is not a
    reliable field mark because native Lacerta and Darevskia species can look
    similar.
  traits:
    - >-
      In Georgia it is confirmed only from the Anaklia Castle area. A similar
      lizard photographed elsewhere should not automatically be treated as this
      species.
    - >-
      It is a small, fast lacertid with a long tail, well-developed limbs, and
      daytime activity. That separates it clearly from legless lizards, but not
      from every native lacertid.
    - >-
      At Anaklia it is especially associated with old stone walls and nearby
      grassy ground. The 2017 study notes that other reptiles recorded in the
      same area did not mainly use stone walls as habitat.
    - >-
      In Georgia it can be confused with the [Pontic meadow
      lizard](darevskia-pontica), [sand lizard](lacerta-agilis), or other
      Darevskia. Locality, habitat, and good photographs are often more reliable
      than dorsal colour alone.
    - >-
      It is non-venomous and presents no medical risk to people. Avoid handling
      wild lizards; photograph the animal and record the exact location instead.
faq:
  - question: Is the Lebanon lizard found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes, but it is confirmed only from the Anaklia area. The 2026 Georgian
      checklist lists it as an unintentionally introduced species.
  - question: Where is it confirmed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      The published Georgian record refers to the area around Anaklia Castle,
      near the mouth of the Enguri, in Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti.
  - question: Is it native to Georgia?
    answer: >-
      No. Genetic data connect the Anaklia population with southern Türkiye, and
      the authors treat it as probably introduced by human activity.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous?
    answer: >-
      No. The Lebanon lizard is non-venomous and harmless to people. Even so,
      wild lizards should not be handled unnecessarily.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      In Georgia, locality is the main clue: the Anaklia Castle area. It is a
      small, fast, day-active lacertid; colour alone is not enough for a reliable
      identification.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      In lowland Turkish populations, adult snout-vent length is roughly 6–7
      cm. Full size data for the Georgian population are not verified on this
      page.
  - question: When is it active in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Full seasonal activity in Georgia has not been published. Active adults
      and young were recorded at Anaklia on 6 May around midday.
  - question: What threatens the Anaklia population?
    answer: >-
      The 2017 authors noted that changes to the Anaklia Castle walls and
      adjacent vegetation could harm the small isolated population.
