commonName: Transcaucasian Ratsnake
location: Georgia — dry rocky and grassy habitats
description: >-
  A harmless, blotched ratsnake of dry rocky and grassland habitats in Georgia.
overview: >-
  The Transcaucasian racer (Zamenis hohenackeri) is a nonvenomous, spotted snake. Its presence in Georgia has been confirmed. Its subspecies Z. h. hohenackeri is found here. It lives mostly in dry, rocky and grassy places. Adults are often less than a metre long. Its spots can make it easy to mistake for another snake, including a viper.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, this species occurs in dry, rocky or grassy places. Published accounts report it from the villages of Gomaro and Boshuri in Adigeni Municipality and from western Georgia. Tbilisi is also mentioned in the material on which the species’ first description was based. On this page, one field record in a region is enough for “Distribution confirmed” status. This does not mean the species occurs throughout that region; the map does not show its full national range.
diet: >-
  It mainly hunts rodents and birds. It also eats lizards. It catches prey with its body. These sources contain no separate study of what it eats in Georgia.
behavior: >-
  It keeps out of sight and is often active at dusk or at night, though it may also appear during the day. Descriptions of the species say it lays 3–7 eggs at a time. These sources do not make clear when it breeds in Georgia.
interaction: >-
  It is not venomous. Do not pick up a wild snake: its spots may make you mistake it for a viper. Do not get closer to check the shape of its pupil or head. If a snake bites you and you are not sure which snake it was, call 112.
conservation: >-
  According to a 2009 global assessment, this species faces a low risk of extinction. It is not listed in Georgia’s 2014 Red List. Neither fact shows how its numbers in Georgia are changing.

  traits:
    - >-
      Its pupils are round. It has no horn on its nose, and the scales on its back are mostly smooth. Pupil shape alone is not enough to identify a snake.
    - >-
      The subspecies found in Georgia usually has two rows of brownish spots along its body, with a pale line between them. The spots sometimes join. A dark stripe is visible behind the eye. Color and pattern can vary from one animal to another.
    - >-
      Adult Aesculapian snakes (Zamenis longissimus) are often more uniform in color, while this species retains its spots. The nose-horned viper has a horn on its nose and scales that are raised in the middle. It is venomous. You can compare these features in a photo, but do not approach a snake to inspect them.
facts:
  - >-
    Strauch described the species in 1873 as Coluber hohenackeri. Older Georgian
    and Soviet books also use Elaphe hohenackeri.
  - >-
    The 2026 checklist suggests an old, unconfirmed Tbilisi report of Elaphe
    situla may instead refer to this snake or Elaphe dione.

    answer: >-

  - question: How can it be identified?
    answer: >-

  - question: How does it differ from the Aesculapian snake?
    answer: >-
