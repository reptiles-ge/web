commonName: Lesser Black-backed Gull
location: Passage migrant and winter visitor in Georgia
description: >-
  A large gull with a dark back and yellow legs. Confirmed in Georgia on the 2024
  national bird checklist as a passage migrant and a winter visitor; not treated
  as a breeding bird.
overview: >-
  The lesser black-backed gull (Larus fuscus) is a large gull in the family
  Laridae, with a dark mantle and yellow legs. The usual Georgian name is
  ფრთაშავი თოლია; checklists also use შავზურგა თოლია. The 2024 annotated
  checklist of the birds of Georgia treats two taxa: Baltic Gull (Larus fuscus
  fuscus), coded M; WV — a passage migrant and winter visitor — and Heuglin’s
  Gull (Larus (fuscus) heuglini), coded WV — a winter visitor. Seasonality is
  classed as high-quality (code A) for both. Occurrence quality is high (H),
  with photograph evidence. Neither taxon has a breeding code (BB or YR-R).
  Adults are about 52–64 cm long at species level. It is not a threat to people.
  IUCN lists the species as Least Concern. The 2024 checklist assigns the same
  category globally and nationally. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
habitat: >-
  Look for it on coasts, wetlands, river deltas, harbours, and open water, often
  with other large gulls. The 2024 checklist confirms country-level migrant and
  winter-visitor status but does not publish a municipality map. Named Georgian
  places with published sampling or a site list: the Kolkheti Lowland Wetlands,
  the Chorokhi River Delta, and the Kura River Valley — Lewis et al. (2013)
  sampled six gull species, including L. fuscus, in those three areas during the
  overwintering period. The paper does not publish separate counts for this
  species at each site, so all three blocks are mapped as winter sampling areas,
  not as a complete range map. Kolkheti SPA N 17 (the same as IBA GEO04; around
  Lake Paliastomi, about 0–10 m) lists the species as M, WV on the site
  checklist. The Chorokhi Delta is in Adjara. The Kura valley crosses several
  administrative regions and is not coloured as a separate region on this page.
  Other coasts or inland waters may be used on passage, but this page does not
  colour them without a compiled gazetteer. This page does not map all of
  Georgia. A separate elevation belt in metres for Georgia is not published here.
diet: >-
  There is no separate weighed Georgian diet study on this page. At species
  level it is an opportunistic omnivore: fish, invertebrates, carrion, eggs and
  chicks of other birds, and refuse at harbours and dumps. That is a general
  trophic niche, not a weighed Georgian menu.
behavior: >-
  It is active by day. It often stands in loose groups with other gulls. Flight
  is strong, with long wings; adults have a dark upperwing. The voice is a
  barking or yelping kraah, like a herring gull but more nasal. The clip is
  xeno-canto XC983544 (Tanguy Loïs, Brest, France) — not a Georgian recording.
  It does not breed in Georgia on the 2024 checklist. At species level, adult
  plumage is usually complete in the fourth calendar year; clutches are often two
  or three eggs — that is wide-range data, not a Georgian nest sample. Nominate
  L. f. fuscus migrates on an eastern route from the Baltic toward Ukraine and
  the Bosporus, then through the eastern Mediterranean toward Africa; that is a
  satellite-tracked broad corridor, not a route of named Georgian birds.
  Heuglin’s form winters from the Middle East to East Africa and India. Exact
  arrival and departure dates for Georgia are not tabled here.
conservation: >-
  BirdLife International lists Larus fuscus as Least Concern on the IUCN Red
  List; the global range and population are large. The 2024 Georgian checklist
  assigns LC as both the global and national category — for L. f. fuscus and for
  heuglini. The species is not read on Georgia’s 2014 Red List (Decree No. 190)
  among the sources used here. Species-level status does not fully cover the
  subspecies picture: Baltic nominate L. f. fuscus is Vulnerable (VU) on the
  HELCOM regional Red List after a long decline; that is the form the 2024
  checklist treats as Georgia’s migrant and winter visitor. Western European
  graellsii / intermedius populations have increased elsewhere. There is no
  separate national trend for Georgia on this page. Lewis et al. (2013) included
  the species in winter gull samples during avian-influenza surveillance; that is
  research context, not a separate national threat assessment. Do not disturb or
  catch wild birds without a permit.
interaction: >-
  It is not venomous and is not a threat to people. The bill is strong — do not
  feed birds at refuse or in harbours, and do not handle them. It does not breed
  in Georgia, so rooftop-colony conflict is not documented here. Leave wild birds
  alone.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A large gull with a dark back and yellow legs. In Georgia it is a passage
    migrant and winter visitor on the 2024 checklist, not a breeder. The usual
    confusion is with paler large gulls — Caspian, Yellow-legged, and Armenian.
  traits:
    - >-
      Length about 52–64 cm at species level; wingspan often about 117–150 cm;
      mass often about 0.5–1.1 kg. Nominate L. f. fuscus averages slightly
      slimmer. There is no separate measured Georgian series on this page.
      Adults have a white head and underparts; the back and upperwing are dark
      grey to almost black — fuscus is darkest, graellsii more slate-grey,
      heuglini intermediate. Legs are yellow. The bill is yellow with a red
      gonys spot. In winter the head may show grey streaking. Females average
      slightly smaller than males; sex is not reliably told by size in the
      field.
    - >-
      Young birds are mottled brown and resemble first-year birds of other large
      gulls; species-level identification from a single photo is often hard.
      Adult plumage is usually complete in the fourth calendar year. Subspecies
      identification in Georgia is realistic only on a well-marked adult: mantle
      shade, wing-tip mirrors, and structure. Many winter records should stay at
      the species complex.
    - >-
      Caspian Gull (Larus cachinnans) is YR-R; M; WV on the 2024 checklist — a
      pale grey back, a long slim bill, often pale legs, not a dark mantle.
      Yellow-legged Gull (Larus michahellis) is YR-R; M; WV — also yellow legs,
      but a much paler back. Armenian Gull (Larus armenicus) is YR-R; M; WV —
      pale grey, often with a dark bill band in winter; Javakheti holds a large
      breeding colony, not of this species. Common Gull (Larus canus) is M; WV —
      noticeably smaller, with a slimmer bill. Great Black-backed Gull (Larus
      marinus) is ND on the checklist (poor documentation): much larger, with
      pink legs. This atlas does not yet have separate pages for those similar
      species.
    - >-
      Harmless. In Georgia in winter or on passage, a large gull with a dark
      back and yellow legs belongs in this complex. A pale grey back with yellow
      legs is usually Yellow-legged, Caspian, or Armenian — check mantle shade
      and head shape.

facts:
  - >-
    Linnaeus described the species in 1758 in Systema Naturae. The 2024 Georgian
    checklist follows the OSME Region List for names: order Charadriiformes,
    family Laridae, genus Larus. This page treats the species as Larus fuscus,
    following IOC and BirdLife. The checklist lists Baltic Gull (Larus fuscus
    fuscus) and Heuglin’s Gull (Larus (fuscus) heuglini) separately; the
    parentheses on heuglini mark that some authorities treat it as a full
    species. Field identification is often to the species complex.
  - >-
    Widely recognised subspecies include fuscus (Baltic and northern
    Scandinavia; winters mainly in eastern Africa and south-west Asia),
    graellsii (Iceland, Britain, western Europe), intermedius (North Sea coast),
    heuglini (northern Siberia), and barabensis (western Siberia / Kazakhstan).
    The 2024 checklist names fuscus and heuglini for Georgia. graellsii and
    intermedius are not entered separately here.
  - >-
    The Georgian name is ფრთაშავი თოლია; SABUKO and Ilia State lists also use
    შავზურგა (or ფრთაშავი) თოლია. English is lesser black-backed gull; the 2024
    checklist calls the nominate Baltic Gull and heuglini Heuglin’s Gull.
    Russian is клуша. Turkish is küçük kara sırtlı martı.
  - >-
    2024 checklist codes: L. f. fuscus — M; WV, data quality A, global and
    national Red List LC, occurrence quality H, evidence PH. L. (fuscus)
    heuglini — WV, same quality and LC / PH. That is country-level status. The
    regional map on this page rests on named wetland samples and the Kolkheti
    SPA list, not on a checklist gazetteer.
faq:
  - question: Is the lesser black-backed gull found in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The 2024 annotated checklist of the birds of Georgia confirms it as a
      passage migrant and winter visitor (M; WV for the nominate; WV for
      Heuglin’s). It is not treated as a breeder.
  - question: Where can I see it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      On coasts and wetlands, especially in autumn and winter. Named published
      places are the Kolkheti Lowland Wetlands (SPA N 17, around Paliastomi),
      the Chorokhi Delta, and the Kura valley winter sampling area. This page
      does not map every region.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      Adults have a dark back and upperwing, yellow legs, and a yellow bill with
      a red spot. Caspian, Yellow-legged, and Armenian gulls have much paler
      backs. Young birds are mottled brown and resemble other large gulls.
  - question: How large is it?
    answer: >-
      About 52–64 cm long at species level; wingspan often about 117–150 cm,
      mass about 0.5–1.1 kg. There is no separate measured Georgian series on
      this page.
  - question: What does it eat?
    answer: >-
      Opportunistically: fish, invertebrates, carrion, and available refuse.
      There is no separate Georgian diet study on this page.
  - question: When is it in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      On passage and in winter, according to the 2024 checklist. Heuglin’s form
      is coded only as a winter visitor. Exact arrival and departure dates are
      not tabled here. A summer breeding population is not confirmed in the
      country.
  - question: Does it breed in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      No, on the sources used here. Neither L. f. fuscus nor heuglini has BB or
      YR-R on the 2024 checklist. The large gull colony in Javakheti belongs to
      Armenian Gull.
  - question: Is it dangerous or protected?
    answer: >-
      It is harmless. It is Least Concern globally and LC nationally on the 2024
      checklist. It is not on the 2014 Red List. The Baltic nominate is
      regionally Vulnerable; that is not Georgia’s legal status. Do not disturb
      wild birds.
