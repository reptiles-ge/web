commonName: False black widow
location: Georgia, including Tbilisi, Kakheti and Adjara
description: >-
  A cobweb spider with a globular abdomen, often mistaken for a true widow. It
  is confirmed in Georgia; a bite can be painful, but this is not the karakurt.
overview: >-
  The false black widow (Steatoda paykulliana) is confirmed in Georgia, with
  records from Tbilisi, Kakheti and Adjara. Females have a dark, rounded abdomen
  often marked with a red or yellow band along its front edge. The spider
  resembles a karakurt but belongs to a different genus; colour alone does not
  always distinguish them. It has venom, and a bite can be painful. Do not touch
  an unidentified spider.
habitat: >-
  The species mainly occurs in dry and semi-dry places, including dry grassland,
  stony slopes and rock crevices. It is also found occasionally in fallow
  orchards. Georgian records include Tbilisi and preserved specimens from
  Sighnaghi, Kodiani and Kveda Chkhutuneti.
diet: >-
  It catches insects and other small prey in an irregular web.
behavior: >-
  The web is often found under stones, in rock crevices, in dry grass,
  and close to the ground. The spider does not build a large, round web.
conservation: >-
  IUCN has not assessed the false black widow. The species is not on Georgia’s
  2014 Red List. Many records are known across the Caucasus, but that does not
  separately show the size or trend of the Georgian population.
interaction: >-
  The false black widow (Steatoda paykulliana) uses venom to subdue prey. Bites
  can occur if the spider is accidentally pressed or picked up, and they may be
  painful. Seek medical care for severe pain or worsening symptoms. If the face
  or throat swells significantly, breathing becomes difficult, or the condition
  worsens sharply, call 112 immediately. Do not touch or pick up the spider. Its
  presence alone does not require destroying its web unless the web directly
  blocks a space you need.

identification:
  summary: >-
    A small spider with a dark, rounded abdomen. It often has a red or yellow
    band on the front of the abdomen. The female is larger than the male. Despite
    the visual similarity, this is not a karakurt.
  traits:
    - >-
      Female body length is about 8–13 mm and male body length is 4.5–8.4 mm.
      Legs are not included in these measurements.
    - >-
      The female carapace is dark brown to almost black. The abdomen is black and
      has a red or yellow band at the front, which forms a more complex pattern
      in some individuals. The legs are brown. The male is smaller; the front
      abdominal band is often whitish and may have an additional lengthwise
      pattern.
    - >-
      The false black widow spins an irregular cobweb. This kind of web is often
      found under stones, in crevices, or close to the ground. A large,
      symmetrical orb web with a white zigzag in the middle is more typical of
      Argiope, not this species.
    - >-
      The false black widow can resemble the karakurt (Latrodectus
      tredecimguttatus). Female karakurts often have red spots on the abdomen,
      but sometimes those spots are absent. Colour alone is not a reliable way
      to separate them. Other Steatoda species also occur in Georgia. If you are
      unsure of the identification, do not touch or pick up the spider.
facts:
  - >-
    The accepted name is Steatoda paykulliana (Walckenaer, 1806). The World
    Spider Catalog treats the species as accepted. Range: Europe, Mediterranean
    to Central Asia, India.
  - >-
    Otto 2022 lists it on the Georgia checklist. Nentwig et al. cite Georgia
    from that source. ZFMK specimens are from Sighnaghi, Kodiani (1,850 m), and
    Kveda Chkhutuneti.
  - >-
    IUCN has not assessed it. It is not on the 2014 Red List.
