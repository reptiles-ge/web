commonName: "Sand Lizard"
location: "Adjara, Guria, Imereti, Samegrelo–Zemo Svaneti, Samtskhe–Javakheti, Kvemo Kartli"
description: >-
  The sand lizard is confirmed in Georgia and harmless to people. It is
  widespread in the west; in the east it occurs above about 600–800 m.
overview: >-
  Lacerta agilis is not venomous. It is widespread in western Georgia; in the east, it occurs above roughly 600–800 m. It has a stocky body and a relatively short head. During the breeding season, males’ sides often turn green, but [identifying Lacerta agilis by color alone](/lizards/identifikacia) is difficult.
habitat: >-
  In the west, this species has also been recorded along the Black Sea coast and in the Colchis Lowland. It lives in a variety of places, including meadows, shrubland and forest edges. In sunny spots, you may find it near grass or a bush where it can hide. It does not live only in sandy places. A 2018 study found the Iori Valley subspecies on the northern outskirts of Tianeti at an elevation of 1,119–1,185 m; it was not found more than 2 km from Tianeti.
diet: >-
  It eats small animals without backbones, including insects and spiders.
behavior: >-
  It is active during the day, warms itself in the sun and hunts. The time it emerges from its winter shelter varies by location. Emergence in the south and in the mountains has been recorded in March–April. A female may lay eggs once or twice a year, 4–14 each time. In general, this species’ eggs hatch in about 50–55 days.
conservation: >-
  A 2009 global assessment found the species to be at low risk, although its numbers were declining. It is not on Georgia’s 2014 Red List. It is listed in Annex II of the Bern Convention. The Iori Valley subspecies lives in a small area. In 2018, it was thought that there was only one population of this subspecies near Tianeti, with fewer than 1,000 animals. This estimate applied only to the area around Tianeti, not to the species as a whole. Reported threats to the species include habitat loss, intensive farming and deaths on roads.
interaction: >-
  It is not dangerous to people: it has no venom. If picked up, it may bite or lose its tail. Observe it and leave it where it is; do not catch a wild lizard.

identification:
  summary: >-
    It has a stocky body and a relatively short head. During the breeding season, males often develop greenish sides; females are usually brownish or gray. Color alone is not enough to tell it apart from the lizards called ზოლიანი ხვლიკი and საშუალო ხვლიკი.
  traits:
    - >-
      Females and young lizards often have dark spots with pale centers on their sides. A male’s green coloring changes with the seasons; in some males, green covers more of the body. To identify the lizard, look at its body shape, markings and surroundings. Do not pick it up to identify it.
    - >-
      [Lacerta strigata](lacerta-strigata) is also found in semi-arid areas. In the east, the lizard called მარდი ხვლიკი is found at higher elevations, but both species were seen at the same site near Tianeti in 2018. So do not identify a lizard’s species by its location and color alone.
    - >-
      [Lacerta media](lacerta-media) is a larger green lizard. It is found mainly in semi-arid areas in the east and is listed as a candidate species in the 2026 list. Where it occurs alongside another green lizard, look at their size and body shape to help tell them apart.
faq:
  - question: Does the sand lizard occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      Yes. It is widespread in the west, while in the east it has been recorded above roughly 600–800 m.
  - question: How can I identify it?
    answer: >-
      It has a stocky body and a relatively short head. During the breeding season, the male’s sides often turn greenish, while the female is usually brownish. Color alone cannot distinguish it from the lizards called ზოლიანი ხვლიკი and საშუალო ხვლიკი in Georgian.
  - question: Is it venomous or dangerous to people?
    answer: >-
      No. It has no venom and is not dangerous to people. If you pick it up, it may bite or lose its tail; leave it where it is.
  - question: How large does it grow?
    answer: >-
      The head and body, excluding the tail, are usually about 7–9 cm long. Including the tail, the total length is about 18–25 cm.
  - question: How does it differ from the striped lizard?
    answer: >-
      In the east, the lizard called მარდი ხვლიკი is found at higher elevations, while the one called ზოლიანი is also found in semi-arid areas. Both have been seen together near Tianeti, so location or coloring alone cannot reliably tell them apart.
  - question: Which subspecies occur in Georgia?
    answer: >-
      The 2026 list names three subspecies: grusinica in the west, brevicaudata in the south and center, and ioriensis in the east. A single feature of their DNA is not enough to distinguish these subspecies completely.
  - question: When is it active?
    answer: >-
      During the day. The time it comes out of its winter shelter varies from place to place. In the south and in the mountains, it has been observed coming out in March–April.
