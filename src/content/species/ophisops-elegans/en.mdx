commonName: "Snake-Eyed Lizard"
location: "Eastern Georgia — local records"
description: >-
  The Snake-Eyed Lizard is a small lizard with snake-like eyes, because its eyelids are fused. It lives in scattered dry spots in eastern Georgia, is harmless and easily goes unnoticed.
overview: >-
  The snake-eyed lizard (Ophisops elegans) is a small lizard with eyes that look like a snake's. The eye is also the easiest way to recognise it: its eyelids are fused and transparent. In every other way it belongs to the true lizard family and has fully developed legs. The 2026 checklist confirms its presence in Georgia. Lizards found at Taribana (Dedoplistskaro) were checked by DNA, and the species has also been seen near Kumisi and Rustavi in Kvemo Kartli. All these places lie in the dry, open parts of eastern Georgia. Even there it does not live everywhere and occurs only in scattered spots. It is harmless to people, but if you see one, there is no need to touch it.
habitat: >-
  In Georgia, the confirmed sites of the Snake-Eyed Lizard lie mainly in the dry east. Lizards found at Taribana (Dedoplistskaro, Kakheti) were checked by DNA and turned out to be this species. In Kvemo Kartli it has also been seen around Kumisi and Rustavi. There it lives in small patches of semi-desert with sparse grass. You are most likely to see it on dry open ground, on stony or grassy patches, and where steppe gives way to scrub. In Georgia it has not been confirmed in forests or damp places.
diet: >-
  The Snake-Eyed Lizard feeds on small invertebrates. In a population studied outside Georgia, in Bursa, insects made up most of its food, especially their larvae.
behavior: >-
  The Snake-Eyed Lizard is active by day and runs fast across the ground. You are most likely to see it in the warm season, when small lizards are active. It lays eggs. Its seasonal calendar, clutch size and breeding time in Georgia have not yet been established. When it senses danger, it dashes off and hides in low plants, between stones or in uneven ground.
conservation: >-
  In Georgia the Snake-Eyed Lizard is considered vulnerable, while worldwide it faces little threat. Here it lives in scattered places, far apart from one another. The real threat to it is the loss or conversion of small patches of dry semi-desert and steppe. The 2026 checklist confirms its presence in Georgia, and the subspecies found here is O. e. elegans. The name Ophisops elegans is currently used in Georgia. However, the lizards grouped under O. elegans in the broad sense differ greatly in their genes, so this group may be revised in the future.

  summary: >-
    The Snake-Eyed Lizard is a small, quick member of the true lizard family. The eye is the key feature: its eyelids are transparent and fused, so the eye looks like a snake's. This sets it apart from many other lizards in Georgia. Its body, legs and scales, though, are those of an ordinary lizard, so don't let the snake-like eye mislead you.
  traits:
    - >-
      The eyelids are fused into a transparent cover over the eye. Because of this, the lizard cannot blink in the usual way. This is the main feature behind the name “snake-eyed”.
    - >-
      The tail is long, often about twice the length of the body. The body is slender and built for quick movement on the ground. A broken or regrown tail makes identification harder, but it does not change the species. So a short tail alone does not mean you have seen a different species.
    - >-
      The back is usually olive to bronze-brown. It has dark spots or dark rows running along its length. Pale stripes often run along the sides, and the belly is light-coloured.
    - >-
      It is a small lizard. General descriptions of the species give a body length of up to about 5 cm and a tail of up to about 10 cm.
    - >-
      The [Asian Snake-Eyed Skink](ablepharus-pannonicus) is much smaller than the Snake-Eyed Lizard. It has smooth scales and often hides among leaves or other cover. The Snake-Eyed Lizard belongs to the true lizard family: its legs are longer, and it runs fast across open ground. To tell the two apart, watch their size and behaviour.
    - >-
      The [Steppe-Runner](eremias-arguta) and the [Rapid Racerunner](eremias-velox) also live in dry, open places, but they lack the snake-like fused eyelid. Green lizards such as the [Striped Lizard](lacerta-strigata) are much larger. So you can tell the Snake-Eyed Lizard from racerunners by its eye, and from green lizards by its size.
