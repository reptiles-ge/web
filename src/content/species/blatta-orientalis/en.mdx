overview: >-
  The Oriental cockroach (Blatta orientalis) is an insect in the cockroach order
  (Blattodea) and the Blattidae family. Adults are about 22–27 mm long. Some are
  dark brown, others almost black. Males have short wings, while females have
  only small, undeveloped wings. Neither males nor females can fly. Older
  sources may also call it Periplaneta orientalis; its currently accepted
  scientific name is Blatta orientalis.

  The Oriental cockroach has been recorded in Georgia. The map shows 75 publicly
  posted observations from Georgia and five photos from the Reptiles.ge gallery
  that include location coordinates. According to the regional table, its
  presence is confirmed in Tbilisi, Kakheti, Abkhazia and Adjara. There are also
  records from Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Samtskhe-Javakheti and the Racha-Lechkhumi and
  Kvemo Svaneti region, but the table does not count its presence in those
  regions as confirmed. This distinction is based on the number of records on
  the map, not on a systematic study of the species’ population.
habitat: >-
  In general, the Oriental cockroach often hides in cool, dark, damp places. In
  buildings, these include basements and the areas around pipes, drains and
  foundations. Outdoors, it hides under debris and leaves. The species has been
  recorded in Georgia, but this list describes its general habits, not each
  place where it has been found in Georgia.
diet: >-
  The Oriental cockroach eats organic waste and various foods. It will eat
  almost anything but prefers food rich in starch. It is also associated with
  rubbish, sewers and decaying material. This is a general description of what
  the species eats.
behavior: >-
  The Oriental cockroach moves mainly at night. The female places her eggs in a
  dark capsule. She carries the capsule with her for a while, then leaves it in
  a warm, sheltered spot near food. The young that hatch from the eggs have no
  wings at first. They shed their skin several times before they become adults.
  How long they take to grow depends on temperature and other conditions.
conservation: >-
  The Oriental cockroach also lives in places shaped by people. In buildings, it
  is considered a pest.
interaction: >-
  The Oriental cockroach is not venomous, but it does carry other risks. Tiny
  pieces of cockroaches’ bodies and their droppings can make allergies or asthma
  symptoms worse in sensitive people. Cockroaches can also contaminate food.
  These are general household risks.

  If you find a cockroach at home, first identify the species. Then check damp
  places where it may hide. Fix water leaks, keep food covered, and seal cracks
  through which it could get into the house. If cockroaches appear again,
  contact a pest control professional. Use chemical products only as directed on
  the label.

  For steps that apply to cockroaches more broadly, read the
  [cockroaches-at-home guide](/insects/taraknebi-sakhlshi).

identification:
  summary: >-
    The adult Oriental cockroach has a flattened, shiny body that is dark brown
    or almost black, and long feelers. Males and females have wings of different
    lengths, so look at the wings as well. Color or size alone is not enough to
    identify the species.
  traits:
    - >-
      It is about 22–27 mm long. The male’s wings do not cover the end of its
      abdomen, while the female has very short, undeveloped wings. Neither can
      fly. In a photo, wing shape and the upper side of the body are more useful
      for identification than dark color alone.
    - >-
      A young Oriental cockroach has no wings. An adult female also has very
      short wings, so the two are hard to tell apart. Look at size and body
      details to separate them. The female’s eggs are enclosed in a single dark
      capsule. Do not rush to identify it from a damaged or blurry photo.
    - >-
      An adult German cockroach (Blattella germanica) is usually smaller and
      brownish, with two dark stripes on the shield just behind its head. An
      Oriental cockroach is larger and uniformly dark, though one feature alone
      is not enough to tell them apart. The [common bed bug](cimex-lectularius)
      is a different insect: the adult is smaller and oval, has no wings it can
      use to fly, and feeds on blood.

    answer: An adult Oriental cockroach is usually about 22–27 mm long.
  - question: Can it fly?
    answer: >-
      No. The adult male has wings, but they do not fully cover its abdomen. The
      female’s wings are very small. Neither the male nor the female can fly.

      foundations. Outdoors, you may find it under debris and leaves.
