overview: >-
  The Caspian whip snake (Dolichophis caspius) is a large, fast colubrid.
  Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026 treat it as a candidate species: it belongs to the
  eastern group of the D. jugularis superspecies (Nagy et al. 2010) and was
  formerly a geographic subspecies of that complex. It is geographically
  isolated from nominotypical D. jugularis. Reported mitochondrial cytochrome b
  differentiation from D. schmidti is about 6–7%; populations may approach or
  contact in the western Caspian area, and population-level studies are still
  needed. It is on the Georgia checklist (older literature: Nikolskii 1913,
  Bannikov et al. 1977). It is often confused with the red-bellied racer; large
  individuals can also be mistaken for the Levantine viper.

    answer: >-
      Tarkhnishvili et al. 2026 place it in the D. jugularis complex.

  - question: Is it venomous?
    answer: >-

  - question: How does it differ from the red-bellied racer?
    answer: >-
      D. caspius is yellow-bellied; D. schmidti is red-bellied. Both are
      candidates in the same complex, so field ID is not always straightforward.
  - question: Is this the Levantine viper?
    answer: >-
      No. The Levantine viper is a true viper with a vertical pupil. This is a
      round-pupilled, non-venomous racer.
