  A small, non-venomous, ground-dwelling forest Darevskia. The 2026 checklist

overview: |-
  Darevskia derjugini is a small forest lizard that lives on the ground. It has no venom and is not dangerous to people. In Georgia, it is found in the west and in the forested belt of the east. [Identifying it by color alone](/lizards/identifikacia) is unreliable.
  Its head and body, excluding the tail, are often about 4–6.5 cm long; its tail is often nearly twice as long as its body.
habitat: |-
  This lizard is found mainly in forests and at forest edges — on the ground, in grass, and among fallen leaves and roots. It can also be seen beside forest roads, in clearings, and along rivers and streams.
  A 2026 list names Lagodekhi, Sioni, Mulakhi, Racha, Baniskhevi and Kintrishi. These are separate locations. Listing them does not mean the lizard is found everywhere between them.
diet: >-
  The lizard known as ართვინის ხვლიკი mainly eats small animals without backbones, including insects and spiders.
behavior: |-
  ართვინის ხვლიკი is active during the day. In warm weather, it basks in the sun at forest edges and in open areas. When threatened, it quickly runs away and hides.
  It lays eggs.
conservation: |-
  A 2009 global assessment found this species close to being at risk of extinction. Across much of the area where it lives, forests are being lost and its habitat is being split into separate areas. Its numbers are therefore thought to be declining.
  The species was not included in Georgia’s 2014 Red List.
interaction: >-
  The lizard called ართვინის ხვლიკი has no venom and poses no risk to human health. If you catch it, it may bite you or drop its tail to defend itself. Do not pick it up—take a photo and leave it where it is.

    It is a small forest lizard that lives on the ground. Its head is less flat than that of rock-dwelling [Darevskia species](/lizards/darevskia). Color alone is not enough to identify it: its physical features, the habitat it lives in, and where it is found also matter.
  traits:
    -  |-
      This lizard’s head is less flat than that of many Darevskia rock lizards. The scales on its back are rounded. They are mostly smooth, though some have a slightly raised line down the middle.
      Some features that help distinguish this lizard, such as the small holes on its thighs, are visible only up close. Do not catch the lizard to count them.
    -  |-
      The back may be dark brown, olive green, or brownish. Pale stripes often run along the sides, and some lizards also have faint pale spots.
      Males often have a greenish belly and may have blue spots on their sides. Females more often have a yellowish or whitish belly. The rear part of the tail is often turquoise or blue in young lizards.
      Their colours can vary, so the species cannot be identified accurately by colour alone.
    -  |-
      The Artvin lizard may resemble [Darevskia praticola](darevskia-praticola), [Darevskia pontica](darevskia-pontica), and some rock-dwelling Darevskia species found in the same area.
      The Artvin lizard moves mainly on the forest floor. Rock-dwelling species, including [Darevskia brauneri](darevskia-brauneri) and [Darevskia obscura](darevskia-obscura), have flatter heads. You may find them in the same valley as the Artvin lizard. D. brauneri has several scales between the central scale on its temple and the scale by its ear. These are visible only in a clear close-up photo. You cannot tell these species apart by color alone.
    -  |-
      In a 2026 Georgian list, this species appears under the Georgian name “კავკასიური ტყის ხვლიკი”. It is also called “ართვინის ხვლიკი”. This second name refers to Artvin in northeastern Turkey. The specimen used to describe the species is believed to have been found there.
      Older sources also use the name Lacerta derjugini. Its scientific name today is Darevskia derjugini.
    -  |-
      The Artvin lizard is found in western Georgia and the forested belt in the east. It is less likely to be seen on dry plains or in open steppe.

    answer: >-
